## 🏛️ Polity: Making of the Constitution & the Constituent Assembly

The **Constituent Assembly (CA)** that framed the Constitution was set up under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)** — indirectly elected by the provincial assemblies.

**Formation & first meetings.**
- The CA **first met on 9 December 1946**; **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** was the temporary (provisional) chairman.
- **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was elected **permanent President** (11 December 1946).
- **Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution (13 December 1946)** — later the basis of the **Preamble**.
- After Partition, the Assembly's effective strength settled around **299 members**.

**Key committees & people.**
- The **Drafting Committee (set up 29 August 1947) had 7 members and was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** — the **"Father/Chief Architect of the Constitution."**
- **Sir B.N. Rau** was the **Constitutional Advisor**; **H.V.R. Iyengar** was Secretary; **Prem Behari Narain Raizada** was the calligrapher.

**Timeline.** The Constitution took **2 years, 11 months and 18 days** to draft. It was **adopted on 26 November 1949** (now **Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas**) and **came into force on 26 January 1950** (Republic Day, honouring the 1930 Purna Swaraj pledge). The original Constitution had **395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules** (it has grown since).

**Borrowed features.** The makers adapted ideas from many sources — e.g., **parliamentary system, rule of law (UK)**; **Fundamental Rights, judicial review (USA)**; **DPSP (Ireland)**; **emergency, federal scheme (Government of India Act 1935)**; **Concurrent List (Australia)**; **fundamental duties, Five-Year Plans (USSR)**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — CA first met **9 Dec 1946**; **Rajendra Prasad** President; **Ambedkar** chaired the **7-member Drafting Committee**; drafting took **2 y 11 m 18 d**; **adopted 26 Nov 1949**, **in force 26 Jan 1950**. Original: **395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules**.

## 🏗️ Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Mahalakshmi (Deep Dive)

**Mahalakshmi** is the women-focused guarantee — one of the most visible of the Six Guarantees. (Treat all amounts as **"verify latest official figure."**)

**Three components.**
1. **Free bus travel for women** — zero-ticket (often called the "pink ticket") travel for women and girls in **TSRTC ordinary and express buses** within the state. This has driven a very large rise in women's bus ridership (**verify latest ridership/ticket data**).
2. **₹500 LPG cylinder** — subsidised cooking-gas refill for eligible (ration-card/white-card) households.
3. **Monthly financial assistance to women** — a cash transfer announced at **₹2,500/month** to eligible women (**verify current rollout status**).

**Purpose & impact.** The scheme targets **women's mobility, economic empowerment and household cost relief**. The free-travel component especially has had documented effects on women's work/education access and TSRTC occupancy (figures change — verify). Enrolment for the cash/LPG components ran through **Praja Palana**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Mahalakshmi** = (1) **free TSRTC bus travel for women**, (2) **₹500 LPG** cylinder, (3) **monthly cash (₹2,500 announced)**. All rupee/ridership figures must be **verified against the latest official data**.

## 🏺 Telangana History: Socio-Cultural Awakening — Andhra Mahasabha, Library Movement & Press

Under Nizam rule, a **cultural and linguistic awakening** among Telugu-speaking people of Hyderabad State laid the groundwork for later political mobilisation.

**Library movement.** The earliest stirrings came through **libraries** that promoted Telugu literacy and reading. **Komarraju Venkata Lakshmana Rao** was a pioneer; the **Sri Krishna Devaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam (founded 1901, Hyderabad)** and the **Vignana Chandrika Mandali** spread Telugu books and learning — a quiet cultural resistance to the Urdu-dominant official sphere.

**Andhra Jana Sangham → Andhra Mahasabha.** Cultural bodies evolved into a broader platform: the **Andhra Jana Sangham (1921)** (later **Nizam Rashtra Andhra Jana Kendra Sangham**) grew into the **Andhra Mahasabha**, whose **first session was held in 1930 at Jogipet (Medak)**, presided over by **Suravaram Pratapareddy**. It championed Telugu language, culture, education and, increasingly, civil/political rights — later splitting into moderate and (communist-influenced) radical streams.

**Press & key figures.** The **press** carried the awakening: **Golconda Patrika** (edited by **Suravaram Pratapareddy**), along with journals like **Meezan** and others, voiced Telugu concerns. **Madapati Hanumantha Rao** is remembered as the **"Andhra Pitamaha"** (grand old man of the Andhra movement in Hyderabad); **Suravaram Pratapareddy** authored the landmark cultural history **"Andhrula Sanghika Charitra."**

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Andhra Mahasabha's first session: 1930 at Jogipet (Medak)**, presided by **Suravaram Pratapareddy**. The **Sri Krishna Devaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam (1901)** anchored the **library movement**; **Golconda Patrika** was a key Telugu paper; **Madapati Hanumantha Rao** = "Andhra Pitamaha."

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Library (1901 Bhasha Nilayam) → Andhra Jana Sangham (1921) → Andhra Mahasabha (1930, Jogipet).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Andhra Mahasabha's first session was at Jogipet (Medak) in 1930**, not Hyderabad city; and it grew out of the **Andhra Jana Sangham (1921)**. Don't confuse it with the Andhra Mahasabha of the Madras Presidency.

💡 **Example:** The library movement's promotion of Telugu reading directly fed the Andhra Mahasabha's later demand for Telugu-medium education and cultural rights under the Nizam.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Golconda Patrika (Suravaram Pratapareddy editor).
- Madapati Hanumantha Rao = "Andhra Pitamaha."

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: The Revolt of 1857

The **Revolt of 1857** — the "First War of Independence" (V.D. Savarkar's term) or the "Sepoy Mutiny" — was the first large-scale challenge to British rule.

**Spark & causes.** The immediate trigger was the **greased cartridges** of the new **Enfield rifle** (rumoured to use cow/pig fat, offending Hindu and Muslim sepoys). **Mangal Pandey** fired the first shot at **Barrackpore (29 March 1857)**; the revolt proper began at **Meerut on 10 May 1857**, and sepoys marched to **Delhi**. Deeper causes were **political** (annexations via the **Doctrine of Lapse**, e.g., Jhansi, Satara; the annexation of Awadh), **economic** (ruin of artisans, heavy revenue), **social/religious** (reforms seen as interference), and **military** (discontent in the Company army).

**Centres & leaders.**
- **Delhi** — **Bahadur Shah Zafar** (proclaimed emperor), general **Bakht Khan**.
- **Kanpur** — **Nana Saheb** and **Tantia Tope**.
- **Lucknow (Awadh)** — **Begum Hazrat Mahal**.
- **Jhansi** — **Rani Lakshmibai**.
- **Bihar (Jagdishpur)** — **Kunwar Singh**.

**Aftermath.** The revolt was **suppressed by 1858**. Its biggest consequence was the **Government of India Act 1858**, which **ended East India Company rule** and transferred governance to the **British Crown** (the **Queen's Proclamation, 1858**, promised non-interference and a Secretary of State for India).

**Hyderabad angle.** The **Nizam largely remained loyal** to the British; but **Turrebaz Khan and Maulvi Allauddin led an attack on the British Residency in Hyderabad (1857)** — a notable local episode of resistance.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Revolt began **Meerut, 10 May 1857** (Mangal Pandey at Barrackpore, 29 Mar 1857). Leaders: **Delhi–Bahadur Shah Zafar/Bakht Khan, Kanpur–Nana Saheb/Tantia Tope, Lucknow–Begum Hazrat Mahal, Jhansi–Rani Lakshmibai, Bihar–Kunwar Singh**. Result: **GoI Act 1858, Crown rule**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Meerut 10 May 1857 → Delhi (Zafar) → Kanpur (Nana/Tantia) → Lucknow (Hazrat Mahal) → Jhansi (Lakshmibai) → Crown rule 1858.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The revolt **started at Meerut (10 May)**; **Mangal Pandey's act (29 Mar, Barrackpore)** was the precursor. In Hyderabad, the **Nizam stayed loyal**, but **Turrebaz Khan/Maulvi Allauddin** attacked the Residency.

💡 **Example:** The **Doctrine of Lapse** (which annexed Jhansi) is why **Rani Lakshmibai** became a central 1857 figure — a direct cause-and-leader link.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Enfield greased cartridges; Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie).
- Leaders by centre (Delhi/Kanpur/Lucknow/Jhansi/Bihar).
- GoI Act 1858 + Queen's Proclamation → Crown rule; Hyderabad Residency attack (Turrebaz Khan).

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Irrigation Projects — Major, Medium & Lift Schemes

![Telangana rivers map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

Because Telangana sits high on the plateau, its irrigation relies on a mix of **reservoirs** and massive **lift schemes** on the Godavari and Krishna.

**Major projects — Godavari basin:**
- **Sri Ram Sagar Project (SRSP) at Pochampad** — a major Godavari reservoir (Nizamabad/Nirmal).
- **Nizam Sagar** (on the Manjira).
- **Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Scheme** — the world's largest multi-stage lift scheme (Medigadda–Annaram–Sundilla barrages).
- **Devadula (J. Chokka Rao) Lift Scheme** — lifts Godavari water to Warangal uplands.

**Major projects — Krishna basin:**
- **Nagarjuna Sagar** — a giant masonry dam (shared with Andhra Pradesh), Nalgonda.
- **Srisailam** — hydro + irrigation reservoir (border).
- **Priyadarshini Jurala** — first/uppermost Krishna project in Telangana.
- **Palamuru-Rangareddy** and **Kalwakurthy** lift schemes (from Srisailam) for south Telangana.

**Medium & minor.** Many **medium projects** (e.g., on tributaries) and a vast network of **minor irrigation tanks (cheruvu)** — revived under **Mission Kakatiya** (tank/cheruvu restoration programme). Tanks are historically central to Telangana's irrigation (the Kakatiya chain-tank legacy).

| River basin | Key projects |
|---|---|
| Godavari | SRSP (Pochampad), Nizam Sagar, Kaleshwaram, Devadula |
| Krishna | Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Jurala, Palamuru-Rangareddy, Kalwakurthy |
| Tanks | Mission Kakatiya (minor irrigation revival) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Godavari basin: **SRSP (Pochampad), Nizam Sagar, Kaleshwaram, Devadula**. Krishna basin: **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Jurala, Palamuru-Rangareddy, Kalwakurthy**. **Mission Kakatiya** revived minor irrigation **tanks (cheruvu)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Godavari: SRSP + Kaleshwaram + Devadula; Krishna: NSP + Srisailam + Jurala + Palamuru.**" Tanks = Mission Kakatiya.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Kaleshwaram/Devadula/SRSP = Godavari**; **Nagarjuna Sagar/Srisailam/Jurala/Palamuru-Rangareddy/Kalwakurthy = Krishna**. Don't put a Krishna scheme on the Godavari or vice-versa. Nizam Sagar is on the **Manjira** (Godavari tributary).

💡 **Example:** **Devadula** lifts Godavari water up to the Warangal plateau — the same "lift because the land is above the river" logic as Kaleshwaram and Palamuru-Rangareddy.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Nagarjuna Sagar & Srisailam (Krishna, shared with AP).
- Jurala = first Krishna project in Telangana.
- Mission Kakatiya = minor-irrigation tank revival.
