## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Schedules, Key Articles & Landmark Cases

### 📖 Deep Dive

The Constitution has **12 Schedules**:

| Schedule | Content |
|---|---|
| 1st | States & Union Territories |
| 2nd | Salaries/emoluments of high offices |
| 3rd | Forms of oaths & affirmations |
| 4th | Allocation of Rajya Sabha seats |
| 5th | Administration of **Scheduled Areas & Tribes** |
| 6th | **Tribal areas** (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram) |
| 7th | **Union/State/Concurrent lists** |
| 8th | **22 languages** |
| 9th | Laws immune from judicial review (1st Amendment) |
| 10th | **Anti-defection** (52nd Amendment) |
| 11th | Panchayats (73rd) |
| 12th | Municipalities (74th) |

**Landmark cases:**

| Case | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** | **Basic structure** doctrine |
| **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** | Due process under Art 21 |
| **Minerva Mills (1980)** | Balance of FR & DPSP |
| **Indra Sawhney (1992)** | 50% reservation cap, creamy layer |
| **S.R. Bommai (1994)** | Art 356, secularism as basic structure |
| **Puttaswamy (2017)** | Right to privacy |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **9th Schedule** (added by the **1st Amendment, 1951**) shields listed laws from judicial review, but **I.R. Coelho (2007)** held that 9th-Schedule laws added **after 24 April 1973** are still subject to the **basic-structure** test. The **10th Schedule** (52nd Amendment, 1985) is the **anti-defection** law.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **8th Schedule = 22 languages**; **10th = anti-defection**; **9th = judicial-review immunity (now limited)**. The **basic-structure doctrine** comes from **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**, not Golaknath.

### 💡 Example
The **basic-structure doctrine (Kesavananda, 1973)** is the single most important constitutional safeguard — it lets courts strike down even a **constitutional amendment** that destroys the Constitution's core.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **7th** = 3 lists; **8th** = 22 languages; **9th** = judicial immunity; **10th** = anti-defection.
- **Kesavananda (1973)** = basic structure; **Puttaswamy (2017)** = privacy.
- **9th Schedule** post-1973 laws → basic-structure test (**I.R. Coelho 2007**).

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which Schedule contains the anti-defection law, and which amendment added it?*
→ The **10th Schedule**, added by the **52nd Amendment (1985)**.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: 1970-2000 — TPS, Naxalism & Identity Politics

### 📖 Deep Dive

After the **1969 agitation** and the **Six-Point Formula (1973)**, the **Telangana Praja Samithi (TPS)** merged back with the **Congress** (Marri Chenna Reddy), and the separate-statehood demand went dormant for a while — but regional grievances persisted.

**Naxalite/Maoist movement:** through the **1970s-80s**, a radical-left peasant movement grew strongly in the Telangana districts of **Karimnagar, Warangal, and Adilabad**. The **Jagityal Jaitrayatra (1978)** was a landmark mobilisation; the **People's War Group (PWG)** was founded in **`1980` by Kondapalli Seetharamaiah**, organising **Rytu Coolie Sanghams** against feudal landlords.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **People's War Group (PWG)** — formed in **1980** — made Telangana (especially the **Karimnagar-Warangal-Adilabad** belt) a centre of the naxalite movement, raising issues of **land, wages, and forest rights** and keeping regional identity politically alive.

**Identity & the 1990s:** economic liberalisation and the **IT boom around Hyderabad (Cyberabad)** deepened the sense of **regional imbalance** (development concentrated in Hyderabad vs backward Telangana districts), setting the stage for the revived statehood movement. The **Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS)** would be founded by **K. Chandrashekar Rao in 2001** (just after this period).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**TPS merges (1973) → naxalism (Jagityal 1978, PWG 1980) → Hyderabad IT boom & regional imbalance (1990s) → TRS 2001**".

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **PWG** was formed in **1980** by **Kondapalli Seetharamaiah**. The **TRS** was founded in **2001** (KCR) — just **after** the 1970-2000 window. The naxalite movement's strength in Telangana is tied to **land/feudal** grievances.

### 💡 Example
The contrast between a booming **IT Hyderabad** and the backward rural Telangana districts in the 1990s crystallised the "**internal-colonialism/regional-imbalance**" argument that powered the 2001-2014 statehood movement.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **TPS merged with Congress** (post-1973).
- **Jagityal Jaitrayatra 1978**; **PWG 1980** (Kondapalli Seetharamaiah).
- 1990s: **Hyderabad IT boom** + **regional imbalance**.
- **TRS founded 2001** (KCR) — just after this period.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who founded the People's War Group and in which year?*
→ **Kondapalli Seetharamaiah**, in **1980**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Rowlatt, Jallianwala Bagh & Khilafat

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Rowlatt Act (1919):** the "**Black Act**" allowed **detention without trial** ("no vakil, no dalil, no appeal"). Gandhi launched the **Rowlatt Satyagraha** — his **first all-India** agitation — with a **hartal on 6 April 1919**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Jallianwala Bagh massacre** occurred on **`13 April 1919`** at **Amritsar**, when **General Dyer** ordered firing on an unarmed **Baisakhi-day** crowd (protesting the arrest of **Satyapal and Saifuddin Kitchlew**), killing hundreds. **Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood** in protest; the **Hunter Commission** inquired. **Udham Singh** later assassinated **Michael O'Dwyer (1940)**.

**Khilafat Movement (1919-24):** Indian Muslims protested the harsh treatment of the **Ottoman Caliph (Turkey)** after WWI (Treaty of Sèvres). Led by the **Ali brothers (Shaukat & Mohammad Ali)** and **Maulana Azad**, it was **linked by Gandhi with the Non-Cooperation Movement** to forge **Hindu-Muslim unity**. It faded after **Turkey (Ataturk) abolished the Caliphate in 1924**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Rowlatt (Black Act) → Jallianwala Bagh 13 Apr 1919 (Dyer) → Khilafat (Ali brothers) + Non-Cooperation**". Tagore returned his knighthood; Udham Singh avenged (1940).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **General Dyer** ordered the firing; **Michael O'Dwyer** was the Punjab Lt-Governor (killed by Udham Singh in 1940) — don't confuse the two. The **Khilafat** issue was **Turkey's Caliph**, and Gandhi **merged** it with Non-Cooperation.

### 💡 Example
The pairing of **Khilafat with Non-Cooperation** produced the high-water mark of **Hindu-Muslim unity** in the freedom struggle — its later collapse (post-1924) is equally examined.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Rowlatt Act 1919** — detention without trial; hartal **6 Apr 1919**.
- **Jallianwala Bagh** — **13 Apr 1919**, Dyer, Amritsar; Tagore renounced knighthood.
- **Khilafat 1919-24** — Ali brothers; linked to Non-Cooperation; ended 1924.
- **Udham Singh** killed **O'Dwyer (1940)**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who ordered the Jallianwala Bagh firing, and who was later assassinated by Udham Singh?*
→ **General Dyer** ordered the firing; **Michael O'Dwyer** was assassinated (1940).

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Rivers — The Godavari System

![Telangana Rivers Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Godavari** is the **largest river in peninsular India** — the "**Dakshina Ganga**." It rises at **Trimbak (Trimbakeshwar), Nashik, Maharashtra**, flows about **`1,465 km`** through **Maharashtra → Telangana → Andhra Pradesh**, and empties into the **Bay of Bengal** (Antarvedi).

**In Telangana:** it enters near **Kandakurthy (Nizamabad)**, where the **Manjira and Haridra** join it (a Triveni Sangamam), passing **Basara** and flowing along the northern belt.

**Major tributaries:**

| Tributary | Note |
|---|---|
| **Pranhita** | **Largest tributary** (formed by Wardha + Wainganga + Penganga); joins at Kaleshwaram |
| **Manjira** | Joins near Kandakurthy; hosts **Nizam Sagar & Singur** |
| **Indravati, Sabari** | Join further downstream |
| **Kinnerasani, Kadam, Manair, Taliperu** | Telangana tributaries |

**Major projects:** **Sri Ram Sagar (Pochampad), Kaleshwaram (Medigadda), Nizam Sagar, Singur, Kadam, Devadula LIS**, and **Bhadrachalam** lies on its banks.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Godavari** ("Dakshina Ganga") rises at **Trimbak (Nashik)**; its **largest tributary is the Pranhita**. **Kaleshwaram** is built near the **Godavari-Pranhita confluence** (Bhupalpally). *(Verify latest project figures.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Godavari = Dakshina Ganga, Trimbak source, Pranhita biggest tributary, Kaleshwaram at the confluence**". Enters Telangana at **Kandakurthy** (with Manjira).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Godavari's source is **Trimbak/Nashik** (Maharashtra), not in Telangana. Its **largest tributary is the Pranhita** (not the Manjira or Indravati). **Kaleshwaram = Godavari** (don't confuse with the Krishna's Palamuru-Rangareddy).

### 💡 Example
The **Pranhita's** huge catchment (Wardha+Wainganga+Penganga) makes the **Kaleshwaram** lift at its confluence with the Godavari a strategically sited project — a geography-plus-engineering point. *(Verify latest.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Godavari** = largest peninsular river ("Dakshina Ganga"), source **Trimbak/Nashik**.
- **Pranhita** = largest tributary; enters Telangana at **Kandakurthy** (Manjira).
- Projects: **SRSP, Kaleshwaram, Nizam Sagar, Singur, Devadula**.
- **Bhadrachalam** on the Godavari.
