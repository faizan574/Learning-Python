## 🏛️ Polity: Governor, Chief Minister & State Legislature

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Governor (Art 153-162).** The **constitutional head** of the state (nominal executive), **appointed by the President** (holds office during the President's pleasure, term 5 years). Qualifications: citizen, **35+**.
- **Powers:** executive (appoints CM, ministers, Advocate General, State EC, Vice-Chancellors), legislative (summon/prorogue, dissolve Assembly, assent to bills, **reserve a bill for the President — Art 200/201**, address, nominate members), and **discretionary powers** (appointing CM in a hung house, recommending **President's Rule (Art 356)**, reserving bills). The Governor has **no pardoning power for death sentences** (only the President, Art 72; Governor's Art 161 excludes death-sentence pardon and court-martial).

**Chief Minister & State Council of Ministers (Art 163-164).** The **real executive**. The CM is appointed by the Governor (leader of the majority in the Assembly); the CoM **aids and advises** the Governor and is **collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly**. **91st Amendment**: state CoM ≤ **15%** of Assembly strength (minimum 12).

**State Legislature (Art 168-212).**
- **Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)** — directly elected; max **500**, min 60; term 5 years; presided by the **Speaker**.
- **Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)** — the **upper house**, present in only some states (incl. Telangana); max **1/3 of the Assembly** strength (min 40); a **permanent** body (1/3 retire every 2 years); members via various constituencies (graduates, teachers, local bodies, MLAs, Governor's nominees). Created/abolished by **Parliament** on the state Assembly's resolution (Art 169).

A **money bill** in a bicameral state originates only in the **Assembly**; the Council can only **delay it 14 days** (weaker than the Rajya Sabha).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Governor (Art 153-162): President-appointed nominal head; reserves bills (Art 200/201), recommends Art 356; **no death-sentence pardon**. CM & CoM (Art 163-164): real executive, **collectively responsible to the Assembly**, ≤15% (91st Amdt). Legislative Council (Art 169): optional, ≤1/3 of Assembly, permanent.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Governor is **appointed** (not elected) and has **discretionary** powers (hung house, Art 356, reserving bills). The **Legislative Council is weaker** than the Rajya Sabha (can only delay a money bill 14 days, ordinary bills ~3-4 months). State CoM responsible to the **Assembly** only.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Can a Governor pardon a death sentence?* → **No** — that power is the President's (Art 72); the Governor's Art 161 excludes death sentences.

- CM & CoM: Art 163-164; responsible to Assembly; 15% cap.
- Assembly max 500; Council ≤1/3 (Art 169), permanent.

## 🏰 Telangana History: Musunuri Nayaks, Recherla Velamas & the Bahmani Kingdom

### 📖 Deep Dive

After the fall of the Kakatiyas (1323), Telangana saw a struggle among successor powers.

**Musunuri Nayaks (c. 1323-1368).** After Warangal fell to the Delhi Sultanate, **Musunuri Prolaya Nayaka** and his cousin **Musunuri Kapaya Nayaka** led a **confederation of ~75 Nayakas** that **recaptured Warangal (~1336)** and expelled the Sultanate's forces — a notable resistance restoring Telugu rule. **Kapaya Nayaka** ruled from Warangal but later faced pressure from the **Bahmanis** and the **Recherla Velamas**, and declined (defeated at Bhimavaram ~1368).

**Recherla Velamas (Rachakonda & Devarakonda).** A **Velama** clan that carved out kingdoms with capitals at **Rachakonda** and **Devarakonda** (Nalgonda region) in the 14th-15th centuries; rivals of the Musunuris and the Reddis; often allied with or opposed the Bahmanis. **Singama Nayaka** and **Anapota/Anavota** were prominent.

**Bahmani Kingdom (1347-1518).** Founded by **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (Zafar Khan)** after a revolt against Muhammad bin Tughlaq; capital first **Gulbarga (Ahsanabad)**, later **Bidar**. It expanded into Telangana (took **Golconda, Warangal region**). The famous minister **Mahmud Gawan** (Persian; reforms, Bidar madrasa) served here. The Bahmani kingdom later **disintegrated into the five Deccan Sultanates** (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar, Berar) — from which the **Qutb Shahis of Golconda** emerged in Telangana.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Musunuri Nayaks** (Prolaya & Kapaya; recaptured Warangal ~1336 from the Sultanate). **Recherla Velamas** (Rachakonda/Devarakonda, Nalgonda). **Bahmani Kingdom** (1347, Hasan Bahman Shah; Gulbarga→Bidar; Mahmud Gawan) → split into 5 Deccan Sultanates → **Qutb Shahis (Golconda)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Musunuri Kapaya Nayaka** led the Telugu recovery of Warangal (~1336). **Recherla Velamas** ruled from **Rachakonda/Devarakonda**. The **Bahmani** kingdom (Gulbarga→Bidar) fragmented into **five** Deccan Sultanates, spawning the **Qutb Shahis**.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Morley-Minto & Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms; Lucknow Pact; Home Rule

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909).** Expanded the legislative councils; introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** (communal representation — "sowed the seeds of partition"); allowed some non-official majorities in provincial councils but retained official control. **John Morley** (Secretary of State), **Lord Minto** (Viceroy).

**Lucknow Pact (1916).** A joint **Congress-Muslim League** agreement (at the Lucknow session) — the **reunion of Moderates and Extremists** in the Congress (Tilak's return) **and** Congress-League unity. The Congress **accepted separate electorates** (a concession to the League) in exchange for a united demand for **self-government/dominion status**. Architects: **Tilak, Jinnah** (then "ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity"), Annie Besant's influence.

**Home Rule Leagues (1916).** Inspired by the Irish Home Rule movement:
- **Tilak's Home Rule League (April 1916, Poona)** — active in Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, C.P., Berar.
- **Annie Besant's Home Rule League (Sept 1916, Madras)** — active in the rest of India; **George Arundale, B. P. Wadia** associates. Besant was briefly interned (1917), sparking protest; she became the **first woman Congress President (1917)**. The movement demanded **self-government (swaraj) within the empire** and revived mass political activity during WWI.

**Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act, 1919).** Introduced **dyarchy in the provinces** (subjects split into "reserved" and "transferred"), bicameralism at the centre, expanded franchise; **Edwin Montagu** (Secretary of State), **Lord Chelmsford** (Viceroy). It was followed by the **Rowlatt Act (1919)** repression.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Morley-Minto 1909** (separate electorates for Muslims). **Lucknow Pact 1916** (Congress-League unity + Moderate-Extremist reunion; Tilak & Jinnah). **Home Rule Leagues 1916** (Tilak-Poona, Besant-Madras). **Montagu-Chelmsford 1919** (dyarchy in provinces).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Separate electorates** = Morley-Minto **1909**. **Dyarchy in provinces** = Montagu-Chelmsford **1919** (abolished later by the 1935 Act). **Lucknow Pact 1916** = Congress **accepted** separate electorates for League unity. Two Home Rule Leagues (**Tilak & Besant**).

### 💡 Example

*Q: Which reform introduced separate electorates for Muslims?* → The **Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909)**.

- Lucknow Pact 1916: Congress-League unity; Tilak-Jinnah.
- Home Rule 1916: Tilak (Poona), Besant (Madras).
- Montagu-Chelmsford 1919: provincial dyarchy.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Nalgonda & Suryapet

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Nalgonda** and **Suryapet** form the **southern-central** part of Telangana, on the **Krishna** river system, bordering Andhra Pradesh.

**Physiography & rivers:** part of the granite-gneiss plateau; the **Krishna** flows along the southern boundary; **Nagarjuna Sagar** dam (shared with AP) is on the Krishna at the Nalgonda border; **Musi** and **Paleru/Dindi** are other streams. The region is **semi-arid** and historically **fluoride-affected** (Nalgonda was infamous for **fluorosis** due to high-fluoride groundwater — a major public-health issue addressed via **Mission Bhagiratha** piped water).

**Economy & minerals:** **limestone** (major cement belt — Nalgonda/Suryapet host large cement plants), **uranium** deposits (Nallamala/Lambapur — controversial mining proposals near the Nallamala tiger reserve), agriculture (paddy under Nagarjuna Sagar command, cotton, redgram).

**Heritage:**
- **Nagarjunakonda / Nagarjuna Sagar** — the submerged **Ikshvaku Buddhist** site (relocated to an island museum); a major Buddhist heritage centre.
- **Rachakonda & Devarakonda forts** (Recherla Velama capitals).
- **Kolanupaka (Kulpakji)** — an important **Jain** temple (Rishabhanatha) and a Someshwara temple (Nalgonda).
- **Yadadri** (Yadagirigutta) — Lakshmi Narasimha temple (now Yadadri Bhuvanagiri, carved from Nalgonda).
- **Panagal** (Chhaya Someshwara temple, famous for the trikuta/"shadow pillar").

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Nalgonda/Suryapet: **Krishna** belt, **Nagarjuna Sagar**; **fluorosis** legacy (fixed via **Mission Bhagiratha**); **limestone/cement** & **uranium** (Nallamala). Heritage: **Nagarjunakonda** (Ikshvaku Buddhist), **Rachakonda/Devarakonda** (Velamas), **Kolanupaka** (Jain), **Panagal** (Chhaya Someshwara).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Nalgonda** = **fluorosis/fluoride** groundwater issue (Mission Bhagiratha response) and **cement (limestone)**; **Nagarjuna Sagar** (Krishna) is here. **Kolanupaka** = Jain temple; **Panagal** = Chhaya Someshwara (shadow pillar).
