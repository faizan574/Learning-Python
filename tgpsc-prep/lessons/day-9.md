## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity

### Let's learn about **DPSP (Articles 36–51) & Fundamental Duties (51A)**

Part IV — the ideals the State should follow — plus the citizen's duties added later. Borrowed from Ireland; the "conscience of the Constitution".

**Directive Principles (Art 36–51):**
- **Non-justiciable** (not enforceable by courts) but **"fundamental in the governance"** of the country (`Art 37`).
- Classified into three types:

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Socialist** | Equal pay for equal work (39d), livelihood (39a), right to work/education (41), living wage (43), free legal aid (39A) |
| **Gandhian** | Village panchayats (40), cottage industries, prohibition of intoxicants (47), cow protection (48), promotion of SC/ST/weaker sections (46) |
| **Liberal-Intellectual** | Uniform Civil Code (44), early childhood care <6 yrs (45), separation of judiciary from executive (50), protection of monuments (49), international peace (51) |

**Fundamental Duties (Art 51A):**
- **Added by the 42nd Amendment (1976)** on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s recommendation (originally **10**; the **11th** — education for children 6–14 — added by the **86th Amendment, 2002**).
- **Non-justiciable**; examples: respect the Constitution, flag & anthem; cherish the freedom struggle ideals; protect sovereignty; promote harmony; safeguard environment; develop scientific temper; etc.

**FR vs DPSP conflict:** **Minerva Mills (1980)** held the **harmony/balance** between Parts III and IV is part of the **basic structure**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — DPSPs are borrowed from **Ireland** (which took them from Spain); they are **non-justiciable** but **fundamental in governance** (`Art 37`). Fundamental Duties came via the **42nd Amendment (1976)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — DPSP three types "**S-G-L**": Socialist, Gandhian, Liberal. UCC = **Art 44**; Panchayats = **Art 40**; Judiciary-Executive separation = **Art 50**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Both DPSP **and** Fundamental Duties are **non-justiciable**. Fundamental Duties = `Art 51A` (only one article). Originally **10** duties; the 11th added in **2002 (86th Amd)**.

**💡 Example:** **MGNREGA** (right to work, `Art 41`) and the **73rd Amendment panchayats** (`Art 40`) show DPSPs being converted into real laws — the classic "directive to statute" question.

**🎯 Exam Pointers:**
- Art 37: DPSP non-justiciable but fundamental in governance.
- Art 39A (free legal aid), 43A (workers' participation), 48A (environment) — added by 42nd Amd.
- Gandhian: 40, 43, 46, 47, 48. Liberal: 44, 45, 49, 50, 51.
- Fundamental Duties: 42nd Amd (1976), Swaran Singh Committee, 10→11 (2002).
- Minerva Mills: FR-DPSP balance = basic structure.

## 📘 Subject 2: Telangana Schemes & Important Incidents

### Let's learn about **Musi Rejuvenation & HYDRAA (Urban Governance)**

Two flagship urban "incidents" of the current government — cleaning the Musi and protecting Hyderabad's lakes/land from encroachment.

**Musi Riverfront / Rejuvenation:**
- Ambitious project to **clean and redevelop the Musi river** through Hyderabad — sewage treatment (STPs), riverfront development, relocation of riverbed dwellers (*verify latest scope/figures*).
- Modeled loosely on Sabarmati/Thames-style riverfronts; controversy over **displacement** and cost.

**HYDRAA (Hyderabad Disaster Response and Assets Protection Agency):**
- Set up in **2024** to **protect government land, lakes (cheruvus), parks and nallahs** from encroachment, and to handle **urban disaster response** (flooding, traffic).
- Became prominent for **demolition drives** on structures encroaching **Full Tank Level (FTL) and buffer zones** of lakes — a major, litigated urban-governance story (*verify current status*).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **HYDRAA (2024)** protects Hyderabad's lakes/government land from encroachment (FTL & buffer zones) and does disaster response; **Musi Rejuvenation** aims to clean and redevelop the Musi.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**HYDRAA = Hyderabad's lake & land protector (FTL demolitions)**; Musi = riverfront clean-up." Both = urban governance 2024–25.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — HYDRAA is about **lakes/land/disaster** (not a welfare scheme). FTL = **Full Tank Level** — encroachments inside FTL/buffer are the demolition targets.

**💡 Example:** When HYDRAA demolished structures on a lake's **FTL/buffer zone**, it triggered debates on encroachment vs due process — a live "urban governance + environment + rule of law" question tying to the Musi clean-up.

**🎯 Exam Pointers:**
- HYDRAA = Hyderabad Disaster Response & Assets Protection Agency, 2024.

- Musi Rejuvenation = clean-up + riverfront (STPs, relocation).
- Both are current-affairs "incidents" — verify latest official details.

## 📘 Subject 3: Telangana History

### Let's learn about **The Asaf Jahis (Nizams of Hyderabad, 1724–1948)**

The dynasty that ruled Hyderabad State — of which Telangana was the core — for over two centuries, from Mughal viceroys to the last princely holdout.

**Founding & the subsidiary alliance:**
- **Mir Qamruddin (Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I)**, a Mughal governor, became independent after the **Battle of Shakar Kheda (1724)** against Mubariz Khan — founding the **Asaf Jahi** dynasty.
- Hyderabad became the **largest princely state**; the Nizam signed the **Subsidiary Alliance with the British (1798)** — the **first Indian ruler** to do so (under Wellesley).

**Administration & reforms:**
- **Salar Jung I** (Diwan, 1853–83) — the great moderniser: new administrative departments, **railways, postal system, currency**, judicial and revenue reforms.
- The Nizams built a composite **Deccani culture** (Urdu, Telugu, Persian); Hyderabad became a centre of learning and architecture.

**Society & the state:**
- Feudal structure of **jagirdars, deshmukhs, and vetti (forced labour)** in the countryside — the roots of the later peasant revolts.
- Nizam VII, **Mir Osman Ali Khan** (1911–48), is a separate deep-dive (tomorrow) — Osmania University, industrialisation, and the 1948 integration.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Asaf Jah I (Nizam-ul-Mulk)** founded the dynasty after the **Battle of Shakar Kheda (1724)**; Hyderabad was the **first Indian state** to join the British **Subsidiary Alliance (1798)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1724 Shakar Kheda (founding) → 1798 Subsidiary Alliance → Salar Jung reforms (1853-83)**." Founder = Asaf Jah I = Nizam-ul-Mulk.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Founder is **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I** (Mir Qamruddin), 1724; **Salar Jung I** was the reforming **Diwan** (not a Nizam). Hyderabad = **first** subsidiary-alliance state (1798).

**💡 Example:** **Salar Jung I's railways and administrative reforms** modernised Hyderabad State — yet the feudal *jagirdari/vetti* system persisted in the villages, setting up the tension that exploded in the 1940s armed struggle.

**🎯 Exam Pointers:**
- Asaf Jah I = Mir Qamruddin, Battle of Shakar Kheda 1724.
- Subsidiary Alliance 1798 (first Indian state); Wellesley.
- Salar Jung I: Diwan 1853–83, modernisation.
- Feudal system: jagirdars, deshmukhs, vetti.
- Largest princely state; Deccani composite culture.

## 📘 Subject 4: Indian National Movement

### Let's learn about **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) & the Swarajists**

Gandhi's first nationwide mass movement — and its dramatic withdrawal.

**Launch & programme:**
- Approved at the **Nagpur Congress (1920)**; goal — **Swaraj within a year**.
- **Non-Cooperation**: boycott of government schools/colleges, courts, councils, foreign cloth; surrender of titles (Gandhi returned his **Kaiser-i-Hind** medal); promotion of **charkha, khadi, national schools** (Jamia Millia, Kashi Vidyapith).
- Merged with the **Khilafat Movement** — peak of Hindu-Muslim unity.

**Spread:** students left colleges; lawyers (**Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Patel, Rajendra Prasad**) gave up practice; no-tax campaigns (Bardoli-area, Guntur, Chirala-Perala, Pedanandipadu).

**Chauri Chaura & withdrawal:**
- On **`5 February 1922`**, a mob at **Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP)** burnt a police station, killing 22 policemen.
- Committed to **non-violence**, Gandhi **withdrew the movement (12 Feb 1922)** — dismaying many leaders; Gandhi was jailed.

**The Swarajists:**
- After the withdrawal, **C.R. Das & Motilal Nehru** formed the **Swaraj Party (1923)** — to **enter legislative councils** and obstruct from within ("**council entry**"), opposing the "No-Changers" (Rajendra Prasad, Patel) who wanted constructive village work.
- Swarajists did well in the **1923 elections** and used councils to embarrass the government.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Gandhi withdrew Non-Cooperation after **Chauri Chaura (5 Feb 1922)** over the violence; the **Swaraj Party (1923, C.R. Das & Motilal Nehru)** then chose **council entry**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Nagpur 1920 launch → Chauri Chaura 1922 stop → Swaraj Party 1923 councils**." No-Changers vs Pro-Changers (Swarajists).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Non-Cooperation launched at **Nagpur (1920)**, withdrawn after **Chauri Chaura (1922)**. Swaraj Party = **Das + Motilal Nehru** (1923), not Jawaharlal.

**💡 Example:** Gandhi calling off a peaking mass movement over **22 deaths at Chauri Chaura** is the textbook case of **means mattering more than ends** in Gandhian politics — a favourite analytical question.

**🎯 Exam Pointers:**
- Nagpur session 1920; Swaraj in a year; charkha/khadi.
- Gandhi returned Kaiser-i-Hind; merged with Khilafat.
- Chauri Chaura 5 Feb 1922; withdrawal 12 Feb 1922.
- Swaraj Party 1923: Das (president), Motilal Nehru (secretary); council entry.
- No-Changers vs Pro-Changers debate.

The human geography — how many people, where they live, and the urban pull of Hyderabad. (Census 2011 is the last full census; **verify against any newer official data**.)

**Population basics (Census 2011):**
- Telangana population **≈ 3.5 crore** (`35 million`) — about 2.9% of India (*verify with latest*).
- **Density** ~`312 persons/km²`; **sex ratio** ~`988` females/1000 males (*verify*).
- **Literacy** ~`66%` (below national average) — with wide urban-rural and gender gaps.

**Urbanisation:**
- Telangana is **more urbanised than the national average**, driven overwhelmingly by **Greater Hyderabad**.
- **Hyderabad district** is the most densely populated & fully urban; **Rangareddy, Medchal-Malkajgiri** form the urban agglomeration.
- Most other districts are **rural and agrarian**; the least urbanised are the tribal north (Adilabad belt) and Palamuru south.

**Tribal & social composition:**
- **STs** ~9%, **SCs** ~15% (*verify latest*); tribal concentration in **Adilabad, Bhadradri, Mahabubabad, Mulugu, Nagarkurnool**.
- The **caste survey (SEEEPC, 2024–25)** updated the social composition (BCs ~56%) — recall Day 7.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana is **more urbanised than India's average**, almost entirely because of **Greater Hyderabad**; Hyderabad district is fully urban and the densest, while the tribal north and Palamuru south are the least urbanised.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**One city, one story**": Hyderabad-Rangareddy-Medchal = the urban engine; the rest = rural Telangana.
