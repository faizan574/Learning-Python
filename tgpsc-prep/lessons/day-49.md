## 🏛️ Polity: Fundamental Rights — Art 19-22

### 📖 Deep Dive

Articles 19-22 form the heart of the **Right to Freedom**.

**Article 19 — Six Freedoms** (available to **citizens** only):
- **19(1)(a)** — speech & expression (includes press, RTI, right to know; not absolute — reasonable restrictions under 19(2): sovereignty, security, public order, decency, defamation, contempt, incitement).
- **19(1)(b)** — assemble peaceably **without arms**.
- **19(1)(c)** — form associations/unions/**co-operative societies** (co-ops added by 97th Amendment).
- **19(1)(d)** — move freely throughout India.
- **19(1)(e)** — reside & settle in any part of India.
- **19(1)(g)** — practise any profession / carry on any occupation, trade or business.
(Note: the original **19(1)(f)** — right to property — was **deleted by the 44th Amendment, 1978**, and made a legal right under **Art 300A**.)

**Article 20 — Protection in respect of conviction for offences.** Three safeguards (available to **all persons**, and **non-suspendable even during Emergency**):
- **No ex-post-facto law** (no punishment under a law made after the act, no heavier penalty than at the time).
- **No double jeopardy** (not prosecuted & punished for the same offence twice).
- **No self-incrimination** (not compelled to be a witness against oneself).

**Article 21 — Protection of life and personal liberty.** "No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**." After **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**, the procedure must be **just, fair and reasonable** (due process read in). Art 21 has been expansively interpreted to include right to **privacy** (*K.S. Puttaswamy, 2017*), dignity, livelihood, clean environment, health, education, speedy trial, etc.

**Article 21A** — **Right to Education** (free & compulsory for children **6-14 years**), added by the **86th Amendment, 2002**.

**Article 22 — Protection against arrest and detention.** Distinguishes **punitive** (ordinary) arrest — right to be informed of grounds, to consult a lawyer, and to be produced before a magistrate within **24 hours** — from **preventive detention** (special safeguards; advisory board, limited period).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Art 19** = 6 freedoms (citizens). **Art 20 & 21** = non-suspendable even in Emergency (44th Amendment). **Art 21A** = RTE (6-14, 86th Amendment). **Art 22** = 24-hour production; preventive-detention safeguards. Right to property deleted (44th Amdt → Art 300A).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Art 20 & 21 cannot be suspended even during a National Emergency** (post-44th Amendment). Art 19 is for **citizens**; Art 20/21/22 for **all persons**. Right to property is **no longer** a Fundamental Right.

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Caste Survey (SEEEPC) & 42% BC Reservation

### 📖 Deep Dive

The Telangana government conducted a comprehensive **Socio-Economic, Educational, Employment, Political and Caste (SEEEPC) Survey** — commonly called the **caste survey / caste census** — in **2024** (household enumeration across the state) to gather caste-wise socio-economic data as a basis for welfare and reservation policy. It is one of the first large-scale state caste surveys after the historic decadal census gap.

Key points (*verify latest official figures/percentages before the exam*, as data and the legislative status keep changing):
- The survey covered a large share of households and captured caste, education, employment, economic and political-participation data.
- Based on the findings, the state moved to **enhance Backward Classes (BC) reservation to `42%`** in education, employment and local bodies — a major policy step. Enabling this required legislative and, for the >50% ceiling issue, engagement with the **Ninth Schedule / central concurrence and judicial scrutiny** (the Indra Sawhney 50% cap looms over such enhancements).
- The **42% BC reservation** in local body elections and its interplay with the overall reservation ceiling has been a live legal/political issue (*verify current status before the exam*).

This connects to the Polity topic (Art 15(4)/16(4), Indra Sawhney 50% cap) — a good cross-subject link.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana's **SEEEPC caste survey (2024)** collected caste-wise socio-economic data statewide; used to justify raising **BC reservation to `42%`** (education/jobs/local bodies). The >50% total-reservation ceiling (Indra Sawhney) is the key legal hurdle. **Verify latest status.**

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The survey's full name is the **SEEEPC** (Socio-Economic, Educational, Employment, Political and Caste) survey — not just a "caste census." Figures and the legal status of 42% BC reservation are evolving: always write "verify latest official figure."

## 🏰 Telangana History: Temples, Monuments & Tourism Heritage

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana's built heritage spans Kakatiya temples to Qutb Shahi monuments:

**Kakatiya-era temples:**
- **Ramappa Temple (Rudreshwara), Palampet, Mulugu** — a **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021)**; built `1213 CE` by **Recharla Rudra** (general of Kakatiya Ganapati Deva); famous for **floating bricks**, Nandi, and Nagara-style sculptures.
- **Thousand Pillar Temple, Hanamkonda (Warangal)** — Kakatiya (1163 CE, Rudradeva), star-shaped, trikutalaya (three shrines: Shiva, Vishnu, Surya).
- **Warangal Fort & Kakatiya Kala Thoranam (Warangal Gate)** — the iconic gateway, part of the state emblem.

**Other temples/pilgrimage:**
- **Bhadrachalam** (Sri Sita Ramachandra Swamy, Bhadradri Kothagudem) — on the Godavari; Ramadasu (Kancherla Gopanna) association.
- **Yadadri (Yadagirigutta)** — Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy; recently redeveloped.
- **Vemulawada** (Raja Rajeshwara Swamy), **Basara** (Gnana Saraswati, one of few Saraswati temples), **Kondagattu** (Anjaneya), **Komuravelli** (Mallanna).

**Qutb Shahi / Islamic monuments (Hyderabad):**
- **Charminar** (1591, Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah), **Golconda Fort** (acoustics, Fateh Rahben gun), **Qutb Shahi Tombs**, **Mecca Masjid**, **Chowmahalla Palace**, **Falaknuma Palace**.

**Waterfalls/eco-tourism:** Bogatha (Telangana's "Niagara"), Kuntala (tallest, on Kadam river, Adilabad), Pochera, Gayathri.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Ramappa** = UNESCO WHS (2021), Kakatiya, Palampet/Mulugu, floating bricks. **Thousand Pillar** & **Warangal Kala Thoranam** = Kakatiya. **Charminar/Golconda/Qutb Shahi Tombs** = Qutb Shahi. **Kuntala** = tallest waterfall.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Ramappa** (not Thousand Pillar) is the **UNESCO** site (2021). Ramappa is in **Mulugu (Palampet)**; the **Kala Thoranam** (Warangal gate) features on the state emblem.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Subhas Chandra Bose & the INA; RIN Revolt

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Subhas Chandra Bose** rose as a radical Congress leader — elected **Congress President at Haripura (1938)** and again at **Tripuri (1939)**, defeating Gandhi's nominee Pattabhi Sitaramayya; but differences with Gandhi led him to **resign** and form the **Forward Bloc (1939)**.

Placed under house arrest, Bose made a **dramatic escape (1941)** to Germany, then reached **Southeast Asia**. He took over the **Indian National Army (INA / Azad Hind Fauj)** — originally formed by **Mohan Singh (1942)** from Indian PoWs, with **Rash Behari Bose**'s Indian Independence League. On **`21 October 1943`**, Bose proclaimed the **Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind)** in Singapore. His slogans: **"Dilli Chalo"** and **"Jai Hind"**; **"Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom."**
- INA had brigades named **Gandhi, Nehru, Azad**, and the **Rani Jhansi Regiment** (women's unit, led by **Capt. Lakshmi Sahgal**).
- The INA fought alongside the Japanese, reaching **Imphal-Kohima (1944)** and hoisting the flag at **Moirang (Manipur)**, but was pushed back.
- Bose reportedly **died in a plane crash at Taipei (Taihoku) on `18 August 1945`** (officially; debated).

**INA Trials (1945-46)** at the **Red Fort** (Shah Nawaz Khan, P.K. Sahgal, Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon) triggered mass sympathy and protests.

**RIN (Royal Indian Navy) Mutiny/Revolt, `February 1946`** — naval ratings in **Bombay** (HMIS Talwar) revolted over pay, food and racial discrimination, spreading to other ports; a significant sign that British control of the armed forces was slipping, hastening the decision to quit India.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Bose: **Haripura/Tripuri** Congress President, **Forward Bloc (1939)**, **Azad Hind Provisional Govt (21 Oct 1943, Singapore)**, "Dilli Chalo/Jai Hind." INA originally by **Mohan Singh (1942)**; **Rani Jhansi Regiment** (Lakshmi Sahgal). **INA trials at Red Fort**; **RIN Revolt Feb 1946** (Bombay).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The INA was **first formed by Mohan Singh (1942)**; Bose **revived and led** it (1943). Bose was Congress President at **Haripura (1938) & Tripuri (1939)**, then formed the **Forward Bloc**.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Population, Demography & Urbanisation

### 📖 Deep Dive

*(Figures below are from **Census 2011** unless noted — the next full census is pending; always "verify latest data" before the exam.)*

- **Population (2011):** Telangana ≈ **`3.5 crore` (35 million)**; it is among the mid-sized states by population.
- **Density:** ≈ **`312` persons/km²** (2011) — moderate.
- **Sex ratio:** ≈ **`988`** females per 1000 males (2011) — relatively favourable, above the national average.
- **Literacy:** ≈ **`66.5%`** (2011) — below the national figure; urban literacy much higher than rural.
- **Urbanisation:** ≈ **`38.9%`** urban (2011), driven heavily by **Hyderabad**; Telangana is more urbanised than the national average, but the urban population is **concentrated around Hyderabad/Ranga Reddy/Medchal**, while many northern/southern districts remain rural.
- **Decadal growth & tribal population:** Scheduled Tribes ≈ **9.3%**, Scheduled Castes ≈ **15.4%** (2011); ST population concentrated in **Adilabad, Bhadradri, Mahabubabad, Mulugu** (Gond, Koya, Lambada).

**Urban structure:** Hyderabad is the dominant metropolis (GHMC); other growing towns — **Warangal, Nizamabad, Karimnagar, Khammam, Ramagundam**. Urban challenges: peri-urban sprawl (ORR/RRR belt), water, and services.
