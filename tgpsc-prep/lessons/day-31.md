## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Fundamental Rights — Art 23-30 & Art 32 (Writs)

### 📖 Deep Dive

This block bundles three rights plus the enforcement mechanism.

**Right against Exploitation (Art 23-24):**
- **Art 23** — prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar (forced labour)** and similar forced labour.
- **Art 24** — prohibits **employment of children below `14` years** in factories, mines, or hazardous work.

**Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25-28):**

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Art 25** | Freedom of conscience; freedom to **profess, practise and propagate** religion |
| **Art 26** | Freedom to **manage religious affairs** |
| **Art 27** | **No taxes** for promotion of any particular religion |
| **Art 28** | **No religious instruction** in wholly State-funded institutions |

**Cultural & Educational Rights (Art 29-30):**
- **Art 29** — protects the **language, script and culture** of any section of citizens.
- **Art 30** — right of **minorities to establish and administer** educational institutions.

**Art 32 — Right to Constitutional Remedies:** the right to move the **Supreme Court** directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Dr. Ambedkar called Art 32 the "heart and soul of the Constitution."** The Supreme Court (Art 32) and High Courts (Art 226) can issue **five writs**: **Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Art 226 (High Court)** is **wider** than **Art 32 (Supreme Court)**: HCs can issue writs both for Fundamental Rights **and for "any other purpose" (ordinary legal rights)**. Art 32 itself is a Fundamental Right and can be suspended only during a National Emergency (except for Art 20-21 enforcement).

The five writs in brief: **Habeas Corpus** ("produce the body" — against illegal detention); **Mandamus** ("we command" — to a public authority to do its duty); **Prohibition** (higher court stops a lower court exceeding jurisdiction); **Certiorari** (higher court quashes a lower court's order); **Quo Warranto** ("by what authority" — challenges a person holding a public office).

### 💡 Example
**Habeas Corpus** can be issued against **both public authorities and private individuals**; **Mandamus and Quo Warranto** lie only against **public** authorities — a favourite discrimination MCQ.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Art 23-24 = **against exploitation**; Art 24 = child labour < **14**.
- Art 25-28 = **religion**; Art 29-30 = **cultural/educational (minorities)**.
- **Art 32** = "heart and soul"; five writs; **Art 226 wider**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which Article did Ambedkar call the "heart and soul" of the Constitution, and why?*
→ **Art 32**, because it provides the **remedy** to enforce all other Fundamental Rights.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Kakatiya Administration, Economy, Society & Architecture

### 📖 Deep Dive

Beyond politics, the **Kakatiyas** built a durable state and a distinctive culture.

**Administration:** the hallmark was the **Nayankara system** — military chiefs (**nayakas**) were assigned land (**nayankaramu**) in return for maintaining troops for the king. This proto-feudal military-tenure system was later inherited and expanded by **Vijayanagara**. The kingdom was divided into **nadus (provinces), sthalas and gramas (villages)**, with local self-government at the village level.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Nayankara system** of the Kakatiyas — land grants to military chiefs in return for service — is a key administrative innovation that influenced the later **Vijayanagara** polity.

**Economy:** predominantly **agrarian**, sustained by extensive **tank (cheruvu) irrigation** — the Kakatiyas built great **artificial lakes** such as **Pakala, Laknavaram, Ramappa, and Ghanpur** tanks. Overseas trade flowed through the **Motupalli** port (the **Abhaya Sasanam** guaranteed safe trade). Textiles and crafts flourished.

**Society & religion:** **Saivism** was dominant (the deity **Swayambhu** at Warangal), alongside **Jainism** and Vaishnavism. Women held a notable position, epitomised by **Rudrama Devi's** rule. **Palkuriki Somanatha** (author of *Basava Purana*) and **Vidyanatha** (*Prataparudra Yasobhushanam*) mark the literary output.

**Architecture (Kakatiya style):** star-shaped (stellate) platforms, ornate **toranas (gateways)**, and richly carved pillars and brackets:

| Monument | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple** | "Floating" **lightweight bricks**, sandbox foundation, **madanika/salabhanjika** sculptures, Nandi — **UNESCO 2021** |
| **Thousand Pillar Temple**, Hanamkonda | **Trikutalaya** (three shrines: Shiva, Vishnu, Surya) |
| **Warangal Fort** | **Swayambhu** temple ruins, four grand **Kirti Toranas** (Kala Thoranam = state emblem) |

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Nayankara + tanks + toranas**": military land-tenure, great irrigation lakes, and gateway architecture define the Kakatiya state. **Ramappa's floating bricks** are its engineering marvel.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Ramappa's bricks are famously **light enough to float** (their key exam fact), and its foundation uses the **"sandbox technique"** for earthquake resistance. The **Thousand Pillar Temple is a trikutalaya** (three shrines).

### 💡 Example
The Kakatiya **tank irrigation** network is the direct ancestor of Telangana's later "Mission Kakatiya" tank-restoration programme — a static-to-modern linkage examiners like.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Nayankara system** → influenced Vijayanagara.
- Great **tanks**: Pakala, Laknavaram, Ramappa, Ghanpur.
- **Ramappa** = floating bricks, sandbox foundation, UNESCO 2021.
- **Thousand Pillar Temple** = trikutalaya (Shiva/Vishnu/Surya).

### ✅ Quick Self-Check

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: WWII, August Offer, Cripps Mission & Quit India

### 📖 Deep Dive

When **World War II** broke out (`Sept 1939`), Viceroy **Linlithgow** declared India a belligerent **without consulting Indian leaders**, prompting the **Congress ministries to resign** (late 1939).

**August Offer (`August 1940`):** to win Indian support for the war, Linlithgow offered: **dominion status** as the objective, expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council, and a promise that no future constitution would be adopted without minority consent (a **minority veto**). **Both** the Congress and the Muslim League **rejected** it.

**Cripps Mission (`March 1942`):** Sir **Stafford Cripps** offered **dominion status after the war**, a constitution-making body, and the **right of provinces to opt out** (a concession toward Pakistan). It failed — Gandhi called it a "**post-dated cheque on a crashing bank**." The Congress rejected the lack of **immediate self-government**; the League rejected the absence of a clear Pakistan.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The failure of the **Cripps Mission (1942)** directly triggered the **Quit India Movement**. The **AICC** passed the **Quit India resolution** at **Bombay on `8 August 1942`**, and Gandhi gave the mantra "**Do or Die (Karo ya Maro)**."

**Quit India Movement (`1942`):** the top leadership was **arrested on `9 August 1942`**, so the movement went **underground and leaderless**. **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisted the flag at **Gowalia Tank (Bombay)**; **Usha Mehta** ran a secret **Congress Radio**; and **parallel governments** arose at **Ballia (UP), Tamluk (Bengal — Jatiya Sarkar), and Satara (Maharashtra — Prati Sarkar)**. It was the **last major mass movement** before independence.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Offer → Cripps → Quit India**": August Offer (1940) rejected → Cripps (1942) "post-dated cheque" → **Quit India (Aug 1942), Do or Die**. Parallel govts: **Ballia, Tamluk, Satara**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Cripps offer's "**opt-out**" clause (provinces could stay out of the Union) was the seed of Partition. Quit India was **spontaneous and underground** because leaders were jailed on day one — not a centrally directed campaign.

### 💡 Example
The **parallel governments** — especially **Tamluk's Jatiya Sarkar** (which ran courts and relief) — show how deep the 1942 upsurge reached at the grassroots.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **August Offer 1940** — dominion status + minority veto; rejected.
- **Cripps 1942** — dominion after war, provincial opt-out; "post-dated cheque."
- **Quit India** — AICC Bombay **8 Aug 1942**, "Do or Die"; leaders arrested **9 Aug**.
- Parallel govts: **Ballia, Tamluk (Jatiya Sarkar), Satara (Prati Sarkar)**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Mahabubnagar / Palamuru

![Mahabubnagar / Palamuru Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/mahabubnagar.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Mahabubnagar (Palamuru)** is Telangana's **southernmost** region. The large erstwhile district has been reorganised into several districts — **Mahabubnagar, Nagarkurnool, Wanaparthy, Jogulamba-Gadwal, and Narayanpet** *(verify current district boundaries)*.

**Physiography & geology:** it sits on some of the **oldest (Archaean) granite-gneiss** rocks of the Deccan plateau. The **Krishna** and its tributary **Tungabhadra** form its southern and south-western boundaries, meeting at **Alampur (Sangameshwara)**.

**Water & irrigation:** historically **drought-prone** (famed for seasonal "Palamuru labour" migration). Key projects: **Priyadarshini Jurala (PJP)**, the **Rajolibanda Diversion Scheme (RDS)**, and lift schemes — **Kalwakurthy, Nettempadu, Koilsagar, Bhima** — plus the mega **Palamuru–Rangareddy Lift Irrigation Scheme** designed to bring Krishna water to the uplands.

**Heritage & culture:**

| Site | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Alampur** | **Navabrahma temples** (Badami Chalukya); **Jogulamba** — one of the **18 Ashtadasa Shakti Peethas** |
| **Pillalamarri** | Giant **700+-year-old banyan tree** (Mahabubnagar) |
| **Gadwal** | Fort and famous **Gadwal sarees**; former **samsthanam** |
| **Wanaparthy** | Prominent former **samsthanam (princely estate)** |
| **Amrabad / Nallamala** | **Amrabad Tiger Reserve** (part of the Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam belt); Mallela Theertham falls |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Alampur** hosts the **Jogulamba** temple, one of the **Ashtadasa (18) Shakti Peethas**, and the **Navabrahma** group of Badami-Chalukya temples on the Tungabhadra. **Pillalamarri** is a centuries-old giant banyan. *(Verify current administrative boundaries and any latest data.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Palamuru = drought + lift schemes + Jogulamba**": drought-prone uplands watered by Jurala/Kalwakurthy/Palamuru-Rangareddy lifts; **Alampur = Jogulamba Shakti Peetha + Navabrahma temples**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Alampur's Jogulamba** is the Shakti Peetha (do not confuse with Bhadrakali at Warangal). The old Mahabubnagar district has been **split** into several districts — quote current boundaries only after **verifying**.

### 💡 Example
The **Palamuru–Rangareddy Lift Irrigation Scheme**, lifting Krishna water hundreds of metres to drought-prone Mahabubnagar uplands, is the flagship attempt to end "Palamuru migration" — a strong geography-plus-development answer.

- Lift schemes: **Jurala, Kalwakurthy, Nettempadu, Palamuru-Rangareddy**.
