## 🏛️ Polity: Amendment (Art 368) & Basic Structure

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Article 368** provides the procedure to amend the Constitution — a balance between **rigidity and flexibility**. There are **three categories** of amendment:

1. **By simple majority** (outside Art 368) — e.g. admission/formation of states (Art 2, 3), citizenship, Schedules like salaries — done like ordinary legislation.
2. **By special majority** (Art 368) — majority of the **total membership** of each House **and** **two-thirds of members present and voting** — for most amendments (FRs, DPSP, etc.).
3. **Special majority + ratification by half the states** — for **federal** provisions (election of President, Union-State legislative distribution, representation of states, the 7th Schedule, Art 368 itself, etc.).

**Basic Structure Doctrine.** Evolution:
- **Shankari Prasad (1951)** & **Sajjan Singh (1965)** — held Parliament could amend **any** part, including FRs.
- **Golaknath (1967)** — reversed: FRs **cannot** be amended.
- **24th Amendment (1971)** — asserted Parliament's power to amend any part including FRs.
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** — the landmark: Parliament can amend any part **but cannot alter the "basic structure"** of the Constitution. (13-judge bench, largest ever.)
- **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)**, **Minerva Mills (1980)**, **Waman Rao (1981)** — applied/strengthened the doctrine (Minerva Mills struck down parts of the 42nd Amendment; limited judicial review and Parliament's amending power are basic features).

**Elements of "basic structure"** (illustrative, not exhaustive): supremacy of the Constitution, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, federalism, secularism, democracy, free & fair elections, sovereignty, unity & integrity, harmony between FRs and DPSP, independence of the judiciary.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Art 368: three amendment types (simple / special / special+state ratification). **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** = basic structure doctrine (amend any part, not the basic structure). **Minerva Mills (1980)** — judicial review & FR-DPSP balance are basic features.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Some amendments (Art 2/3 state formation, Schedules) need only a **simple majority** — outside Art 368. **Golaknath** barred FR amendment; **Kesavananda** allowed it **subject to basic structure**. Basic structure is **judge-made** (not in the text).

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Praja Palana, Praja Vani & Administrative Changes

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Praja Palana** — a large-scale **application/grievance drive** launched by the new government (Dec 2023–Jan 2024) to collect applications from citizens for the **Six Guarantees**, via camps at gram panchayats/wards; the data feeds beneficiary identification (linked to the caste/SEEEPC survey). It was a mass outreach to enrol eligible people for welfare.

**Praja Vani** — a **weekly grievance-redressal programme** (held at the Secretariat/collectorates, typically on a fixed day) where citizens submit petitions directly to the government for resolution — a direct public-interface mechanism (successor in spirit to earlier grievance days).

**Administrative & symbolic changes** by the new government (verify latest):
- **Renaming** — e.g. **"Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Telangana Secretariat"** (the new Secretariat named after Ambedkar); a **125-ft Ambedkar statue** was unveiled at Hyderabad (2023).
- **State emblem / Telangana Thalli** — revision of the **Telangana Thalli** statue/idol imagery and state symbolism; **Formation Day** celebrations format.
- Restoration of **"Jai Telangana"**-era symbolism and district/scheme rebranding.

*(Exact dates, statue heights and renaming specifics should be verified before the exam.)*

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Praja Palana** = mass application drive for the Six Guarantees (camps, links to caste survey). **Praja Vani** = weekly grievance-redressal petitions programme. Administrative changes: **Ambedkar Secretariat** & 125-ft statue, revised **Telangana Thalli**/state symbolism.

## 🏰 Telangana History: Pre-Satavahana Telangana (Megaliths, Kotilingala, Asmaka)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Before the Satavahanas, Telangana had a rich **prehistoric and early-historic** record.

**Prehistory:** Palaeolithic tools have been found across the Deccan; important **Mesolithic/Neolithic** and **megalithic** sites dot Telangana. Notable prehistoric rock-art/ash-mound and Neolithic sites include areas around **Karimnagar, Warangal, Nalgonda, Mahabubnagar**.

**Megalithic culture (Iron Age, c. 1000 BCE onward):** characterised by **burial monuments** — **cairn circles, cists, dolmens, menhirs**, with **Black-and-Red Ware (BRW)** pottery and iron implements. Major megalithic sites in Telangana: **Nagarjunakonda region, Ramannapeta, Maski-type**, and notably **around Karimnagar/Warangal**. These indicate settled agro-pastoral iron-using communities.

**Kotilingala (Kotalingala), Jagtial/Karimnagar** — a key **early-historic urban site** on the Godavari; yielded **punch-marked and early Satavahana coins** (including coins of **Simuka** and pre-Satavahana local rulers like **Gobada, Samagopa**), suggesting it was an early Satavahana **capital/mint** and a pre-Satavahana settlement — crucial evidence for Telangana's antiquity.

**Asmaka (Assaka) Mahajanapada** — one of the **16 Mahajanapadas** (the **only one south of the Vindhyas**), located along the **Godavari** (its capital often identified as **Potali/Podana — possibly Bodhan** in Telangana/adjacent region). It shows Telangana's participation in the 6th-century-BCE political landscape.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Megalithic Iron Age: cairns/cists/menhirs + **Black-and-Red Ware** + iron. **Kotilingala** (Karimnagar/Jagtial) = early-historic site/mint with pre-Satavahana & Simuka coins. **Asmaka** = the **only southern Mahajanapada** (Godavari; capital Potali/Podana ~ Bodhan).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Asmaka (Assaka)** is the **only Mahajanapada south of the Vindhyas**. **Kotilingala** is the key pre/early-Satavahana site (Simuka coins). Megalithic marker = **Black-and-Red Ware** + iron.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: The Revolt of 1857 (Round 4)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Revolt of 1857** (First War of Independence / Sepoy Mutiny) was the first large-scale uprising against the **English East India Company**.

**Causes:**
- **Political** — Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie; annexation of Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, etc.), annexation of **Awadh (1856)** on grounds of misgovernance.
- **Economic** — heavy revenue demands, deindustrialisation, drain of wealth.
- **Social-religious** — fears over interference (Sati abolition, widow remarriage), Christian missionary activity.
- **Military** — sepoy grievances (pay, promotion), and the **immediate spark**: the **greased cartridges** (Enfield rifle) rumoured to be smeared with cow/pig fat.

**Course:** Began at **Meerut on `10 May 1857`**; sepoys marched to **Delhi** and proclaimed **Bahadur Shah Zafar** (Mughal) as the symbolic leader. Spread across the Gangetic plain and central India.

**Centres & leaders:**
- **Delhi** — Bahadur Shah II (General Bakht Khan).
- **Kanpur** — **Nana Saheb** (with Tantia Tope).
- **Lucknow (Awadh)** — **Begum Hazrat Mahal**.
- **Jhansi** — **Rani Lakshmibai**.
- **Bareilly** — Khan Bahadur Khan; **Bihar (Jagdishpur)** — **Kunwar Singh**.

**Suppression & aftermath:** By 1858 the British crushed it. The **Government of India Act 1858** ended Company rule — India came under the **British Crown** (Secretary of State for India + Viceroy; first Viceroy **Lord Canning**). **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1858)** promised non-interference in religion and honouring treaties. The revolt failed due to **lack of unity, central leadership, planning, and limited geographic spread** (south largely unaffected).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — 1857: spark = **greased cartridges**; began **Meerut, 10 May 1857**; symbolic head **Bahadur Shah Zafar**. Leaders: Nana Saheb (Kanpur), Lakshmibai (Jhansi), Hazrat Mahal (Lucknow), Kunwar Singh (Bihar). Aftermath: **GoI Act 1858** → Crown rule; first Viceroy **Canning**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The revolt began at **Meerut (10 May 1857)**, not Delhi (Delhi was seized next). It ended **Company rule** via the **1858 Act** (Crown took over). The **south** was largely unaffected.

### 💡 Example

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Warangal & the Kakatiya Belt

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Warangal (Orugallu)** — the **Kakatiya capital** and Telangana's second-largest urban area (now split into **Warangal, Hanamkonda, Jangaon, Mahabubabad, Mulugu, Bhupalpally** districts).

**Physiography:** part of the plateau; the **Godavari** and tributaries (**Manair, Akeru**) drain the region; extensive **Kakatiya-era tank irrigation** (chain tanks) — **Pakhal, Ramappa, Laknavaram, Ghanpur** lakes are historic tanks, now also tourist/eco spots.

**Heritage:**
- **Warangal Fort** & **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam** (the iconic gateway on the state emblem).
- **Thousand Pillar Temple** (Hanamkonda) and **Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple** (Mulugu — UNESCO 2021).
- **Bhadrakali Temple** (Warangal).

**Economy & culture:** **Kazipet** — a major railway junction; **Kakatiya Mega Textile Park** (Warangal) and a growing textile/handloom hub; **Warangal durries/carpets** (GI). **Eturnagaram** and **Pakhal** wildlife sanctuaries (Mulugu/Warangal); **Medaram** (Sammakka-Saralamma Jatara) in Mulugu. Agriculture: paddy, cotton, chilli.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Warangal (Orugallu)** = Kakatiya capital; **Kala Thoranam** (state emblem), **Thousand Pillar Temple**, **Ramappa (UNESCO)**, **Bhadrakali**. Historic **Kakatiya tanks** (Pakhal, Laknavaram, Ramappa lake). **Kazipet** rail junction; **Warangal durries** (GI); **Medaram** jatara (Mulugu).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Kala Thoranam** (Warangal Gate) features on the **state emblem**. **Ramappa** (UNESCO 2021) is in **Mulugu** (part of the greater Warangal/Kakatiya belt). Kakatiyas pioneered **tank (cheruvu) irrigation**.
