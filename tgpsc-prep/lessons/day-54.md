## 🏛️ Polity: Prime Minister & Council of Ministers

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Prime Minister (Art 75).** The **real head of the executive** (the President is nominal). Appointed by the President — conventionally the **leader of the majority party** in the Lok Sabha. The PM:
- Heads the **Council of Ministers (CoM)**; recommends who is appointed as ministers; allocates portfolios; can recommend dissolution of the Lok Sabha.
- Is the **channel of communication** between the President and the CoM (Art 78).
- Chairs the Cabinet and key bodies (NITI Aayog etc.).

**Council of Ministers (Art 74).** Aids and advises the President; advice is **binding** (42nd Amendment; 44th allows one reconsideration). Three tiers: **Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers**. The **Cabinet** is the inner core.

**Key provisions:**
- **Art 74** — CoM to aid & advise the President.
- **Art 75** — appointment, tenure, responsibility of ministers.
  - Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President** (effectively PM).
  - The CoM is **collectively responsible** to the **Lok Sabha** (Art 75(3)) — a no-confidence motion against the CoM, if passed, brings down the whole government.
  - Individual responsibility (75(2)) — pleasure of the President.
  - **91st Amendment (2003):** total size of the CoM (incl. PM) **≤ 15%** of the Lok Sabha strength; a minister disqualified under the anti-defection law cannot be a minister.
- A minister **not a member of either House** must get elected within **6 months** (Art 75(5)).

**Collective responsibility** is the bedrock of parliamentary government: ministers **swim and sink together**; a decision of the Cabinet binds all ministers, who must publicly support it or resign.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — PM = real executive (Art 75), leader of LS majority; **Art 74** (CoM aids/advises, binding advice) & **Art 75** (collective responsibility to **Lok Sabha**). **91st Amendment**: CoM ≤ **15%** of LS. Non-member minister → elected within **6 months**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The CoM is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (not Rajya Sabha). Ministers hold office during the **President's pleasure** (on PM's advice). CoM size cap (**15%**) came via the **91st Amendment (2003)**.

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Irrigation Policy — Kaleshwaram & Palamuru-Rangareddy

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana's irrigation strategy centres on **lift-irrigation** to raise river water to the upland plateau.

**Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Project (KLIS)** — a multi-stage lift scheme on the **Godavari** (Medigadda/Lakshmi barrage at the confluence with the Pranahita), designed to lift water across barrages (**Medigadda → Annaram → Sundilla**) into a chain of reservoirs (**Mid Manair, Mallanna Sagar, Kondapochamma Sagar**) using massive pumps/underground surge pools. It was projected to irrigate a very large ayacut across many districts.
- **Controversy:** structural problems (sinking of **Medigadda barrage piers, Oct 2023**) led to a **judicial/technical inquiry (Justice P. C. Ghose Commission)** into design, cost overruns and quality (*verify latest status of the commission's findings and repairs before the exam*).

**Palamuru-Rangareddy Lift Irrigation Scheme (PRLIS)** — a mega lift scheme drawing **Krishna** water (from Srisailam backwaters) to the **drought-prone southern districts** (Mahabubnagar, Nagarkurnool, Rangareddy, etc.), with large reservoirs and high-head pumps — a key drought-mitigation project (*verify latest progress and inter-state water allocation status*).

**Policy themes:** **Mission Kakatiya** (restoring minor irrigation tanks), inter-state river disputes (**Krishna & Godavari Water Disputes Tribunals**, KRMB/GRMB — river management boards), and the shift from tanks/wells to large lift schemes.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **KLIS** = multi-stage **Godavari** lift (Medigadda/Mallanna Sagar); **Medigadda pier sinking (Oct 2023)** → **Justice P. C. Ghose Commission**. **PRLIS** = **Krishna** lift for the drought-prone south. Themes: Mission Kakatiya (tanks), KRMB/GRMB (river boards). **Verify latest.**

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Kaleshwaram = Godavari**; **Palamuru-Rangareddy = Krishna**. The **Ghose Commission** probes the Medigadda/KLIS issues. Don't confuse the two rivers or the commissions.

## 🏰 Telangana History: Badami Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas & Kalyani Chalukyas (incl. Vemulawada)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Between the Vishnukundins and the Kakatiyas, Telangana was ruled/influenced by a succession of Deccan powers:

**Badami (Western) Chalukyas (6th-8th c.)** — from **Vatapi (Badami, Karnataka)**; **Pulakeshin II** (greatest) defeated Harsha on the Narmada and clashed with the Pallavas. Their influence extended into Telangana; the **Vemulawada Chalukyas** were a feudatory branch.

**Vemulawada Chalukyas (c. 750-973 CE)** — a **feudatory** dynasty ruling from **Vemulawada (Rajanna Sircilla district)**, subordinate to the **Rashtrakutas**. They patronised **Jainism** (Vemulawada was a major Jain center — **Bommalamma Gutta**) and Telugu-Sanskrit literature; **Pampa** (the "Adikavi" of Kannada, author of *Vikramarjuna Vijaya / Pampa Bharata* and *Adipurana*) was associated with the Vemulawada court under **Arikesari II**.

**Rashtrakutas (753-973 CE)** — overthrew the Badami Chalukyas; capital **Manyakheta (Malkhed)**; **Dantidurga** (founder), **Krishna I** (built the **Kailasa temple at Ellora**), **Amoghavarsha I** (wrote **Kavirajamarga** in Kannada; patron of Jainism), **Govinda III**, **Krishna III**. A dominant Deccan empire; strong Telangana presence.

**Kalyani (Western) Chalukyas (973-1189 CE)** — revived Chalukya power after the Rashtrakutas; capital **Kalyani (Basavakalyan)**; **Tailapa II** (founder), **Someshvara I**, **Vikramaditya VI** (greatest; the **Vikrama era**; his court poet **Bilhana** wrote *Vikramankadeva Charita*, and **Vijnaneshwara** wrote the legal text **Mitakshara**). The **Kakatiyas** began as their feudatories.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Vemulawada Chalukyas** (feudatory of Rashtrakutas; Jain center; **Pampa** at **Arikesari II**'s court). **Rashtrakutas** (Manyakheta; Amoghavarsha–Kavirajamarga; Kailasa temple, Ellora). **Kalyani Chalukyas** (Vikramaditya VI; Mitakshara by Vijnaneshwara).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Vemulawada** (Rajanna Sircilla) = feudatory Chalukyas under **Rashtrakutas**; **Pampa** (Kannada Adikavi) was patronised there. **Mitakshara** (Hindu law) was written under the **Kalyani Chalukya** Vikramaditya VI by **Vijnaneshwara**.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: INC Formation & the Moderate Era (1885-1905)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Indian National Congress** was founded in **December 1885** at **Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay**, with **A. O. Hume** (a retired British civil servant) as the key organiser; the **first session** had **72 delegates**, presided over by **W. C. Bonnerjee** (first president). (The "safety valve" theory about Hume's motives is much debated.)

**The Moderate Phase (1885-1905):** leaders like **Dadabhai Naoroji** ("Grand Old Man of India"; **Drain of Wealth** theory; *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*; first Indian in the British House of Commons), **Gopal Krishna Gokhale** (Gandhi's political guru; Servants of India Society), **Pherozeshah Mehta**, **Surendranath Banerjee**, **Badruddin Tyabji** (first Muslim president), **W. C. Bonnerjee**, **R. C. Dutt**.

**Methods & goals:** the Moderates believed in **constitutional agitation** — petitions, prayers, meetings, resolutions ("**3 P's**"), and faith in British sense of justice. They sought **administrative and economic reforms**, more Indians in the legislature/services, reduction of military expenditure, and did **not** demand full independence (aimed at self-government within the empire).

**Achievements:** economic critique (drain theory exposed colonial exploitation), the **Indian Councils Act 1892** (expanded councils slightly), created a national platform and political consciousness. **Criticism:** methods were slow and elitist ("political mendicancy," said the Extremists).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — INC founded **1885 (Bombay)** by **A. O. Hume**; first president **W. C. Bonnerjee**; 72 delegates. Moderates (1885-1905): **Naoroji (Drain of Wealth), Gokhale, Mehta, Banerjee**; methods = **petitions/prayers (3 P's)**, constitutional; goal = reforms/self-government, not independence.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — INC's **first president = W. C. Bonnerjee** (Hume was the organiser/general secretary, not president). Moderates sought **reforms within the empire** (the "Drain of Wealth" is **Dadabhai Naoroji**). The Indian **Councils Act 1892** was a Moderate-era gain.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Who propounded the "Drain of Wealth" theory?* → **Dadabhai Naoroji**.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- INC 1885, Bombay; Hume (organiser); Bonnerjee (1st president).
- Moderates: Naoroji, Gokhale, Mehta, Banerjee, Tyabji.
- Methods: 3 P's (petition, prayer, protest); constitutional.
- Indian Councils Act 1892 (limited reform).

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Nizamabad & Kamareddy

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Nizamabad (Indur)** and **Kamareddy** form the north-western part of Telangana, bordering Maharashtra/Karnataka.

**Physiography & rivers:** part of the plateau drained by the **Godavari** and its tributary the **Manjira** (which also flows through Sangareddy/Medak). The **Nizam Sagar** dam is built on the **Manjira** (a major early irrigation project, near Achampet/Banswada). The **Sri Ram Sagar Project (SRSP)** at **Pochampad** is on the **Godavari** near Nizamabad — a key multipurpose project (also called Pochampad).

**Agriculture & economy:** Nizamabad is a leading **turmeric** and **maize/sugarcane/red jowar** producer; it hosts a major **turmeric market** and the demand for a **Turmeric Board** (a National Turmeric Board was announced with HQ at Nizamabad — *verify latest status*). Also known for **paddy** and **sugar factories** (Nizam Sugars). **Armoor** is a maize/turmeric hub.

**Heritage & culture:** **Nizamabad Fort (Quilla)**, **Sarangapur Hanuman temple**, **Domakonda Fort** (Kamareddy — a heritage award-winning fort), **Kanteshwar/Neelakanteshwara temple**. Ali Sagar and Alisagar reservoir; the region has a mix of Telangana and border-Marathi/Kannada cultural influences.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Nizamabad (Indur)/Kamareddy: **Nizam Sagar** on the **Manjira**; **SRSP/Pochampad** on the **Godavari**. Leading **turmeric** (Turmeric Board HQ push, verify) & maize (Armoor). Heritage: **Domakonda Fort** (Kamareddy), Nizamabad Fort, Sarangapur Hanuman.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Nizam Sagar = Manjira** (tributary); **SRSP/Pochampad = Godavari** (main). Nizamabad is India's key **turmeric** belt (Turmeric Board demand). **Domakonda Fort** is in Kamareddy.
