## 🏛️ Polity: Supreme Court & High Courts — Judicial Review, PIL, Collegium

The **Supreme Court (`Art 124–147`)** heads a **single integrated judiciary**; **High Courts (`Art 214–231`)** are the apex courts of states.

**Supreme Court.** Currently **34 judges** (1 CJI + 33, by law). Appointed by the **President**; judges hold office till **65 years**. Jurisdictions: **Original** (`Art 131` — Centre-state/inter-state disputes), **Writ** (`Art 32`), **Appellate** (civil, criminal, constitutional — `Art 132-134`), **Advisory** (`Art 143` — President seeks opinion), and it is a **Court of Record** (`Art 129`). **Judicial review** = power to examine the constitutionality of laws/executive actions (implied; basis `Art 13`, 32, 226). Removal of a judge: **"proved misbehaviour or incapacity"** via a parliamentary address (special majority) — same process as impeachment of judges.

**High Courts.** `Art 214` (one for each state; some common to 2+ states/UTs). Judges appointed by the President; retire at **62**. Writ jurisdiction under **`Art 226`** is **wider** than SC's `Art 32` (covers FRs **and** other legal rights). The **Telangana High Court** was established as a separate HC (bifurcated from the common AP HC) at **Hyderabad in 2019** (verify date).

**Collegium system.** Judges of the SC/HCs are appointed through the **collegium** — the **CJI + senior-most judges** recommend appointments/transfers; evolved through the **Three Judges Cases** (1981, 1993, 1998). The **99th Amendment / NJAC Act (2014)** tried to replace it with a **National Judicial Appointments Commission**, but the SC **struck it down (2015)** as violating **judicial independence (basic structure)** — so the collegium continues.

**PIL (Public Interest Litigation).** A relaxation of **locus standi** allowing any public-spirited person to approach the court for the public good (pioneered by Justices **P.N. Bhagwati & V.R. Krishna Iyer**, late 1970s–80s). It expanded access to justice (environment, bonded labour, rights).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — SC = **34 judges**, retire **65**; HC judges retire **62**; HC `Art 226` writ power is **wider** than SC `Art 32`. **Collegium** (CJI + senior judges) appoints judges; **NJAC (99th Amd, 2014) struck down (2015)**. **PIL** relaxed locus standi (Bhagwati/Krishna Iyer).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**SC 34, retire 65; HC retire 62, Art 226 wider; collegium (NJAC struck down 2015); PIL = public access.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **SC judges retire at 65, HC at 62** (don't swap). **Art 226 (HC) is wider** than Art 32 (SC) — covers legal rights too. The **NJAC** was **struck down (2015)**; the collegium continues. Original jurisdiction (`Art 131`) = federal disputes, not PILs.

💡 **Example:** A citizen filing to protect a polluted lake uses **PIL**; a law violating a FR is voided through **judicial review**.

## 🤝 Schemes: Central Schemes Active in Telangana

Many **Centrally Sponsored / Central Sector Schemes** operate in Telangana, often **overlapping** with state schemes — a frequent comparison topic. (Treat coverage figures as **verify latest.**)

**Agriculture/income.** **PM-KISAN** — ₹6,000/year to farmer families in 3 instalments (central). It **overlaps** with the state's **Rythu Bharosa/Rythu Bandhu** (investment support). **PM Fasal Bima Yojana** (crop insurance) overlaps with **Rythu Bima** (state life insurance for farmers).

**Health.** **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY** — ₹5 lakh/family health cover (central). It **overlaps** with the state's **Rajiv Aarogyasri** (₹5–10 lakh cover; verify). Telangana implements a combined/aligned scheme (verify current branding).

**Food/finance/housing.** **PDS/NFSA** (food security) with state rice schemes; **PM Jan Dhan Yojana** (banking); **PM Awas Yojana (PMAY)** housing (overlaps with **Indiramma Indlu / 2BHK**); **Ujjwala** LPG (overlaps with Mahalakshmi ₹500 LPG); **PM-POSHAN** (mid-day meal); **Jal Jeevan Mission** (overlaps with **Mission Bhagiratha**); **MGNREGA** (rural employment); **PM Mudra**, **Ayushman/ABHA**, **Swachh Bharat**.

**Centre–state funding note.** Centrally Sponsored Schemes are **cost-shared** (e.g., 60:40 Centre:State, or 90:10 for special-category/NE); Central Sector Schemes are **100% central**. Telangana often **tops up** or runs **parallel** state schemes.

## 🏺 History: Telangana Literature & Language

Telangana has a rich **Telugu literary** tradition, from classical poets to modern movement writers — a staple TGPSC culture topic.

**Classical.** **Palkuriki Somanatha** (13th c., Kakatiya era) — pioneer of the **desi (native) tradition**, wrote **Basava Purana** and **Panditaradhya Charitra** in Telugu (dvipada metre); a major Veerashaiva literary figure. **Bammera Potana** (15th c., Warangal/Bammera) — translated the **Bhagavatam (Andhra Maha Bhagavatam)** into Telugu; revered for devotion and refusing royal patronage. **Gona Budda Reddy** — **Ranganatha Ramayanam** (first Telugu Ramayana, dvipada).

**Modern & movement poets.**
- **Dasarathi Krishnamacharya** — revolutionary poet against Nizam rule ("**Agnidhara**"); "**Na Telangana koti ratanala veena**."
- **Kaloji Narayana Rao** — "**Praja Kavi**" (people's poet); birthday **9 Sept** is **Telangana Language Day (Telangana Bhasha Dinotsavam)**; wrote "**Naa Godava**."
- **C. Narayana Reddy (Cinare)** — **Jnanpith** awardee ("**Viswambhara**").
- **Gaddar** & **Suddala Hanmanthu** — revolutionary balladeers (Telangana armed struggle & statehood movement songs).
- **Kaloji & Dasarathi** symbolise the Telangana cultural assertion.

**Language & reference works.** The **Telangana dialect** (distinct vocabulary/idiom); **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** compiled "**Andhrula Sanghika Charitra**" (social history) and ran **Golconda Patrika**; the **Vignana Sarvaswam** (encyclopaedia) tradition. **Telangana Sahitya Akademi** promotes state literature.

|---|---|
| Palkuriki Somanatha | Basava Purana (desi/dvipada) |

| Dasarathi | Agnidhara; anti-Nizam poetry |
| Kaloji | Praja Kavi; Lang. Day 9 Sept |
| C. Narayana Reddy | Jnanpith (Viswambhara) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Palkuriki Somanatha** (Basava Purana, desi/dvipada); **Bammera Potana** (Andhra Maha Bhagavatam); **Dasarathi** (Agnidhara, anti-Nizam); **Kaloji** (Praja Kavi; **9 Sept = Telangana Language Day**); **C. ## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Government of India Act 1935 & Provincial Ministries

The **Government of India Act, 1935** was the last major constitutional statute of British India and a **direct source** of much of the Indian Constitution.

**Background.** Based on the **Simon Commission report**, the **Round Table Conferences**, and a **White Paper (1933)**; it was the **lengthiest** British Act then (321 sections, 10 schedules).

**Key features.**
- **All-India Federation** (proposed): uniting British provinces and **princely states** — but it **never came into being** (princely states didn't accede).
- **Provincial Autonomy:** abolished **dyarchy in provinces** (1919) and introduced **provincial autonomy** — provinces became autonomous units with **responsible government**; Governors retained **discretionary/special** powers.
- **Dyarchy at the Centre:** introduced (federal subjects split reserved/transferred) — but **never implemented** (federation didn't form).
- **Bicameral legislatures** in some provinces; **expanded franchise** (~14% of population).
- Provided for a **Federal Court (1937)**, **RBI**, and a **Federal Public Service Commission**.
- **Separate electorates** extended (to depressed classes, women, labour).

**Provincial ministries (1937).** Under the 1935 Act, **elections (1937)** were held; **Congress won majorities** in most provinces and formed **ministries in 8 (of 11) provinces** (Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar, CP, Orissa, and later NWFP, Assam). The Congress ministries **resigned in 1939** (protesting India's involvement in WWII without consultation) — the League observed "**Deliverance Day**."

| Feature | 1935 Act |
|---|---|
| Federation | all-India (never formed) |
| Provinces | provincial autonomy (dyarchy abolished) |
| Centre | dyarchy introduced (never implemented) |
| Institutions | Federal Court, RBI, FPSC |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **GoI Act 1935**: **provincial autonomy** (abolished 1919 provincial dyarchy), **dyarchy at the Centre** (never implemented), proposed **all-India Federation** (never formed), **Federal Court (1937)**, **RBI**. **1937 elections → Congress ministries in 8 provinces**, resigned **1939** (WWII).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1935: provincial autonomy (dyarchy OUT in provinces), dyarchy IN at Centre (never used), Federation (never formed). 1937 Congress ministries → resign 1939.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — 1935 Act: **dyarchy abolished in provinces** but **introduced at the Centre** (opposite of 1919). The **Federation never came into being**; **central dyarchy never implemented**. Congress formed ministries in **1937**, resigned **1939**.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"Act that gave provincial autonomy"* → **GoI Act 1935**; *"year Congress provincial ministries resigned"* → **1939**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- GoI Act 1935: provincial autonomy; dyarchy at Centre (unimplemented); all-India Federation (unformed).
- Federal Court 1937; RBI; Federal PSC; expanded franchise (~14%).
- 1937 elections: Congress ministries in 8 provinces.
- Ministries resigned 1939 (WWII); League's "Deliverance Day."

## 🗺️ Geography: Area Focus — Medak-Sangareddy-Siddipet

The **Medak-Sangareddy-Siddipet** belt in **central/north-western Telangana** (around Hyderabad's hinterland) is a mixed **agrarian-industrial** region on the **Godavari (Manjeera)** side, with major reservoirs and heritage.

**Location & physiography.** Old composite Medak split into **Medak, Sangareddy, Siddipet** (verify). It straddles the **Godavari basin** (the **Manjeera**, a Godavari tributary, is key) with granite terrain and black/red soils; part lies in the **Hyderabad peri-urban** growth corridor.

**Water & agriculture.** The **Singur dam** (on the Manjeera, Sangareddy) and **Nizam Sagar** (downstream) are key reservoirs; the **Kaleshwaram**-linked **Mallannasagar / Kondapochamma Sagar** reservoirs are in **Siddipet** (the project's showcase reservoirs). Crops: paddy, maize, cotton, sugarcane, vegetables (peri-urban).

**Industry.** **Patancheru-Bollaram** (Sangareddy) is a major **industrial/pharma** cluster (and a noted **pollution** hotspot historically); **Sadasivpet, Zaheerabad** (with a **NIMZ / Bharat/auto** industrial corridor — verify); **Siddipet** developed as a model town. **ICRISAT** (international crops research institute) is at **Patancheru**.

**Heritage & sites.** **Medak Cathedral** (one of Asia's largest churches, early 20th c.); **Medak Fort**; **Pocharam** & **Manjeera Wildlife Sanctuary** (Manjeera — crocodile/bird sanctuary near Sangareddy); **Edupayala** (Medak, Durga jatara); **Kondapochamma Sagar** (Siddipet). **Komuravelli Mallanna** temple (Siddipet).
