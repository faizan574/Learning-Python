## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 Supreme Court & High Courts: Judicial Review, PIL & Collegium

The judiciary is the **guardian of the Constitution** and of fundamental rights. India has an **integrated, single** judicial system with the **Supreme Court** at the apex.

### 📖 Concept

**Supreme Court (`Art 124–147`).**
- **Composition:** Chief Justice + other judges (current sanctioned strength **34** incl. CJI — `verify latest`).
- **Appointment:** judges appointed by the **President**; in practice via the **Collegium** (CJI + senior-most judges).
- **Qualifications:** citizen; **5 yrs as HC judge** OR **10 yrs HC advocate** OR a **distinguished jurist**.
- **Tenure:** until **65 years**; removable only by **impeachment** (proved misbehaviour/incapacity), same process as removing a judge — resolution by **special majority** in both Houses.

**Jurisdiction of the Supreme Court:**

| Type | Article | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Original** | `Art 131` | Centre-State / inter-State disputes |
| **Writ (FR enforcement)** | `Art 32` | Issue 5 writs to protect fundamental rights |
| **Appellate** | `Art 132-134` | Constitutional, civil, criminal appeals |
| **Advisory** | `Art 143` | President seeks SC's opinion (not binding) |
| **Court of Record** | `Art 129` | Records + contempt power |

**High Courts (`Art 214–231`).** One per state (some **common** to states/UTs); judges appointed by **President** (via collegium: CJI, SC collegium + HC Chief Justice + Governor consultation); retire at **62**. **Writ jurisdiction under `Art 226` is WIDER** than the SC's `Art 32` — a HC can issue writs **for fundamental rights AND "for any other purpose"** (legal rights), whereas SC's `Art 32` is only for fundamental rights.

**Judicial Review** — the power to examine the constitutionality of laws/executive actions and declare them **void** if they violate the Constitution. Part of the **basic structure** (`Kesavananda`, `Minerva Mills`). Rooted in `Art 13`.

**PIL (Public Interest Litigation)** — relaxed **locus standi**: any public-spirited person can approach the court for those unable to do so themselves. Pioneered by **Justices P. N. Bhagwati & V. R. Krishna Iyer** (late 1970s–80s).

**Collegium System** — judges appointing judges, evolved via the **Three Judges Cases**:
- **First Judges Case (1981)** — executive primacy.
- **Second Judges Case (1993)** — **collegium** established (CJI + 2 senior judges); judicial primacy.
- **Third Judges Case (1998)** — collegium expanded to **CJI + 4 senior-most judges**.
- **NJAC (99th Amendment, 2014)** — struck down (2015) as violating judicial independence → collegium continues.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **`Art 32`** (SC, only FRs) is a **fundamental right itself** ("heart and soul" — Ambedkar); **`Art 226`** (HC) is **wider** — FRs **and** other legal rights. **Judicial review** & **independence of judiciary** are **basic structure**; the **collegium** came from the **Second Judges Case (1993)**.

💡 **Example:** A citizen whose fundamental right is violated can go **directly** to the Supreme Court under `Art 32` (a guaranteed right) — but for an ordinary **legal** right, only a **High Court** under `Art 226` will do, not `Art 32`.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Art 124** (SC establishment), **129** (court of record), **131** (original), **143** (advisory).
- **Art 226 wider than Art 32**; HC judges retire at **62**, SC at **65**.
- **Collegium:** 2nd Judges Case (1993); NJAC struck down (2015).
- **PIL:** Bhagwati & Krishna Iyer; relaxed locus standi.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**32 = FR only (a right itself, SC); 226 = FR + more (wider, HC).** Retire 65 (SC)/62 (HC). Collegium = judges pick judges (1993)."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — `Art 32` is available **only for fundamental rights** and **cannot be suspended except during a National Emergency** (and never for Art 20 & 21 after the 44th Amendment). `Art 226` is broader but is a **constitutional (not fundamental) right**.

*(Art 226 — High Courts; the Second Judges Case, 1993.)*

## 🏛️ Subject 2: Telangana Schemes & Important Incidents
### 🎯 Central Schemes Active in Telangana (PM-KISAN, Ayushman & Comparisons)

Alongside state schemes (the Six Guarantees), several **Union government** schemes operate in Telangana — often **overlapping** with state schemes, a favourite comparison question.

### 📖 Concept

**Major central schemes & their state overlaps:**

| Central scheme | Purpose | State-scheme overlap in Telangana |
|---|---|---|
| **PM-KISAN** | ₹6,000/yr to farmers (₹2,000 × 3) | Overlaps **Rythu Bharosa** (state investment support) |
| **Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY)** | ₹5 lakh health cover | Overlaps **Rajiv Aarogyasri** (state health scheme) |
| **PMAY (Awas Yojana)** | Rural/urban housing | Overlaps **Indiramma Indlu** (state housing) |
| **Ujjwala (PMUY)** | LPG connections | Overlaps **Mahalakshmi ₹500 LPG** |
| **PM Poshan / Jal Jeevan Mission** | Nutrition / tap water | JJM ↔ **Mission Bhagiratha** (state piped water) |
| **MGNREGA** | Rural employment guarantee | Wage employment; state adds works |
| **PM Fasal Bima / KCC** | Crop insurance / credit | Farm support overlap |

**Key comparison themes:**
- **PM-KISAN vs Rythu Bharosa:** PM-KISAN is **₹6,000/yr per landholding family** (all India, capped); Rythu Bharosa is a **per-acre** state investment support (amount `verify latest`). A farmer may get **both**.
- **Ayushman Bharat vs Aarogyasri:** both give cashless treatment; Telangana historically ran its own **Aarogyasri** (higher coverage in some areas); the state's decision on **convergence/expansion** is a current-affairs point (`verify latest`).
- **Mission Bhagiratha** (state piped drinking water to every household) is often cited as a model that predates/parallels **Jal Jeevan Mission**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Central schemes (**PM-KISAN, Ayushman Bharat/PM-JAY, PMAY, Ujjwala, JJM, MGNREGA**) run in Telangana **alongside** overlapping state schemes (**Rythu Bharosa, Aarogyasri, Indiramma Indlu, Mahalakshmi LPG, Mission Bhagiratha**). Beneficiaries can often avail **both**.

💡 **Example:** A small farmer in Telangana may receive **PM-KISAN (₹6,000/yr, central)** *and* **Rythu Bharosa (per-acre, state)** — the exam tests whether you know these are **separate** schemes from **different governments** that can **coexist**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **PM-KISAN = ₹6,000/yr** (₹2,000 × 3 instalments), Union scheme.
- **Ayushman Bharat = ₹5 lakh** family health cover (PM-JAY).
- Pair each **central** scheme with its **state** counterpart (Bharosa/Aarogyasri/Indlu/Bhagiratha).
- Treat **coverage amounts & latest convergence decisions** as "verify latest official figure."

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**KISAN↔Bharosa (farm), Ayushman↔Aarogyasri (health), PMAY↔Indlu (house), Ujjwala↔Mahalakshmi LPG, JJM↔Bhagiratha (water).** Centre + State can stack."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Don't confuse the **central** scheme with its **state** namesake — they are from **different governments** with different eligibility/amounts, and a beneficiary may get **both**. Amounts and convergence status are current-affairs — **verify the latest official figure**.

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Operation Polo & Hyderabad State (1948–1956)

**Operation Polo** — the Indian Army's **"Police Action"** of **September 1948** — ended the Nizam's rule and merged Hyderabad into the Indian Union; the **Hyderabad State** then existed until the **1956** reorganisation.

### 📖 Concept

**Background.** At Independence (1947), the **Nizam Mir Osman Ali Khan** sought to remain **independent** (or join Pakistan), signed a **Standstill Agreement (Nov 1947)** with India, but the **Razakars** (under **Kasim Razvi**) unleashed violence; the Telangana Armed Struggle was raging.

**Operation Polo (13–17 September 1948).**
- Indian Army action codenamed **"Operation Polo"** (also called **Police Action**), led by **Major General J. N. Chaudhuri**.
- Lasted about **5 days (13–17 Sept 1948)**; the Nizam's forces & Razakars surrendered on **17 September 1948**.
- Hyderabad **acceded to India**; the Nizam remained as **Rajpramukh**; **Kasim Razvi** was arrested.
- **17 September** is observed in Telangana (as **"Telangana Liberation Day / Praja Palana Dinam"** — the naming has been politically contested; `verify current official designation`).
- The **Sunderlal Committee** was appointed to investigate the communal violence during/after the action (`verify`).

**Hyderabad State (1948–1956).**
- After the action, Hyderabad became a **Part B State** of the Indian Union.
- **First general elections (1952):** **Dr Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** became the first (and only) elected **Chief Minister** of Hyderabad State.
- **Mulki agitations** and the **1952 "Idli-Sambar / Non-Mulki Go Back"** movement over jobs foreshadowed later tensions.
- The **States Reorganisation Commission (SRC, Fazl Ali, 1953–55)** recommended reorganisation on linguistic lines.
- **1956:** Under the **States Reorganisation Act 1956** and the **Gentlemen's Agreement**, the **Telugu-speaking Telangana** region of Hyderabad State merged with **Andhra State** to form **Andhra Pradesh (1 Nov 1956)**; the **Marathi** areas went to Bombay and **Kannada** areas to Mysore.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Operation Polo (13–17 Sept 1948)**, led by **Maj. Gen. J. N. Chaudhuri**, ended the Nizam's rule; **Kasim Razvi/Razakars** surrendered on **17 Sept 1948**. **Hyderabad State (Part B)** existed until **1 Nov 1956**, when Telangana merged into **Andhra Pradesh** (SRC + Gentlemen's Agreement).

💡 **Example:** The Nizam had signed only a **Standstill Agreement (1947)**, not accession — so India's **Police Action (1948)** integrated Hyderabad by force; the Nizam then became **Rajpramukh**. That sequence (Standstill → Razakar violence → Operation Polo → Rajpramukh) is the exam narrative.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Operation Polo = Sept 1948; J. N. Chaudhuri; Kasim Razvi/Razakars**; 17 Sept surrender.
- **Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** = first CM of Hyderabad State (1952).
- **Sunderlal Committee** (violence probe); **SRC (Fazl Ali)**.
- **1 Nov 1956** = Andhra Pradesh formed (Gentlemen's Agreement).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Standstill (1947) → Razakars → Operation Polo (Sept 1948, Chaudhuri) → Rajpramukh Nizam → Burgula CM (1952) → AP (1956).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Hyderabad's **accession came via military action (1948)**, not a voluntary signing like other princely states. **17 Sept 1948** is the action's end date; the **1956** merger (into AP) is a separate, later event — don't conflate them.

*(Major General J. N. Chaudhuri; Dr Burgula Ramakrishna Rao, 1952.)*

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement
### 🎯 WWII, the August Offer, Cripps Mission & Quit India

The Second World War (1939–45) reshaped the freedom struggle: Congress ministries resigned, Britain made offers to secure Indian cooperation, and the movement climaxed in **Quit India (1942)**.

### 📖 Concept

**Congress ministries resign (1939).** India was declared a belligerent in WWII **without consulting** Indian leaders; the **Congress provincial ministries resigned** (Oct/Nov 1939) in protest. The **Muslim League** observed **"Deliverance Day."**

**August Offer (Aug 1940).** By **Viceroy Linlithgow**: promised **Dominion Status** as the goal, expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council, and that no future constitution would be adopted **without minorities' consent**. **Rejected** by Congress (wanted independence) and inadequate for the League.

**Individual Satyagraha (1940–41).** A limited, symbolic protest; **Vinoba Bhave** was the **first** satyagrahi, **Nehru** the second — to assert the right to free speech against the war.

**Cripps Mission (March 1942).** **Sir Stafford Cripps** offered, after the war: **Dominion Status** with the right to frame a **new constitution**, and provinces/states could **opt out** (seed of Pakistan). Congress rejected it (**Gandhi: "a post-dated cheque"**; some add "on a crashing/failing bank"); the **opt-out** clause and lack of immediate self-government doomed it.

**Quit India Movement (Aug 1942) — "Do or Die."**
- The **AICC** passed the **Quit India Resolution** at **Bombay (Gowalia Tank) on 8 August 1942**; Gandhi gave the mantra **"Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro)**.
- On **9 August 1942**, the entire top leadership was **arrested** (**Operation Zero Hour**); a leaderless, spontaneous **mass upsurge** followed — strikes, sabotage, **parallel governments (Ballia, Satara, Tamluk/Midnapore)**.
- Key underground leaders: **Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali** (hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank), **Usha Mehta** (Congress Radio).
- Suppressed by 1943, but it made British withdrawal inevitable; the last great mass movement.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Quit India** began with the **8 Aug 1942** Gowalia Tank resolution and Gandhi's **"Do or Die"**; leaders arrested **9 Aug 1942**. The **Cripps Mission (1942)** was the **"post-dated cheque"** with an **opt-out** clause; the **August Offer (1940)** promised Dominion Status.

💡 **Example:** The **Cripps** opt-out clause (provinces could stay out of the Union) is what Gandhi and Congress found unacceptable — it legitimised **partition**; the League, conversely, welcomed that very clause. One clause, opposite reactions.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **August Offer 1940 (Linlithgow)**; **Individual Satyagraha — Vinoba Bhave first**.
- **Cripps Mission 1942 (Stafford Cripps)** — "post-dated cheque", opt-out clause.
- **Quit India — 8 Aug 1942, Gowalia Tank, "Do or Die"**; Aruna Asaf Ali; parallel govts (Satara, Tamluk, Ballia).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1939 resign → 1940 August Offer → 1940-41 Individual Satyagraha (Vinoba) → 1942 Cripps (post-dated cheque) → Aug 1942 Quit India (Do or Die).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Cripps Mission (1942)** offered Dominion Status **after** the war with an **opt-out**; it was **not** the August Offer (1940). And **Quit India** was **leaderless/spontaneous** after 9 Aug arrests — not centrally directed. Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted the Gowalia Tank flag.

*("A post-dated cheque"; Gowalia Tank, Bombay, 8 August 1942.)*

## 🗺️ Subject 5: Telangana Geography (with Map)
### 🎯 Area Focus: Nizamabad–Kamareddy

**Nizamabad** (Indur) — north-central Telangana on the **Godavari** — is turmeric and maize country, the SRSP command area, and a temple-and-fort heritage zone.

**🗺️ Reference map — Telangana regions (Nizamabad-Kamareddy in the north-central zone):**

![Telangana regions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

*(If the map doesn't display in email, open the lesson on GitHub — the SVG is at `tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg`.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Location & administration.** The undivided Nizamabad district (north-central, bordering Maharashtra & Karnataka to the west) was reorganised (2016) into **Nizamabad** and **Kamareddy** districts (`verify`). Nizamabad is a major regional city and rail junction.

**Physiography & geology.**
- Mixed terrain of the Deccan plateau — **granite–gneiss** with pockets of **black and red soils**; the **Godavari** flows along the north with tributaries **Manjira** and **Haldi/Nalla vagu**.
- Fertile command area of the **Sri Ram Sagar Project (SRSP, Pochampad)** on the **Godavari** (Nizamabad) — the region's irrigation backbone.
- **Nizam Sagar** dam on the **Manjira** (Kamareddy/Nizamabad border) — an older major reservoir.

**Agriculture (the economic identity).**
- **Turmeric** — Nizamabad is one of India's largest turmeric markets; farmers long demanded a **Turmeric Board** (a **National Turmeric Board** was announced — `verify HQ/status`).
- **Maize** — Nizamabad is a leading maize-producing district; also **red jowar, soybean, sugarcane, paddy** (SRSP command).

**Heritage & sites (cultural reference):**
- **Nizamabad Fort (Indur/Quilla)** on a hill; **Neela Kanteshwara temple**.
- **Sri Raja Rajeshwara Swamy Temple, Vemulawada** (Rajanna Sircilla side but culturally linked) — actually **Vemulawada** is in the adjacent **Rajanna Sircilla** district; **Domakonda Fort** (Kamareddy) — a heritage samsthanam fort.
- **Sarangapur Hanuman temple**; **Ashok Sagar / Alisagar** reservoirs (tourism).
- **Kamareddy** — known for the **Kamareddy declaration/BC** politics (`verify current affairs`) and agricultural markets.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Nizamabad's identity is **turmeric (National Turmeric Board demand) + maize**, watered by the **SRSP (Pochampad, Godavari)** and **Nizam Sagar (Manjira)**; heritage includes **Indur Fort**, **Domakonda Fort (Kamareddy)** and **Sarangapur Hanuman temple**.

💡 **Example:** "Geology + culture of Nizamabad" → **Godavari/Manjira irrigation (SRSP, Nizam Sagar) enabling turmeric & maize** (geography/economy) with **Indur Fort + Domakonda + Neela Kanteshwara** (culture) — and the **Turmeric Board** is the current-affairs hook.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
