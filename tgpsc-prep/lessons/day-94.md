## 🏛️ Polity: Amendment (Art 368) & the Basic Structure Doctrine

`Art 368` in **Part XX** lays down how the Constitution is amended — a balance between **flexibility and rigidity**, limited by the **Basic Structure doctrine**.

**Procedure (`Art 368`).** An amendment bill can be introduced in **either House** (not a joint sitting for disagreement), needs a **special majority** — a majority of the **total membership** of each House **and** two-thirds of **members present and voting**. Three categories:
1. **Simple majority** (outside `Art 368` — e.g., admission of states, creation of legislative councils) — not counted as formal amendments.
2. **Special majority** (most of the Constitution — FRs, DPSP).
3. **Special majority + ratification by half the state legislatures** (federal provisions — election of President, distribution of powers, Seventh Schedule, `Art 368` itself, representation of states).

**Evolution of the doctrine.**
- ***Shankari Prasad (1951)*** & ***Sajjan Singh (1965)***: Parliament **can** amend FRs (law under `Art 13` ≠ constitutional amendment).
- ***Golaknath (1967)***: FRs **cannot** be amended (amendment is "law" under `Art 13`).
- **24th Amendment (1971):** restored Parliament's power to amend any part, including FRs.
- ***Kesavananda Bharati (1973)***: landmark — Parliament can amend any part **but cannot destroy the "Basic Structure."** (overruled Golaknath partly).
- **42nd Amendment (1976):** tried to make amending power unlimited (`Art 368(4)(5)`).
- ***Minerva Mills (1980)***: struck those clauses; **limited amending power & FR-DPSP balance are Basic Structure**.
- ***Waman Rao (1981)***: Basic Structure applies to amendments **after 24 April 1973** (Kesavananda date).

**Basic Structure elements (illustrative, not exhaustive):** supremacy of the Constitution, rule of law, judicial review, federalism, secularism, separation of powers, free & fair elections, parliamentary system, sovereignty, unity & integrity.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — ***Kesavananda Bharati (1973)*** established the **Basic Structure doctrine**: Parliament can amend any part under `Art 368` but **cannot destroy the basic structure**. **Special majority** = majority of total membership + 2/3 present & voting; **federal provisions** also need **ratification by half the states**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Shankari→Sajjan (can) → Golaknath (can't) → 24th → Kesavananda (basic structure) → Minerva (balance).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — There is **no joint sitting** for a constitutional amendment disagreement (unlike ordinary bills). Basic Structure is **judge-made** (not in the text). Prior assent of the **President is mandatory** (24th Amd — President must give assent). Ratification needs **half (not two-thirds) of states**.

💡 **Example:** A law abolishing **judicial review** would be struck down as violating the **Basic Structure**, even if passed by special majority.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Art 368: special majority; federal items + state ratification (half).
- Golaknath (1967) → 24th Amd (1971) → Kesavananda (1973) → Minerva (1980).
- Basic Structure: judicial review, federalism, secularism, free elections, etc.
- No joint sitting for amendment; President must assent (24th).

## 🤝 Schemes: Praja Palana, Praja Vani & Administrative Changes

The Telangana government's **grievance-redressal and governance-branding** measures — **Praja Palana** (for the Six Guarantees enrolment) and **Praja Vani** (weekly grievance day) — plus **administrative renamings and the state emblem**.

**Praja Palana.** A mass **application/enrolment drive (Dec 2023–Jan 2024)** to collect citizens' applications for the **Six Guarantees** (via a detailed form at gram/ward level). It doubled as a **socio-economic data** collection exercise and the base for later welfare targeting. (Verify the exact dates and application numbers.)

**Praja Vani.** A **weekly public grievance-redressal programme** (held at the Secretariat/collectorates, typically on a fixed day) where citizens submit petitions directly to the government for time-bound resolution — a revival/rebranding of earlier grievance mechanisms (like the old "Praja Darbar"/"Grievance Day").

**Administrative changes.** The government undertook **renamings and symbol changes**, notably reviving/altering the **state emblem** and **Telangana Thalli** (state mother goddess) statue/representation, and restoring **Telangana Formation Day** observances. Secretariat and scheme branding (e.g., Indiramma) changed with the government. (These are politically sensitive; verify current official status and exact design.)

## 🏺 History: Formation of Telangana & After (2014–Present)

After formation on **2 June 2014**, Telangana's political history moved through **two TRS/BRS terms** and a **Congress** government from 2023 — with major governance initiatives and continuing debates.

**Formation & first years.** **K. Chandrashekar Rao (KCR)** became the **first Chief Minister** (TRS). The new state faced **bifurcation challenges** — division of assets/employees (as per the AP Reorganisation Act), power/water sharing (Krishna & Godavari tribunals), and **Hyderabad as the common capital** (10 years). Early flagship programmes: **Mission Bhagiratha** (piped drinking water), **Mission Kakatiya** (tank restoration), **Rythu Bandhu** (investment support), **Rythu Bima**, **Kaleshwaram** lift irrigation, **KCR Kits**, **Aasara pensions**, and new **Secretariat/integrated districts** (districts reorganised from 10 to 33, verify).

**2018 & 2023 elections.** TRS won **2014 and 2018**; the party was **renamed Bharat Rashtra Samithi (BRS) in 2022** (national ambitions). In the **2023 Assembly election**, the **Congress** won; **A. Revanth Reddy** became Chief Minister, implementing the **Six Guarantees**.

**Key developments.** The **Kaleshwaram project** (and the **Medigadda barrage** issues) came under a judicial commission; the **caste survey (SEEEPC)** and **42% BC reservation** push; **HYDRAA** and **Musi rejuvenation**; new **district reorganisation**. Water disputes (Krishna allocation, Godavari projects) with Andhra continue before tribunals/boards (KRMB, GRMB). (Verify current figures and legal statuses.)

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana formed **2 June 2014** (KCR, TRS, first CM); TRS won 2014 & 2018, **renamed BRS (2022)**; **Congress won 2023** (CM **A. Revanth Reddy**, Six Guarantees). Flagships: **Mission Bhagiratha, Mission Kakatiya, Rythu Bandhu, Kaleshwaram**; districts reorganised (10→33, verify).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**2014 KCR/TRS → 2018 re-elected → 2022 BRS → 2023 Congress (Revanth Reddy, Six Guarantees).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — TRS **renamed BRS in 2022** (not a new party). **Revanth Reddy** is the current CM (post-2023). District count and project figures are **evolving — verify**. Hyderabad common-capital period is **10 years** from 2014.

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Non-Cooperation Movement & the Swarajists

The **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)** was Gandhi's first nationwide mass movement; its sudden withdrawal led to the rise of the **Swarajists**.

**Launch & programme.** Approved at the **Calcutta special session (Sep 1920)** and **Nagpur session (Dec 1920)**, NCM combined with the **Khilafat** cause. The programme: **surrender of titles/honours, boycott of government schools, colleges, courts, councils, foreign goods**; promotion of **swadeshi, khadi, national schools, panchayats**, and (later) **non-payment of taxes**. Gandhi promised **"Swaraj within a year."** Mass participation was unprecedented (students, lawyers like Nehru/Patel/Rajendra Prasad gave up practice; women joined).

**Chauri Chaura & withdrawal.** On **5 February 1922 (Chauri Chaura, UP)**, a mob burned a police station killing policemen. Gandhi, shocked by the violence, **withdrew the movement (Bardoli resolution, Feb 1922)**. He was arrested and sentenced (1922). The abrupt halt disappointed many leaders.

**Swarajists.** After NCM's withdrawal, a split emerged in Congress:
- **"Pro-changers"** — **C.R. Das & Motilal Nehru** formed the **Swaraj Party (1 Jan 1923)** to **contest council elections** and wreck the reforms **from within** (council-entry).
- **"No-changers"** — **Rajaji, Patel, Rajendra Prasad** wanted to continue **constructive work** (khadi, boycott) outside councils.

The Swarajists did well in the **1923 elections** (Central Legislative Assembly), obstructed government business, and exposed the hollowness of the 1919 reforms. The movement waned after **C.R. Das's death (1925)**.

| Phase | Detail |
|---|---|
| NCM launch | Nagpur 1920; Khilafat-linked; boycott programme |
| Chauri Chaura | 5 Feb 1922 → withdrawal |
| Swaraj Party | 1923; C.R. Das & Motilal Nehru; council-entry |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **NCM (1920–22)** launched at **Nagpur (Dec 1920)**, linked with **Khilafat**; withdrawn after **Chauri Chaura (5 Feb 1922)**. **Swaraj Party (1 Jan 1923, C.R. Das & Motilal Nehru)** = pro-changers contesting councils; no-changers did constructive work.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Nagpur 1920 (launch) → Chauri Chaura 5 Feb 1922 (withdrawal) → Swaraj Party 1923 (Das & Motilal).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Chauri Chaura = 5 Feb 1922** triggered the **withdrawal**. **Swaraj Party = C.R. Das + Motilal Nehru (1923)**, "pro-changers." NCM's methods were **non-violent non-cooperation** (boycott), not armed revolt.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"event that led Gandhi to withdraw NCM"* → **Chauri Chaura (1922)**; *"party formed for council-entry (1923)"* → **Swaraj Party**.

## 🗺️ Geography: Area Focus — Nizamabad-Kamareddy

The **Nizamabad-Kamareddy** region in **north-western Telangana** is a fertile, **Godavari-basin** agrarian belt known for **turmeric, maize, and sugarcane**, with historic and devotional sites.

**Location & physiography.** Part of the old composite Nizamabad district (now **Nizamabad** and **Kamareddy**; verify). It lies in the **Godavari basin** (the **Manjeera** — a Godavari tributary — and the Godavari itself drain it), with fertile black/red soils and a relatively higher groundwater table. The **Sri Ram Sagar Project (SRSP/Pochampad)** on the Godavari is the lifeline reservoir for this belt.

**Water & agriculture.** **SRSP (Pochampad)** irrigates large tracts; **Nizam Sagar** (on the Manjeera) is a historic reservoir. Major crops: **turmeric** (Nizamabad is a leading turmeric market — a **National Turmeric Board** was set up at Nizamabad, verify), **maize**, **sugarcane**, **paddy**, and **soybean**. Nizamabad hosts a large **agricultural market**.

**Heritage & sites.** **Basara** (Jnana Saraswati Temple — one of few Saraswati temples, on the Godavari) is in the belt (Nirmal/old Nizamabad border — verify district). **Nizamabad Fort (Quila)**, **Ashok Sagar**, **Domakonda Fort** (Kamareddy), and the **Pocharam Wildlife Sanctuary/dam** are notable. The region has an **Armoor** (rock formations) and strong NRI/Gulf-migration links.
