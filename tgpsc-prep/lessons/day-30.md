## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Fundamental Rights — Art 19-22 (Freedoms & Personal Liberty)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Articles `19-22` are the heart of **personal freedom** in Part III.

**Article 19 — Six freedoms** (available to **citizens**):

| Clause | Freedom |
|---|---|
| 19(1)(a) | Speech and expression |
| 19(1)(b) | Assemble **peaceably and without arms** |
| 19(1)(c) | Form associations/unions/cooperatives |
| 19(1)(d) | Move freely throughout India |
| 19(1)(e) | Reside and settle in any part of India |
| 19(1)(g) | Practise any profession / carry on any trade |

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **right to property [old 19(1)(f)]** was **deleted** by the **44th Amendment (1978)** and made an ordinary legal right under **Art 300A**. Each freedom carries **reasonable restrictions** — e.g. `19(2)` allows limits on speech for sovereignty, security, public order, decency, defamation, contempt of court, and incitement.

**Article 20 — Protection in respect of conviction:** (1) no **ex-post-facto** law, (2) no **double jeopardy**, (3) no **self-incrimination**.

**Article 21 — Protection of life and personal liberty:** "No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**."

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — In **Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (`1978`)**, the Supreme Court held that the procedure under **Art 21** must be **just, fair and reasonable** (importing "due process"). **Art 21A (86th Amendment, 2002)** made **free & compulsory education for ages 6-14** a fundamental right. Privacy was declared a fundamental right under Art 21 in **K.S. Puttaswamy (`2017`)**.

**Article 22 — Protection against arrest & detention:** an ordinary arrestee must be **informed of grounds**, allowed to **consult a lawyer**, and **produced before a magistrate within 24 hours**. These do **not** apply to **enemy aliens** or **preventive detention** (where an advisory board reviews detention beyond `3 months`).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**20 and 21 can never be suspended**": even during a National Emergency (post-44th Amendment), Articles **20 and 21 remain enforceable** (Art 359).

### 💡 Example
The right to a **clean environment**, **livelihood**, **speedy trial**, and **privacy** have all been read into **Art 21** — the most expansively interpreted fundamental right.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Art 19 = **six** freedoms; property removed by **44th Amendment**.
- Art 20 = ex-post-facto, double jeopardy, self-incrimination.
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** = due process into Art 21.
- **Art 22** = arrest within **24 hrs**; preventive detention exceptions.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: The Kakatiyas — Political History

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Kakatiyas** (`c. 1000–1323 CE`), ruling from **Orugallu (Warangal)**, gave Telangana its first great imperial identity. They began as feudatories of the **Rashtrakutas** and then the **Kalyani Chalukyas**, becoming independent in the 12th century.

**Key rulers:**

| Ruler | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Prola II** | Expanded Kakatiya power as a Chalukya feudatory |
| **Rudradeva (Prataparudra I)** | First **independent** king; built the **Thousand Pillar Temple** at Hanamkonda; **Anumakonda (Hanamkonda) inscription** |
| **Ganapatideva** (`1199–1262`) | Greatest & longest reign; unified the Telugu lands; issued the **Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam** (safe-trade charter) |
| **Rudrama Devi** (`1262–1289`) | One of India's few **women rulers**; ruled titled "**Rudradeva Maharaja**"; praised by the traveller **Marco Polo** |
| **Prataparudra II** (`1289–1323`) | **Last** Kakatiya king; resisted the Delhi Sultanate |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Under the Kakatiyas were built the **Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple** (by **Recharla Rudra** under Ganapatideva, `1213`; **UNESCO World Heritage, 2021**), the **Thousand Pillar Temple**, and the **Warangal Fort** with the **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam** — now the **state emblem** of Telangana.

The dynasty fell to the **Delhi Sultanate**: after raids by **Malik Kafur** (`1310`), Warangal was finally taken in **`1323`** by **Ulugh Khan (later Muhammad bin Tughlaq)**; **Prataparudra II** was captured and reportedly died en route to Delhi.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Rudradeva builds, Ganapati unifies, Rudrama rules, Prataparudra falls (1323)**". **Kala Thoranam = state emblem**; **Ramappa = UNESCO 2021**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Rudrama Devi** was a **woman ruler** who took a male title; **Marco Polo** (not Ibn Battuta) praised her administration. Ramappa's builder is **Recharla Rudra**, its deity **Rudreshwara**.

### 💡 Example
The **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam** (Warangal gateway) featured on the **Telangana state emblem** directly links medieval history to modern state identity — a classic static-current linkage.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Capital **Orugallu (Warangal)**; first independent king **Rudradeva**.
- **Ganapatideva** — Motupalli charter; **Rudrama Devi** — woman ruler, Marco Polo.
- **Ramappa** — Recharla Rudra, 1213, **UNESCO 2021**.
- Fall in **1323** to the Delhi Sultanate (Prataparudra II last).

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which Kakatiya monument became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021, and who built it?*
→ The **Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple**, built by **Recharla Rudra** (under Ganapatideva).

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Government of India Act 1935 & Provincial Ministries

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Government of India Act, `1935`** was the **longest** Act passed by the British Parliament and the immediate constitutional basis for independent India's framework. It drew on the **Simon Report, the Round Table Conferences, the Nehru Report, and the 1933 White Paper**.

**Main provisions:**

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **All-India Federation** | Of provinces + princely states — **never came into being** (states didn't join) |
| **Provincial Autonomy** | **Abolished dyarchy in provinces**; ministers responsible to legislatures (from `1 April 1937`) |
| **Dyarchy at Centre** | Introduced but **never implemented** |
| **Three Lists** | Federal, Provincial, Concurrent — basis of the **7th Schedule** |
| **Federal institutions** | **Federal Court (1937)**, **Reserve Bank of India** |
| **Franchise** | Extended (to ~`10%` of population); separate electorates continued |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Under **Provincial Autonomy**, elections were held in **`1937`**; the **Congress won majorities** and formed ministries in **most provinces** (about **8, later 11**). These **Congress ministries resigned in `1939`** when Britain declared India a party to **WWII without consulting Indians**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Federation failed, Autonomy worked**": the all-India federation never happened, but **provincial autonomy** did (1937 elections → Congress ministries → resigned 1939). The **three lists** of 1935 became our **7th Schedule**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Provincial autonomy **replaced** dyarchy in the provinces, but dyarchy was newly introduced **at the Centre** (and never came into force). Governors kept wide **discretionary/special powers**, so autonomy was **limited**.

### 💡 Example
Much of India's **federal structure** — the division of powers via three lists, and even the office structures — was **carried forward** from the 1935 Act into the Constitution, which is why it is called a major "source".

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Longest** British Act; based on Simon/RTCs/Nehru Report/White Paper.
- **Provincial autonomy** from **1937**; **all-India federation never formed**.
- Congress ministries **1937**, resigned **1939** (WWII).
- Three lists → **7th Schedule**; Federal Court & RBI established.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which feature of the 1935 Act actually came into force, and which never did?*
→ **Provincial autonomy** came into force (1937); the **all-India federation** never did.

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana's economy is anchored by **Hyderabad**, one of India's leading industrial and technology hubs.

**Industry clusters:**

| Sector | Highlights |
|---|---|
| **IT / ITES** | **HITEC City / Cyberabad**; a major software-export centre (verify latest export figures) |
| **Life sciences / Pharma** | **Genome Valley** (India's first organised life-sciences cluster); Hyderabad called the "**Bulk Drug/Vaccine capital**"; proposed **Hyderabad Pharma City (Mucherla)** |
| **Public-sector / Defence** | **BHEL, HAL, BDL, DRDO labs, NFC (Nuclear Fuel Complex)**, Ordnance Factory Medak |

**Transport network:**

| Mode | Highlights |
|---|---|
| **Road** | **Outer Ring Road (ORR)** ~`158 km` around Hyderabad; proposed **Regional Ring Road (RRR)** ~`340 km`; NH grid |
| **Rail** | **South Central Railway** HQ at **Secunderabad**; **MMTS** suburban service |
| **Metro** | **Hyderabad Metro Rail** |
| **Air** | **Rajiv Gandhi International Airport, Shamshabad** |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Genome Valley** near Hyderabad is India's **first organised life-sciences/biotech cluster**, and the city is a global **vaccine-manufacturing** hub. The **South Central Railway** is headquartered at **Secunderabad**. *(Verify the latest ORR/RRR lengths, IT export and pharma output figures.)*

### 💡 Example
