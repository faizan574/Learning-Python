## 🏛️ Polity: The Preamble and its Keywords

The **Preamble** is the **introductory statement** of the Constitution — the "identity card" or "political horoscope" (K.M. Munshi) that sets out its **source, nature, objectives and date**. It is based on the **Objectives Resolution (Nehru, 1946)**.

**The text and keywords.** The Preamble declares India to be a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic** and secures to all citizens:
- **Justice** — social, economic and political;
- **Liberty** — of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
- **Equality** — of status and of opportunity;
- **Fraternity** — assuring the **dignity of the individual** and the **unity and integrity** of the nation.

**Source of authority.** It opens with **"We, the People of India"** — the ultimate source of the Constitution's authority is the **people** (popular sovereignty).

**42nd Amendment (1976).** Added three words: **"Socialist," "Secular,"** (to the description of the Republic) and **"Integrity"** (to Fraternity). So the original 1949 Preamble did **not** contain Socialist/Secular/Integrity.

**Legal status (cases).**
- **Berubari Union (1960)** — the Supreme Court held the Preamble is **not a part** of the Constitution (not enforceable).
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** — overruled that view: the Preamble **IS a part** of the Constitution and **can be amended** (but not its **basic structure**).
- The Preamble is **non-justiciable** (not enforceable in court on its own).

| Keyword | Meaning/Source |
|---|---|
| Sovereign | free from external control |
| Socialist | (42nd Amdt) democratic socialism |
| Secular | (42nd Amdt) no state religion |
| Democratic | govt by the people |
| Republic | elected head of state |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **42nd Amendment (1976)** added **"Socialist," "Secular," "Integrity."** **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** held the Preamble **is part** of the Constitution and amendable (not its basic structure); it is **non-justiciable**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — Republic description: "**S-S-S-D-R**" → Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic. Objectives: "**Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity (JLEF).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The original (1949) Preamble did **NOT** have "Socialist/Secular/Integrity" — added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**. Berubari said the Preamble is *not* part of the Constitution; **Kesavananda reversed this**.

💡 **Example:** In **Kesavananda Bharati**, the Court used the Preamble's ideals to help define the **basic structure** — so the Preamble is a key interpretive tool even though non-justiciable.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Based on Objectives Resolution (Nehru, 1946).
- 42nd Amendment added Socialist, Secular, Integrity.
- Berubari (not part) → Kesavananda (part, amendable).
- "We, the People" = popular sovereignty.

## 🏗️ Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Rythu Bharosa, Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa & the ₹2 Lakh Loan Waiver

This cluster is the **farmer/agriculture guarantee** within the Six Guarantees. (Treat all amounts as **"verify latest official figure."**)

**Rythu Bharosa.** An **investment-support scheme for farmers** — a per-acre, per-year cash transfer to landholding cultivators (announced at **₹15,000 per acre per year** — **verify current rate and ceiling/eligibility**). It is the successor framework to the earlier Rythu Bandhu, now restructured under the new government.

**Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa.** Extends support to **landless agricultural labourers** (who own no land and so miss out on per-acre schemes) — an annual assistance announced at **₹12,000 per year** (**verify**) to the landless farm-worker households, addressing an equity gap in farm support.

**Crop-loan waiver (₹2 lakh).** A **waiver of crop loans up to ₹2 lakh** per eligible farmer family, implemented in phases through direct bank credit (**verify coverage/number of beneficiaries and disbursal status**).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Rythu Bharosa** = per-acre investment support (₹15,000/acre announced); **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** = support for **landless** farm labourers (₹12,000/yr announced); plus a **crop-loan waiver up to ₹2 lakh**. **Verify all figures.**

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Bharosa for land-owners (per acre); Atmiya Bharosa for the landless; ₹2 lakh loan waiver on top.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Distinguish **Rythu Bharosa (landholders, per acre)** from **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa (landless labourers)** — a frequent mix-up. Amounts and rollout status change — mark "verify latest."

💡 **Example:** A landowning farmer gets Rythu Bharosa per acre and may also benefit from the ₹2-lakh loan waiver; a landless farm labourer instead receives Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa — closing the equity gap.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Rythu Bharosa: per-acre investment support (verify rate).
- Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa: landless farm labourers (verify amount).
- Crop-loan waiver up to ₹2 lakh.
- Successor to Rythu Bandhu framework.

## 🏺 Telangana History: Tribal & Peasant Revolts — Ramji Gond & Komaram Bheem

Telangana's tribal belt (especially **Adilabad**) produced two iconic figures of resistance against the Nizam and the exploitative forest/revenue system.

**Ramji Gond (c. 1857–1860).** A **Gond leader** of the Adilabad region who led a **Gond–Rohilla uprising** against the Nizam's administration and the British during/after the 1857 period, resisting oppressive tax and forest policies. He was captured and **hanged**; legend holds that many of his followers were executed on a large banyan ("Nallavagu / marri") tree — remembered as a symbol of tribal sacrifice.

**Komaram Bheem (c. 1900–1940).** The most celebrated **Gond revolutionary**, from the **Adilabad** forests, who led a revolt against the Nizam's government over tribal land and forest rights in the **1930s**. His rallying cry was **"Jal, Jangal, Zameen"** (water, forest, land) — asserting tribal ownership of their habitat. He operated from **Jodeghat** and was **killed in a confrontation with the Nizam's forces in 1940**. His struggle prompted the Nizam to commission the **anthropologist Haimendorf's study of the Gonds**, leading to some tribal-protective measures. He is a cultural icon of Telangana (the state has named institutions/projects after him — e.g., **Komaram Bheem Asifabad district**).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Komaram Bheem** (Gond, Adilabad) led a 1930s revolt with the slogan **"Jal, Jangal, Zameen,"** based at **Jodeghat**, **died 1940**. **Ramji Gond** led a Gond–Rohilla revolt around **1857–60** and was hanged. A Telangana district is named **Komaram Bheem Asifabad**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Ramji Gond (1857–60, hanged); Komaram Bheem (1930s, Jodeghat, 'Jal Jangal Zameen', 1940).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **"Jal, Jangal, Zameen"** is **Komaram Bheem's** slogan (not Ramji Gond's). Komaram Bheem's base was **Jodeghat** and he **died in 1940**; Ramji Gond belongs to the **1857-era** revolt.

💡 **Example:** Komaram Bheem's revolt and the resulting **Haimendorf study** led to administrative attention to Gond land/forest rights — a tribal-history and policy crossover.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Ramji Gond: Adilabad, Gond-Rohilla, ~1857–60, hanged.
- Komaram Bheem: Jodeghat, "Jal Jangal Zameen," died 1940.
- Haimendorf anthropological study of Gonds followed.
- District named Komaram Bheem Asifabad.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Pre-Congress Associations & Socio-Religious Reform

Before the Indian National Congress (1885), **political associations** and a wave of **socio-religious reform** prepared the ground for organised nationalism.

**Pre-Congress political associations.**
- **Landholders' Society (1838, Bengal)** — first political association (zamindar interests).
- **British Indian Association (1851, Calcutta)**.
- **East India Association (1866, London)** — founded by **Dadabhai Naoroji**.
- **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870)** — M.G. Ranade and others.
- **Indian Association (1876, Calcutta)** — **Surendranath Banerjee & Anandamohan Bose** (opposed to the Ilbert Bill/civil-service age issues); the most important pre-Congress body.
- **Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884)** and **Bombay Presidency Association (1885)**.

**Socio-religious reform movements.**
- **Brahmo Samaj (1828, Raja Ram Mohan Roy)** — monotheism; **abolition of Sati (1829)** via his efforts with Bentinck.
- **Prarthana Samaj (1867, Bombay)** — Atmaram Pandurang, Ranade.
- **Arya Samaj (1875, Swami Dayananda Saraswati)** — **"Back to the Vedas,"** Shuddhi movement.
- **Ramakrishna Mission (1897, Swami Vivekananda)** — service and Vedanta.
- **Aligarh Movement (Sir Syed Ahmed Khan)** — modern education for Muslims (MAO College → AMU).
- **Satyashodhak Samaj (1873, Jyotiba Phule)** — anti-caste, social justice.
- **Theosophical Society** (Blavatsky/Olcott; later Annie Besant); **Young Bengal (Henry Derozio)**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Indian Association (1876)** = most important pre-Congress body (Surendranath Banerjee). **Brahmo Samaj (1828, Ram Mohan Roy) → Sati abolished 1829**; **Arya Samaj (1875, Dayananda) = "Back to the Vedas"**; **Ramakrishna Mission (1897, Vivekananda)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1828 Brahmo (Roy) → 1875 Arya (Dayananda) → 1897 Ramakrishna (Vivekananda).**" Associations: "**1876 Indian Association (Surendranath).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Brahmo Samaj = Ram Mohan Roy (1828)**; **Arya Samaj = Dayananda (1875, Back to the Vedas)** — don't swap founders. **Sati was abolished in 1829** (Bentinck, after Roy's campaign).

💡 **Example:** The **Indian Association (1876)** organised all-India campaigns (e.g., on the civil-service age limit), directly foreshadowing the Congress's national role from 1885.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Landholders' Society 1838; Indian Association 1876 (Surendranath Banerjee).
- Brahmo 1828 (Roy); Arya 1875 (Dayananda); Ramakrishna 1897 (Vivekananda).
- Prarthana Samaj (Ranade); Satyashodhak (Phule); Aligarh (Sir Syed).
- Sati abolished 1829; Dadabhai Naoroji → East India Association 1866.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Forests, Wildlife Sanctuaries & Tiger Reserves

![Telangana regions map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

Telangana's forests are concentrated in the **north (Adilabad belt) and the Nallamala south**, mostly **tropical dry deciduous** (teak-dominated) with thorn forest in drier tracts.

**Forest cover.** Roughly **a quarter of the state's area** is forest (**verify latest India State of Forest Report figure**). Teak (**Tectona grandis**) is the signature species; bamboo, terminalia and other deciduous species are common.

**Tiger reserves (2).**
- **Amrabad Tiger Reserve** — in the **Nallamala (Krishna belt, south)**; one of **India's largest tiger reserves by area** (contiguous with Nagarjunsagar-Srisailam across the AP border). Home to the **Chenchu** tribe.
- **Kawal Tiger Reserve** — in the **old Adilabad (Godavari belt, north)**.

**National Parks (3), all near Hyderabad.**
- **Kasu Brahmananda Reddy (KBR) National Park**,
- **Mahavir Harina Vanasthali National Park**,
- **Mrugavani National Park**.

**Key Wildlife Sanctuaries.**
- **Eturnagaram (Mulugu)** — one of the **oldest/largest** sanctuaries.
- **Pakhal, Kinnerasani, Pranahita, Shivaram** (Godavari belt);
- **Manjira** (crocodile/bird sanctuary on the Manjira reservoir);
- **Pocharam, Kawal, Nagarjunsagar-Srisailam** (part in Telangana).

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Tiger Reserves | Amrabad (Nallamala/south), Kawal (Adilabad/north) |
| National Parks | KBR, Mahavir Harina Vanasthali, Mrugavani (near Hyderabad) |
| Wildlife Sanctuaries | Eturnagaram, Pakhal, Kinnerasani, Pranahita, Manjira |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana has **2 tiger reserves** — **Amrabad (Nallamala, south — among India's largest)** and **Kawal (Adilabad, north)** — and **3 national parks near Hyderabad (KBR, Mahavir Harina Vanasthali, Mrugavani)**. Dominant forest: **tropical dry deciduous (teak)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**2 tigers: Amrabad (south/Nallamala) + Kawal (north/Adilabad); 3 NPs around Hyderabad (KBR, Mahavir, Mrugavani).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Amrabad** is in the **south (Nallamala/Krishna)**; **Kawal** in the **north (Adilabad/Godavari)** — don't swap. The **three national parks are all near Hyderabad**; the big tiger reserves are at the state's edges.

💡 **Example:** **Amrabad Tiger Reserve** links back to the Day-80 environmental topic (uranium-mining concern in Nallamala) — the same forest, two syllabus angles.

- Eturnagaram = oldest/largest WLS (Mulugu); Manjira = crocodile sanctuary.
