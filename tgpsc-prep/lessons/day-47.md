## 🏛️ Polity: Fundamental Rights — Art 12-14

### 📖 Deep Dive

Fundamental Rights are in **Part III (Art `12-35`)**, justiciable and enforceable by the courts. The opening articles set the stage.

**Article 12 — Definition of "State".** Crucial because Fundamental Rights are (mostly) claimable **against the State**. "State" includes:
- The **Government and Parliament of India** (executive + legislature of the Union),
- The **Government and Legislature of each State**,
- All **local authorities** (municipalities, panchayats, district boards), and
- **Other authorities** within India or under the control of the Government of India.
The phrase **"other authorities"** has been widely interpreted — bodies that are instrumentalities/agencies of the State (statutory corporations, and per case law, entities under deep and pervasive State control) fall within Art 12 (e.g. *Ajay Hasia*, *R. D. Shetty* tests). The **judiciary** is generally not "State" when performing judicial functions.

**Article 13 — Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights.** Any law that takes away or abridges Fundamental Rights is **void** to the extent of the contravention. It embodies **judicial review**. Key doctrines:
- **Doctrine of eclipse** — a pre-Constitution law inconsistent with FRs is not dead but **eclipsed**, revivable if the FR is amended.
- **Doctrine of severability** — only the **offending part** is struck down, not the whole law.
- Art 13(4) (via the **24th Amendment**) clarified that a **constitutional amendment** under Art 368 is **not** "law" under Art 13 — but **Kesavananda Bharati** subjected amendments to the **basic structure** test.

**Article 14 — Equality before law & equal protection of the laws.** Guarantees to **any person** (citizen or not): **equality before law** (British concept — no one above the law) and **equal protection of the laws** (American concept — equal treatment in like circumstances). It permits **reasonable classification** (intelligible differentia + rational nexus to the objective) but forbids **class legislation** and **arbitrariness** (*E. P. Royappa*, *Maneka Gandhi* — arbitrariness violates Art 14).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Part III = Art 12-35. **Art 12** defines "State" (incl. "other authorities"); **Art 13** = judicial review (eclipse & severability); **Art 14** = equality before law + equal protection (reasonable classification allowed, arbitrariness barred).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Art 14 applies to "any person"** (not only citizens). "Equality before law" (negative, British) ≠ "equal protection of laws" (positive, American) — Art 14 has **both**.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Does Article 14 apply to foreigners?* → **Yes** — it uses "any person," so both citizens and non-citizens are protected.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Part III: **Art 12-35**.
- Art 12 "other authorities" → instrumentality tests (*Ajay Hasia*).
- Art 13: eclipse, severability; amendment vs basic structure.
- Art 14: reasonable classification = intelligible differentia + rational nexus.

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Cheyutha Pensions & Rajiv Aarogyasri

### 📖 Deep Dive

Two welfare pillars covering **social security** and **health**:

1. **Cheyutha (Aasara/Cheyutha pensions)** — monthly social-security pensions for vulnerable groups: **old-age, widows, persons with disability (PwD/differently-abled), beedi workers, single women, HIV+ (ART) patients, weavers, toddy tappers**, etc. The manifesto raised pension amounts (commonly cited figures: around **`₹4,000`/month** for old-age/widow/etc. and higher for the disabled — *verify latest sanctioned amounts and eligibility age before the exam*, as amounts and the qualifying age have been revised over time). Delivered largely through the earlier **Aasara** framework.

2. **Rajiv Aarogyasri** — Telangana's flagship **cashless health-insurance** scheme for BPL/eligible families, covering hospitalisation and specified surgical/medical procedures at empanelled hospitals. Coverage limits have been enhanced under successive governments (commonly cited enhanced cover up to **`₹5 lakh`** per family per year, and reports of moves toward higher limits — *verify latest coverage cap and package list before the exam*). It complements the central **Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY)** for eligible beneficiaries.

Together they address the **"Cheyutha" guarantee** (a helping hand) — income security in old age/vulnerability and protection against catastrophic health expenditure.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Cheyutha** = monthly pensions for old-age/widows/PwD/beedi workers etc. (amounts revised — **verify**). **Rajiv Aarogyasri** = cashless health cover for eligible families (enhanced up to `₹5 lakh`, **verify**); complements **Ayushman Bharat**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Pension amounts and the eligibility **age** have changed across governments — always write "verify latest official figure." Aarogyasri is a **state** health scheme (distinct from the central Ayushman Bharat, though they overlap).

## 🏰 Telangana History: Handlooms & Handicrafts (GI Products)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana is rich in **Geographical Indication (GI)**-tagged crafts:

- **Pochampally Ikat** (Bhoodan Pochampally, Yadadri Bhuvanagiri) — the famous **tie-and-dye Ikat** silk/cotton sarees; Pochampally is called the "**Silk City**"; a GI product (and the village recognised among best tourism villages).
- **Gadwal sarees** (Jogulamba Gadwal) — cotton body with a silk (zari) pallu/border, known for the "**kotakomma**" and light weight (could be folded into a matchbox); GI-tagged.
- **Narayanpet sarees** (Narayanpet) — cotton/silk sarees with distinctive borders; GI-tagged.
- **Siddipet Gollabhama** sarees — cotton sarees with the **Gollabhama** (milkmaid) motif; GI.
- **Cheriyal scroll paintings** (Warangal region) — **Nakashi** art narrative scroll paintings; GI.
- **Nirmal paintings & toys** (Nirmal, Adilabad) — soft-wood toys and paintings with golden hues; GI.
- **Bidriware** (originally Bidar, practised in the Deccan) — silver-inlay metal craft on blackened alloy; GI (Bidar/Karnataka, but historically Deccan).
- **Karimnagar silver filigree** — fine silver-wire ornamental work; GI.
- **Dokra (Adilabad)** metal casting (lost-wax) and **Warangal durries** (carpets) — GI-tagged.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Pochampally Ikat** (Silk City), **Gadwal** (silk-border cotton), **Narayanpet**, **Siddipet Gollabhama**, **Cheriyal (Nakashi) scrolls**, **Nirmal toys/paintings**, **Karimnagar silver filigree**, **Warangal durries** — all Telangana GI crafts.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Match craft to place: **Pochampally**–Ikat, **Gadwal/Narayanpet**–sarees, **Cheriyal**–scroll paintings, **Nirmal**–toys/paintings, **Karimnagar**–silver filigree. Cheriyal art style = **Nakashi**.

### 💡 Example

*Q: The Cheriyal scroll paintings belong to which art tradition?* → The **Nakashi** narrative-scroll painting tradition (Warangal region).

- Gadwal = light silk-bordered cotton saree.
- Cheriyal = Nakashi scroll paintings.
- Nirmal = toys & paintings; Karimnagar = silver filigree.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Government of India Act, 1935

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Government of India Act, 1935** was the last major constitutional statute before independence and a **direct source of much of our Constitution**. It followed the Simon Report, the Round Table Conferences and the White Paper.

**Key features:**
- **All-India Federation** — proposed a federation of **British provinces + princely states**. The states were to join voluntarily; because enough princely states never acceded, the **federation never came into being**. (Only the provincial part operated.)
- **Provincial Autonomy** — abolished **dyarchy in the provinces** (introduced by the 1919 Act) and gave provinces **autonomous** self-government with responsible ministries (elected legislatures). Provincial elections were held in **1937**, and Congress formed ministries in most provinces.
- **Dyarchy at the Centre** — introduced **dyarchy at the federal level** (federal subjects split into "reserved" and "transferred") — but this never came into force since the federation failed.
- **Bicameralism** — provided a bicameral legislature in several provinces.
- **Distribution of powers** — three lists: **Federal, Provincial, and Concurrent** (a model our Constitution's 7th Schedule follows); **residuary powers** with the Governor-General.
- **Safeguards & special responsibilities** for the Governor-General and Governors (they retained overriding powers).
- Established the **Federal Court (1937)**, the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**, and reorganised provinces (Sind separated, Orissa created; Burma separated by a related Act).
- Extended **communal/separate electorates** further.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — GoI Act 1935: **provincial autonomy** (dyarchy abolished in provinces), **dyarchy at Centre** (never enforced), **All-India Federation** (never formed), **three legislative lists**, **Federal Court** & **RBI**. Provincial elections **1937**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The 1935 Act **abolished** dyarchy **in the provinces** but **introduced** it **at the Centre**. The **federation never came into being**; only provincial autonomy actually operated.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Which Act abolished dyarchy in the provinces and introduced provincial autonomy?* → The **Government of India Act, 1935** (elections held 1937).

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Provinces: **autonomy** (dyarchy abolished); Centre: **dyarchy** (not enforced).
- Three lists → basis of 7th Schedule; residuary with Governor-General.
- Federal Court (1937), RBI established.
- 1937 provincial elections; Congress ministries in most provinces.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Forests, Wildlife Sanctuaries & Tiger Reserves

### 📖 Deep Dive

Forests cover roughly **`24%`** of Telangana's geographical area (*verify latest Forest Survey of India figure*), mostly **Southern Tropical Dry Deciduous** type, richest in the **northern districts** (Adilabad, Kumram Bheem, Mancherial, Bhadradri Kothagudem). Major species: **teak, terminalia, bamboo**, and the state tree **Jammi (Prosopis)**.

**Tiger Reserves (2):**
- **Kawal Tiger Reserve** — in the **Adilabad/Mancherial** region (Godavari-Pranahita catchment).
- **Amrabad Tiger Reserve** — in the **Nallamala hills (Nagarkurnool/Mahabubnagar)**, one of India's **largest** tiger reserves by area; part of the erstwhile Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam block. (Home to the **Chenchu** tribe.)

**Notable Wildlife Sanctuaries / National Parks:**
- **Kinnerasani WLS** (Bhadradri Kothagudem), **Eturnagaram WLS** (Mulugu — one of the oldest), **Pakhal WLS** (Warangal), **Pranahita WLS** (Adilabad/Kumram Bheem), **Shivaram & Kawal** (Mancherial/Adilabad).
- **National Parks:** **Mrugavani NP**, **Mahavir Harina Vanasthali NP**, and **Kasu Brahmananda Reddy (KBR) NP** — all around **Hyderabad**.

The **state symbols** include: state animal **Jinka (Chinkara/Blackbuck-linked deer — verify current designation)**, state bird **Palapitta (Indian Roller)**, state tree **Jammi Chettu**, state flower **Tangedu**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Forest cover ~**24%** (verify). **2 Tiger Reserves: Kawal (Adilabad) & Amrabad (Nallamala)**. NPs around Hyderabad: **Mrugavani, Mahavir Harina Vanasthali, KBR**. Eturnagaram & Pakhal are key sanctuaries.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Telangana has **two** tiger reserves — **Kawal** (north) and **Amrabad** (south, Nallamala). Amrabad is among India's **largest** by area. Don't confuse the three Hyderabad national parks with sanctuaries elsewhere.
