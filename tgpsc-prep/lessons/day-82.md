## 🏛️ Polity: Constitutional Bodies — EC, UPSC/TGPSC, CAG, AG & SC/ST/BC Commissions

**Constitutional bodies** derive their existence directly from the Constitution (unlike statutory bodies created by law). Their independence is protected by security of tenure, fixed service conditions and difficult removal.

**Election Commission (`Art 324`).** A **multi-member** body — the **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)** and (currently) two Election Commissioners. It superintends elections to **Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President** (not local bodies — those go to the State Election Commission). The **CEC can be removed only like a Supreme Court judge** (special majority of Parliament); ordinary ECs are removed on the CEC's recommendation. Tenure: **6 years or age 65**, whichever is earlier.

**UPSC & State PSC/TGPSC (`Art 315–323`).** The **Union Public Service Commission** conducts all-India and central recruitment; the **State PSC (TGPSC)** does so for the state. The Chairman/members of UPSC are appointed by the **President** (State PSC by the **Governor**), but in **both cases removal is by the President** on grounds of misbehaviour (after a Supreme Court inquiry) — a key safeguard of the state commission's independence.

**Comptroller & Auditor General (CAG, `Art 148–151`).** The **guardian of the public purse** — audits all accounts of the Union and the States and reports to the President/Governor, whose reports are laid before the legislature and examined by the **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)**. Appointed by the President, removable **like a Supreme Court judge**; tenure **6 years or 65**. Dr. Ambedkar called it the **most important officer** under the Constitution.

**Attorney General (`Art 76`).** The **highest law officer of India**, appointed by the President, holding office **during the President's pleasure**; the state equivalent is the **Advocate General (`Art 165`)**.

**SC/ST/BC Commissions.** **National Commission for SCs (`Art 338`)**, **National Commission for STs (`Art 338A`)**, and the **National Commission for Backward Classes (`Art 338B`)** — the last gained **constitutional status via the 102nd Amendment Act, 2018**.

| Body | Article | Removal |
|---|---|---|
| Election Commission | `324` | CEC like SC judge |
| UPSC / State PSC | `315–323` | by President (SC inquiry) |
| CAG | `148` | like SC judge |
| Attorney General | `76` | President's pleasure |
| NCBC | `338B` | — (102nd Amdt) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **CAG (Art 148)** and **CEC** are removed only by the **Supreme-Court-judge procedure**. The **NCBC** got constitutional status via the **102nd Amendment (2018)** under **Art 338B**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**EC-324, UPSC-315, CAG-148, AG-76, SC/ST/BC-338/338A/338B.**" Removal-"like-a-judge" club: **CEC + CAG**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — A **State PSC member** is appointed by the **Governor** but can be **removed only by the President** (not the Governor) — this split is a classic question. And the Election Commission does **not** conduct **local-body** elections (that is the State Election Commission under `Art 243K/243ZA`).

💡 **Example:** When the PAC examines the CAG's audit report on a government scheme, it is exercising the legislature's financial oversight — the CAG makes the executive accountable to Parliament.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- 102nd Amendment 2018 → NCBC constitutional (Art 338B).
- State PSC: Governor appoints, President removes.
- EC multi-member since 1993; CEC removal = special majority.

## 🏗️ Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Irrigation Policy — Kaleshwaram & Palamuru-Rangareddy

Telangana's irrigation strategy centres on **lift-irrigation megaprojects** that pump river water up onto the high Deccan plateau.

**Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Scheme (KLIS).** On the **Godavari**, billed as the **world's largest multi-stage lift-irrigation project**. Its core is a chain of three barrages — **Medigadda (Lakshmi), Annaram (Saraswati), and Sundilla (Parvati)** — plus massive pump houses, reservoirs and tunnels lifting water across districts. In **October 2023**, **piers of the Medigadda barrage sank/subsided**, triggering a safety crisis. The **National Dam Safety Authority (NDSA)** investigated structural defects, and the state government set up the **Justice P.C. Ghose Commission of Inquiry** to probe the project's planning, design and execution (**verify latest status/findings**).

**Palamuru-Rangareddy Lift Irrigation Scheme (PRLIS).** On the **Krishna**, drawing from the **Srisailam backwaters** to supply the chronically drought-prone **Palamuru (Mahabubnagar)–Rangareddy–Nalgonda** belt and provide drinking water to Hyderabad. It too is a multi-stage lift scheme with large reservoirs; its clearances and inter-state water-share disputes with Andhra Pradesh have been contentious (**verify latest status**).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Kaleshwaram** (Godavari) = three barrages **Medigadda–Annaram–Sundilla**; the **Medigadda pier subsidence (Oct 2023)** led to **NDSA** scrutiny and the **Justice P.C. Ghose Commission**. **Palamuru-Rangareddy** lifts **Krishna** water from Srisailam to south Telangana.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Kaleshwaram = Godavari = MAS (Medigadda-Annaram-Sundilla); Palamuru = Krishna = Srisailam to Palamuru.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Kaleshwaram draws from the **Godavari**; Palamuru-Rangareddy from the **Krishna** — don't swap the rivers. Cost figures, lift capacities and TMC allocations change — mark them "verify latest official figure."

💡 **Example:** The Medigadda barrage subsidence became a major governance/accountability issue — linking irrigation engineering to the dam-safety framework (NDSA) and a judicial commission.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- KLIS: Godavari; Medigadda/Annaram/Sundilla; Oct 2023 subsidence; NDSA; Ghose Commission.
- PRLIS: Krishna/Srisailam; drought-prone Palamuru.
- Lift irrigation needed because land slopes away from rivers (NW→SE).
- Inter-state Krishna water-sharing (KWDT) context.

## 🏺 Telangana History: Musunuri Nayaks, Recherla Velamas & the Bahmani Kingdom

After the fall of Warangal (1323), Telangana entered a turbulent interregnum before new Telugu powers reasserted control.

**Musunuri Nayaks.** A confederacy of Telugu warrior chiefs who rebelled against Delhi Sultanate (Tughlaq) rule. **Prolaya Nayaka** began the resistance (his **Vilasa grant** is a key source documenting Tughlaq oppression and the liberation struggle). His cousin **Kapaya Nayaka (Musunuri Kapaneedu)** **recaptured Warangal around 1336** and briefly united much of the Telugu country, allying with other chiefs and the nascent Vijayanagara. His power later declined against the Recherla Velamas and the Bahmanis.

**Recherla Velamas (Rachakonda & Devarakonda).** A powerful Velama Nayaka line that carved out kingdoms based at **Rachakonda** and **Devarakonda** (Nalgonda region). **Singama Nayaka**, **Anapota** and **Madhava Nayaka** were prominent; they contended with the Musunuris, the Reddy kingdoms, Vijayanagara and the Bahmanis for control of the Deccan.

**Bahmani Kingdom (1347).** Founded by **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (Hasan Gangu)** after a revolt of Deccan nobles against the Tughlaqs — the **first independent Muslim kingdom of the Deccan**, with capital at **Gulbarga (Ahsanabad)**, later shifted to **Bidar (Muhammadabad)**. The Bahmanis clashed repeatedly with Vijayanagara (Raichur Doab) and the Velama/Reddy chiefs over Telangana. The great minister **Mahmud Gawan** expanded and reformed the state. After his execution the kingdom **fragmented into the five Deccan Sultanates** — including the **Qutb Shahis of Golconda (1518)**, who would rule Telangana next.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Kapaya Nayaka** recaptured **Warangal (~1336)** from the Tughlaqs; the **Vilasa grant** (Prolaya Nayaka) is the key source. The **Bahmani kingdom (1347)** was founded by **Hasan Gangu** at **Gulbarga**, later **Bidar**; it broke into the **Deccan Sultanates** (Golconda Qutb Shahis 1518).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Musunuri frees Warangal (1336) → Velamas at Rachakonda/Devarakonda → Bahmani (1347, Gulbarga→Bidar) → splits into 5, Golconda 1518.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Musunuri Nayaks** (Prolaya & Kapaya) are distinct from the **Recherla Velamas** (Rachakonda/Devarakonda) — both are post-Kakatiya Telugu powers but different lines. The Bahmani capital moved **Gulbarga → Bidar** (not the reverse).

💡 **Example:** The **Vilasa grant** of Prolaya Nayaka vividly describes the devastation under Tughlaq rule and the Telugu chiefs' war of liberation — a prime primary source for this period.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Rachakonda & Devarakonda = Velama capitals (Nalgonda).
- Bahmani 1347, Hasan Gangu, Gulbarga→Bidar; Mahmud Gawan.
- Bahmani → 5 Deccan Sultanates (Qutb Shahi, Adil Shahi, Nizam Shahi, Imad Shahi, Barid Shahi).

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: WWII, August Offer, Cripps Mission & Quit India

The 1939–1942 years moved India from constitutional bargaining to the decisive mass revolt of Quit India.

**WWII & Congress resignation (1939).** Britain declared India a belligerent in WWII **without consulting Indian leaders**. In protest, the **Congress ministries resigned (Oct–Nov 1939)**; the Muslim League observed **"Deliverance Day."**

**August Offer (1940).** Viceroy **Linlithgow's** proposal: **Dominion Status** as the long-term goal, expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council with Indians, and a war advisory council. Crucially it conceded that no future constitution would be adopted without the consent of **minorities** (a veto to the League). The **Congress rejected** it (as inadequate); the League welcomed the minority assurance. It led to the **Individual Satyagraha (1940)** — Vinoba Bhave was the first satyagrahi, Nehru the second.

**Cripps Mission (1942).** Sent by Churchill under **Sir Stafford Cripps** as Japan advanced. It offered **Dominion Status** with an elected **Constituent Assembly** after the war, and the **right of provinces/states to opt out** (the seed of partition). All parties rejected it — Gandhi famously called it a **"post-dated cheque"** (on a crashing bank).

**Quit India Movement (1942).** On **8 August 1942**, the **AICC at Bombay (Gowalia Tank)** passed the **Quit India Resolution**; Gandhi gave the mantra **"Do or Die."** The British arrested the entire leadership on **9 August**, and the movement became a **spontaneous mass upsurge** — strikes, sabotage, and **parallel governments** at **Ballia (UP), Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal), and Satara (Maharashtra)**. **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank; **Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia** led the underground. It was crushed by 1943 but proved Britain could no longer govern India by consent.

| Event | Year | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Congress resign | 1939 | over WWII declaration |
| August Offer | 1940 | Dominion status; minority veto; rejected |
| Individual Satyagraha | 1940 | Vinoba 1st, Nehru 2nd |
| Cripps Mission | 1942 | "post-dated cheque"; opt-out clause |
| Quit India | 1942 | "Do or Die"; parallel govts |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Quit India**: resolution **8 Aug 1942** at **Gowalia Tank (Bombay)**; slogan **"Do or Die."** Parallel governments at **Ballia, Tamluk, Satara**. Cripps (1942) = **"post-dated cheque"** with the province **opt-out** clause.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1939 resign → 1940 August Offer + Individual Satyagraha → 1942 Cripps fails → 1942 Quit India (Do or Die).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **August Offer (1940)** first introduced the **minority-consent** condition (effectively a League veto); the **Cripps Mission (1942)** first offered the **opt-out / Constituent Assembly**. Don't attribute the opt-out to the August Offer.

💡 **Example:** The **Tamluk Jatiya Sarkar** (Midnapore) ran a parallel administration — courts, relief, and even a volunteer force (Vidyut Vahini) — during Quit India.

- Individual Satyagraha: Vinoba Bhave (1st), Nehru (2nd).
- Gowalia Tank = August Kranti Maidan; 8 Aug 1942.
- Parallel govts: Ballia, Tamluk, Satara (Prati Sarkar).

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Rivers — The Godavari System

![Telangana rivers map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

The **Godavari**, India's second-longest river and the **"Dakshina Ganga,"** is the lifeline of northern Telangana.

**Entry & course.** The Godavari rises at **Trimbak (Nashik, Maharashtra)** and enters Telangana in the north. A notable point is **Kandakurthi (Nizamabad)**, the **triveni sangamam** where the **Godavari, Manjira and Haridra** meet. It then flows past **Basara** (Saraswati temple), through the SRSP command, past **Kaleshwaram** and **Bhadrachalam**, before exiting toward Andhra Pradesh. It drains the **largest share** of Telangana's area.

**Major tributaries (in/through Telangana):**
- **Manjira** — a major right-bank tributary; hosts **Nizam Sagar** and **Singur** dams (drinking water for Hyderabad); Manjira Wildlife Sanctuary.
- **Pranhita** — formed by the **Wardha + Wainganga** confluence; the largest tributary by discharge, joining near Kaleshwaram.
- **Indravati** — joins in the northeast (Chhattisgarh border region).
- **Maner (Manair)** — flows through Karimnagar (Lower & Mid Manair dams).
- **Kinnerasani, Taliperu** — in the Bhadradri-Khammam area.

**Key projects on the Godavari system:**
- **Sri Ram Sagar Project (SRSP) at Pochampad** (Nizamabad/Nirmal) — a major multipurpose reservoir on the Godavari.
- **Kaleshwaram** barrages (Medigadda/Annaram/Sundilla).
- **Nizam Sagar & Singur** (on Manjira).
- **Lower Manair Dam** (on Maner).

| Tributary | Note |
|---|---|
| Manjira | Nizam Sagar, Singur dams |
| Pranhita | Wardha + Wainganga; largest discharge |
| Indravati | NE, near Chhattisgarh |
| Maner (Manair) | Karimnagar; Manair dams |
| Kinnerasani/Taliperu | Khammam-Bhadradri |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Godavari = "Dakshina Ganga"**, rises at **Trimbak (Nashik)**; **Kandakurthi** = Godavari–Manjira–Haridra **triveni sangamam**. **Pranhita** (Wardha+Wainganga) is the biggest tributary; **SRSP/Pochampad** is the major Godavari project in Telangana.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "Godavari tributaries: **PM-II-MK** → **P**ranhita, **M**anjira, **I**ndravati, **M**aner, **K**innerasani." Dams: "**SRSP on Godavari; Nizam Sagar/Singur on Manjira.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Nizam Sagar and Singur are on the Manjira** (a Godavari tributary), not on the Godavari main stem. The **Pranhita** is formed by **Wardha + Wainganga** (not a single source) — a common distractor.

💡 **Example:** Because the Godavari enters high in the north and the land slopes southeast, lift schemes like **Kaleshwaram** pump its water back up to command higher northern tracts.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Kandakurthi triveni (Godavari+Manjira+Haridra); Basara.
- SRSP at Pochampad; Kaleshwaram; Nizam Sagar/Singur on Manjira.
- Pranhita = Wardha + Wainganga; largest tributary by discharge.
