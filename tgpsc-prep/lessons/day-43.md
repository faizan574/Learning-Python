## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: The Union & Its Territory (Art 1-4)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Article 1** declares: **"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."** The **territory of India** comprises: (i) the **States**, (ii) the **Union Territories**, and (iii) any **territories acquired**.

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Art 1** | India = **Union of States**; defines the territory |
| **Art 2** | Parliament may **admit or establish new states** (not part of India) |
| **Art 3** | Parliament may **form new states** and alter areas, boundaries, or names of existing states |
| **Art 4** | Laws under Art 2-3 are **not** constitutional amendments (Art 368) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — For a bill under **Art 3**, the **President's recommendation** is required and the bill must be **referred to the concerned State Legislature for its views** — but those views are **not binding** on Parliament, which can pass it by a **simple majority**. Telangana was formed under **Art 3** (AP Reorganisation Act, 2014).

The phrase **"Union of States"** (not "Federation") signifies, per Ambedkar, that the **Union is indestructible** but the **states are not** — India was **not formed by an agreement** among states and no state can secede.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Art 2** deals with **new** states (admission/establishment); **Art 3** with **reorganising existing** ones. State-legislature views under Art 3 are **only consultative**. Boundary changes are **not** amendments (Art 4), so a **simple majority** suffices.

### 💡 Example
The creation of **Telangana (2014)** by an ordinary law (AP Reorganisation Act) under **Art 3** — with the state assembly's view sought but not decisive — shows Parliament's supremacy over state boundaries.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Art 1** = Union of States; **Art 2** = new states; **Art 3** = reorganise existing.
- Art 3 bill: **President's recommendation** + state view (**non-binding**), simple majority.
- **Art 4** = not a constitutional amendment.
- "Union of States" = **indestructible union**, states not indestructible.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Is the State Legislature's view binding on Parliament for a boundary-change bill under Art 3?*
→ **No** — it is only **consultative**.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: The Statehood Movement (2001-2014)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The revived statehood movement began when **K. Chandrashekar Rao (KCR)** founded the **Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS)** on **`27 April 2001`**, demanding a separate Telangana.

**Key milestones:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **2001** | **TRS founded** (KCR) |
| **2004, 2009** | TRS allied with Congress in elections |
| **29 Nov 2009** | KCR's **fast-unto-death** |
| **9 Dec 2009** | Home Minister **Chidambaram** announced the process to form Telangana (then paused amid Andhra protests) |
| **Feb 2010** | **Sri Krishna Committee** (Justice B.N. Srikrishna) constituted; report **Dec 2010** (six options) |
| **2011** | **Sakala Janula Samme** (42-day general strike); Million March, Sagara Haram (JAC-led) |
| **30 Jul 2013** | Congress Working Committee resolved to form Telangana |
| **Feb 2014** | **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014** passed |
| **2 Jun 2014** | **Telangana formed** — KCR first CM |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Sri Krishna Committee (2010)** examined the Telangana issue and offered **six options** in its report. The **Joint Action Committees (JACs)** — the political JAC led by **Prof. Kodandaram** — organised mass actions like the **Sakala Janula Samme (2011)**. Statehood came via the **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **TRS founded 2001** (KCR); **Sri Krishna Committee 2010** (six options); **state formed 2 June 2014**. The **9 Dec 2009 Chidambaram announcement** was **paused** days later — don't treat it as the final formation.

### 💡 Example
The **Sakala Janula Samme (2011)** — a 42-day strike by employees, students, and JACs — showed the mass depth of the movement and pressured the eventual 2013-14 decision.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **TRS 2001** (KCR); KCR fast **29 Nov 2009**.
- **Chidambaram announcement 9 Dec 2009** (paused).
- **Sri Krishna Committee 2010** — six options; JAC (**Kodandaram**).
- **AP Reorganisation Act 2014** → Telangana **2 Jun 2014**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who chaired the committee that examined the Telangana demand, and how many options did it give?*
→ **Justice B.N. Srikrishna**; **six** options.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Non-Cooperation Movement & the Swarajists

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)** — Gandhi's first mass nationwide struggle — was launched at the Congress's **Nagpur session (December 1920)** and **merged with the Khilafat** cause.

**Programme:** surrender of **titles**, boycott of **government schools, colleges, courts, councils, and foreign goods**, promotion of **khadi, national schools, and swadeshi**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The movement was **abruptly suspended by Gandhi on `12 February 1922`** after the **Chauri Chaura incident (5 Feb 1922, UP)**, where a mob burned a police station killing 22 policemen. Gandhi felt the movement had turned violent.

**The Swarajists:** after the suspension, **C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru** founded the **Swaraj Party (1 January 1923)**. They favoured **"council entry"** — contesting elections to the legislative councils to **obstruct the government from within** — against the **"No-Changers"** (Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad), who preferred **constructive village work**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Nagpur 1920 → Non-Cooperation + Khilafat → Chauri Chaura (5 Feb 1922) → suspended (12 Feb) → Swaraj Party (Das & Motilal Nehru, 1923)**".

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Chauri Chaura** caused the **suspension** (not the launch). The **Swarajists (Das & Motilal Nehru)** wanted **council entry**; the **No-Changers** opposed it. The movement combined **Non-Cooperation with Khilafat**.

### 💡 Example
The **Swaraj Party's** decision to enter councils and obstruct from within (winning seats in the 1923 elections) was a strategic shift from **boycott** to **participation-to-obstruct** — a recurring debate in the freedom struggle.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Non-Cooperation 1920-22**, launched **Nagpur (Dec 1920)**, with Khilafat.
- **Chauri Chaura 5 Feb 1922** → suspended **12 Feb 1922**.
- **Swaraj Party 1923** — Das & Motilal Nehru; **council entry**.
- **No-Changers** = Rajagopalachari, Patel, Rajendra Prasad.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which incident led Gandhi to suspend the Non-Cooperation Movement, and when?*
→ The **Chauri Chaura incident (5 Feb 1922)**; suspended **12 Feb 1922**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Rivers — The Krishna System

![Telangana Rivers Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Krishna** is the **second-largest river of peninsular India** (after the Godavari). It rises at **Mahabaleshwar** (Western Ghats, Maharashtra) and flows ~`1,400 km` through **Maharashtra → Karnataka → Telangana → Andhra Pradesh** to the **Bay of Bengal** (Hamsaladeevi).

**In Telangana:** it enters along the **Mahabubnagar-Narayanpet** stretch and cuts through the **Nallamala** hills at Srisailam.

**Major projects & tributaries:**

| Feature | Note |
|---|---|
| **Priyadarshini Jurala (PJP)** | First major project as the Krishna enters Telangana |
| **Srisailam / Nagarjuna Sagar** | Large dams (shared with AP); Nagarjuna Sagar = masonry dam |
| **Tungabhadra** | Joins at **Alampur (Sangameshwara)**, Jogulamba-Gadwal |
| **Musi** | Rises near Vikarabad, flows through Hyderabad, joins at **Wadapally** (Nalgonda) |
| **Lift schemes** | **Kalwakurthy, Nettempadu, Koilsagar, Palamuru-Rangareddy** |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The Krishna's first major Telangana project is **Priyadarshini Jurala**; the **Tungabhadra** joins at **Alampur**, and the **Musi** (through Hyderabad) joins at **Wadapally**. Water is shared under the **Krishna Water Disputes Tribunal (Bachawat / Brijesh Kumar)**. *(Verify latest project figures.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Ma-Ka-Te-A** journey; **Jurala first**, **Tungabhadra at Alampur**, **Musi at Wadapally**; Palamuru-Rangareddy lifts Krishna water." Source **Mahabaleshwar**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Krishna source = **Mahabaleshwar** (Maharashtra). **Tungabhadra joins at Alampur** (not the Godavari side). **Palamuru-Rangareddy = Krishna**; **Kaleshwaram = Godavari** — keep them apart.

### 💡 Example
The **Musi**, though flowing through Hyderabad, is a **Krishna tributary** (joining at Wadapally) — a neat cross-subject link with the Musi-pollution/rejuvenation topic. *(Verify latest.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Krishna = 2nd-largest peninsular river; source **Mahabaleshwar**.
- **Jurala** = first Telangana project; **Nagarjuna Sagar** = masonry dam.
- **Tungabhadra at Alampur**; **Musi at Wadapally**.
- **Palamuru-Rangareddy** lift scheme (Krishna).
