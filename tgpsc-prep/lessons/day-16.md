## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 Centre–State Relations & Federalism (7th Schedule, GST Council, Finance Commission)

India is a **federation with a unitary bias** — "an indestructible union of destructible states." Centre–State relations are **legislative, administrative and financial** (`Art 245–293`).

### 📖 Concept

**Legislative relations (`Art 245–255`).** Division of powers via the **Seventh Schedule**:

| List | Who legislates | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Union List** (`List I`, ~97/100 subjects) | Parliament | Defence, foreign affairs, railways, banking, currency |
| **State List** (`List II`, ~66/61 subjects) | State legislature | Police, public health, agriculture, land |
| **Concurrent List** (`List III`, ~47/52) | Both (Union prevails on conflict) | Criminal law, marriage, education, forests |

**Residuary powers** (not in any list) → **Parliament** (`Art 248`). Parliament can legislate on a State subject in special cases: **Rajya Sabha resolution (Art 249)**, **national emergency (Art 250)**, **states' request (Art 252)**, **international agreements (Art 253)**.

**Administrative relations (`Art 256–263`).** States must comply with Union laws; the Centre can give **directions**; the **Inter-State Council (Art 263)** coordinates. **All-India Services** (IAS/IPS/IFoS) serve both.

**Financial relations (`Art 268–293`).**
- **Finance Commission (`Art 280`)** — constituted every **5 years** by the President; recommends **distribution of taxes** between Centre and states (vertical) and **among states** (horizontal), and **grants-in-aid**. *(Current: 15th FC period; 16th FC constituted — verify latest.)*
- **GST & the GST Council (`Art 279A`)** — created by the **101st Amendment (2016)**; the Council (Union FM + state FMs) decides rates, exemptions, etc. **Union has 1/3 weight, states 2/3; decisions need a 3/4 majority**.
- **Grants:** statutory grants (Art 275), discretionary grants (Art 282).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Seventh Schedule** has **three lists** (Union/State/Concurrent); **residuary powers rest with Parliament** (Art 248). The **Finance Commission (Art 280)** is set up **every 5 years**; the **GST Council (Art 279A, 101st Amendment)** decides GST with a **3/4 majority** (Union 1/3 + states 2/3 weight).

💡 **Example:** On a **Concurrent List** subject like **education**, if a state law conflicts with a Union law, the **Union law prevails** (`Art 254`) — unless the state law has received Presidential assent. That's cooperative federalism with a Union tilt.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Union > State > Concurrent** lists; **residuary → Parliament**.
- **Art 249 (RS resolution), 250 (emergency), 252 (state request), 253 (treaty)** let Parliament enter the State List.
- **Finance Commission: Art 280, every 5 years**; **GST Council: Art 279A, 101st Amendment, 3/4 majority**.
- **Inter-State Council: Art 263** (Sarkaria/Punchhi Commissions on Centre-State relations).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**7th Schedule = 3 lists; residuary to Centre. FC = Art 280 (5-yearly money-sharing); GST Council = Art 279A (101st Amdt, ¾ vote).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Residuary powers go to the Union/Parliament** (unlike the USA where they rest with states). On the **Concurrent List**, the **Union law prevails** (Art 254). The **Finance Commission** is constitutional (Art 280); **NITI Aayog** is **not** (it replaced the Planning Commission, non-constitutional).

*(With Parliament (Art 248); Art 279A, inserted by the 101st Amendment.)*

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Mulki Rules & the 1969 Jai Telangana Agitation

The **violation of the Mulki rules** (jobs-for-locals safeguards of the Gentlemen's Agreement) sparked the **1969 Jai Telangana movement** — the first mass demand for a separate Telangana.

### 📖 Concept

**Mulki rules (background).** "**Mulki**" = a local/domicile of Hyderabad State. The **Mulki rules** (dating to the Nizam's era, **1919** formalisation) reserved **government jobs** for locals who had resided in the state for a qualifying period. Carried into the **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** as a safeguard for Telangana within Andhra Pradesh.

**The grievance.** After the 1956 merger, **non-Mulkis (mainly from Andhra region)** were alleged to have taken Telangana government jobs in violation of the safeguards; **surplus funds** were said to be diverted; developmental neglect grew.

**The 1969 agitation ("Jai Telangana").**
- Began in **January 1969** (student protests at **Khammam**, spreading across Telangana) demanding **enforcement of safeguards** and later a **separate Telangana**.
- Students, employees and intellectuals led it; the **Telangana Praja Samithi (TPS)** was formed (**Marri Chenna Reddy** emerged as a key leader).
- The movement turned intense; **police firing** killed **~300+ people** (`verify figure`) — a defining trauma.
- **1972 "Jai Andhra" agitation** (in the Andhra region, over the Mulki rules being upheld by the **Supreme Court in 1972**) was the mirror-image reaction.
- Ultimately led to the **Six-Point Formula (1973)** and **Art 371D (1974)** — special provisions for **equitable opportunities** (local cadres, Presidential order on jobs/education) for AP, replacing the Mulki rules.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **1969 Jai Telangana agitation** (starting Jan 1969, Khammam) arose from **violation of the Mulki rules**; it birthed the **Telangana Praja Samithi (Marri Chenna Reddy)** and led (via the 1972 Jai Andhra reaction) to the **Six-Point Formula (1973)** and **Article 371D (1974)**.

💡 **Example:** The **Mulki rules → 1969 agitation → Art 371D** chain is the exam's favourite cause-effect: a jobs safeguard, its violation, a mass movement, and a constitutional remedy (local cadres, Presidential order).

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Mulki = local/domicile**; rules reserve **jobs** for locals (1919 formalisation).
- **1969 Jai Telangana**: starts Jan 1969; **TPS / Marri Chenna Reddy**.
- **1972 Jai Andhra** (mirror movement) after SC upheld Mulki rules.
- Remedy: **Six-Point Formula (1973) → Article 371D (1974)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Mulki jobs violated → 1969 Jai Telangana (TPS, Chenna Reddy) → 1972 Jai Andhra → Six-Point Formula (1973) → Art 371D (1974).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **1969 = Jai Telangana** (separation demand); **1972 = Jai Andhra** (opposite reaction). Both trace to the **Mulki** issue. The constitutional remedy is **Art 371D (1974)**, not the original Gentlemen's Agreement. Verify the casualty figure.

*(Violation of the Mulki (jobs-for-locals) rules; Article 371D, 1974.)*

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement
### 🎯 Cabinet Mission to Independence & Partition

The final act (1946–47): the **Cabinet Mission**, the **Interim Government**, **Direct Action**, the **Mountbatten Plan**, and the **Indian Independence Act** partitioning British India.

### 📖 Concept

**Cabinet Mission (1946).** Three British ministers (**Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A. V. Alexander**) came to plan the transfer of power:
- **Rejected** the demand for a full Pakistan; proposed a **loose three-tier federation** (weak Centre — defence, foreign affairs, communications; provinces grouped in sections A/B/C).
- Proposed a **Constituent Assembly** (elected indirectly by provincial assemblies) and an **Interim Government**.
- Both Congress and League initially accepted, then fell out over the **grouping** interpretation.

**Interim Government (Sept 1946)** — headed by **Nehru**; the League joined later (**Liaquat Ali Khan** as Finance Member) but obstructed from within.

**Direct Action Day (16 August 1946)** — the League's call led to **communal riots** (esp. **Calcutta killings**), hardening the case for Partition.

**Mountbatten Plan / June 3rd Plan (1947).** Lord **Mountbatten** (last Viceroy) proposed **Partition**: provinces/assemblies to decide; Bengal & Punjab to be **partitioned**; a **referendum** in NWFP & Sylhet; **princely states** free to accede to either dominion (or notionally independent).

**Indian Independence Act 1947.** Passed by the British Parliament: created **two dominions — India and Pakistan — on 15 August 1947**; ended British paramountcy over princely states; each dominion got a **Constituent Assembly** with full legislative sovereignty. The **Radcliffe Line** demarcated the boundary (Punjab & Bengal), announced after independence, triggering massive migration and violence.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Cabinet Mission (1946)** rejected full Pakistan and proposed a **weak federal Centre + Constituent Assembly**; its breakdown, **Direct Action Day (16 Aug 1946)**, and the **Mountbatten (June 3) Plan** led to the **Indian Independence Act 1947** creating **two dominions on 15 Aug 1947** (boundary by the **Radcliffe Line**).

💡 **Example:** The **Cabinet Mission's "grouping"** of provinces was the fatal ambiguity — Congress and the League read it oppositely, the plan collapsed, and **Direct Action Day** pushed events irreversibly toward **Partition**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Cabinet Mission (1946): Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander**; rejected Pakistan; Constituent Assembly + Interim Govt.
- **Direct Action Day = 16 Aug 1946** (Calcutta killings).
- **Mountbatten/June 3 Plan (1947)**; **Indian Independence Act 1947 → two dominions, 15 Aug 1947**.
- **Radcliffe Line** = boundary (Punjab & Bengal).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Cabinet Mission (1946, weak-Centre federation) → Direct Action (16 Aug 1946) → Mountbatten June-3 Plan → Independence Act 1947 → two dominions (15 Aug 1947), Radcliffe Line.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Cabinet Mission rejected Pakistan** and proposed a **united India with a weak Centre** — it was NOT a partition plan (that was **Mountbatten's**). The **Constituent Assembly** was formed under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**, not the 1947 Act.

## 🗺️ Subject 5: Telangana Geography (with Map)
### 🎯 Area Focus: Khammam–Bhadradri (Kothagudem)

**Khammam** — Telangana's southeastern gateway on the **Godavari** — is coal, chillies, forests and the temple town of **Bhadrachalam**.

**🗺️ Reference map — Telangana regions (Khammam-Bhadradri in the southeast):**

![Telangana regions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

*(If the map doesn't display in email, open the lesson on GitHub — the SVG is at `tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg`.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Location & administration.** The undivided Khammam district (southeast, bordering **Andhra Pradesh** and **Chhattisgarh**) was reorganised (2016) into **Khammam** and **Bhadradri Kothagudem** districts (`verify`). Part of the old district (some mandals) was transferred to **AP** in 2014 (Polavaram-affected areas).

**Physiography & geology.**
- Mixed terrain: **Deccan plateau** with **Gondwana coal-bearing rocks** in the **Godavari valley**; **red and black soils**.
- The **Godavari** flows through **Bhadrachalam**; tributaries **Kinnerasani, Munneru, Sabari (Kolab)**.
- **Kothagudem / Yellandu / Manuguru** — major **Singareni coal** mining belt; **Kothagudem Thermal Power Station (KTPS)** — a major power hub.

**Forests & tribes.** Among Telangana's **most forested** regions — teak, bamboo; **Kinnerasani Wildlife Sanctuary**; large **Adivasi (Koya, Konda Reddi, Lambada)** population; the **Bhadradri** region is culturally tribal + pilgrimage.

**Agriculture & economy.** **Paddy, chillies** (Khammam is a major **chilli/mirchi** market), **cotton, tobacco**; **coal** (Singareni) and **thermal power**; **Kinnerasani** reservoir irrigation.

**Heritage & sites (cultural reference):**
- **Bhadrachalam** — the famous **Sri Sita Ramachandra Swamy Temple** on the **Godavari**, associated with **Kancherla Gopanna (Bhakta Ramadasu)**; a major pilgrimage (**Sri Rama Navami** celebrations).
- **Khammam Fort (Stambhadri)** — a historic hill fort.
- **Kinnerasani** wildlife/reservoir (tourism); **Parnasala** (near Bhadrachalam) — linked to the Ramayana (Sita's abode).
- **Papikondalu** (Godavari gorge) tourism (shared with AP).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Khammam-Bhadradri = **Singareni coal + Kothagudem thermal power (KTPS)** in the **Godavari/Gondwana** belt, **chillies** in agriculture, dense **teak forests & Adivasi (Koya)** culture, crowned by the **Bhadrachalam Sita Ramachandra temple** (Bhakta Ramadasu).

💡 **Example:** "Geology + culture of Khammam" → **Gondwana coal (Singareni/Kothagudem) + Godavari valley + chilli agriculture** (geology/economy) with **Bhadrachalam temple + Koya tribal heritage** (culture) — the full area-focus pairing.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Kothagudem = Singareni coal + KTPS thermal**; **Manuguru, Yellandu** coal.
- **Khammam = major chilli market**; **Kinnerasani** sanctuary/reservoir.
- **Bhadrachalam = Sita Ramachandra temple, Bhakta Ramadasu, on the Godavari**.
- Reorganised into **Khammam + Bhadradri Kothagudem** (2016); some mandals to AP (2014).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Khammam-Bhadradri = Coal (Kothagudem/Singareni) + Chillies + Godavari temple (Bhadrachalam, Ramadasu) + Koya forests.**"
