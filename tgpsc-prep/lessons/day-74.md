## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: President & Vice-President

### Deep Dive

**The President (Art 52-62)** — the **head of state** and formal head of the Union executive (**Art 53**; powers exercised on the aid & advice of the Council of Ministers, Art 74).
- **Qualifications:** citizen of India, **35+**, qualified to be a Lok Sabha member, and not holding an office of profit.
- **Election:** by an **electoral college** of **elected MPs + elected MLAs** (incl. Delhi & Puducherry), via **proportional representation, single transferable vote, secret ballot** (nominated members and MLCs **do not** vote).
- **Term:** 5 years; **removal by impeachment (Art 61)** for "violation of the Constitution" (quasi-judicial, by Parliament).
- **Powers:** executive, **legislative** (summons/dissolves, ordinances **Art 123**, assent/veto — **absolute, suspensive, pocket**), **judicial** (pardon **Art 72**), financial, and **emergency** (Art 352/356/360).

**The Vice-President (Art 63-71).**
- **Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.**
- **Election:** by an electoral college of **both Houses of Parliament** (elected + nominated members) — **not** state legislatures.
- **Qualifications:** citizen, **35+**, qualified to be a **Rajya Sabha** member.
- **Term:** 5 years; acts as President during a vacancy (max 6 months).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — President: electoral college = **elected MPs + elected MLAs**; impeachment under **Art 61**; pardon under **Art 72**; ordinance under **Art 123**. Vice-President: elected by **both Houses**, ex-officio **Rajya Sabha Chairman**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **President's** electoral college includes **MLAs**; the **Vice-President's does not** (only MPs). **Nominated members vote** in the VP election but **not** in the President's. Impeachment = "violation of the Constitution" (President); VP removed by a Rajya Sabha resolution (effective majority) agreed by Lok Sabha.

💡 **Example:** A Union Territory with a legislature (Delhi, Puducherry) has its MLAs voting for the **President** — a frequently-tested nuance.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- President: Art 52-62; college = elected MPs+MLAs; STV; impeachment Art 61.
- Powers: Art 72 (pardon), 123 (ordinance), vetoes (absolute/suspensive/pocket).
- VP: Art 63-71; both Houses; RS Chairman; 35+ (RS qualification).
- Nominated members: vote for VP, not President.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Who votes in the Vice-Presidential election?* → members of **both Houses of Parliament** (elected + nominated).

## 🗳️ Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Gruha Jyothi & Indiramma Indlu

### Deep Dive

**Gruha Jyothi** — free electricity to eligible domestic consumers:
- Provides **free power up to 200 units/month** per eligible household (consumption above the slab is billed normally). *(Verify eligibility conditions and the exact slab treatment.)*
- Aim: relief on household energy bills, especially for lower-income families; implemented via the DISCOMs with state subsidy.

**Indiramma Indlu** — the **housing** guarantee:
- Financial assistance to build a house for the **houseless poor** (per-unit aid; a figure around **₹5 lakh** per house has been discussed — *verify the current amount, number of units sanctioned, and site-allotment rules*).
- Targets those **without a pucca house**; successor/parallel to earlier 2BHK (Dignity Housing) efforts.

**Significance.** Together they address two basic needs — **electricity** (Gruha Jyothi) and **shelter** (Indiramma Indlu) — within the Six Guarantees, complementing central schemes (Ujjwala/PMAY).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Gruha Jyothi** = free electricity up to **200 units/month**; **Indiramma Indlu** = housing aid for the houseless poor (per-unit financial assistance — verify amount).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **200-unit** slab and the Indiramma Indlu **per-house amount/units** are revised and phased — write **"verify latest official figure."** Gruha Jyothi = **power**; Indiramma Indlu = **housing** (don't swap).

💡 **Example:** A household consuming **≤ 200 units** pays nothing under **Gruha Jyothi**; a houseless family receives construction aid under **Indiramma Indlu** — the two basic-needs legs of the guarantees.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Indiramma Indlu: housing for the houseless (per-unit aid; verify amount).
- Complement central Ujjwala/PMAY.

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Temples, Monuments & Tourism Heritage

### Deep Dive

Telangana's **built heritage** spans dynasties — a high-yield culture area:

- **Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple, Palampet** — Kakatiya; **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021)**; "floating-brick" shikhara.
- **Thousand Pillar Temple, Hanamkonda** — Kakatiya (Rudreshwara, Trikutalaya).
- **Bhadrachalam** (Bhadradri-Kothagudem) — **Sri Rama temple** on the Godavari (Kancherla Gopanna/Bhakta Ramadasu).
- **Yadadri (Yadagirigutta)** — **Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy** temple (recently renovated).
- **Vemulawada** (Rajanna-Sircilla) — **Sri Raja Rajeshwara Swamy** temple ("Dakshina Kashi").
- **Basar** (Nirmal) — **Gnana Saraswati** temple.
- **Alampur** (Jogulamba Gadwal) — **Navabrahma temples** + **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**.
- **Charminar (1591)** — built by **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**, icon of Hyderabad.
- **Golconda Fort** and the **Qutb Shahi Tombs** — Qutb Shahi dynasty, Hyderabad.
- **Waterfall/eco-tourism:** **Kuntala, Bogatha** (Bhadradri; tallest of some lists).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Ramappa (Palampet)** = UNESCO WHS 2021 (Kakatiya); **Charminar (1591)** = Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah; **Yadadri** = Lakshmi Narasimha; **Vemulawada** = Raja Rajeshwara ("Dakshina Kashi"); **Bhadrachalam** = Rama temple.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Ramappa** (not Thousand Pillar) is the UNESCO site. **Charminar built 1591** by **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** (also founded Hyderabad). Basar = **Saraswati**; Alampur = **Jogulamba** — keep deity-to-place pairings exact.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Morley-Minto & Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms; Lucknow Pact; Home Rule Leagues

### Deep Dive

**Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909).** Expanded legislative councils and introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** (communal representation) — a divisive feature. It gave **limited, indirect** Indian representation (no responsible government).

**Lucknow Pact (1916).** A joint **Congress-Muslim League** agreement (facilitated by Tilak & Jinnah): Congress **accepted separate electorates** in return for **League support** for self-government demands — a high point of Hindu-Muslim unity and of Moderate-Extremist reunion (both wings rejoined Congress here).

**Home Rule Leagues (1916).** Modeled on the Irish movement, demanding **self-government (Home Rule)**:
- **Tilak's Home Rule League** — launched **April 1916** (Maharashtra, Karnataka, etc.).
- **Annie Besant's Home Rule League** — **September 1916** (Madras and rest of India).
They **revitalised the national movement** during WWI and spread political consciousness.

**Montagu Declaration (August 1917) & Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (GoI Act, 1919).** The **August Declaration (1917)** promised **"gradual development of responsible government."** The **1919 Act** introduced:
- **Dyarchy in the provinces** (subjects split into "reserved" and "transferred").
- A **bicameral central legislature**; expanded (but limited) franchise.
- Separation of central and provincial budgets; office of **High Commissioner** for India.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Morley-Minto 1909** = separate electorates. **Lucknow Pact 1916** = Congress-League unity + Moderate-Extremist reunion. **Home Rule 1916** = Tilak (April) + Besant (Sept). **Montagu-Chelmsford 1919** = **dyarchy in provinces**; preceded by the **August Declaration 1917**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Separate electorates** came with **Morley-Minto (1909)**, accepted by Congress at **Lucknow (1916)**. **Dyarchy in provinces** = **1919 Act** (shifted to Centre by the 1935 Act). Two Home Rule Leagues: **Tilak (April)**, **Besant (September)** 1916.

💡 **Example:** The **Lucknow Pact (1916)** — Congress conceding separate electorates for League unity — is a classic "turning-point" PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Morley-Minto 1909: separate electorates (communal).
- Lucknow Pact 1916: Congress-League unity; Moderate-Extremist reunion.
- Home Rule 1916: Tilak (April), Besant (September).
- Montagu Declaration 1917; GoI Act 1919: provincial dyarchy, bicameral centre.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which reform introduced separate electorates?* → the **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Nizamabad-Kamareddy

### Deep Dive

The **Nizamabad-Kamareddy** belt lies in **northern Telangana**, between the **Godavari and Manjira** rivers (*verify current district boundaries*). **Nizamabad** (historically **Indur**) is an important agricultural-commercial hub; **Kamareddy** was carved out as a separate district.

**Rivers & irrigation.** Bordered/crossed by the **Godavari** and its tributary the **Manjira**. Major projects: **Nizam Sagar** (on the **Manjira**), **Sri Ram Sagar Project (SRSP)** at **Pochampad** (Godavari, Nizamabad), **Singur** (Manjira), and **Kaddam**.

**Agriculture.** The belt is a leading producer of **turmeric** (Nizamabad hosts a major **turmeric market**, and the **National Turmeric Board** is headquartered at Nizamabad — *verify*), **maize**, **sugarcane**, **red jowar** and **soybean**; **Armoor** is known for intensive farming.

**Heritage & tourism.** **Basar** (Gnana Saraswati temple, on the Godavari — administratively in Nirmal but culturally near this belt), **Domakonda Fort** (Kamareddy), **Sarangapur Hanuman temple**, **Ali Sagar** and **Pocharam** reservoirs, and the **Nizamabad Fort (Quilla)**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Nizamabad (Indur) = **turmeric** hub (National Turmeric Board, verify); rivers **Godavari & Manjira**; projects **Nizam Sagar (Manjira)**, **SRSP/Pochampad (Godavari)**, **Singur (Manjira)**; **Domakonda Fort** (Kamareddy).
