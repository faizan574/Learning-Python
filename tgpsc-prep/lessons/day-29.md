## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Fundamental Rights — Art 15-18 (Equality & Reservations)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Articles `15-18` form the **equality code** of the Constitution (Part III), building on the general guarantee of `Art 14`.

**Article 15 — Prohibition of discrimination** on grounds only of **religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth**:

| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| 15(1),(2) | No discrimination by State; equal access to shops, wells, roads, public places |
| 15(3) | State may make **special provisions for women and children** |
| 15(4) | Special provisions for **advancement of SC/ST & SEBCs** (added by **1st Amendment, 1951**) |
| 15(5) | Reservation in **educational institutions (incl. private)** (**93rd Amendment, 2005**) |
| 15(6) | **10% EWS** reservation (**103rd Amendment, 2019**) |

**Article 16 — Equality of opportunity in public employment:**

| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| 16(4) | Reservation for **backward classes** inadequately represented |
| 16(4A) | Reservation in **promotion** for SC/ST (**77th Amendment, 1995**) |
| 16(4B) | Carry-forward of unfilled vacancies (**81st Amendment, 2000**) |
| 16(6) | **10% EWS** in employment (**103rd Amendment, 2019**) |

**Article 17 — Abolition of untouchability**: its practice in any form is forbidden and punishable (**Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955**).

**Article 18 — Abolition of titles**: the State shall not confer titles (except **military and academic** distinctions).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — In **Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (`1992`)** the Supreme Court capped reservations at **`50%`**, introduced the **creamy layer** for OBCs, and barred reservation in promotions — the last prompting the **77th Amendment (16(4A))**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Bharat Ratna / Padma awards are NOT titles** under Art 18 and can be conferred, but they **cannot be used as prefixes or suffixes** to a name (**Balaji Raghavan case, 1996**). The `50%` cap can be **exceeded** only in extraordinary situations (and EWS's 10% is over and above it — upheld in **Janhit Abhiyan, 2022**).

### 💡 Example
The **103rd Amendment (2019)** added `15(6)` and `16(6)` for a **10% EWS** quota — reservation based on **economic** criteria for the first time, upheld by a 3:2 SC verdict in `2022`.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- `15(4)` = 1st Amendment (1951); `15(5)` = 93rd (2005); EWS `15(6)/16(6)` = 103rd (2019).
- `16(4A)` promotion = 77th; `16(4B)` carry-forward = 81st.
- **Indra Sawhney (1992)**: 50% cap + creamy layer.
- **Art 18**: military/academic distinctions allowed; awards ≠ titles.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which case laid down the 50% reservation ceiling and the creamy-layer principle?*
→ **Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)** (the Mandal case).

## 🏆 Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: The Six Guarantees (Overview)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Six Guarantees (Aaru Guarantees)** were the Congress's core promises in the **2023 Telangana Assembly election**, and form the governing welfare framework since **`7 December 2023`**. Each guarantee bundles several sub-schemes.

| # | Guarantee | Core promises (verify latest official figures) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mahalakshmi** | Free TGSRTC bus travel for women; monthly cash support to women; subsidised **₹500 LPG** cylinder |
| 2 | **Rythu Bharosa** | Per-acre investment support to farmers; support to tenant farmers/agri-labour; paddy bonus |
| 3 | **Gruha Jyothi** | **200 units** free electricity to eligible households |
| 4 | **Indiramma Indlu** | Financial assistance for house construction; house sites for the poor |
| 5 | **Yuva Vikasam** | Student support via a **Vidya Bharosa** card for education |
| 6 | **Cheyutha** | Monthly pensions (elderly, widows, disabled) + **Rajiv Aarogyasri** enhanced health cover |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — There are **exactly six** guarantees. Learn the **names and their theme** (women, farmers, power, housing, youth/education, pensions/health) — the exact rupee amounts keep changing, so **verify the latest official figure** before the exam.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Do not confuse the **guarantee names** with their sub-schemes. E.g. **Mahalakshmi** (women) bundles free bus travel + cash + LPG; **Cheyutha** (pensions) bundles Aarogyasri. Mixing these up is a classic negative-marking trap.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Badami Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas & Kalyani Chalukyas (with Vemulawada)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Between the 6th and 12th centuries, Telangana was ruled or dominated by three great Deccan powers in succession, with a key **local dynasty at Vemulawada**.

**Badami (Western) Chalukyas (`6th–8th c.`):** capital **Vatapi (Badami)**. Greatest ruler **Pulakeshin II** (`c. 610–642`), who **defeated Harshavardhana** on the Narmada — recorded in the **Aihole inscription** of court-poet **Ravikirti**. They controlled parts of western Telangana. Overthrown by the Rashtrakuta **Dantidurga** (`c. 753`).

**Rashtrakutas (`753–973`):** capital **Manyakheta (Malkhed)**. Founder **Dantidurga**; **Krishna I** built the monolithic **Kailasa temple at Ellora**; **Amoghavarsha I** (a great patron, wrote **Kavirajamarga** in Kannada) ruled long from Manyakheta. Arab travellers ranked the Rashtrakuta king among the **great kings of the world**. Ended by **Tailapa II** of Kalyani.

**Kalyani (Later Western) Chalukyas (`973–1189`):** capital **Kalyani (Basavakalyan)**. Founder **Tailapa II** (overthrew the Rashtrakutas). **Vikramaditya VI** (started the **Vikrama era**; patron of **Bilhana** (*Vikramankadevacharita*) and **Vijnaneshwara** (*Mitakshara*, the great law digest)). The **Kakatiyas** rose as their feudatories.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Vemulawada Chalukyas** (`c. 750–973`, capital **Vemulawada**, present **Rajanna Sircilla** district) were feudatories of the Rashtrakutas. **Arikesari II (Baddega)** patronised **Pampa**, the "**Adikavi**" (father) of Kannada literature, who wrote **Adipurana** and **Vikramarjuna Vijaya (Pampa Bharata)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Badami → Rashtrakuta → Kalyani**": Vatapi, then Manyakheta, then Kalyani. **Pulakeshin II beat Harsha**; **Krishna I built Ellora Kailasa**; **Vemulawada = Pampa**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Distinguish the **three** Chalukya branches: **Badami** (Vatapi), **Kalyani** (later western), and **Vengi** (eastern). Vemulawada is a **Telangana feudatory** line — its Pampa link is a high-frequency question.

### 💡 Example
The **Mitakshara** by **Vijnaneshwara** (Kalyani Chalukya court) is a foundational text of Hindu law still cited in inheritance cases — a static fact with modern relevance.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Pulakeshin II** — Aihole inscription (Ravikirti), defeated Harsha.
- **Krishna I** — Kailasa temple, Ellora; **Amoghavarsha I** — Kavirajamarga, Manyakheta.
- **Vikramaditya VI** — Vikrama era; Bilhana & Vijnaneshwara.
- **Vemulawada Chalukyas** — Arikesari, patron of **Pampa**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which Telangana feudatory dynasty patronised the Kannada poet Pampa, and from which capital?*
→ The **Vemulawada Chalukyas**, from **Vemulawada** (Rajanna Sircilla).

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Revolutionary Movement Phase II (Bhagat Singh, Azad, Surya Sen)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The second wave of revolutionary nationalism (`1920s–1930s`) was more organised and socialist in outlook than the first.

**Hindustan Republican Association (HRA), 1924** → the **Kakori conspiracy (`1925`)**: a train dacoity to fund revolution. **Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Roshan Singh, and Rajendra Lahiri** were hanged (`1927`).

**Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA), 1928**: reorganised at **Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi**, with a **socialist** goal, led by **Chandrashekhar Azad** and **Bhagat Singh**.

**Key actions:**
- **Saunders murder (Dec `1928`, Lahore)** — by **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru & Azad**, avenging the death of **Lala Lajpat Rai**.
- **Central Legislative Assembly bomb (`8 April 1929`)** — **Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt** threw bombs "**to make the deaf hear**" and courted arrest.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev** were hanged on **`23 March 1931`** (Lahore Conspiracy Case) — observed as **Shaheed Diwas**. **Chandrashekhar Azad** died fighting at **Alfred Park, Allahabad** on **`27 February 1931`**.

**Surya Sen (Masterda):** led the **Chittagong Armoury Raid (`18 April 1930`)**; associates **Pritilata Waddedar** and **Kalpana Datta** brought **women** into armed struggle. Surya Sen was hanged on **`12 January 1934`**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Kakori → HSRA → Assembly bomb → 23 March**": HRA/Kakori (1925), HSRA (1928), Assembly bomb (1929), Bhagat Singh's martyrdom (23 March 1931). **Chittagong = Surya Sen**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Kakori (1925)** belongs to the **HRA**; the **Assembly bomb (1929)** and Saunders killing to the **HSRA**. Bhagat Singh's essay "**Why I Am an Atheist**" reflects the movement's ideological maturity — don't reduce it to mere violence.

### 💡 Example
Bhagat Singh's slogan "**Inquilab Zindabad**" (Long Live the Revolution), raised during the Assembly bomb case, became a rallying cry of the entire freedom movement.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Kakori (1925)** — HRA; Bismil, Ashfaqulla hanged 1927.
- **HSRA (1928)** — Azad, Bhagat Singh.
- **Assembly bomb** — 8 Apr 1929 (Bhagat Singh & B.K. Dutt).
- **23 March 1931** — Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev hanged; **Surya Sen** — Chittagong 1930.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who led the Chittagong Armoury Raid, and in which year?*
→ **Surya Sen (Masterda)**, in **`1930`**.
