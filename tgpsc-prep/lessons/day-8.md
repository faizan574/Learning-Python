## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity

### Let's learn about **Fundamental Rights: Articles 23–30 & Article 32 (Writs)**

The remaining Fundamental Rights — against exploitation, for religion, for minorities — and the **Right to Constitutional Remedies** that makes them all enforceable.

**Right against Exploitation (Art 23–24):**
- `Art 23`: prohibits **human trafficking, begar (forced labour)** and similar forced labour (State can impose compulsory service for public purposes without discrimination).
- `Art 24`: prohibits employment of **children below 14** in factories, mines or hazardous work.

**Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25–28):**
- `Art 25`: freedom of **conscience & free profession, practice, propagation** of religion (subject to public order, morality, health).
- `Art 26`: freedom to **manage religious affairs**.
- `Art 27`: no **tax** for promotion of a particular religion.
- `Art 28`: freedom from **religious instruction** in wholly State-funded institutions.

**Cultural & Educational Rights (Art 29–30):**
- `Art 29`: protection of **language, script, culture** of any section; no denial of admission on grounds of religion/race/caste/language.
- `Art 30`: right of **minorities** (religious & linguistic) to **establish and administer educational institutions**.

**Article 32 — Right to Constitutional Remedies ("heart and soul" — Ambedkar):**
- Right to move the **Supreme Court** directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
- The SC (Art 32) and High Courts (Art 226) can issue **five writs**:

| Writ | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | "produce the body" — against illegal detention |
| **Mandamus** | "we command" — directs a public authority to do its duty |
| **Prohibition** | stops a lower court exceeding jurisdiction (before) |
| **Certiorari** | quashes a lower court's order (after) |
| **Quo-Warranto** | "by what authority" — challenges a person holding a public office |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Article 32** is itself a Fundamental Right and the **"heart and soul"** of the Constitution (Ambedkar). HC writ power (`Art 226`) is **wider** — it covers Fundamental Rights *and* other legal rights.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — Five writs "**HM-PCQ**": Habeas corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-warranto.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — `Art 32` (SC) enforces **only Fundamental Rights**; `Art 226` (HC) is **broader** (FRs + legal rights). Habeas corpus can be issued against **private** individuals too; Mandamus **cannot** be issued against a private person or the President/Governor.

**💡 Example:** During the Emergency, **Habeas corpus** petitions were central to the *ADM Jabalpur (1976)* case (later overruled) — showing why Art 32's writ jurisdiction is the ultimate safeguard of liberty.

**🎯 Exam Pointers:**
- Art 24: child labour < 14 in hazardous work.
- Art 25 words: profess, practise, propagate.
- Art 30: minority educational institutions.
- Art 32 = Fundamental Right; can't be suspended except by emergency (Art 359).
- Prohibition = before judgment; Certiorari = after (both against courts/tribunals).
- Mandamus not against private persons; Quo-warranto for public office only.

## 📘 Subject 2: Telangana Schemes & Important Incidents

### Let's learn about **SIR — Special Intensive Revision of Electoral Rolls**

A major national current-affairs "incident": the Election Commission's deep re-verification of voter rolls, and the enumeration form every voter must know.

**What is SIR?**
- **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** is an **intensive, house-to-house re-verification of the entire electoral roll** by the **Election Commission of India (ECI)** — far deeper than the routine annual "summary revision".
- It began with **Bihar in 2025** (ahead of its assembly polls) and was extended in phases to more states/UTs, with Telangana in the later phases (*verify current status*).

**The SIR (Enumeration) Form:**
- **Booth Level Officers (BLOs)** distribute a **pre-printed enumeration form** to every existing elector.
- The voter fills details and (where required) attaches **documents establishing eligibility/citizenship**, then returns it.
- Names of those who don't submit risk moving to the **draft-exclusion list**; a **claims-and-objections** window allows re-inclusion.

**Why it matters:**
- Decides **who can vote**; sparked debate over **migrant workers, documentation and possible disenfranchisement**; produced litigation reaching the **Supreme Court**.
- A hot topic straddling **Polity (Art 324, ECI)** and **Current Affairs**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **SIR = Special Intensive Revision** of electoral rolls by the **ECI** (constitutional body under `Art 324`); it began with **Bihar (2025)**, with door-to-door **BLO** enumeration forms.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**SIR: Special Intensive Revision → BLO form → submit or risk deletion → claims window**."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — SIR is by the **ECI**, not the state government. It is **not** the caste survey (SEEEPC). Non-submission can lead to **draft-roll exclusion**, correctable via claims/objections.

**💡 Example:** A Telangana daily-wage migrant working in Mumbai must ensure their SIR form is submitted back home (via family/BLO), or their name may drop from the draft roll — the "migrant disenfranchisement" concern at the centre of the debate.

**🎯 Exam Pointers:**
- SIR = Special Intensive Revision; conducted by **ECI (Art 324)**.
- First in Bihar (2025); phased to other states.
- BLO distributes enumeration forms door-to-door.
- Non-return → draft exclusion; claims & objections for re-inclusion.
- Distinguish from routine **summary revision** and from **SEEEPC** caste survey.

## 📘 Subject 3: Telangana History

### Let's learn about **The Qutb Shahis & the Founding of Hyderabad**

From the Bahmani break-up rose Golconda's dynasty — builders of a diamond-rich capital and a composite Telugu-Persian culture.

**Founding & rulers:**
- **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk**, a Bahmani governor, declared independence at **Golconda in `1518`**, founding the **Qutb Shahi** dynasty.
- **Golconda fort** — famed for acoustics, water systems and the **diamond trade** (Koh-i-Noor, Hope diamonds passed through Golconda markets).
- **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** founded the city of **Hyderabad in `1591`** on the **Musi** river and built the **Charminar** (commemorating the end of an epidemic); Mecca Masjid was begun in this era.
- **Ibrahim Qutb Shah** ("Malkibharama" to Telugu poets) — generous patron of **Telugu literature**.

**Culture & end:**
- Patronised **Telugu** alongside Persian/Deccani Urdu; Muhammad Quli himself wrote poetry (his *kulliyat*).
- **Bhakta Ramadasu (Kancherla Gopanna)**, the Bhadrachalam devotee-poet, served under **Abul Hasan Tana Shah**.
- **Aurangzeb captured Golconda in `1687`** after a long siege (betrayal at the gates), ending the dynasty; the last ruler **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** died a Mughal prisoner.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded Hyderabad in 1591** and built the **Charminar**; the dynasty began at **Golconda (1518)** and fell to **Aurangzeb (1687)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — Qutb Shahi timeline "**1518 Golconda → 1591 Hyderabad/Charminar → 1687 Aurangzeb**." Founder = Sultan Quli; city-founder = Muhammad Quli.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Sultan Quli** founded the dynasty (1518, Golconda); **Muhammad Quli** founded the city of Hyderabad (1591). Different rulers — a classic swap.

**💡 Example:** The **Charminar (1591)** stands where Hyderabad was founded, at the crossroads of trade routes — one monument answering "when, why, by whom": Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, plague's end, new capital.

**🎯 Exam Pointers:**
- Founder: Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk (1518, Golconda).
- Hyderabad & Charminar: Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah (1591).
- Ibrahim Qutb Shah = Telugu patron ("Malkibharama").
- Golconda = diamond trade (Koh-i-Noor).
- Ramadasu under Tana Shah; Golconda fell to Aurangzeb 1687.
- Qutb Shahi tombs (Ibrahim Bagh) = major heritage site.

## 📘 Subject 4: Indian National Movement

### Let's learn about **Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh & the Khilafat Movement**

1919: a repressive law, a massacre, and a pan-Islamic cause converge — setting the stage for Gandhi's first mass movement.

**Rowlatt Act (`1919`):**
- Officially the **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act** — allowed **detention without trial**; "**no vakil, no dalil, no appeal**".
- Gandhi launched the **Rowlatt Satyagraha** — his first **all-India** agitation (hartals, protests).

**Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (`13 April 1919`):**
- At Amritsar, on **Baisakhi day**, General **Reginald Dyer** ordered troops to fire on a peaceful gathering in an enclosed garden; hundreds killed (official ~379, Congress estimates far higher).
- **Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood**; the **Hunter Commission** inquired; **Udham Singh** later (1940) assassinated Michael O'Dwyer in London.

**Khilafat Movement (`1919–1924`):**
- Indian Muslims protested the harsh treatment of the **Ottoman Caliph (Khalifa)** after WWI (Treaty of Sèvres).
- Led by the **Ali brothers (Muhammad Ali & Shaukat Ali)**, Maulana Azad, Hasrat Mohani.
- **Gandhi** supported it to forge **Hindu-Muslim unity** and merged it with **Non-Cooperation (1920)**.
- Collapsed after **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk abolished the Caliphate (1924)** in Turkey.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919)** under General **Dyer** was the turning point that ended Indian faith in British justice; the **Rowlatt Act** (detention without trial) triggered Gandhi's first all-India satyagraha.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — 1919 chain: "**Rowlatt (no trial) → Jallianwala (Dyer, Baisakhi) → Khilafat (Ali brothers) → Non-Cooperation (1920)**."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Dyer** ordered the firing; **Michael O'Dwyer** was the Punjab Lt. Governor (killed by Udham Singh, 1940) — different men, often confused. Rowlatt = 1919, Non-Cooperation = 1920.

**💡 Example:** **Tagore's renunciation of his knighthood** after Jallianwala Bagh turned moral outrage into a national statement — the kind of symbolic protest that fed Gandhi's coming mass movement.

**🎯 Exam Pointers:**
- Rowlatt Act 1919: detention without trial; Gandhi's first all-India satyagraha.
- Jallianwala Bagh: 13 April 1919, Baisakhi, Dyer, Amritsar; Hunter Commission.
- Tagore returned knighthood; Gandhi returned Kaiser-i-Hind (during Non-Cooperation).
- Khilafat: Ali brothers; abolished by Atatürk 1924.
- Khilafat + Non-Cooperation merged (1920) — Hindu-Muslim unity peak.

What lies under Telangana's soil — and the plants that turn it into power.

**Coal — the Singareni story:**
- Coal lies in the **Gondwana rocks of the Godavari valley** — peninsular India's **only** major coalfield — mined by **Singareni Collieries Company Ltd (SCCL)**, HQ at **Kothagudem**.
- Coal was first noticed near **Yellandu**; SCCL is jointly owned by **Telangana (51%) and the Centre (49%)**.
- Mining towns: Kothagudem, Bellampalli, Mancherial, Godavarikhani, Ramagundam.

**Other minerals:**

| Mineral | Where |
|---|---|
| **Granite** | Karimnagar (tan-brown, exported), Warangal, statewide |
| **Limestone** | Nalgonda-Suryapet-Miryalaguda cement belt, Adilabad |
| **Iron ore** | **Bayyaram** (Khammam-Mahabubabad) — steel-plant demand |
| Others | Quartz, feldspar, dolomite, mica, coal-bed methane |

**Power (energy):**
- **Ramagundam NTPC** — a major thermal station; **Kothagudem, Bhadradri, Yadadri** thermal plants (SCCL + Genco).
- Hydel: **Nagarjunasagar, Srisailam (left bank), Pulichintala, Priyadarshini Jurala, Nizamsagar, Lower Jurala**.
- Solar push statewide; **Ramagundam floating solar** (on the NTPC reservoir) is a notable green project (*verify capacity*).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana's coal is in the **Godavari-valley Gondwana** belt (south India's only coalfield), mined by **SCCL** (State 51% : Centre 49%), HQ **Kothagudem**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Coal = Gondwana/Godavari/Singareni**; Iron = **Bayyaram**; Limestone = **Nalgonda cement belt**; Granite = **Karimnagar**."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — SCCL ownership is **State 51% : Centre 49%** (not the reverse). Coal = **Gondwana** rocks (not Cuddapah/Deccan). Iron-ore hub = **Bayyaram**.

**💡 Example:** **Ramagundam** ties minerals to power: Godavari-valley **coal** feeds the **NTPC thermal plant**, and a **floating solar** array now sits on its reservoir — one town spanning fossil and green energy, a favourite integrated question.
