## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Prime Minister & Council of Ministers

### Deep Dive

**The Prime Minister (Art 75).** The PM is the **real executive head** (the President is the nominal head). Appointed by the **President** — conventionally the **leader of the majority** in the Lok Sabha.
- **Art 74:** a **Council of Ministers (CoM) headed by the PM** aids and advises the President; this advice is **binding** (clarified by the **42nd & 44th Amendments**).
- **Art 75:** ministers are appointed by the President **on the PM's advice**; the CoM is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** [Art 75(3)]; a minister holds office during the **pleasure of the President**.
- **Art 78:** the PM communicates the CoM's decisions to the President.

**Council of Ministers — structure:**
- **Cabinet Ministers** (senior, head key ministries, form the Cabinet),
- **Ministers of State (MoS)** — independent charge or attached,
- **Deputy Ministers**.
*(The "Cabinet" is a smaller inner body; the "Council of Ministers" is the full set.)*

**91st Amendment (2003):** the **total size of the CoM ≤ 15%** of the strength of the Lok Sabha (and of a State Assembly); also anti-defection disqualification bars ministership.

**Collective vs individual responsibility.** **Collective** — the whole CoM is answerable to the Lok Sabha (a no-confidence motion unseats the government). **Individual** — a minister is answerable for their department (and holds office at the President's pleasure).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Art 74** (CoM aids & advises President, binding); **Art 75** (appointment, **collective responsibility to Lok Sabha**); **Art 78** (PM-President communication). **91st Amendment**: CoM **≤ 15%** of Lok Sabha.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Collective responsibility is to the Lok Sabha** (the popular house), even for Rajya Sabha ministers. The President's advice from the CoM is **binding** (post-42nd/44th). "Cabinet" ⊂ "Council of Ministers."

💡 **Example:** A no-confidence motion passing in the **Lok Sabha** forces the **entire CoM** to resign — collective responsibility in action.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- PM = real executive; appointed by President (majority leader).
- Art 74 (aid & advise, binding), 75 (collective responsibility, Lok Sabha), 78.
- CoM tiers: Cabinet, MoS, Deputy; Cabinet ⊂ CoM.
- 91st Amendment: CoM ≤ 15% of Lok Sabha.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *To which House is the Council of Ministers collectively responsible?* → the **Lok Sabha**.

## 🗳️ Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Cheyutha Pensions & Rajiv Aarogyasri

### Deep Dive

**Cheyutha (Aasara) pensions.** A monthly **social-security pension** for vulnerable groups — **old-age, widows, persons with disability, beedi workers, weavers, toddy tappers, single women, HIV-positive persons, filaria/dialysis patients**, etc. (eligibility by category). The **monthly amount** has been enhanced over time (*verify the current figure and any lowered eligibility age*).

**Rajiv Aarogyasri.** A **cashless health-insurance** scheme for eligible (largely BPL) families, covering **hospitalisation and listed procedures** at empanelled hospitals; the **coverage ceiling** has been raised in phases (a figure such as **₹10 lakh per family** has been discussed — *verify the current cover, procedures list and convergence with Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY*).

**Significance.** The two address **income security for the vulnerable** (Cheyutha) and **health-shock protection** (Aarogyasri) — the welfare "safety-net" legs of the Six Guarantees, overlapping with central **NSAP** pensions and **PM-JAY** health cover.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Cheyutha/Aasara** = monthly pensions for the vulnerable (old age, widow, disabled, occupational groups). **Rajiv Aarogyasri** = cashless hospital health insurance for eligible families (ceiling raised in phases — verify).

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Cuisine, Attire, Tribes & State Symbols

**Tribes.** Major scheduled tribes: **Gond, Koya, Lambada (Banjara), Chenchu, Kolam, Andh, Pardhan, Thoti** — concentrated in the forested north and the Nallamala.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Gandhi's Arrival & Early Satyagrahas

### Deep Dive

**Gandhi's return.** Mohandas Gandhi returned from **South Africa in 1915**, where he had developed **Satyagraha** (truth-force, non-violent resistance). On **Gokhale's** advice he toured India for a year, then set up the **Sabarmati (Satyagraha) Ashram** (Ahmedabad). His first Indian experiments used satyagraha on **local, economic grievances**.

**The three early satyagrahas (1917-18):**
- **Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar)** — **Gandhi's first** in India; against the **tinkathia** system forcing indigo cultivation; won relief for indigo **ryots**. (Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad assisted.)
- **Kheda Satyagraha (1918, Gujarat)** — **peasants** sought **revenue remission** after crop failure; a **no-tax** campaign (with **Vallabhbhai Patel**, Indulal Yagnik) — partial success.
- **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)** — **textile workers** vs mill-owners over wages; Gandhi used his **first hunger strike (fast)**; arbitration gave workers a raise.

These established Gandhi's method of **mass, non-violent, issue-based mobilisation** and brought him to national prominence before the Rowlatt/Non-Cooperation phase.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Gandhi returned **1915**; **Champaran (1917, Bihar, indigo/tinkathia)** = first satyagraha in India; **Kheda (1918, Gujarat, revenue remission, with Patel)**; **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918, first fast)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Champaran (1917)** is the **first** Indian satyagraha (indigo). **Kheda** = peasants/revenue (Gujarat), **Ahmedabad** = mill workers (first hunger strike) — keep the three distinct by issue and group.

💡 **Example:** **Champaran (indigo farmers)** vs **Kheda (revenue-paying peasants)** vs **Ahmedabad (industrial workers)** is the classic "match the satyagraha" PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Gandhi returned 1915; mentor Gokhale; Sabarmati Ashram.
- Champaran 1917 (Bihar, indigo/tinkathia) — first satyagraha.
- Kheda 1918 (Gujarat, revenue remission, Patel).
- Ahmedabad Mill Strike 1918 (first fast/hunger strike).

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which was Gandhi's first satyagraha in India?* → **Champaran (1917)**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Karimnagar-Jagtial

### Deep Dive

The **Karimnagar-Jagtial** belt (with **Peddapalli** and **Rajanna-Sircilla**) lies in **north-central Telangana** on the **Godavari** and its tributary the **Maner (Manair)** (*verify current district boundaries*).

**Rivers & irrigation.** The **Godavari** and **Maner** cross the belt; projects include the **Lower Manair Dam (LMD, Karimnagar)**, **Mid-Manair Dam**, **SRSP Stage-II**, and **Kaleshwaram** command-area canals. The region is relatively **well-irrigated** (part of the "rice bowl").

**Economy & minerals.** **Karimnagar** is famed for **granite** (a major **black-granite export** hub) and **silver filigree** (exquisite silverwork — GI). **Sircilla** is a **textile/powerloom** town. **Peddapalli (Ramagundam)** hosts the **NTPC thermal plant** and **Singareni coal** — a major **power-coal** node.

**Heritage & tourism.** **Vemulawada** (Rajanna-Sircilla) — the **Sri Raja Rajeshwara Swamy** temple ("Dakshina Kashi"); **Kondagattu** (Jagtial) — **Anjaneya (Hanuman)** temple; **Dharmapuri** (Jagtial) — **Narasimha** temple on the Godavari; **Elgandal Fort** (Karimnagar); **Kaleshwaram** (Mukteshwara temple at the Godavari-Pranahita confluence, Jayashankar Bhupalpally — nearby).

![Telangana regions base map — locate the Karimnagar-Jagtial belt on the Godavari-Maner](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Karimnagar = **granite** export + **silver filigree** (GI); **Sircilla** = textiles; **Ramagundam (Peddapalli)** = NTPC + Singareni coal; rivers **Godavari & Maner** (Lower/Mid Manair dams); **Vemulawada** (Raja Rajeshwara) & **Kondagattu** (Anjaneya).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Silver filigree = Karimnagar** (not Nirmal); **Ramagundam** (power/coal) is in **Peddapalli**. **Vemulawada** (Raja Rajeshwara) is in **Rajanna-Sircilla** — keep place-to-feature pairings exact.

💡 **Example:** **Karimnagar silver filigree** and **Ramagundam NTPC** are the belt's anchor "craft" and "power" PYQs respectively.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Karimnagar: granite export, silver filigree (GI).
- Sircilla: textiles/powerlooms; Ramagundam (Peddapalli): NTPC + coal.
- Rivers: Godavari & Maner; LMD, Mid-Manair, SRSP-II.
- Temples: Vemulawada (Raja Rajeshwara), Kondagattu (Anjaneya), Dharmapuri (Narasimha).
