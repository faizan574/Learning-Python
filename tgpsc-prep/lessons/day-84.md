## 🏛️ Polity: Schedules, Key Articles & Landmark Cases (Quick Revision)

This consolidation ties together the **12 Schedules**, the **most-tested articles**, and the **landmark judgments** that shaped the Constitution.

**The 12 Schedules.**

| Sch | Subject |
|---|---|
| 1st | States & UTs (territories) |
| 2nd | Salaries/emoluments of officials |
| 3rd | Forms of oaths/affirmations |
| 4th | Allocation of Rajya Sabha seats |
| 5th | Administration of Scheduled Areas & STs |
| 6th | Tribal areas (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram) |
| 7th | Union, State & Concurrent Lists |
| 8th | 22 official languages |
| 9th | Laws immune from judicial review (land reform; post-1973 subject to basic structure) |
| 10th | Anti-defection |
| 11th | Panchayats (29 subjects) |
| 12th | Municipalities (18 subjects) |

**Key articles to never forget:** `Art 1` (Union & territory), `Art 14` (equality), `Art 19` (freedoms), `Art 21` (life & liberty), `Art 32` (writs — "heart and soul," Ambedkar), `Art 40` (panchayats, DPSP), `Art 51A` (duties), `Art 72/161` (pardon — President/Governor), `Art 148` (CAG), `Art 280` (Finance Commission), `Art 324` (EC), `Art 352/356/360` (emergencies), `Art 368` (amendment).

**Landmark cases.**
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** — the **Basic Structure doctrine**; Parliament can amend but not destroy the basic structure.
- **Golaknath (1967)** — FRs cannot be amended (later overruled in effect by Kesavananda).
- **Minerva Mills (1980)** — balance between FRs and DPSP is part of the basic structure.
- **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** — `Art 21` "procedure established by law" must be **just, fair and reasonable** (due process widened).
- **S.R. Bommai (1994)** — `Art 356` subject to judicial review; floor test.
- **Indra Sawhney (1992)** — upheld OBC reservation with a **creamy-layer** limit and 50% ceiling.
- **Puttaswamy (2017)** — **Right to Privacy** is a fundamental right under `Art 21`.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** gave the **Basic Structure doctrine**; **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** widened `Art 21`; **Puttaswamy (2017)** made **privacy** a fundamental right. The **9th Schedule** (land-reform immunity) is, post-1973, still testable against the basic structure (**I.R. Coelho, 2007**).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**7th = three lists, 8th = languages, 9th = immunity, 10th = defection, 11th = panchayats (29), 12th = municipalities (18).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **6th Schedule** covers tribal areas of **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram** only; the **5th Schedule** covers other Scheduled Areas. The 9th Schedule is **not** absolutely immune anymore (I.R. Coelho).

💡 **Example:** Aadhaar's constitutionality was tested against the **Puttaswamy** privacy standard — a direct application of the 2017 judgment.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- 8th Schedule: 22 languages; 10th: anti-defection.
- Basic structure: Kesavananda 1973; Minerva Mills 1980.
- Art 32 = heart & soul; Art 21 widened by Maneka Gandhi.
- Indra Sawhney: creamy layer + 50% cap.

## 🏗️ Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Central Schemes in Telangana (PM-KISAN, Ayushman & Overlaps)

Many **central (Union) schemes** run in parallel with **Telangana's own schemes** — the exam tests the **pairing and the overlap**. Treat all amounts as **"verify latest official figure."**

**Agriculture.**
- **PM-KISAN** (central) — income support (₹6,000/year in three instalments) to landholding farmers.
- **Rythu Bharosa / Rythu Bandhu** (state) — Telangana's investment-support scheme; runs **alongside** PM-KISAN (a farmer may receive both).

**Health.**
- **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY** (central) — health cover for eligible families.
- **Rajiv Aarogyasri** (state) — Telangana's long-running health-insurance scheme; the state integrates/tops up coverage (verify current limit).

**Housing.**
- **PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana)** (central) vs **Indiramma Indlu / 2BHK** (state).

**Energy / LPG.**
- **PM Ujjwala Yojana** (central LPG connections) vs **Mahalakshmi ₹500 LPG** (state subsidy).

**Others.** Jal Jeevan Mission (tap water), MGNREGA (rural employment), PM Poshan (mid-day meal), PMGSY (rural roads) — all operate in Telangana with state counterparts/convergence.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Key central–state pairs: **PM-KISAN ↔ Rythu Bharosa** (farmer income), **Ayushman PM-JAY ↔ Rajiv Aarogyasri** (health), **PMAY ↔ Indiramma Indlu** (housing), **Ujjwala ↔ Mahalakshmi ₹500 LPG** (cooking gas). Amounts/coverage — **verify latest**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**KISAN–Bharosa, Ayushman–Aarogyasri, PMAY–Indiramma, Ujjwala–Mahalakshmi.**" (Central ↔ State twins.)

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — A beneficiary can often receive **both** the central and state scheme (e.g., PM-KISAN + Rythu Bharosa). Don't assume they are mutually exclusive. All rupee figures change — mark "verify."

💡 **Example:** A small farmer may get **PM-KISAN (₹6,000/yr central)** and the state's **Rythu Bharosa** support in the same year — convergence, not conflict.

- PM-JAY vs Aarogyasri; PMAY vs Indiramma Indlu.

- Convergence/overlap, not exclusivity.

## 🏺 Telangana History: Asaf Jahis (1724–1911) — Salar Jung Reforms & Subsidiary Alliance

The **Asaf Jahi dynasty (the Nizams of Hyderabad)** ruled from **1724 to 1948**, the longest-lasting princely power in the Deccan.

**Foundation.** **Mir Qamaruddin Khan, titled Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I**, a Mughal governor of the Deccan, asserted autonomy as Mughal authority collapsed; his victory at the **Battle of Shakar Kheda (1724)** is taken as the dynasty's effective founding. Hyderabad became a de-facto independent state.

**Subsidiary Alliance.** **Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II)** was the **first Indian ruler to sign Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance (1798)** — stationing British troops, paying for them, and surrendering foreign relations, which made Hyderabad a British-protected state. (The **Berar** region's assignment to the British to pay for the subsidiary force became a long-running issue.)

**Salar Jung reforms.** **Mir Turab Ali Khan, Salar Jung I**, was the reforming **Diwan (PM)** (c. 1853–1883) under Nizams IV–VI. He modernised the administration: reorganised the state into **districts (zillas) with Talukdars**, overhauled **revenue, police, judiciary, postal and railway** systems, and promoted **education** — laying the institutional base of the modern Hyderabad State.

**The seven Nizams.** From **Asaf Jah I (1724)** to **Mir Osman Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VII)**, who **acceded in 1911** and was the **last Nizam** (reigned until the 1948 integration). The period **1724–1911** thus spans the founding to Osman Ali Khan's accession.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Asaf Jah I founded the dynasty (1724, Shakar Kheda)**; **Nizam Ali Khan signed the Subsidiary Alliance (1798)** — the first to do so; **Salar Jung I** was the great reforming Diwan (c. 1853–83); **Mir Osman Ali Khan acceded in 1911** (7th and last Nizam).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1724 founded (Asaf Jah I) → 1798 Subsidiary Alliance (Nizam Ali) → Salar Jung reforms (1850s–80s) → 1911 Osman Ali Khan.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Nizam of Hyderabad was the first to sign the Subsidiary Alliance (1798)**, not the last. **Salar Jung** was the **Diwan/PM-reformer**, not a Nizam. Osman Ali Khan (7th) is the **last** Nizam.

💡 **Example:** Salar Jung I's district (zilla) system and revenue reforms created the administrative skeleton that the modern Telangana districts still echo.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Asaf Jah I, Shakar Kheda 1724; capital eventually Hyderabad.
- Subsidiary Alliance 1798 (Nizam Ali Khan, first signatory).
- Salar Jung I: districts, revenue, railways, education.
- Seven Nizams; Osman Ali Khan (VII) acceded 1911.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Cabinet Mission to Independence & Partition

The final act (1946–47) moved from a last attempt at a united India to Partition and freedom.

**Cabinet Mission (1946).** Three British ministers — **Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps and A.V. Alexander** — came to plan the transfer of power. The plan **rejected the demand for a separate Pakistan** but proposed a **loose federation** with **grouping of provinces** (Groups A/B/C), a **Constituent Assembly**, and an **interim government**. Both Congress and the League initially accepted parts, then diverged over the grouping clause.

**Constituent Assembly & Interim Government.** The **Constituent Assembly was elected (1946)** and first met in **December 1946**; the **Interim Government under Nehru took office (2 September 1946)**. The **Muslim League launched Direct Action Day (16 August 1946)**, triggering the **Great Calcutta Killings** and widespread communal violence.

**Mountbatten & the 3 June Plan (1947).** **Lord Mountbatten** (the last Viceroy) announced the **3 June 1947 Plan** for partition and an earlier transfer of power. The **Indian Independence Act 1947** (passed by the British Parliament, received assent **18 July 1947**) partitioned British India into **India and Pakistan**.

**Partition & Independence.** The **Radcliffe Boundary Commission** (Sir Cyril Radcliffe) drew the borders in Punjab and Bengal (announced after 15 August). **Pakistan became independent on 14 August and India on 15 August 1947.** Partition caused massive migration and communal violence.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Cabinet Mission (1946)** rejected Pakistan but proposed grouping + Constituent Assembly; **Direct Action Day = 16 Aug 1946**; **3 June Plan (Mountbatten) 1947**; **Indian Independence Act 1947**; **Radcliffe** drew the border; **Independence 15 Aug 1947**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Cabinet Mission (1946) → Interim Govt (Sept 1946) → Direct Action (16 Aug 1946) → 3 June Plan → Independence Act → 15 Aug 1947.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Cabinet Mission rejected Pakistan** (it proposed a united federation with grouping). It was the **3 June Plan (1947)**, not the Cabinet Mission, that accepted partition. **Radcliffe** headed the boundary commission.

💡 **Example:** The Constituent Assembly that framed our Constitution was itself a product of the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)** — its elected body continued as the sovereign Constituent Assembly after independence.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Cabinet Mission members: Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander.
- Direct Action Day 16 Aug 1946; Interim Govt 2 Sept 1946.
- Mountbatten = last Viceroy; 3 June 1947 Plan.
- Indian Independence Act 1947; Radcliffe Line; 15 Aug 1947.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Climate, Rainfall Patterns & Droughts

![Telangana regions map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

Telangana has a **tropical, broadly semi-arid to dry sub-humid climate** — hot summers and monsoon-dependent rainfall.

**Seasons.** Three main seasons: **summer (Mar–May, very hot** — north Telangana towns like Ramagundam/Nizamabad record extreme heat), the **southwest monsoon (June–Sept)**, and a short **post-monsoon/winter** (Oct–Feb). Summers are severe on the plateau; winters are mild.

**Rainfall.** The **southwest (SW) monsoon** delivers the **bulk (~80%+)** of the annual rainfall; the **northeast (NE) monsoon** adds a smaller amount (more to the southern/eastern fringe). The **average annual rainfall is roughly 900–960 mm** (**verify latest official figure**), with **north/northeast Telangana (Adilabad, Khammam) wetter** and the **southwest (Mahabubnagar/Palamuru) drier** — a key gradient.

**Droughts.** Southern and western Telangana — historically **Mahabubnagar (Palamuru), Nalgonda, Rangareddy** — are **drought-prone** due to lower, erratic rainfall and the rain-shadow/plateau position; this is precisely why **lift-irrigation schemes (Palamuru-Rangareddy, Kalwakurthy)** were built. Rainfall variability and dry spells within the monsoon are recurrent agricultural risks.

| Feature | Detail (verify) |
|---|---|
| Climate type | tropical semi-arid → dry sub-humid |
| Main rains | SW monsoon (Jun–Sep), ~80%+ |
| Avg annual rainfall | ~900–960 mm (verify) |
| Wetter belt | north/NE (Adilabad, Khammam) |
| Drier/drought belt | SW (Mahabubnagar, Nalgonda) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **SW monsoon (Jun–Sep)** gives Telangana most of its rain (~80%+); **average annual rainfall ≈ 900–960 mm (verify)**; **north/NE Telangana is wetter**, the **SW (Palamuru belt) is drought-prone**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**SW monsoon = most rain; NW/SW districts = driest (Palamuru) → lift schemes.**" Wet north-east, dry south-west.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **southwest monsoon** (not the NE monsoon) is Telangana's main rain source, unlike coastal Tamil Nadu/south AP which rely more on the NE monsoon. Exact rainfall figures vary yearly — mark "verify."

💡 **Example:** The drought-prone **Palamuru (Mahabubnagar)** belt's low, erratic rainfall is the direct rationale for the **Palamuru-Rangareddy lift scheme** drawing Krishna water.
