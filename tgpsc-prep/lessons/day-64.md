## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Union and its Territory (Articles 1-4)

### Deep Dive

**Part I (Articles 1-4)** of the Constitution defines the **Union and its territory** — the legal identity of the country and how it may grow or be reorganised.

**Article 1 — Name and territory.** "**India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.**" The framers deliberately chose "**Union**" (Ambedkar's phrase) over "Federation" to stress two ideas: (i) the Indian union is **not the result of an agreement** among states, and (ii) **no state has the right to secede** — it is an *indestructible Union of destructible states*. The **territory of India** comprises three categories: `(a) territories of the States`, `(b) the Union Territories`, and `(c) territories that may be acquired`. Note: "territory of India" is **wider** than "Union of States" — it also includes UTs and acquired areas.

**Article 2 — New states from outside.** Parliament may **admit** into the Union, or **establish**, new states "on such terms and conditions as it thinks fit" (relates to territories *not* already part of India).

**Article 3 — Reorganising existing states.** Parliament may (a) form a new state, (b) increase/decrease any state's area, (c) alter boundaries, (d) alter the **name** of a state. **Procedure:**
- A bill needs the **President's prior recommendation**.
- The President **refers the bill to the concerned State Legislature** for its views within a specified period — but these views are **not binding** on Parliament.
- The bill is passed by a **simple majority** (not a special/Art 368 majority).

**Article 4** — laws made under Arts 2 & 3 are **not** deemed constitutional amendments under Art 368 (so simple majority suffices).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana was formed as India's **29th state** on `2 June 2014` under the **Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014**, via the ordinary Art 3 route (simple majority).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Ceding Indian territory to a foreign country** cannot be done under Art 3; it needs a **constitutional amendment (Art 368)** — held in the *Berubari Union* case (1960). The `100th Amendment (2015)` gave effect to the India–Bangladesh **Land Boundary Agreement**.

💡 **Example:** When Parliament renamed and reorganised states, it used Art 3 — but the transfer of enclaves to Bangladesh required the 100th Amendment, showing the Art 3 vs Art 368 boundary in action.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Art 1: "Union of States"; territory = States + UTs + acquired territories.
- Art 3 bill: President's recommendation + reference to state legislature (views **non-binding**) + **simple majority**.
- Berubari (1960): cession to a foreign state ⇒ Art 368 amendment.
- State legislature's opinion under Art 3 does **not** bind Parliament.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Can Parliament change a state's name against that state's wishes?* → **Yes** — the state's views are advisory only; a simple majority in Parliament suffices under Art 3.

## 🗳️ Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: SIR (Special Intensive Revision) & Electoral-Roll Issues

### Deep Dive

**SIR = Special Intensive Revision** of electoral rolls, an exercise conducted by the **Election Commission of India (ECI)** to prepare a **clean, error-free voter list** — removing ineligible/duplicate/deceased entries and adding eligible new voters, often through **house-to-house (door-to-door) enumeration** with fresh forms.

**Legal basis.** The ECI's power to prepare and revise electoral rolls flows from **Article 324** (superintendence of elections), **Article 326** (adult suffrage — every citizen `≥ 18` years, not otherwise disqualified), and the **Representation of the People Act, 1950** (roll preparation) read with the **Registration of Electors Rules, 1960**. A *revision* can be **intensive** (fresh enumeration) or **summary** (updating the existing roll); "**Special Intensive Revision**" combines a special drive with intensive enumeration.

**Why it matters (the debate).**
- **In favour:** removes bogus/duplicate voters, migrated/deceased entries; improves roll accuracy and prevents impersonation.
- **Concerns raised:** risk of **wrongful deletion / disenfranchisement**, heavy **documentary burden** on genuine voters (proof of eligibility/citizenship), and **timing** when carried out close to elections. Adequate notice, claims-and-objections windows, and appeal mechanisms are the safeguards.

**Process (typical):** draft roll publication → **claims and objections** period → disposal of claims → **final roll** publication. Booth-Level Officers (**BLOs**) verify entries; Electoral Registration Officers (**EROs**) decide inclusions/deletions; appeals lie to the DEO/CEO.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Voter eligibility (Art 326): a **citizen of India**, **`≥ 18` years** on the qualifying date, **ordinarily resident** in the constituency, and **not disqualified** (unsound mind, certain offences, etc.).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **qualifying date** for age eligibility and the exact SIR schedule/rounds are notified by the ECI and change per cycle — **verify the latest official ECI notification and figures** before the exam; do not rely on old dates.

💡 **Example:** During an intensive revision, a voter who has moved must file a fresh **Form 6** (new inclusion) / **Form 8** (correction/shift); failure to respond within the claims window can lead to deletion — hence the disenfranchisement concern.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- SIR conducted by **ECI**; legal base = **Art 324, 326 + RP Act 1950**.
- Intensive = fresh enumeration; summary = update existing roll.
- Key officials: **BLO → ERO → DEO/CEO** (appeals).
- Balance: roll purity **vs** protection against wrongful deletion.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which Article guarantees universal adult franchise?* → **Article 326** (`18` years, from the 61st Amendment, 1988, which lowered it from 21).

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Operation Polo & Hyderabad State (1948-56)

### Deep Dive

At Independence (`15 Aug 1947`), **Hyderabad State** under **Nizam Mir Osman Ali Khan** was the largest princely state and refused to accede to either India or Pakistan, signing a one-year **Standstill Agreement** with India (Nov 1947) while seeking independence.

**Build-up.** The Nizam's government leaned on the **Razakars**, a paramilitary militia led by **Qasim Razvi** of the Ittehad-ul-Muslimeen, whose violence against civilians and the **Telangana peasant armed struggle** (Communist-led, 1946-51) destabilised the state. Negotiations failed.

**Operation Polo — "Police Action."** The Indian Army moved in on `13 September 1948` under **Major General J.N. Chaudhuri**; the operation lasted about **five days**, and the Nizam's forces surrendered on `17 September 1948` (Hyderabad Liberation / Merger Day). Hyderabad was integrated into the Indian Union.

**Aftermath & violence.** The **Sunderlal Committee** was appointed to inquire into communal violence during and after the action; its report documented large-scale casualties (**verify the exact figures — estimates vary widely**).

**Administration 1948-56.**
- **Military governor:** Maj. Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri (1948-49).
- **Civil administration:** **M.K. Vellodi** (a senior civil servant) became Chief Minister (1949-52); the Nizam was designated **Rajpramukh**.
- **First elected government (1952):** **Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** became the first popularly elected Chief Minister of Hyderabad State.

**Merger into Andhra Pradesh (1956).** Following the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** (on the SRC/Fazl Ali Commission recommendations), the Telugu-speaking **Telangana** region of Hyderabad State merged with **Andhra State** to form **Andhra Pradesh** on `1 November 1956`, preceded by the **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** meant to safeguard Telangana's interests.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Operation Polo: `13–17 Sept 1948`; commander **Maj. Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri**; Nizam **Mir Osman Ali Khan**; Razakar leader **Qasim Razvi**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Do **not** confuse the dates: **Police Action** = Sept 1948; **formation of Andhra Pradesh** = `1 Nov 1956`. The Nizam became **Rajpramukh** (not Governor) of Hyderabad State.

💡 **Example:** The **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** — safeguards on employment, education and a regional council — is a recurring PYQ link between this period and the later statehood movement (violations of these safeguards fuelled the demand).

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Standstill Agreement (Nov 1947); Operation Polo (13-17 Sept 1948).
- Sunderlal Committee → violence inquiry.
- Vellodi (appointed CM) → Burgula Ramakrishna Rao (first elected CM, 1952).
- SRA 1956 → Andhra Pradesh on 1 Nov 1956; Gentlemen's Agreement.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Who led the Indian forces in Operation Polo?* → **Major General J.N. Chaudhuri**, later India's Army Chief.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Government of India Act, 1935 & Provincial Ministries

### Deep Dive

The **Government of India Act, 1935** was the **longest** piece of legislation enacted by the British Parliament till then, drawing on the **Simon Commission report**, the **Round Table Conferences (1930-32)**, the **Nehru Report**, and the Joint Select Committee. It became the main constitutional blueprint India inherited in 1947.

**Key provisions:**
1. **All-India Federation** — proposed to unite British provinces and princely states. It **never came into being** because the required number of princely states did not accede.
2. **Provincial Autonomy** — **abolished dyarchy in the provinces** (introduced by the 1919 Act) and made provinces **autonomous units**; provincial ministers became responsible to elected legislatures.
3. **Dyarchy at the Centre** — the discredited dyarchy was **shifted to the federal (central) level** (federal subjects split into reserved and transferred) — but this part never operated as the federation failed.
4. **Three lists** — Federal, Provincial, and Concurrent (a template for the present 7th Schedule).
5. **Governor's special responsibilities & discretionary powers** — significant overriding powers retained.
6. **Extended franchise** — enlarged the electorate to roughly **`10%`–`14%` of the population** (*verify the exact figure*), still limited by property/education qualifications.
7. Provided for a **Federal Court** (established `1937`), the **Reserve Bank of India**, a Federal Public Service Commission, and reorganisation (e.g., separation of Burma, creation of Sind and Orissa).

**Provincial elections & ministries (1937).** Under the Act, elections were held in early **1937**; the **Indian National Congress** emerged as the largest party, winning majorities in most provinces and forming **ministries in 8 of the 11 provinces** (later joined by more). These **Congress ministries (1937-39)** resigned in **1939** to protest India's inclusion in **World War II** without consultation.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — GoI Act 1935: **abolished provincial dyarchy → provincial autonomy**; introduced **dyarchy at the Centre** (never implemented); **Federal Court est. 1937**; Congress formed ministries in **8 provinces** after the 1937 elections.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **All-India Federation never materialised**; **provincial autonomy did** work. Dyarchy was **removed from provinces** and (on paper) **moved to the Centre** — the reverse of the 1919 arrangement.

💡 **Example:** Congress ministries resigning in **1939** over WWII set the stage for the **"Day of Deliverance" (Dec 1939)** by the Muslim League and later the **August Offer (1940)** — a common PYQ chain.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Basis: Simon Report + Round Tables + Nehru Report + JSC.
- Provincial autonomy (dyarchy abolished in provinces); dyarchy shifted to Centre (never operated).
- Three lists (Federal/Provincial/Concurrent); Federal Court 1937.
- 1937 elections → Congress ministries in 8/11 provinces → resigned 1939 (WWII).

✅ **Quick self-check:** *What replaced provincial dyarchy under the 1935 Act?* → **Provincial autonomy** with responsible ministries.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Climate, Rainfall Patterns & Droughts

### Deep Dive

Telangana has a **semi-arid to sub-humid tropical climate** (mainly Köppen **Aw** tropical-savanna and **BSh** semi-arid). The year has three broad seasons: a **hot summer** (March–May), the **southwest monsoon** (June–September), and a **cool, dry winter** (November–February), with a brief post-monsoon (October).

**Temperature.** Summers are severe — May is the hottest month, with maxima often **`42–45 °C`** in northern/eastern plains (e.g., Ramagundam, Nalgonda belt; *verify latest records*). Winters are mild (minima ~`13–16 °C`).

**Rainfall.** The **southwest (SW) monsoon** delivers the bulk — roughly **`80%`** of annual rainfall — with the **northeast (NE) monsoon** and pre-monsoon thunderstorms adding the rest. The state's **average annual rainfall is ~`900–950 mm`** (*verify the latest official normal*), but it is **spatially uneven**:
- **Higher rainfall** in the north and east — **Adilabad, Khammam, Bhadradri** (~`1000–1200 mm`) under stronger monsoon influence.
- **Lower rainfall** in the **southwest — Mahabubnagar (Palamuru), parts of Rangareddy and Nalgonda** (~`500–650 mm`), a relative **rain-shadow / dry belt**.

**Droughts.** The **southern and southwestern districts** are the most **drought-prone**, owing to low and erratic rainfall, high evapotranspiration, and dependence on the monsoon. Recurrent droughts historically drove **migration (Palamuru labour)** and underlie the state's emphasis on **lift-irrigation** (Kaleshwaram, Palamuru-Rangareddy) to drought-proof agriculture.

![Telangana regions and physiographic base map — used here to read the north-east (wetter) to south-west (drier) rainfall gradient](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — SW monsoon (June–Sept) gives ~`80%` of rainfall; state normal ~`900–950 mm` (verify); wettest **NE (Adilabad/Khammam)**, driest **SW (Mahabubnagar)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Rainfall figures and district "normals" are revised by IMD/state agencies — **write "verify latest official figure"** rather than quoting a stale exact number. The rainfall gradient runs **NE (high) → SW (low)**.

💡 **Example:** Mahabubnagar's chronic rain deficit and out-migration are the classic PYQ illustration of Telangana's drought geography — the rationale for the **Palamuru-Rangareddy lift scheme**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Climate: tropical semi-arid (Köppen Aw / BSh); 3 seasons.
- SW monsoon ≈ 80% of rain; NE monsoon minor.
- Wettest NE (Adilabad, Khammam); driest SW (Mahabubnagar).
- Drought-prone SW belt → migration → lift-irrigation response.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which monsoon gives Telangana most of its rain?* → the **southwest monsoon (June–September)**.
