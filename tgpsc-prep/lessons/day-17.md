## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 Emergency Provisions (Art 352, 356, 360)

The **emergency provisions** (`Part XVIII, Art 352–360`) let the Centre assume sweeping powers in a crisis — converting the federal structure to a **unitary** one. Dr Ambedkar called them a **safety valve**.

### 📖 Concept

**National Emergency (`Art 352`).**
- Grounds: **war, external aggression, or armed rebellion** (the term "internal disturbance" was replaced by "**armed rebellion**" by the **44th Amendment, 1978**).
- Proclamation needs **written recommendation of the Cabinet**; approved by **both Houses within 1 month** (special majority); once approved, lasts **6 months**, extendable indefinitely (6 months at a time).
- Effects: Centre can legislate on State subjects; **Fundamental Rights under Art 19 suspended** (only if emergency is due to war/external aggression); **Art 20 & 21 can NEVER be suspended** (44th Amendment). Lok Sabha term extendable.
- Declared **thrice**: **1962 (China), 1971 (Pakistan), 1975 (internal — the controversial one)**.

**President's Rule / State Emergency (`Art 356`).**
- Grounds: failure of **constitutional machinery** in a state (Governor's report or otherwise).
- Approved by both Houses within **2 months**; lasts **6 months**, extendable up to a **maximum of 3 years** (with conditions after 1 year).
- Effects: state executive taken over by the Centre; **state legislature dissolved or suspended**; Parliament legislates for the state. **`S. R. Bommai (1994)`** made it subject to **judicial review** and curbed its misuse.

**Financial Emergency (`Art 360`).**
- Grounds: threat to the **financial stability/credit** of India.
- Approved within **2 months**; remains until revoked (no maximum).
- Effects: Centre can direct states on financial matters, reduce salaries (incl. judges).
- **Never been declared.**

| Type | Article | Ground | Max duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| National | 352 | war/aggression/armed rebellion | indefinite (6-mo renewals) |
| President's Rule | 356 | failure of constitutional machinery | up to 3 years |
| Financial | 360 | financial instability | until revoked (never used) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **National Emergency (Art 352)**: war/aggression/**armed rebellion**; **Art 20 & 21 can never be suspended** (44th Amdt). **President's Rule (Art 356)**: max **3 years**, curbed by **S. R. Bommai (1994)**. **Financial Emergency (Art 360)**: never declared.

💡 **Example:** After the misuse of Art 356 to dismiss opposition state governments, the **S. R. Bommai (1994)** judgment made President's Rule **justiciable** — the floor test in the Assembly, not the Governor's subjective satisfaction, became the test of majority.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Art 352** thrice: **1962, 1971, 1975**; **44th Amdt** changed "internal disturbance" → "armed rebellion" and protected **Art 20/21**.
- **Art 356 max = 3 years**; **Bommai (1994)** = judicial review + floor test.
- **Art 360 never used**.
- National emergency approval: **1 month**; 356/360: **2 months**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**352 = National (war/rebellion, 20&21 safe); 356 = President's Rule (max 3 yrs, Bommai); 360 = Financial (never used).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Art 20 and 21 can NEVER be suspended**, even during a National Emergency (44th Amendment). **Art 356 (President's Rule)** has a **3-year max**; **Art 360 (Financial)** has **never** been invoked — don't confuse the three.

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 1970–2000: TPS, the Naxalism Phase & Identity Politics

After the 1969 agitation, the decades **1970–2000** saw the ebb of the first statehood movement, the rise of **Naxalism** in Telangana, and shifting **identity/regional politics** that kept the Telangana question alive.

### 📖 Concept

**Aftermath of 1969 & the TPS.**
- The **Telangana Praja Samithi (TPS)** under **Marri Chenna Reddy** won big in the **1971 Lok Sabha** elections (10/14 Telangana seats) on the separate-Telangana plank — but **merged with the Congress (1971-72)**, and Chenna Reddy later became **CM of united AP**, disappointing separatists.
- The **1972 Jai Andhra** movement (Andhra region) and the resulting **Six-Point Formula (1973)** and **Article 371D (1974)** sought to settle regional grievances (local cadres, Presidential order on jobs/education, Central University, etc.).

**The Naxalite/Maoist phase.**
- Rooted in the **Naxalbari (1967)** movement (West Bengal), radical-left armed struggle spread to Telangana, especially **North Telangana (Karimnagar, Adilabad, Warangal, Khammam)**.
- The **People's War Group (PWG)** was formed in **1980** (led by **Kondapalli Seetharamaiah**) — active on land, forest and tribal issues (**"land to the tiller"**, anti-feudal). Later merged into the **CPI (Maoist), 2004**.
- Prompted heavy state security response; deeply shaped Telangana's rural politics and culture (revolutionary poetry/song — **Gaddar, Jana Natya Mandali**).

**Identity & regional politics (1980s-90s).**
- The **TDP (N. T. Rama Rao, 1982)** rose on **Telugu pride ("Telugu self-respect")** — a pan-Telugu identity that partly submerged the Telangana question.
- Yet **regional disparities** (irrigation, jobs, Krishna/Godavari water, education) kept **Telangana sub-regional consciousness** simmering — cultural assertion, "**Telangana Jana Sabha**," and civil-society mobilisation.
- Sets the stage for the **2001 formation of the TRS (K. Chandrashekar Rao)** and the renewed statehood movement (next topic).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **1970–2000**: the **TPS merged into Congress (1971-72)**; **Six-Point Formula (1973) & Art 371D (1974)** addressed grievances; **Naxalism (PWG, 1980, Kondapalli Seetharamaiah)** gripped North Telangana; **TDP/Telugu identity (1982)** temporarily overshadowed — but Telangana consciousness persisted, birthing the **TRS (2001)**.

💡 **Example:** The **PWG (1980)** channelled tribal/peasant grievances over **land and forest** into an armed movement in North Telangana — the same "Jal-Jangal-Jameen" thread from Komaram Bheem's era, now in a Maoist form.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **TPS → Congress merger (1971-72)**; Chenna Reddy CM of united AP.
- **Six-Point Formula (1973), Art 371D (1974)**.
- **PWG formed 1980, Kondapalli Seetharamaiah**; North Telangana.
- **TDP/NTR 1982** (Telugu identity); **TRS formed 2001 (KCR)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**TPS fizzles (merges 1972) → Six-Point/371D → PWG Naxalism (1980) → TDP Telugu pride (1982) → TRS revives Telangana (2001).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **TPS merged with the Congress (1971-72)** rather than achieving statehood — a key disappointment. **PWG = 1980 (Kondapalli Seetharamaiah)**, distinct from the original **Naxalbari (1967)**. The **TRS (2001, KCR)** belongs to the *next* phase, not this one's core.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement
### 🎯 Integration of the Princely States & the Making of the Republic

At Independence, **~562 princely states** had to be integrated. **Sardar Patel** and **V. P. Menon** achieved this "**bloodless revolution**," and the **Constitution (1950)** completed the making of the Republic.

### 📖 Concept

**The princely states problem.** The **Indian Independence Act 1947** ended British **paramountcy**, leaving states technically free to join India, Pakistan, or stay independent. **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (Home Minister, "**Iron Man**") with **V. P. Menon** (Secretary, States Department) persuaded almost all to accede via the **Instrument of Accession** (ceding **defence, foreign affairs, communications**).

**The hard cases:**
- **Junagadh** — Nawab acceded to Pakistan; a **plebiscite (1948)** brought it into India.
- **Hyderabad** — the Nizam sought independence; integrated by **Operation Polo (Sept 1948)** (Day 14).
- **Kashmir** — Maharaja **Hari Singh** signed the **Instrument of Accession (Oct 1947)** after a Pakistani tribal invasion; led to the **1947-48 war** and **Article 370** (special status; abrogated **2019**).

**Consolidation.** The states were reorganised — merged into unions/provinces, then into **Part A/B/C states**, later reorganised **linguistically by the SRC/States Reorganisation Act 1956**. The **privy purses** (payments to ex-rulers) were **abolished by the 26th Amendment (1971)**.

**Making of the Republic.**
- The **Constituent Assembly** (formed under the **Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946**; first met **9 Dec 1946**) framed the Constitution; **Dr B. R. Ambedkar** chaired the **Drafting Committee**.
- The Constitution was **adopted on 26 November 1949** and **came into force on 26 January 1950** (chosen to honour the 1930 **Purna Swaraj Day**) — India became a **sovereign democratic republic**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Sardar Patel & V. P. Menon** integrated **~562 princely states** via the **Instrument of Accession** (defence, foreign affairs, communications). Hard cases: **Junagadh (plebiscite), Hyderabad (Operation Polo 1948), Kashmir (accession Oct 1947, Art 370)**. The Constitution was **adopted 26 Nov 1949, enforced 26 Jan 1950**.

💡 **Example:** Most states signed the **Instrument of Accession** peacefully, but **Junagadh, Hyderabad and Kashmir** each needed a different solution — **plebiscite, military action, and accession-after-invasion** respectively. That trio is the exam's favourite comparison.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Patel + V. P. Menon**; **Instrument of Accession** (3 subjects).
- **Junagadh (plebiscite), Hyderabad (Operation Polo), Kashmir (Art 370)**.
- **Privy purses abolished — 26th Amendment (1971)**.
- **Constitution: adopted 26 Nov 1949, in force 26 Jan 1950**; Ambedkar = Drafting Committee chair.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Patel + Menon = 562 states via Instrument of Accession. Junagadh (vote), Hyderabad (army), Kashmir (Art 370). Republic: 26 Nov 1949 adopted → 26 Jan 1950 enforced.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Constitution was **adopted on 26 Nov 1949** but **came into force on 26 Jan 1950** — two different dates. **Privy purses** were abolished by the **26th Amendment (1971)**, not at integration.

*(Sardar Patel & V. P. Menon, via the Instrument of Accession; adopted 26 Nov 1949, enforced 26 Jan 1950.)*

## 🗺️ Subject 5: Telangana Geography (with Map)
### 🎯 Area Focus: Nalgonda–Suryapet

**Nalgonda** — south-central Telangana on the **Krishna** — is Nagarjuna Sagar, fluoride-affected groundwater, uranium in the Nallamala, and a rich Buddhist-to-Kakatiya heritage.

**🗺️ Reference map — Telangana regions (Nalgonda-Suryapet in the south):**

![Telangana regions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

*(If the map doesn't display in email, open the lesson on GitHub — the SVG is at `tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg`.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Location & administration.** The undivided Nalgonda district (south-central, bordering **Andhra Pradesh** across the Krishna) was reorganised (2016) into **Nalgonda, Suryapet, Yadadri Bhongir** (`verify`). 

**Physiography & geology.**
- **Archaean granite–gneiss** terrain; **red and black soils**; part of the **Nallamala hills** (with **Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve**) in the southeast.
- The **Krishna** flows along the south — **Nagarjuna Sagar** (one of the world's largest masonry dams) is on the Krishna at the Nalgonda–Guntur border; **Musi** (a Krishna tributary) also drains the district.
- **Fluoride contamination** of groundwater is a serious **health issue** (dental/skeletal fluorosis) — Nalgonda is a classic case; addressed by **Mission Bhagiratha** (piped surface water).
- **Uranium** deposits in the **Nallamala/Lambapur-Peddagattu** area — an environmental concern.

**Agriculture & economy.** **Paddy** (Nagarjuna Sagar canals), **cotton, red gram, chillies**; **granite** and **cement** (limestone) industries; **Suryapet** and **Miryalaguda** are major towns/markets.

**Heritage & sites (cultural reference):**
- **Nagarjunakonda** (submerged by Nagarjuna Sagar; relics relocated to an island museum) — a major **Buddhist (Ikshvaku)** site (linked to Day 3 history).
- **Yadadri (Yadagirigutta)** — the **Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy Temple**, recently grandly reconstructed (Yadadri Bhongir district).
- **Bhongir (Bhuvanagiri) Fort** — a dramatic monolithic-rock hill fort (Western Chalukya/Kakatiya).
- **Kolanupaka (Kolanpak)** — ancient **Jain temple** (Someswara/Jain heritage) and Veerashaiva centre.
- **Pillalamarri (Nalgonda)** — Kakatiya-era temples (distinct from the Mahabubnagar banyan).
- **Panagal** (near Nalgonda) — **Chhaya Someswara & Pachala Someswara** temples (Kakatiya/Kunduru Cholas).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Nalgonda-Suryapet = **Krishna/Nagarjuna Sagar** irrigation on **granite** terrain, notorious for **fluoride** groundwater (fluorosis) and **Nallamala uranium**; heritage spans **Nagarjunakonda (Buddhist/Ikshvaku)**, **Yadadri temple**, **Bhongir Fort** and **Kolanupaka Jain temple**.

💡 **Example:** "Geology + culture of Nalgonda" → **granite terrain + fluoride groundwater + Nagarjuna Sagar (Krishna)** (geology/health) with **Nagarjunakonda Buddhist site + Yadadri + Bhongir Fort** (culture) — and **Mission Bhagiratha** as the fluoride remedy.

- **Uranium — Nallamala/Lambapur** (environmental issue).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Nalgonda = Nagarjuna Sagar + fluoride (Bhagiratha fix) + Nallamala uranium; Nagarjunakonda (Buddhist), Yadadri & Bhongir Fort for culture.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Nagarjunakonda** is a **Buddhist (Ikshvaku)** site submerged by **Nagarjuna Sagar** (relics on an island museum). Nalgonda's signature problem is **fluoride** (not arsenic). **Yadadri** and **Bhongir** are in the carved-out **Yadadri Bhongir** district. Verify the reorganised list.
