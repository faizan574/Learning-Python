## 🏛️ Polity: Fundamental Rights — Articles 12, 13 & 14

**Fundamental Rights (FRs)** are in **Part III (`Art 12–35`)**. Articles 12–14 lay the foundation: who is bound, what makes a law void, and the master right to equality.

**`Art 12` — Definition of "State."** FRs are mainly enforceable against the **"State,"** which `Art 12` defines broadly to include: the **Government and Parliament of India**, the **Government and Legislature of each state**, **all local authorities** (municipalities, panchayats), and **other authorities** (statutory/non-statutory bodies under government control). This wide definition decides **against whom** a citizen can claim FRs.

**`Art 13` — Laws inconsistent with FRs.** Any law that violates a FR is **void** to the extent of the inconsistency; `Art 13` is the **basis of judicial review**. "Law" includes ordinances, orders, bye-laws, rules, and even **custom/usage**. Associated doctrines: **eclipse** (a pre-Constitution law conflicting with FRs is dormant, not dead), **severability** (only the invalid part is struck), and **waiver** (FRs **cannot** be waived).

**`Art 14` — Equality before law & equal protection of the laws.** Guarantees:
- **Equality before law** (British/Dicey's **rule of law** — no one is above the law);
- **Equal protection of the laws** (American concept — like should be treated alike).
It permits **reasonable classification** (based on an **intelligible differentia** with a **rational nexus** to the object) but **prohibits arbitrariness** (*E.P. Royappa*, *Maneka Gandhi*). Exceptions: `Art 361` (immunity of the President/Governors).

| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| `Art 12` | Definition of "State" |
| `Art 13` | Laws void if they violate FRs (judicial review) |
| `Art 14` | Equality before law + equal protection |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — `Art 12` defines **"State"** (govt, legislatures, local & other authorities); `Art 13` makes FR-violating laws **void** (judicial review, incl. custom); `Art 14` = **equality before law (rule of law) + equal protection**, allowing **reasonable classification** but barring **arbitrariness**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**12 = who (State), 13 = void law (review), 14 = equality (classification OK, arbitrariness NO).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — FRs **cannot be waived** (no waiver doctrine). `Art 14`'s "equal protection" allows **reasonable classification** (not "class legislation"). The **right to property was removed** from FRs by the **44th Amendment (1978)** — now only a legal right under `Art 300A`.

💡 **Example:** In *Maneka Gandhi (1978)*, `Art 14`'s bar on **arbitrariness** was linked with Art 19 and 21 — any state action must be **fair, just and reasonable**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Part III = Art 12–35; six categories of FRs now.
- Art 12 "other authorities"; Art 13 judicial review + eclipse/severability.
- Art 14: reasonable classification (differentia + nexus); no arbitrariness.
- Right to property → legal right (Art 300A, 44th Amendment).

## 🏗️ Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Cheyutha Pensions & Rajiv Aarogyasri

Two **social-security** guarantees: pensions for the vulnerable and health cover for the poor. (Treat all amounts as **"verify latest official figure."**)

**Cheyutha (Aasara/social-security pensions).** Provides an **enhanced monthly pension** to vulnerable groups — **old-age, widows, persons with disabilities, beedi workers, single women, weavers, toddy tappers, HIV patients, etc.** The announced amount was **₹4,000 per month** (higher for persons with disabilities — **verify current rates and eligibility age**). It is a direct income-support safety net under the Six Guarantees.

**Rajiv Aarogyasri (health cover).** Telangana's flagship **cashless health-insurance scheme** for eligible (white-card) families, providing free treatment of listed procedures at **empanelled government/private hospitals**. The coverage was announced as **enhanced up to ₹10 lakh per family per year** (**verify current ceiling and procedure list**). It is one of India's pioneering state health-assurance schemes.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Cheyutha = monthly social-security pensions (₹4,000 announced; more for PwD)**; **Rajiv Aarogyasri = cashless health cover (up to ₹10 lakh/family announced)**. Both under the Six Guarantees. **Verify all amounts/eligibility.**

## 🏺 Telangana History: Operation Polo & Hyderabad State (1948–56)

**Operation Polo** — the **Police Action** of **13–17 September 1948** — integrated the Nizam's Hyderabad State into India, and the following years (to 1956) saw it governed as a separate state before merger.

**Operation Polo (Sept 1948).** As the Nizam held out and the **Razakars (Qasim Rizvi)** terrorised the populace, the Government of India launched a military operation (code-named **Polo**). The Indian Army, led by **Major General J.N. Chaudhuri**, entered Hyderabad; the Nizam's forces collapsed within days and the **Nizam surrendered on 17 September 1948**. **Qasim Rizvi was arrested**; the Razakars were disbanded. (The **Sunderlal Committee** later inquired into the communal violence of the period.) **17 September** is now observed officially (as Telangana Liberation/Integration Day — naming has been politically debated).

**Hyderabad State (1948–56).** After integration, Hyderabad was administered first under a **Military Governor (Maj. Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri, 1948–49)**, then under a civilian **Chief Minister/Chief Civil Administrator M.K. Vellodi (1950–52)**. The **first general elections (1952)** brought **Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** as the **first elected Chief Minister** of Hyderabad State (1952–56).

**Towards merger.** The **States Reorganisation Commission (SRC, Fazl Ali Commission)** recommended reorganisation on linguistic lines; despite reservations, the **Telangana region of Hyderabad State was merged with Andhra** to form **Andhra Pradesh in 1956**, with safeguards in the **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** — whose later violations fuelled the statehood movement.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Operation Polo (13–17 Sept 1948)** under **Maj. Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri**; Nizam surrendered **17 Sept 1948**; Qasim Rizvi arrested. Hyderabad State 1948–56: Chaudhuri (military) → Vellodi (civil) → **Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** (first elected CM, 1952); merged into **AP in 1956** (Gentlemen's Agreement).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1948 Polo (Chaudhuri, 17 Sept surrender) → Vellodi → Burgula (first elected CM) → 1956 merger (Gentlemen's Agreement).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Operation Polo = 1948 Police Action** (not 1947). **J.N. Chaudhuri** led it and became military governor; **Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** was the first **elected** CM (Vellodi was appointed, not elected).

💡 **Example:** The **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** safeguards for Telangana (jobs, education, revenue) — and their later breach — became the historical grievance behind the 1969 and post-2000 statehood agitations.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Operation Polo 13–17 Sept 1948; J.N. Chaudhuri; Nizam surrendered 17 Sept.
- Razakars/Qasim Rizvi; Sunderlal Committee.
- Vellodi (civil administrator) → Burgula (first elected CM 1952).
- SRC/Fazl Ali → AP 1956; Gentlemen's Agreement.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Partition of Bengal, Swadeshi & the Extremists; Surat Split

The **1905–1908** years marked the shift from Moderate petitioning to mass agitation and the rise of the **Extremists**.

**Partition of Bengal (1905).** Viceroy **Lord Curzon** partitioned Bengal (effective **16 October 1905**), ostensibly for administrative convenience but widely seen as **"divide and rule"** — splitting Hindu-majority west from Muslim-majority east Bengal. It provoked outrage and the **Swadeshi Movement**.

**Swadeshi & Boycott Movement.** The response combined **Swadeshi** (use of Indian goods), **boycott** of British goods, **national education** (Bengal National College), and **passive resistance**. **Rakhi-bandhan** (Tagore's idea) symbolised unity. This was the first truly mass-based agitation.

**The Extremists (Lal–Bal–Pal).** A militant trio led the new assertive nationalism: **Lala Lajpat Rai** (Punjab), **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** ("**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**"), and **Bipin Chandra Pal** (Bengal), with **Aurobindo Ghosh**. They rejected "political mendicancy" and demanded **Swaraj**, using boycott, swadeshi and self-reliance.

**Surat Split (1907).** At the **Surat session (1907)**, the Congress **split into Moderates and Extremists** over the pace and methods of agitation (and the presidency); the party was weakened for years until reunited at **Lucknow (1916)**.

**Annulment (1911).** The partition of Bengal was **revoked in 1911** (Delhi Durbar), and the **capital was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Partition of Bengal by Curzon (1905) → Swadeshi/Boycott**; **Extremists = Lal-Bal-Pal** (Tilak: "Swaraj is my birthright"); **Surat Split 1907** (Moderates vs Extremists); **partition annulled 1911**, capital moved **Calcutta → Delhi**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1905 Curzon partition → Swadeshi → Lal-Bal-Pal → 1907 Surat split → 1911 annulled (capital to Delhi).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The partition was by **Curzon (1905)** and **annulled in 1911** (not by the same event). The **Surat Split was 1907** (reunion at Lucknow 1916). "Swaraj is my birthright" = **Tilak**.

💡 **Example:** The **Swadeshi Movement's** boycott of British textiles gave a direct economic weapon to nationalism — a template later scaled up by Gandhi's Non-Cooperation.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Partition of Bengal: Curzon, effective 16 Oct 1905; annulled 1911.
- Swadeshi, boycott, national education, passive resistance.
- Lal-Bal-Pal + Aurobindo; Tilak's "Swaraj is my birthright."
- Surat Split 1907; capital shifted to Delhi (1911).
