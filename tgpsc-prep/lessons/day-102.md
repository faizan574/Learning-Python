## 🏛️ Polity: Local Government — 73rd & 74th Amendments, Telangana Panchayat Raj Act

Local self-government was constitutionalised by the **73rd Amendment (1992, for rural Panchayati Raj)** and the **74th Amendment (1992, for urban Municipalities)**, both effective **1993**. They added **Part IX** (`Art 243–243O`, Panchayats) with the **Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)**, and **Part IXA** (`Art 243P–243ZG`, Municipalities) with the **Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects)**.

**73rd Amendment — key features.** A **three-tier structure** (village, intermediate, district) — the intermediate tier is optional for states with population **under 20 lakh**. **Gram Sabha** (`Art 243A`) is the foundation. Mandatory provisions: **five-year term**; elections within **six months** if dissolved; **reservations** for SCs/STs (proportional to population) and **not less than one-third for women** (Telangana later raised this to **50%**); a **State Election Commission** (`Art 243K`) to conduct elections; and a **State Finance Commission** (`Art 243I`) every five years to review panchayat finances. Reservation for **Backward Classes** is **optional** (left to states).

**74th Amendment — key features.** Three types of urban bodies: **Nagar Panchayat** (transitional area), **Municipal Council** (smaller urban area), and **Municipal Corporation** (larger urban area). Provides for **Ward Committees** (`Art 243S`), **District Planning Committees** (`Art 243ZD`) and **Metropolitan Planning Committees** (`Art 243ZE`) for integrated planning.

**Telangana Panchayat Raj Act, 2018.** Replaced the earlier 1994 Act. It strengthened the three-tier system, fixed accountability of Sarpanches and Panchayat Secretaries, provided for a dedicated cadre of Panchayat Secretaries, emphasised **greenery/Haritha Haram** and sanitation duties, and tightened disqualification norms (e.g., functionaries with more than two children in some provisions — *verify current provisions*). Telangana provides **50% reservation for women** in local bodies.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **73rd (Part IX, `Art 243–243O`, Eleventh Schedule — 29 subjects)** = rural; **74th (Part IXA, `Art 243P–243ZG`, Twelfth Schedule — 18 subjects)** = urban; both **1992, effective 1993**. **5-year term**, elections in **6 months** if dissolved, **State Election Commission** (`Art 243K`), **State Finance Commission** (`Art 243I`), **⅓ women** (Telangana 50%). Telangana PR Act **2018**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**73 = rural (11th Schedule, 29); 74 = urban (12th Schedule, 18); SEC conducts, SFC funds; women ⅓ (TG 50%).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Eleventh Schedule = **29** subjects (rural); Twelfth = **18** (urban) — don't swap. **State Election Commission** (`Art 243K`) ≠ the central Election Commission (`Art 324`). **SC/ST reservation is mandatory**; **BC reservation is optional** (state's discretion). Intermediate tier optional only below **20 lakh** population.

💡 **Example:** If a Gram Panchayat is dissolved, fresh elections must be held within **six months** — the new body serves only the **remainder** of the original five-year term (not a fresh five years).

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- 73rd → Part IX, Art 243–243O, 11th Schedule (29); 74th → Part IXA, Art 243P–243ZG, 12th Schedule (18).
- Gram Sabha Art 243A; SEC Art 243K; SFC Art 243I (every 5 years).
- Women reservation ⅓ (constitutional minimum); Telangana 50%.
- Three urban bodies: Nagar Panchayat / Municipal Council / Municipal Corporation.
- Telangana Panchayat Raj Act, 2018 (replaced 1994 Act).

## 🏗️ Schemes: Gruha Jyothi & Indiramma Indlu

Two of the government's welfare guarantees address **electricity** and **housing** for the poor.

**Gruha Jyothi (free electricity).** Provides **up to 200 units of free electricity per month** to eligible domestic households. Households consuming within the 200-unit cap pay **zero** on their monthly bill; consumption beyond the limit is billed as per slab (the exact billing treatment for crossing 200 units — *verify latest official guidelines*). Eligibility is tied to being a domestic consumer with ration-card/white-card linkage and residency conditions (*verify current eligibility*). The scheme aims to cut the energy burden on low-income families and is delivered via the DISCOMs with DBT-style subsidy reconciliation.

**Indiramma Indlu (housing).** A housing scheme providing financial assistance to construct a **pucca house** for eligible poor/houseless families, reviving the "Indiramma" housing brand. The announced unit assistance was around **₹5 lakh per house** (*verify latest official figure and whether it varies by category/location*), released in **instalments linked to construction stages** (basement → lintel → roof → completion). Beneficiaries are identified through surveys; priority to the houseless, SC/ST, and those living in dilapidated dwellings. Those who already own land get assistance to build; a component addresses the landless (*verify current design*).

Both schemes sit within the **Six Guarantees** framework (Gruha Jyothi under the electricity guarantee; Indiramma Indlu under housing). They are Aadhaar- and ration-linked for targeting, with grievance redress through **Praja Palana** applications.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Gruha Jyothi** = up to **200 free units/month** of electricity to eligible domestic households. **Indiramma Indlu** = housing assistance (~**₹5 lakh/house**, *verify*) for a pucca house, released in **stage-wise instalments**. Both under the **Six Guarantees**; ration/Aadhaar-linked targeting.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Gruha Jyothi = 200 free units (light); Indiramma Indlu = pucca house (roof).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Gruha Jyothi's free-power cap is **200 units/month** (not 100 or 300). Indiramma **Indlu** = **housing**; don't confuse with **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** (landless-labour income support) or **Indiramma Aarogyasri** etc. All rupee figures and the exact unit-crossing billing rule are recent — **verify latest official data**.

💡 **Example:** A household using 180 units in a month pays nothing under Gruha Jyothi; a family rebuilding a collapsed mud house receives Indiramma Indlu assistance in instalments as each construction stage is verified.

## 🏺 History: Pre-Satavahana Telangana — Megaliths, Kotilingala & Asmaka

Before the Satavahanas rose to power, Telangana passed through a **prehistoric-to-early-historic transition** marked by **megalithic (Iron Age) cultures**, early urban mints like **Kotilingala**, and the **Asmaka (Assaka) mahajanapada**.

**Megalithic culture.** From roughly the **first millennium BCE** (Iron Age, *verify precise local chronology*), communities across Telangana built **megaliths** — large stone burial/memorial monuments. Types include **cairn circles, cist burials, dolmens, menhirs,** and **sarcophagi**. Grave goods (iron tools/weapons, black-and-red ware pottery) reveal **iron metallurgy, settled agriculture, and ancestor-reverence**. Important megalithic sites in/around Telangana include **Maski, Ramachandrapuram, Dhulikatta environs**, and numerous spots in the Karimnagar–Nalgonda belts (*verify site attributions*). These cultures form the bridge to the early historic period.

**Kotilingala.** On the banks of the **Godavari** (in present **Jagtial/Karimnagar** region), **Kotilingala** was an **early fortified town and mint**. Coins of early rulers — including **Simuka** (the Satavahana founder) and local rulers like **Gobada/Samagopa** (*verify names/readings*) — were found here, making Kotilingala crucial evidence that the **Satavahana power base lay in Telangana**, not only the coastal Andhra region. Excavations revealed fortifications, punch-marked and inscribed coins, and early-historic habitation.

**Asmaka (Assaka) Mahajanapada.** Among the **sixteen mahajanapadas** of the 6th century BCE listed in Buddhist/Jaina texts, **Asmaka** was the **only one located in the south (Deccan)** — generally placed on the **Godavari**, with its capital often identified as **Potana/Potali (Bodhan?)** (*verify identification*). Its mention shows that Telangana/the Deccan was integrated into the north-Indian political-cultural world as early as the mahajanapada age. Paithan (Pratishthana) nearby became a great trade centre.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Megaliths** (Iron Age): cairns, cists, dolmens, menhirs; iron tools + black-and-red ware. **Kotilingala** (on the Godavari, Jagtial/Karimnagar) = early mint with **Simuka's coins** → Satavahana base in Telangana. **Asmaka** = the **only southern mahajanapada** (on the Godavari).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Megaliths → iron + burials; Kotilingala → Simuka's coins (Telangana base); Asmaka → the lone southern mahajanapada.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Asmaka** is the **only Deccan/southern** mahajanapada among the sixteen (the rest are northern). **Kotilingala** is pre/early-Satavahana evidence (coins of Simuka), not a Kakatiya site. Megaliths are **Iron Age** burials, not Palaeolithic. Many site/coin identifications are debated — write "verify" where unsure.

💡 **Example:** The discovery of **Simuka's coins at Kotilingala** is the standard exam point used to argue the **Telangana origin** of the Satavahanas.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Megalith types: cairn circle, cist, dolmen, menhir, sarcophagus; black-and-red ware; iron.
- Kotilingala — Godavari, Jagtial/Karimnagar; early mint; Simuka coins.
- Asmaka — only southern mahajanapada; on the Godavari; capital Potana/Potali (verify).
- Pre-Satavahana → bridges Iron Age to early-historic Deccan.

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Integration of Princely States (incl. Hyderabad) & Making of the Republic

At independence there were **~565 princely states** under British paramountcy. The **Indian Independence Act 1947** lapsed paramountcy, leaving each state free to join India, join Pakistan, or stay independent — a potential balkanisation.

**Integration drive.** **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (Deputy PM & Home Minister), aided by **V.P. Menon** (Secretary, States Department), persuaded almost all states to accede via the **Instrument of Accession** (ceding defence, external affairs, communications) before 15 August 1947. Patel's firmness earned him the title **"Iron Man of India."** Three states resisted:
- **Junagadh** — a Nawab acceded to Pakistan despite a Hindu-majority population; a **plebiscite (1948)** confirmed accession to India.
- **Hyderabad** — see below.
- **Kashmir** — acceded to India (Oct 1947) after a Pakistani tribal invasion, via the Instrument of Accession signed by Maharaja Hari Singh.

**Hyderabad & Operation Polo.** The **Nizam, Mir Osman Ali Khan**, sought independence; the **Razakars** (militia under Qasim Razvi) unleashed violence. After failed negotiations and a **Standstill Agreement**, India launched **"Operation Polo"** (the Police Action) on **13 September 1948**; Hyderabad surrendered on **17 September 1948**, merging into the Indian Union. (This date is observed in Telangana — the terminology and commemoration have evolved; *verify current official observance*.)

**Making of the Republic.** The **Constituent Assembly** (first met **9 December 1946**), chaired by **Dr Rajendra Prasad**, with the **Drafting Committee under Dr B.R. Ambedkar**, adopted the Constitution on **26 November 1949** (**Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas**); it came into force on **26 January 1950**, when India became a **sovereign democratic republic** and Dr Rajendra Prasad became the first President. States were later reorganised linguistically by the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Patel + V.P. Menon** integrated **~565** states (**Instrument of Accession**). Holdouts: **Junagadh** (plebiscite → India), **Hyderabad** (**Operation Polo**, **13–17 Sept 1948**), **Kashmir** (acceded Oct 1947). Constitution **adopted 26 Nov 1949**, **in force 26 Jan 1950**. SRA **1956**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Patel = Iron Man; Instrument of Accession; three holdouts (Junagadh, Hyderabad, Kashmir); Operation Polo 13–17 Sept 1948; Republic 26 Jan 1950.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Constitution **adopted 26 Nov 1949** but **enforced 26 Jan 1950** — don't merge the dates. **Operation Polo = Hyderabad (1948)**, not Kashmir. Accession ceded only **three subjects** (defence, external affairs, communications) initially. **V.P. Menon** (not Krishna Menon) was Patel's key aide.

💡 **Example:** Junagadh and Hyderabad show two different tools — a **plebiscite** settled Junagadh, while **military action (Operation Polo)** settled Hyderabad — both ending in accession to India.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- ~565 states; Patel & V.P. Menon; Instrument of Accession (3 subjects).
- Junagadh (plebiscite 1948); Hyderabad (Operation Polo 13–17 Sept 1948); Kashmir (Oct 1947).
- Constituent Assembly first met 9 Dec 1946; Prasad president, Ambedkar drafting.
- Constitution adopted 26 Nov 1949; in force 26 Jan 1950.
- States Reorganisation Act 1956.

## 🗺️ Geography: Rivers — The Godavari System in Depth

![Map of Telangana's river systems — Godavari and Krishna](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

The **Godavari** is the **largest river of peninsular India** (the "**Dakshina Ganga**" / Vriddha Ganga) and the **lifeline of Telangana**, draining the greater part of the state.

**Source & course.** It rises at **Trimbak (Triambakeshwar), Nashik district, Maharashtra**, in the Western Ghats (~**1,067 m**, *verify*), and flows generally **west-to-east/south-east** ~**1,465 km** to the **Bay of Bengal**, forming a large delta near Rajahmundry (in Andhra Pradesh). It enters Telangana in the north and traverses the northern districts before forming part of the Telangana–Andhra/Chhattisgarh margins.

**Major tributaries (within/relevant to Telangana).**
- **Left bank:** **Pranhita** (formed by **Wardha + Wainganga + Penganga**) — the **largest tributary** by volume; **Indravati**; and the **Sabari** — these join in the north-east.
- **Right bank:** **Manjira** (an important right-bank tributary passing **Nizamabad/Medak**; **Nizam Sagar** and **Singur** dams are on the Manjira); **Maner (Manair)**; **Kinnerasani**.

**Key projects on the Godavari system in Telangana.**
- **Sriram Sagar Project (SRSP / Pochampad)** — a major multipurpose project on the Godavari near **Nizamabad**.
- **Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Scheme** — on the Godavari (near the Pranhita confluence at **Kaleshwaram**, Jayashankar Bhupalpally), one of the world's largest lift-irrigation projects (*verify latest status/figures*).
- **Nizam Sagar** and **Singur** on the Manjira; **Lower Manair Dam** on the Manair; the older **Kaddam** project.
- Pilgrim/cultural centre **Bhadrachalam** lies on the Godavari.

**Significance.** The Godavari basin covers the larger share of Telangana's area; it supports irrigation (SRSP, Kaleshwaram), drinking water, and power. The **Pranhita–Chevella** concept was reworked into Kaleshwaram.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Godavari** = largest peninsular river ("Dakshina Ganga"); source **Trimbak, Nashik (Maharashtra)**; length **~1,465 km**; **Bay of Bengal**. Largest tributary **Pranhita** (Wardha+Wainganga+Penganga). Right-bank **Manjira** (Nizam Sagar, Singur). Projects: **SRSP/Pochampad**, **Kaleshwaram LIS**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Godavari = Dakshina Ganga, from Trimbak to Bay of Bengal; Pranhita biggest tributary; Manjira has Nizam Sagar/Singur; SRSP + Kaleshwaram.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Godavari rises in **Maharashtra (Trimbak)**, NOT in Telangana. **Pranhita** (left bank) is the **largest tributary**; **Manjira** is a **right-bank** tributary. **Kaleshwaram** is on the **Godavari** (Pranhita confluence), not the Krishna. Verify all elevation/length/project figures before the exam.

💡 **Example:** Water lifted at **Kaleshwaram** from the Godavari (near the Pranhita confluence) is pumped across Telangana — a flagship example of **lift irrigation** on the Godavari system.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Source Trimbak (Nashik, Maharashtra); ~1,465 km; Bay of Bengal; "Dakshina Ganga."
- Largest tributary Pranhita (Wardha+Wainganga+Penganga); also Indravati, Sabari (left bank).
- Right-bank Manjira → Nizam Sagar & Singur dams; Maner → Lower Manair.
- Projects: SRSP/Pochampad, Kaleshwaram LIS, Kaddam; Bhadrachalam pilgrimage.
