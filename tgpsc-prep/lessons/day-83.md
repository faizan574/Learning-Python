## 🏛️ Polity: Non-Constitutional Bodies — NITI Aayog, NHRC, CIC & Lokpal

**Non-constitutional bodies** are not created by the Constitution itself. They are either **executive** (formed by a government resolution) or **statutory** (created by an Act of Parliament). Knowing which is which — and the parent Act — is the exam's favourite distinction.

**NITI Aayog (2015) — executive body.** The **National Institution for Transforming India** replaced the **Planning Commission** by a **Cabinet resolution** (so it is **neither constitutional nor statutory**). It is a **think-tank/advisory** body promoting **cooperative federalism**; the **Prime Minister is Chairperson**, with a Vice-Chairperson, a CEO, and a **Governing Council** of all Chief Ministers and Lt. Governors. Unlike the Planning Commission, it has **no power to allocate funds** to states.

**NHRC (1993) — statutory.** The **National Human Rights Commission** was set up under the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**. Its chairperson is a **retired Chief Justice of India or a Supreme Court judge** (widened by the **2019 amendment**). Its powers are largely **recommendatory** — it inquires into violations and recommends action, but cannot itself punish.

**CIC (2005) — statutory.** The **Central Information Commission** was created under the **Right to Information Act, 2005**. The Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners are appointed by the **President** on the recommendation of a committee (PM, Leader of Opposition, a Union Minister). It hears RTI appeals/complaints at the central level (State Information Commissions at the state level).

**Lokpal (2013) — statutory.** The **anti-corruption ombudsman** at the Union level under the **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013**; India's **first Lokpal (Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose) was appointed in 2019**. States have **Lokayuktas**. The Lokpal can inquire into corruption allegations against public functionaries, including (with safeguards) the Prime Minister.

| Body | Year | Type | Parent authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| NITI Aayog | 2015 | **executive** | Cabinet resolution |
| NHRC | 1993 | statutory | Protection of Human Rights Act |
| CIC | 2005 | statutory | RTI Act |
| Lokpal | 2013 | statutory | Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **NITI Aayog is an executive body** (Cabinet resolution, 2015), not statutory/constitutional. **NHRC (1993), CIC (2005), Lokpal (2013)** are **statutory**. First Lokpal appointed **2019** (Justice P.C. Ghose).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**NITI = No statute (executive); NHRC/CIC/Lokpal = Named Acts (1993/2005/2013).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — NHRC's powers are **recommendatory**, not binding. NITI Aayog, unlike the old Planning Commission, **does not allocate funds**. CIC and Lokpal are **statutory** (don't call them constitutional).

💡 **Example:** If a citizen's RTI appeal is denied by a department, the final appeal at the Centre lies with the **CIC** — a statutory body under the RTI Act.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- NHRC chair: retired CJI/SC judge (2019 amendment).
- CIC: RTI Act 2005; Lokpal Act 2013, first Lokpal 2019.
- Statutory vs constitutional vs executive classification.

## 🏺 Telangana History: Qutb Shahis — Political & Cultural History, Founding of Hyderabad

The **Qutb Shahi dynasty (1518–1687)** ruled the **Golconda Sultanate**, one of the five Deccan kingdoms that emerged from the Bahmani collapse — and the dynasty that **founded Hyderabad**.

**Political history.**
- **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk** declared independence from the Bahmanis around **1518**, founding the dynasty with its citadel at **Golconda**.
- **Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah** (the "Malkibharam" of Telugu sources) strengthened the kingdom and built the **Hussain Sagar** (1562, via Hussain Shah Wali).
- **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** (5th sultan) **founded the city of Hyderabad in 1591** and built the **Charminar (1591)** as its centrepiece; he was also a celebrated poet (Deccani Urdu).
- **Abdullah Qutb Shah** and finally **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (the last), whose minister-era saw figures like **Akkanna and Madanna**. **Aurangzeb (Mughals) besieged and annexed Golconda in 1687**, ending the dynasty.

**Cultural history.** The Qutb Shahis were great patrons: they promoted **Telugu alongside Persian** (unusual for a Deccan Muslim court), fostered **Deccani Urdu** poetry, and left a rich architectural legacy — the **Charminar, Mecca Masjid (begun under the Qutb Shahis), the Qutb Shahi Tombs (Ibrahim Bagh), and Golconda Fort's acoustics and water system**. **Golconda** was the world's diamond-trading capital (the **Kollur mine** region yielded famous stones, including the **Koh-i-Noor**).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded Hyderabad and built the Charminar in 1591.** The dynasty began with **Sultan Quli (~1518)** at **Golconda** and ended when **Aurangzeb annexed Golconda in 1687**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Sultan Quli starts (1518) → Hussain Sagar (1562) → Muhammad Quli founds Hyderabad + Charminar (1591) → Tana Shah falls to Aurangzeb (1687).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Charminar (1591)** was built by **Muhammad Quli** Qutb Shah (the 5th ruler), not Sultan Quli (the founder). Golconda fell to **Aurangzeb in 1687** (after an eight-month siege), not to Alauddin Khalji.

💡 **Example:** The **Qutb Shahi Tombs** at Ibrahim Bagh (a cluster of domed granite tombs) are being restored as a heritage precinct — a frequent culture/geography cross-over question.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Founder Sultan Quli (~1518); capital Golconda.
- Muhammad Quli: Hyderabad + Charminar (1591); Deccani poet.
- Hussain Sagar 1562 (Ibrahim Quli era).
- Golconda annexed by Aurangzeb 1687; Tana Shah last ruler.

✅ **Quick self-check:** Who founded Hyderabad and built the Charminar? *(Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, 1591.)*

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Subhas Chandra Bose, the INA & the RIN Revolt

The militant-nationalist stream reached its climax with **Subhas Chandra Bose** and the **Indian National Army (INA)**, and the **1946 Royal Indian Navy mutiny**.

**Bose's break with the Congress.** Bose won the Congress presidency at **Tripuri (1939)**, defeating Gandhi's candidate **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**, but resigned under pressure from the Gandhian old guard and founded the **Forward Bloc (1939)**. Interned at the outbreak of war, he **escaped from India (1941)**, reached **Germany**, then travelled by submarine to **Southeast Asia**.

**The Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj).** First raised by **Captain Mohan Singh (1942)** from Indian POWs in Southeast Asia (with Japanese help), it was **revived and led by Bose from 1943**. Bose proclaimed the **Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind Government) at Singapore (21 Oct 1943)**. The INA had a women's unit — the **Rani of Jhansi Regiment** under **Captain Lakshmi Sahgal**. Slogans: **"Dilli Chalo"** and **"Jai Hind."** The INA fought alongside the Japanese in the **Imphal–Kohima** campaign (1944) but was pushed back; Bose reportedly died in a plane crash (Taipei, **18 Aug 1945** — details debated).

**INA Trials (1945–46).** At the **Red Fort**, Britain tried INA officers **Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Kumar Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon** — deliberately one Hindu, one Muslim, one Sikh — which backfired, sparking **nationwide sympathy and protests**; the Congress defended them (Bhulabhai Desai, Nehru).

**RIN Mutiny (Feb 1946).** Ratings of the **Royal Indian Navy** mutinied, starting on **HMIS Talwar, Bombay**, over pay, food, racial discrimination and nationalist feeling; it spread to many ships/shore establishments. Though called off (after Patel's and the League's intervention), it **shook British confidence** in holding India by force.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Bose proclaimed the **Azad Hind Government at Singapore (21 Oct 1943)**; INA slogans **"Dilli Chalo" / "Jai Hind"**; **INA trials at the Red Fort (1945–46)**; **RIN mutiny began on HMIS Talwar, Bombay (Feb 1946)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Tripuri 1939 → Forward Bloc → escape 1941 → INA revived 1943 (Azad Hind, Singapore) → Red Fort trials 1945 → RIN mutiny 1946.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The INA was **first formed by Mohan Singh (1942)**; Bose **revived** it in 1943 (he did not originally found it). The **RIN** was the **Navy** (not Army) mutiny of 1946.

💡 **Example:** The INA Red Fort trials turned military "treason" defendants into **national heroes**, making the loyalty of Indian armed forces doubtful — a key factor in Britain's decision to quit.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Tripuri 1939; Forward Bloc; defeated Pattabhi Sitaramayya.
- Mohan Singh (1942) → Bose (1943); Azad Hind govt Singapore.
- Rani Jhansi Regiment (Lakshmi Sahgal).
- Red Fort trials 1945–46; RIN mutiny (Talwar) Feb 1946.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Rivers — The Krishna System

![Telangana rivers map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

The **Krishna**, the state's second major river, drains **southern Telangana** and hosts some of its biggest reservoirs.

**Source & course.** The Krishna rises at **Mahabaleshwar (Western Ghats, Maharashtra)** and enters Telangana in the southwest (near **Jurala/Alampur, Mahabubnagar-Jogulamba Gadwal**). It flows along and through southern Telangana — past **Srisailam** and **Nagarjuna Sagar** — before continuing into Andhra Pradesh. It drains a smaller share of Telangana than the Godavari but feeds the most populous southern belt and Hyderabad.

**Major projects (Krishna basin in Telangana):**
- **Priyadarshini Jurala Project** — the **first/uppermost** major project on the Krishna in Telangana (Gadwal).
- **Srisailam Dam** — a large reservoir (hydroelectric + irrigation) on the Telangana–Andhra border; feeds the **Palamuru-Rangareddy** and **Kalwakurthy** lift schemes.
- **Nagarjuna Sagar** — one of the **largest masonry dams** in the world (shared with Andhra Pradesh), on the Krishna at Nalgonda.

**Major tributaries (in/around Telangana):**
- **Tungabhadra** — joins the Krishna in the Krishna basin (southern edge, near AP/Karnataka).
- **Bhima** — a right-bank tributary (upstream, mainly Karnataka/Maharashtra).
- **Musi** — flows through **Hyderabad** and joins the Krishna near Wadapally.
- **Dindi, Paleru, Munneru, Halia** — smaller tributaries in Nalgonda/Khammam.

| Tributary | Note |
|---|---|
| Tungabhadra | southern edge (Krishna basin) |
| Bhima | upstream right-bank |
| Musi | through Hyderabad → Krishna |
| Dindi/Paleru/Munneru | Nalgonda-Khammam |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Krishna rises at **Mahabaleshwar**; **Jurala** is the **first major project** in Telangana on the Krishna; **Srisailam** and **Nagarjuna Sagar** (one of the world's largest masonry dams) are the big reservoirs; the **Musi** (Hyderabad's river) is a Krishna tributary.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Krishna in Telangana: Jurala (first) → Srisailam → Nagarjuna Sagar.**" Tributaries: "**Tungabhadra, Bhima, Musi, (Dindi/Paleru/Munneru).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Musi is a Krishna tributary** (not Godavari), even though it rises near Hyderabad. **Jurala** is the uppermost Krishna project in Telangana; **Srisailam/Nagarjuna Sagar** are shared with Andhra Pradesh.

💡 **Example:** The **Palamuru-Rangareddy** and **Kalwakurthy** lift schemes draw from the **Srisailam** backwaters to irrigate drought-prone southern Telangana — tying the Krishna system to irrigation policy.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Source Mahabaleshwar; enters near Jurala/Alampur.
- Jurala (first), Srisailam, Nagarjuna Sagar (masonry dam).
- Musi = Krishna tributary (Hyderabad).
- Krishna basin feeds Palamuru-Rangareddy & Kalwakurthy lifts.
