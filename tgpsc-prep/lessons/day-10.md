## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 Amendment of the Constitution (Art 368) & the Basic Structure Doctrine

The Constitution is neither rigid nor flexible — it is **partly both**. Article 368 gives Parliament the power to amend, but the **Basic Structure doctrine** (a judicial invention, not a written article) sets the outer limit.

### 📖 Concept

`Article 368` deals with the **power and procedure** of amendment. There are **three methods** of amending the Constitution:

| Method | Majority needed | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| By **simple majority** (outside Art 368) | Ordinary law majority | Admission of new states, citizenship, creation/abolition of Legislative Councils |
| By **special majority** (Art 368) | Majority of total membership of each House **+** 2/3 of members present & voting | Fundamental Rights, DPSP, most of the Constitution |
| **Special majority + ratification** by half the states | Special majority **+** ≥ 50% state legislatures | Federal provisions — election of President, distribution of legislative powers, representation of states, Art 368 itself |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Only the **Parliament** can initiate an amendment (not state legislatures, unlike the USA). A money bill has nothing to do with it; an amendment bill can start in **either House**, and there is **no provision for a joint sitting** to resolve a deadlock.

**The Basic Structure journey** — a tug-of-war between Parliament and the Supreme Court:
- `Shankari Prasad (1951)` & `Sajjan Singh (1965)` — Court held Parliament **can** amend Fundamental Rights (Art 368 is above Art 13).
- `Golaknath (1967)` — reversed: FRs are **outside** amending power.
- `24th Amendment (1971)` — Parliament struck back: it **can** amend any part incl. FRs.
- `Kesavananda Bharati (1973)` — the landmark: Parliament can amend **any** part **but cannot destroy the "basic structure"**. (13-judge bench, largest ever; verdict 7:6.)
- `Minerva Mills (1980)` — struck down parts of 42nd Amendment; held **judicial review** and **balance between FRs and DPSP** are basic features.

**Elements of "basic structure"** (illustrative, not exhaustive): supremacy of the Constitution, republican & democratic form, secularism, separation of powers, federalism, judicial review, rule of law, free & fair elections, parliamentary system, independence of judiciary.

**Major amendments to remember:**

| Amdt | Year | Nickname / content |
|---|---|---|
| **42nd** | 1976 | "**Mini-Constitution**" — added Socialist, Secular, Integrity to Preamble; Fundamental Duties; DPSP given precedence; curtailed judicial review (largely reversed later) |
| **44th** | 1978 | Undid many 42nd changes; **Right to Property** made a legal right (Art 300A); Art 352 "internal disturbance" → "**armed rebellion**" |
| **73rd** | 1992 | **Panchayati Raj** (Part IX, 11th Schedule) |
| **74th** | 1992 | **Municipalities** (Part IXA, 12th Schedule) |
| **86th** | 2002 | **Right to Education** — Art 21A; made education a Fundamental Duty |
| **101st** | 2016 | **GST** |
| **103rd** | 2019 | **10% EWS reservation** |

💡 **Example:** In `Kesavananda`, the Court did **not** stop the government from amending property rights — it struck down only the clause that would have barred courts from **reviewing** amendments. That is the doctrine in action: amend freely, but the "identity" of the Constitution is untouchable.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- The Basic Structure doctrine originates from `Kesavananda Bharati (1973)` — **not** from any article of the Constitution.
- 42nd = Mini-Constitution (Swaran Singh Committee, during Emergency); 44th = restorative.
- Ratification by states needs only **half** the states — and there is **no time limit** for them to ratify.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**42 broke it, 44 fixed it; 73–74 to the grassroots; 86 to school; 101 taxed it (GST); 103 gave EWS.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Preamble** IS amendable (settled in Kesavananda; 42nd Amendment did amend it) — but it cannot be amended so as to destroy the basic structure. Also: states **cannot** initiate amendments.

*(Kesavananda Bharati, 1973; the 42nd Amendment, 1976.)*

## 🏛️ Subject 2: Telangana Schemes & Important Incidents
### 🎯 Praja Palana, Praja Vani & Administrative Changes (Renamings, Emblem)

The Congress government (from Dec 2023) signalled its "people-first" branding through two grievance mechanisms and a set of symbolic administrative changes.

### 📖 Concept

**Praja Palana (ప్రజాపాలన)** — a mass application drive held in **late December 2023–January 2024** to enrol citizens for the **Six Guarantees**. Applications were collected at Gram Panchayat and municipal ward level; it doubled as the base data for the **caste survey (SEEEPC)** and scheme roll-out. Meaning: literally "**rule/governance of the people**".

**Praja Vani (ప్రజావాణి)** — a **weekly grievance-redressal programme** (held on a fixed day, typically **Tuesday**) where citizens submit petitions directly at the Secretariat and collectorates. Modelled on direct public-audience systems; meaning "**voice of the people**". *(Verify the current fixed day/format before the exam.)*

**Administrative & symbolic changes:**
- **State Secretariat** named **Dr B. R. Ambedkar Telangana State Secretariat** (inaugurated 2023) and the tallest **Ambedkar statue (125 ft)** near Hussain Sagar.
- **"Praja Bhavan"** — the government renamed the former **Pragati Bhavan** (the previous CM's camp office) to **Praja Bhavan**, symbolically opening it to the public.
- **State emblem / "Telangana Thalli" (mother of Telangana)** — the new government redesigned the **Telangana Thalli statue/image**, altering its depiction (e.g. holding **bathukamma** and crops), which became a political controversy. *(Verify the exact official emblem/Thalli specifications and unveiling date — the design was contested.)*
- **Formation Day** — shifted emphasis/observance framing (verify latest official position).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Praja Palana** = one-time mass enrolment drive for the Six Guarantees (Dec 2023–Jan 2024); **Praja Vani** = recurring weekly grievance-redressal. Don't confuse the two.

💡 **Example:** A petition about a delayed **Rythu Bharosa** payment would be submitted through **Praja Vani** (ongoing grievance), whereas the citizen's **original application** to be enrolled for the scheme came through **Praja Palana** (the enrolment drive).

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- The **Telangana Thalli** redesign was politically contested — expect a current-affairs question; **verify latest official figure/date**.
- Praja Palana feeds the **caste survey** database (link to Day 7's SEEEPC topic).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Palana = one big enrolment; Vani = weekly voice.** Pragati became **Praja**; the Secretariat became **Ambedkar's**."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Exact dates, the emblem/Thalli specifics and the fixed Praja Vani day are current-affairs items that may have changed — **verify the latest official figure** before relying on a specific detail.

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Mir Osman Ali Khan Era: Modernisation & Osmania University

The **7th and last Nizam**, `Mir Osman Ali Khan` (reigned **1911–1948**), presided over Hyderabad State's peak — a modernising, wealthy princely state that nonetheless ended with `Operation Polo`.

### 📖 Concept

**Accession & stature.** Mir Osman Ali Khan succeeded `Mahbub Ali Khan` in **1911**. Hyderabad State was the **largest and richest princely state**; the Nizam was styled **"Faithful Ally of the British Crown"** (a title reflecting his WWI contributions) and was famously reputed one of the world's wealthiest men (the *Time* 1937 cover).

**Modernisation & institutions (the exam core):**

| Institution / work | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Osmania University** | Founded **1918** — the **first university in India with Urdu as the medium of instruction**; established by a firman of the Nizam. Arts College building (Osman Ali). |
| **Hyderabad (Nizam's) State Railway** | Expanded rail network across the state |
| **Osmania General Hospital & Unani hospital** | Public health infrastructure |
| **High Court, Assembly (Jubilee Hall), Town Hall** | Indo-Saracenic public buildings by architect Vincent Esch |
| **Nizamsagar dam (Manjira)** & irrigation works | Agricultural modernisation |
| **Osman Sagar (Gandipet, 1920)** & **Himayat Sagar** | Built on the **Musi** after the devastating **1908 Musi flood** (during his predecessor) to supply and protect Hyderabad |
| **Hyderabad Civil Service, currency (Osmania Sicca/Hali Sicca) & own postage** | State had its own currency and stamps |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Osmania University (1918)** was the **first Indian university to use Urdu as the medium of instruction** — a signature achievement of Mir Osman Ali Khan.

**The socio-political undercurrent.** Beneath the modernisation, Hyderabad State remained feudal (jagirdari/deshmukh system), with **Telugu-speaking Telangana** underrepresented in an administration dominated by **Urdu** and **Mulki/non-Mulki** tensions. This fed the later `Andhra Mahasabha`, the **library movement**, and ultimately the **Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–51)** — the topics ahead.

**The end.** After 1947 the Nizam sought independence (backed by the `Razakars` under Kasim Razvi). The Government of India launched **`Operation Polo` (Police Action)** in **September 1948**; Hyderabad was integrated into the Indian Union. Mir Osman Ali Khan was made the first **Rajpramukh** of Hyderabad State (1950–56).

💡 **Example:** When you see "**Urdu medium, 1918, Hyderabad**" in a question, the answer is **Osmania University** — and its founder-patron is **Mir Osman Ali Khan**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Reign: **1911–1948**; **7th and last** Nizam.
- **Osmania University — 1918 — Urdu medium** (first of its kind).
- **Osman Sagar (Gandipet)** & **Himayat Sagar** built after the **1908 Musi flood** for water supply/flood control.
- Ended by **Operation Polo, Sept 1948**; later **Rajpramukh** of Hyderabad State.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**O for Osman: Osmania University, Osman Sagar, Osmania Hospital** — the last Nizam's initials are all over modern Hyderabad."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Osman Sagar** and **Himayat Sagar** are on the **Musi/Esi**, built for Hyderabad's water & flood protection — don't confuse them with the **Nizamsagar** (on the **Manjira**, for irrigation). The Musi flood was **1908** (predecessor's reign), the reservoirs came under **Osman Ali Khan**.

*(1918; first Indian university with Urdu as the medium of instruction.)*

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement
### 🎯 Simon Commission, Nehru Report & the Lahore Session

Between the Non-Cooperation era and Civil Disobedience lies the constitutional churn of **1927–1929**: a boycotted commission, India's first home-grown constitution draft, and the declaration of **Purna Swaraj**.

### 📖 Concept

**Simon Commission (1927–28).** The British appointed the **Indian Statutory Commission** under **Sir John Simon** (Nov 1927) to review the working of the `Government of India Act 1919` and suggest reforms. It had **7 members — all British, no Indian** — hence "**all-white commission**". Every major party boycotted it; the slogan was "**Simon Go Back**". At **Lahore (1928)**, `Lala Lajpat Rai` was injured in a lathi charge during an anti-Simon protest and died soon after — avenged by the revolutionaries (Saunders killing).

**The Nehru Report (1928).** The British Secretary of State **Lord Birkenhead** challenged Indians to produce an agreed constitution. An **All-Parties Conference** appointed a committee under **Motilal Nehru** (Jawaharlal as secretary). Key recommendations:

| Nehru Report proposed | Note |
|---|---|
| **Dominion Status** | Not full independence — this split the Congress |
| **No separate electorates** | Joint electorates with reserved seats for minorities (angered the Muslim League) |
| **Fundamental Rights** listed | First Indian charter of rights |
| Responsible govt, bicameral legislature, linguistic provinces | Federal structure |

**Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)** were the Muslim League's counter to the Nehru Report's rejection of separate electorates — a key milestone toward the two-nation demand.

**Lahore Session (December 1929).** Presided over by **Jawaharlal Nehru** (his first as Congress President), on the banks of the **Ravi**:
- Declared **`Purna Swaraj` (Complete Independence)** as the goal (rejecting Dominion Status).
- Resolved to launch **Civil Disobedience**.
- Fixed **26 January 1930** as the first **Independence Day (Purna Swaraj Day)** — the reason **26 January** was later chosen for the Republic (1950).
- The **tricolour was hoisted** at midnight on 31 Dec 1929.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Lahore Session (Dec 1929, Jawaharlal Nehru presiding)** adopted **Purna Swaraj** and fixed **26 January 1930** as the first Independence Day — later chosen as **Republic Day**.

💡 **Example:** "Simon Go Back" and the Nehru Report are cause-and-effect: because the Simon Commission had **no Indian**, Britain was taunted that Indians couldn't agree on a constitution — the **Nehru Report** was the answer, and its "Dominion Status" ceiling is exactly what the **Lahore Session** rejected two years later.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Simon Commission = **all-British, 7 members, 1927**, reviewed the **1919 Act**.
- Nehru Report (1928) demanded **Dominion Status** + **no separate electorates** + **Fundamental Rights**.
- Lahore 1929 → **Purna Swaraj**, **JLN president**, **26 Jan 1930** first Independence Day.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Simon (all-white) → Nehru Report (Dominion) → Lahore (Purna Swaraj).** 1927 → 1928 → 1929, one step per year."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Nehru Report (1928)** demanded **Dominion Status**, NOT full independence — that's why the younger leaders (Jawaharlal, Bose) were dissatisfied and pushed the **Purna Swaraj** resolution at **Lahore (1929)**. Don't confuse the **Nehru Report (1928)** with the **Motilal–Jawaharlal** authorship (Motilal chaired).

**✅ Quick self-check:** What did the Lahore Session declare as the national goal, and what date did it fix as the first Independence Day?
*(Purna Swaraj; 26 January 1930.)*
