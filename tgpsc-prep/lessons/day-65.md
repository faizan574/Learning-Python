## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Making of the Constitution & Constituent Assembly

### Deep Dive

The **Constituent Assembly** was constituted under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)** — an **indirectly elected** body (members chosen by provincial assemblies). Total strength was **`389`** (`292` from provinces + `93` from princely states + `4` from chief commissioners' areas); after **Partition**, the effective strength fell to about **`299`**.

**Key milestones:**
- **First sitting:** `9 December 1946`; **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** was the temporary (interim) chairman.
- **Permanent President:** **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** (elected `11 December 1946`).
- **Objectives Resolution:** moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru** on `13 December 1946` — it laid down the ideals and later inspired the **Preamble**.
- **Constitutional Advisor:** **Sir B.N. Rau**.
- **Drafting Committee:** set up on `29 August 1947`, chaired by **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** (7 members).
- **Adopted:** `26 November 1949` (**Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas**); **came into force:** `26 January 1950`.
- **Time taken:** about **`2 years, 11 months, 18 days`**.

**Major committees:** Union Powers Committee & Union Constitution Committee (**Nehru**), Provincial Constitution Committee (**Sardar Patel**), Drafting Committee (**Ambedkar**), and the Rules/Steering committees.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Constitution **adopted `26 Nov 1949`**, **enforced `26 Jan 1950`**. `26 January` was chosen to honour the **Purna Swaraj Day (1930)**. Ambedkar chaired the **Drafting Committee** (formed `29 Aug 1947`).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Don't confuse dates: **first meeting `9 Dec 1946`**, **adopted `26 Nov 1949`**, **commenced `26 Jan 1950`**. The Assembly was **indirectly elected**, *not* on universal adult franchise.

💡 **Example:** The **Objectives Resolution (1946)** — sovereignty vested in the people, justice, liberty, equality — became the philosophical backbone of the **Preamble**, a favourite PYQ link.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Basis: **Cabinet Mission Plan 1946**; strength `389` → `299` post-Partition.
- Interim chair **S. Sinha**; President **Rajendra Prasad**; Advisor **B.N. Rau**.
- Drafting Committee chair **Ambedkar**; Objectives Resolution by **Nehru**.
- Adopted `26 Nov 1949`; enforced `26 Jan 1950`; ~`2 yr 11 mo 18 days`.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Who was the constitutional advisor to the Assembly?* → **Sir B.N. Rau** (prepared the initial draft).

## 🗳️ Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Musi Rejuvenation, HYDRAA & Urban Governance

### Deep Dive

**Musi River Rejuvenation.** The **Musi**, a Krishna tributary flowing through Hyderabad, has long suffered from **sewage inflow, encroachment and pollution**. The state's **Musi Riverfront Development** initiative aims to **clean, treat sewage, relocate encroachments and develop a riverfront** (often compared to the Sabarmati model). Components typically include **sewage-treatment plants (STPs)**, embankments, and resettlement. *Verify the latest official project cost, timeline and status before the exam.*

**HYDRAA — Hyderabad Disaster Response and Assets Protection Agency.** Constituted in **2024** to (i) **protect government assets, lakes, parks and open spaces**, (ii) **remove encroachments** within lake **Full Tank Level (FTL)** and **buffer zones**, and (iii) act as a **disaster-response** body for the Hyderabad metropolitan area. It brought urban encroachment and lake-protection into sharp public focus.

**Urban governance context.** These bodies address the classic Hyderabad urban problems — **lake shrinkage, unauthorised construction on water bodies, flooding, and drainage**. Key concepts: **FTL** (the maximum water level of a tank/lake) and the **buffer zone** (a protected margin around it) where construction is restricted.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **HYDRAA (2024)** protects lakes/open spaces and enforces **FTL & buffer-zone** limits in Greater Hyderabad; the **Musi Riverfront** project targets sewage treatment and riverfront redevelopment.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Project figures (cost, number of STPs, families relocated, encroachments cleared) are **frequently updated** — write **"verify latest official figure"** rather than quoting a stale number. Distinguish **FTL** (water level) from **buffer zone** (protected land margin).

💡 **Example:** Demolition of structures found within a lake's FTL/buffer by HYDRAA is a recurring current-affairs item linking **urban ecology, disaster management and governance**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **HYDRAA** = Hyderabad Disaster Response & Assets Protection Agency (est. **2024**).
- Mandate: lake/asset protection, FTL & buffer enforcement, disaster response.
- **Musi Riverfront** = clean-up + STPs + riverfront development (verify status).
- Key terms: **FTL**, **buffer zone**, encroachment.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *What does FTL stand for?* → **Full Tank Level** — the maximum storage level of a tank/lake, beyond which construction is restricted.

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Merger, Gentlemen's Agreement & Violations

### Deep Dive

After the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** (on the **Fazl Ali Commission / SRC** recommendations), the Telugu-speaking **Telangana** region of the former Hyderabad State was merged with **Andhra State** to form **Andhra Pradesh** on `1 November 1956`. Telangana leaders feared that the more developed coastal Andhra region would dominate in jobs, education and revenue — so a set of safeguards was negotiated.

**The Gentlemen's Agreement (1956).** Signed by leaders of both regions (Feb 1956), it promised safeguards for Telangana, broadly:
- A **Telangana Regional Committee (TRC)** for regional legislative oversight.
- **Domicile/"Mulki" preference** for Telangana people in state government jobs.
- **Telangana's surplus revenues** to be spent on **Telangana's development**.
- **Reservation in educational admissions** for Telangana students.
- A convention that if the **Chief Minister** was from one region, the **Deputy Chief Minister** would be from the other.

*(Verify the exact clause list before the exam — accounts vary in detail.)*

**Violations & consequences.** Over the following years, Telangana leaders alleged **breaches** — diversion of surplus funds, non-locals in reserved posts (Mulki violations), and weak enforcement of the TRC. These grievances culminated in the **1969 Jai Telangana / Separate Telangana agitation**, and later the **Six-Point Formula (1973)** (which replaced Mulki rules) and **Article 371-D** safeguards.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Andhra Pradesh formed `1 Nov 1956`; the **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** created the **Telangana Regional Committee** and Mulki/revenue/education safeguards. Alleged violations fuelled the **1969 agitation**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Gentlemen's Agreement was a **political agreement**, not a statute; its weak enforceability is precisely why violations occurred. `Article 371-D` (special provisions for AP) came **later** (via the **32nd Amendment, 1973**).

💡 **Example:** Non-implementation of **Mulki rules** (local-employment safeguards) is the direct historical thread from `1956` → the `1969` agitation → the eventual **statehood movement** — a frequent PYQ chain.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- SRA 1956 → AP on `1 Nov 1956`; Gentlemen's Agreement (Feb 1956).
- Safeguards: **TRC**, Mulki jobs, surplus revenue, education, CM/Dy-CM convention.
- Violations → **1969 agitation** → Six-Point Formula (1973) → **Art 371-D**.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which body was created to give Telangana legislative oversight under the Agreement?* → the **Telangana Regional Committee (TRC)**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: WWII, August Offer, Cripps Mission & Quit India

### Deep Dive

**Outbreak of WWII (1939).** The Viceroy declared India a belligerent **without consulting Indian leaders**; in protest the **Congress ministries (formed 1937) resigned** in late 1939.

**August Offer (1940).** Viceroy **Linlithgow's** proposal: **dominion status** as the long-term goal, expansion of the **Executive Council** to include more Indians, and a war advisory council. It was **rejected** by both Congress (wanted immediate self-government) and the Muslim League. It did, however, concede that the **minority (League) could not be coerced** into a constitution.

**Cripps Mission (1942).** Sir **Stafford Cripps** came with proposals amid the war crisis: **Dominion status** after the war, an elected **Constituent Assembly** to frame the constitution, and the **right of provinces/states to not accede** (a seed of Partition). Congress rejected it — Gandhi called it a **"post-dated cheque on a crashing bank."**

**Quit India Movement (1942).** The **AICC session at Bombay (Gowalia Tank)** passed the **Quit India Resolution** on `8 August 1942`; Gandhi gave the call **"Do or Die."** On `9 August 1942`, the entire top leadership was **arrested**, and the movement became **leaderless and spontaneous**:
- **Underground leaders:** Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, **Aruna Asaf Ali** (hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank), Usha Mehta (secret Congress Radio).
- **Parallel governments (Prati Sarkar):** **Ballia** (UP, Chittu Pandey), **Tamluk** (Bengal — Jatiya Sarkar), **Satara** (Maharashtra — longest-lasting).

It was suppressed by 1943 but marked the **final mass upsurge** before Independence.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Cripps (1942)** = "post-dated cheque"; **Quit India** launched `8 Aug 1942` (Gowalia Tank), slogan **"Do or Die"**; leaders arrested `9 Aug 1942`. Parallel governments at **Ballia, Tamluk, Satara**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **August Offer = 1940** (Linlithgow); **Cripps Mission = 1942** (Stafford Cripps). Don't merge them. The **Cripps** proposal first introduced the **right of provinces not to accede** (later a Partition seed).

💡 **Example:** **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisting the Congress flag at **Gowalia Tank** and **Usha Mehta's** underground **Congress Radio** are classic Quit India PYQ personalities.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Congress ministries resigned **1939**; August Offer **1940**; Cripps **1942**; Quit India **Aug 1942**.
- Cripps: dominion status + Constituent Assembly + right not to accede → rejected.
- Quit India: Gowalia Tank, "Do or Die," leaders jailed 9 Aug, parallel govts.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Who called the Cripps offer a "post-dated cheque"?* → **Mahatma Gandhi** (some versions add "on a crashing/failing bank").

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Soils & Agriculture (Crops, Seasons, Horticulture)

### Deep Dive

**Soils.** Telangana's soils are dominated by **red soils** (locally *chalka/dubba*), covering the largest share of the state (*verify latest percentage*). Other types:
- **Red sandy/loamy soils** — most widespread; low fertility, need fertiliser/irrigation; suit coarse grains, pulses, groundnut.
- **Black cotton soils (regur)** — clayey, moisture-retentive; found in patches (e.g., parts of Adilabad, Khammam); good for **cotton**.
- **Alluvial soils** — along river valleys (Godavari, Krishna); fertile, suit **paddy**.
- **Laterite & problem soils** — localised.

**Cropping seasons.**
- **Kharif (Vanakalam):** sown with the **SW monsoon (June–July)**, harvested Oct–Nov — the main season (paddy, cotton, maize, pulses).
- **Rabi (Yasangi):** Oct–Nov to Feb–Mar, on residual moisture/irrigation (paddy, maize, groundnut).

**Major crops.** **Paddy (rice)** is the leading crop (boosted by lift-irrigation like Kaleshwaram); **cotton, maize, red gram (tur), chillies, turmeric, groundnut, jowar** are important. Telangana is a **major producer of cotton, chillies (Khammam/Warangal), turmeric (Nizamabad) and maize** (*verify current rankings*).

**Horticulture.** **Mango** (a leading horticultural crop), **sweet orange/mosambi** (Nalgonda belt), **grapes, guava, vegetables and oil palm** (newly promoted) — supported by micro-irrigation.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Red soils** dominate Telangana; **black (regur)** soils suit **cotton**; **alluvial** valleys suit **paddy**. Seasons: **Kharif/Vanakalam** (SW monsoon) and **Rabi/Yasangi** (winter).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Crop-area and production **rankings shift year to year** — write **"verify latest figure"** rather than a fixed number. Note the **local names**: *Vanakalam* = Kharif, *Yasangi* = Rabi.

💡 **Example:** **Nizamabad turmeric** and **Warangal/Khammam chillies** are classic "crop–district" PYQ pairings; **Nalgonda sweet oranges** for horticulture.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Dominant soil: **red** (chalka/dubba); **regur** for cotton; **alluvial** for paddy.
- Kharif = **Vanakalam**; Rabi = **Yasangi**.
- Key crops: paddy, cotton, maize, red gram, chillies, turmeric.
- Horticulture: mango, mosambi (Nalgonda), grapes, oil palm.
