## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 The President & Vice-President of India

The **President** is the constitutional **head of state** and "first citizen"; the **Vice-President** is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. Both are nominal executives — real power rests with the Council of Ministers.

### 📖 Concept

**President — election & eligibility (`Art 52–62`).**
- Elected **indirectly** by an **electoral college** = elected MPs (both Houses) + elected MLAs of states **+ elected MLAs of Delhi & Puducherry**. Nominated members and Legislative Council members do **not** vote.
- Method: **proportional representation by single transferable vote**, secret ballot; value of votes weighted so there's **uniformity among states** and **parity between the Union and the states**.
- Qualifications: citizen of India, **35+ years**, qualified to be a **Lok Sabha** member, must not hold an office of profit.
- **Term: 5 years**; eligible for **re-election any number of times**. Resigns to the **Vice-President**.

**Value of votes (learn the formulae):**

```
Value of one MLA's vote  = (State population / total elected MLAs) ÷ 1000
Value of one MP's vote    = (total value of all MLAs' votes) / (total elected MPs)
```

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The President's electoral college has **elected** MPs & MLAs (plus Delhi & Puducherry MLAs). **Nominated members do NOT participate** in the President's election — but they **do** vote in the **Vice-President's** election.

**Impeachment (`Art 61`)** — the only removal process, for **"violation of the Constitution"**. Either House can initiate; the charge needs a **resolution by ≥ 1/4 of the total members** (14 days' notice) passed by a **2/3 majority of the total membership** of that House; the other House investigates and must also pass it by **2/3 of total membership**. **Nominated members participate** in impeachment (it's not an "election"). No state legislature is involved.

**Key powers.** Executive (appointments — PM, judges, Governors, CEC, CAG), legislative (summon/prorogue, dissolve Lok Sabha, assent to bills, **Ordinance under Art 123**), judicial (**pardoning under Art 72**), financial (money bills need prior recommendation), military (Supreme Commander), and emergency (`Art 352, 356, 360`). Most exercised on the **aid and advice** of the Council of Ministers (`Art 74`, binding after the 42nd/44th Amendments — can return once for reconsideration).

**Veto powers:** **Absolute, Suspensive, Pocket** (no time limit to act on a bill — famously used by Zail Singh). India has **no qualified veto**.

**Vice-President (`Art 63–71`).**
- Elected by an **electoral college of BOTH Houses of Parliament** (Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha), **including nominated members** — **no state legislatures** involved.
- Qualifications: 35+, qualified to be a **Rajya Sabha** member.
- **Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**; acts as President during a vacancy (**max 6 months**, within which a new President must be elected).
- Removed by a Rajya Sabha resolution (**effective majority**) agreed to by the Lok Sabha — **no formal "impeachment" grounds** needed.

| Feature | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Electoral college | Elected MPs + MLAs (+ Delhi, Puducherry) | Both Houses of Parliament only |
| Nominated members vote? | **No** | **Yes** |
| Min. age | 35 | 35 |
| Term | 5 years | 5 years |
| Removal | Impeachment (Art 61) | RS resolution + LS agreement |

💡 **Example:** In the 2022 Vice-Presidential election, **nominated** Rajya Sabha members were eligible to vote — which they would **not** be in a Presidential election. That single difference is a frequent one-mark question.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Value of MLA vote uses **1971 population** (frozen by the 84th Amendment) — `verify` the base year in the question.
- President's term vacancy must be filled within **6 months**; VP has **no such fixed rule** to fill his own vacancy immediately (RS Deputy Chairman presides meanwhile).
- Disputes over the President's/VP's election → **Supreme Court** (its decision final).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**President: elected people only (no nominated), impeached for violating the Constitution. Vice-President: Parliament only (nominated included), chairs Rajya Sabha.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **MLAs vote for the President but NOT the Vice-President.** And **nominated members vote for VP but NOT President.** These two facts are mirror-image traps.

**✅ Quick self-check:** Who elects the Vice-President, and do nominated members participate?
*(An electoral college of both Houses of Parliament; yes, nominated members do vote.)*

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Socio-Cultural Awakening: Andhra Mahasabha, Library Movement & the Press

Under the Nizam, a Telugu socio-cultural renaissance grew into a **political awakening** — the seedbed of the later Armed Struggle and statehood sentiment.

### 📖 Concept

**The Library Movement (Grandhalaya Udyamam).** The earliest expression of awakening — reading rooms and libraries promoting **Telugu language, literacy and identity** against the state's Urdu dominance.
- **Sri Krishnadevaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam** — Hyderabad, **1901** — the first major Telugu library (founded by Komarraju Lakshmana Rao, Munagala Raja Nayani Venkata Ranga Rao and others).
- **Rajaraja Narendra Andhra Bhasha Nilayam** — Hanamkonda, **1904**.
- These libraries became centres for Telugu consciousness; the movement is linked with **Komarraju Lakshmana Rao** and the **Vignana Chandrika Mandali** (publishing Telugu knowledge texts).

**The Press.** Newspapers/journals carried the awakening:
- **Golconda Patrika** (1926) — edited by **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy**, a landmark Telugu newspaper.
- **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** also compiled *"Andhrula Sanghika Charitra"* (Social History of the Andhras) and edited the *Golconda Kavula Sanchika* (anthology answering the taunt that Telangana had no poets).
- Other journals: **Neelagiri, Tenugu, Sujata** etc. (`verify specifics`).

**Andhra Mahasabha (founded 1930).** The apex platform of the awakening.
- **First session: Jogipet (Medak), 1930**, presided over by **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy**.
- Began as a **socio-cultural** body (Telugu language, education, social reform — anti-vetti/forced labour, anti-untouchability) and gradually turned **political**, demanding responsible government.
- By the **1940s it split** — a **moderate** wing and a **Communist-led radical** wing (associated with the **Andhra Mahasabha (Communist)**), the latter spearheading the **Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–51)** against **vetti (bonded labour)** and landlordism.
- Parallel bodies: **Andhra Jana Sangham (1921)** — an earlier precursor; the **Andhra Saraswata Parishad**; and for other communities the **Maharashtra Parishad** and **Karnataka Parishad**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **first Andhra Mahasabha** met at **Jogipet (1930)** under **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy**; it evolved from socio-cultural reform into a political force, later splitting into moderate and **Communist** wings — the latter led the **Armed Struggle**.

💡 **Example:** The taunt that "Telangana has no Telugu poets" was answered by **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy's** *Golconda Kavula Sanchika* — a cultural act that fed political self-respect. That link (culture → politics) is the theme of this whole topic.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **1901 Sri Krishnadevaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam (Hyderabad)** — first major Telugu library.
- **Andhra Mahasabha — 1930 — Jogipet — Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** (first president).
- **Golconda Patrika (1926)** — Suravaram Pratapa Reddy.
- The **Communist wing** of the Andhra Mahasabha led the **1946–51 Armed Struggle** (next in the History track).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Libraries (1901) → Press (Golconda Patrika 1926) → Andhra Mahasabha (Jogipet 1930) → Armed Struggle (1946).** Suravaram Pratapa Reddy links the press and the Mahasabha."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Distinguish **Andhra Jana Sangham (1921)**, the earlier body, from the **Andhra Mahasabha (1930)**. And the first Mahasabha was at **Jogipet (Medak)**, not Hyderabad.

**✅ Quick self-check:** Where and when did the first Andhra Mahasabha meet, and who presided?
*(Jogipet, Medak district, 1930; Suravaram Pratapa Reddy.)*

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement
### 🎯 Civil Disobedience, the Round Table Conferences & the Poona Pact

From the **Purna Swaraj** resolution (Lahore 1929) flowed the **Civil Disobedience Movement** — begun with the **Dandi Salt March** — running in parallel with the **Round Table Conferences** and culminating in the **Poona Pact**.

### 📖 Concept

**Dandi March & Civil Disobedience (1930).** Gandhi launched the movement by breaking the **salt law**:
- **12 March – 6 April 1930**: Gandhi marched **~240 miles (~385 km)** from **Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi** with 78 followers and made salt, defying the salt monopoly.
- Unlike Non-Cooperation (which *withheld* cooperation), Civil Disobedience **actively broke laws** — no-tax campaigns, boycott of foreign cloth & liquor, defiance of forest laws.
- Notable: **Dharasana Salt Works** raid; women's mass participation; in the **North-West Frontier**, **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** ("Frontier Gandhi") led the **Khudai Khidmatgars** (Red Shirts).

**Round Table Conferences (London):**

| RTC | Date | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | 1930–31 | Congress **boycotted** (leaders jailed); League, princes, others attended |
| **Second** | 1931 | Gandhi attended as **sole Congress representative** (after the **Gandhi–Irwin Pact, March 1931**); deadlocked over **minorities/communal representation** |
| **Third** | 1932 | Congress absent again; little outcome; fed into the **GoI Act 1935** |

**Gandhi–Irwin Pact (March 1931)** — Congress suspended CDM, agreed to join the 2nd RTC; government released political prisoners (except those in violence cases).

**Communal Award & the Poona Pact (1932).** This is the exam core:
- **Communal Award (Aug 1932)** by British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** granted **separate electorates** to minorities **including the Depressed Classes (Dalits)**.
- Gandhi opposed separate electorates for Dalits (fearing it would split Hindu society) and began a **fast unto death** in **Yerwada Jail (Poona)**.
- **Poona Pact (24 September 1932)** — agreement between **Gandhi and Dr B. R. Ambedkar**: replaced separate electorates with a **single (joint) electorate** but **reserved seats** for the Depressed Classes — and **increased** their reserved seats substantially (from the Award's ~71 to **148** in provincial legislatures).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Poona Pact (1932)** between **Gandhi and Ambedkar** replaced the Communal Award's **separate electorates** for Depressed Classes with **reserved seats in joint electorates**, raising their seats to **148** in the provinces.

💡 **Example:** The Communal Award gave Dalits **separate electorates** (their own voters, own candidates). Gandhi's fast forced the **Poona Pact**, which kept Dalits within the **common Hindu electorate** but **guaranteed reserved seats** — the model later adopted in the Constitution's reservation of seats (Art 330/332).

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Dandi March: **12 March 1930**, Sabarmati → Dandi (~240 miles), broke the **salt law**.
- **2nd RTC (1931)** — Gandhi the **sole Congress** delegate; **1st and 3rd** — Congress absent.
- **Poona Pact — 24 Sep 1932 — Gandhi & Ambedkar** — joint electorate + reserved seats (148).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Salt (Dandi 1930) → Round Tables (1930-32) → Communal Award → Poona Pact (Gandhi + Ambedkar, joint electorate + reserved seats).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Poona Pact did NOT abolish reservation** — it abolished **separate electorates** for Dalits and replaced them with **reserved seats within joint electorates** (and increased the number). Don't say it "removed Dalit representation."

**✅ Quick self-check:** Who signed the Poona Pact, and what did it replace?
*(Gandhi and Ambedkar, 1932; it replaced separate electorates for the Depressed Classes with reserved seats in a joint electorate.)*

## 🗺️ Subject 5: Telangana Geography (with Map)
### 🎯 Area Focus: Mahabubnagar / Palamuru (Geology + Culture)

**Palamuru** (Mahabubnagar) is the southern gateway of Telangana — ancient granite terrain, chronic drought answered by giant lift schemes, and a distinct cultural identity.

**🗺️ Reference map — Mahabubnagar / Palamuru region:**

![Mahabubnagar region](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/mahabubnagar.svg)

*(If the map doesn't display in email, open the lesson on GitHub — the SVG is at `tgpsc-prep/maps/mahabubnagar.svg`.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Location & administrative note.** Mahabubnagar (historically **Palamooru / Rukmammapeta**, renamed after Nizam **Mahbub Ali Khan**) lies in **southern Telangana**, bordering the Krishna river and (across it) Karnataka/Andhra. The old large district was **reorganised (2016)** into several districts: **Mahabubnagar, Nagarkurnool, Wanaparthy, Jogulamba Gadwal, Narayanpet** (`verify exact list`).

**Geology & physiography.**
- Part of the ancient **Archaean granite–gneiss (Peninsular Gneissic Complex)** — hard crystalline rock, **thin red sandy/chalka soils**, low water-retention → drought-prone.
- The **Krishna river** forms the southern boundary; **Jurala (Priyadarshini Jurala)** is the first major project on the Krishna as it enters Telangana; **Rajolibanda (RDS)** diversion is a long-standing inter-state issue with Karnataka (`verify`).
- **Amrabad Plateau / Nallamala Hills** in the east (Nagarkurnool side) — part of the **Amrabad Tiger Reserve**, Deccan plateau uplands with **uranium** deposits (Nallamala — an environmental concern).
- Chronic **fluoride** in groundwater (health issue) and historic **migration/labour** ("Palamuru labour") due to drought.

**Irrigation lifelines (drought remedy).**
- **Palamuru–Rangareddy Lift Irrigation Scheme (PRLIS)** — a mega lift scheme drawing Krishna water (from **Srisailam** backwaters) up to the plateau to irrigate Mahabubnagar–Rangareddy (`verify current status/capacity`).
- **Kalvakurthy Lift Irrigation Scheme**, **Bhima**, **Koil Sagar**, **Nettempadu** — lift schemes designed to end Palamuru's water poverty.
- **Jurala** & **Srisailam** (on the Krishna) anchor the region's water and hydropower.

**Culture & heritage (the "cultural reference" of the area):**
- **Pillalamarri** — a giant ~700-year-old banyan tree near Mahabubnagar town; nearby **Pillalamarri** temples (Chennakesava/Someswara, Kakatiya-era).
- **Alampur** (Jogulamba Gadwal) — the **"Western Gateway of Srisailam"**: the **Nava Brahma temples** (Badami Chalukya, 7th c.) and the **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha** (one of the 18 Maha Shakti Peethas) on the Tungabhadra–Krishna confluence.
- **Gadwal & Narayanpet sarees** — famous **handloom** GI textiles (Gadwal = cotton body + silk pallu/zari; Narayanpet silk).
- **Wanaparthy, Gadwal, Amarchinta, Jataprole (Kollapur)** — historic **samsthanams** (feudatory estates) with palaces and patronage of Telugu literature.
- **Koilsagar, Kollapur** temples; **Mannanur** (gateway to Amrabad/Nallamala tiger reserve).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Palamuru sits on **Archaean granite** with **thin red soils** → drought-prone; its cultural crown is **Alampur** (Nava Brahma temples + **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**) and **Gadwal/Narayanpet** handloom sarees; **Palamuru–Rangareddy LIS** is its water lifeline.
