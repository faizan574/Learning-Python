## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Supreme Court & High Courts (Judicial Review, PIL, Collegium)

### Deep Dive

**Supreme Court (Art 124-147).** Head of the integrated judiciary.
- **Composition:** CJI + other judges (sanctioned strength **34 including the CJI**, *verify*). **Appointment** by the President via the **collegium** (CJI + senior-most judges).
- **Jurisdiction:** **Original (Art 131)** — Centre-State/inter-State disputes; **Writ (Art 32)** — enforcement of FRs; **Appellate** (constitutional, civil, criminal, special leave **Art 136**); **Advisory (Art 143)** — President may seek opinion.
- **Court of record (Art 129)**; power of **judicial review** (to strike down unconstitutional laws/acts).

**High Courts (Art 214-231).** One per state (some shared). **Writ jurisdiction (Art 226)** is **wider than the SC's Art 32** — HCs issue writs for **FRs and other legal rights**. Power of **superintendence (Art 227)** over subordinate courts.

**Collegium system.** A judge-led body (CJI + senior SC judges) recommends appointments/transfers of higher-judiciary judges; the **NJAC Act (99th Amendment) was struck down (2015)**, retaining the collegium.

**PIL (Public Interest Litigation).** A tool of **judicial activism** — **relaxed locus standi** lets any public-spirited person (or the court suo motu) seek relief for the disadvantaged; expanded access to justice from the late 1970s-80s.

**Removal of judges.** By **impeachment (Art 124(4))** for **"proved misbehaviour or incapacity"** — a special-majority address by both Houses.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — SC jurisdiction: **original (131), writ (32), appellate (136), advisory (143)**; **court of record (129)**. HC writ power **Art 226 (wider than 32)**. **Collegium** appoints (NJAC struck down 2015). **PIL** = relaxed locus standi.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Art 226 (HC) is wider** than **Art 32 (SC)** — HCs cover all legal rights, not just FRs. The **collegium** (not NJAC) governs appointments after the 2015 verdict. Judges are removed by **"proved misbehaviour or incapacity,"** not a simple vote.

💡 **Example:** A citizen filing a **PIL** for prisoners' rights (relaxed locus standi) under **Art 32/226** is the classic judicial-activism PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- SC: Art 124; original 131, writ 32, appellate 136, advisory 143; court of record 129.
- HC: Art 214; writ 226 (wider), superintendence 227.
- Collegium (NJAC struck down 2015); PIL (locus standi relaxed).
- Removal: impeachment, Art 124(4).

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which article gives High Courts their (wider) writ power?* → **Article 226**.

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Pre-Satavahana Telangana (Megaliths, Kotilingala, Asmaka)

### Deep Dive

Before the Satavahanas, Telangana saw an **Iron Age megalithic culture** and an early **mahajanapada**.

**Megalithic culture (Iron Age, c. 1000 BCE onward).** Characterised by **large-stone (megalith) burials** — **cairns, cists, dolmens, menhirs** — with iron tools, black-and-red ware pottery. Telangana sites include **Dhulikatta, Kotilingala, Peddabankur, Rameshwaram** (Karimnagar region). These reflect settled agrarian/iron-using communities.

**Kotilingala (Jagtial/Karimnagar, on the Godavari).** A significant **pre-Satavahana township** and **mint** — **coins of early local rulers (Gobada, Samagopa, Narana)** and the earliest Satavahana coins (**Simuka**) were found here, making it a key site for Deccan's early history.

**Asmaka (Assaka) Mahajanapada.** Among the **16 Mahajanapadas** of the 6th century BCE, **Asmaka** was the **only one located in the south (Deccan)**, on the **Godavari**, with its capital at **Potana / Paudanya** (often placed in the Telangana-region/Godavari belt). It links Telangana to the pan-Indian mahajanapada age.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Pre-Satavahana Telangana: **megalithic** burials (Dhulikatta, Peddabankur, Kotilingala); **Kotilingala** = early mint/township (pre-Satavahana & Simuka coins); **Asmaka** = the **only southern Mahajanapada** (Godavari, capital Potana).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Asmaka** is the **southern-most** of the 16 Mahajanapadas (on the Godavari). **Kotilingala** is a **pre-Satavahana** coin/township site (don't date it to the Kakatiyas). Megaliths = **Iron Age burials**, not temples.

💡 **Example:** "Which Mahajanapada was located in the south, on the Godavari?" → **Asmaka (Assaka)** — a standard ancient-history PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Megalithic Iron Age: cairns/cists/dolmens; Dhulikatta, Peddabankur, Kotilingala.
- Kotilingala: pre-Satavahana coins (Gobada, Samagopa) + Simuka; early mint.
- Asmaka: only southern Mahajanapada, Godavari, capital Potana/Paudanya.
- Black-and-red ware; iron tools.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which was the only Mahajanapada in the Deccan/south?* → **Asmaka (Assaka)**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Non-Cooperation Movement & the Swarajists

### Deep Dive

**Non-Cooperation Movement (NCM), 1920-22.** The first **mass** Gandhian movement, launched after the **Jallianwala Bagh** and **Khilafat** grievances. Approved at the **Calcutta special session (1920)** and **Nagpur session (Dec 1920)**:
- **Boycott** of government **schools, colleges, courts, councils, titles and foreign goods**;
- **Promotion** of **national schools, panchayats, khadi/swadeshi, Hindu-Muslim unity, and removal of untouchability**.
- Combined with the **Khilafat movement** (Hindu-Muslim unity peak).

**Withdrawal — Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922).** After a mob burned a police station at **Chauri Chaura (UP)** killing policemen, Gandhi — committed to non-violence — **called off the movement (Feb 1922)**, disappointing many; he was later arrested.

**The Swarajists (Swaraj Party, 1923).** Formed by **C.R. Das (President)** and **Motilal Nehru (Secretary)** after NCM's withdrawal, advocating **"council entry"** — contesting **legislative council elections** to **obstruct/expose** the government from within (the "**pro-changers**"), against the "**no-changers**" (Gandhians focused on constructive work). They won seats in the 1923 elections and used the councils as a political platform.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **NCM 1920-22** (boycott schools/courts/councils/titles/foreign goods; + Khilafat); withdrawn after **Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922)**. **Swaraj Party (1923): C.R. Das & Motilal Nehru** — **council entry** ("pro-changers").

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — NCM was withdrawn due to **Chauri Chaura (1922)**, not success. The **Swarajists favoured entering councils** ("pro-changers"); the **no-changers** opposed it. NCM was **non-violent non-cooperation**, not civil disobedience (that was 1930).

💡 **Example:** **C.R. Das + Motilal Nehru founding the Swaraj Party** for council entry after Chauri Chaura is the standard post-NCM PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- NCM 1920-22: Nagpur session; boycott + swadeshi/khadi; + Khilafat.
- Withdrawn: Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922).
- Swaraj Party 1923: C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru; council entry.
- Pro-changers vs no-changers.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which incident led Gandhi to call off the Non-Cooperation Movement?* → **Chauri Chaura (February 1922)**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Nalgonda-Suryapet

### Deep Dive

The **Nalgonda-Suryapet** belt lies in **southern Telangana**, in the **Krishna basin** (*verify current district boundaries: Nalgonda, Suryapet, Yadadri-Bhuvanagiri*).

**Rivers & irrigation.** The **Krishna** and its tributaries **Musi, Dindi, Paleru, Halia** cross the belt. Key projects: **Nagarjuna Sagar** (on the **Krishna**, one of the world's largest masonry dams — shared), **AMRP / SLBC (Srisailam Left Bank Canal — Alimineti Madhava Reddy project)**, **Udayasamudram, Musi project**.

**Groundwater issue — fluorosis.** Parts of **Nalgonda** are a classic example of **dental & skeletal fluorosis** from **high-fluoride groundwater** — a major public-health geography topic (addressed by **Mission Bhagiratha** surface-water supply). **Uranium** deposits near the **Nallamala (Lambapur-Peddagattu)** raise environmental concerns.

**Heritage & tourism.** **Kolanupaka (Kulpakji)** — an ancient **Jain temple** (Nalgonda); **Phanigiri** — a **Buddhist site** (Suryapet); **Pillalamarri (Nalgonda)** — the **Pachala Someswara/Erakeshwara** Kakatiya temples; **Bhongir (Bhuvanagiri) Fort** (nearby); **Yadadri (Yadagirigutta)** is in the adjacent Yadadri-Bhuvanagiri district.

![Telangana regions base map — locate the Nalgonda-Suryapet belt in the Krishna basin (south)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Nalgonda-Suryapet = southern Krishna basin; **Nagarjuna Sagar** (Krishna); **fluorosis** (high-fluoride groundwater, Nalgonda); **Kolanupaka** Jain temple; **Phanigiri** Buddhist site; **uranium** near Nallamala.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Fluorosis** is the classic **Nalgonda** groundwater problem (dental/skeletal). **Kolanupaka = Jain**, **Phanigiri = Buddhist** — keep the religion-site pairing exact. Nagarjuna Sagar is on the **Krishna**.

💡 **Example:** **Nalgonda fluorosis** (and Mission Bhagiratha as the surface-water remedy) is the belt's anchor health-geography PYQ; **Kolanupaka Jain temple** the heritage one.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Krishna basin; tributaries Musi, Dindi, Paleru.
- Nagarjuna Sagar (Krishna); SLBC/AMRP; Udayasamudram.
- Fluorosis (Nalgonda groundwater); uranium (Nallamala).
- Kolanupaka (Jain), Phanigiri (Buddhist), Pillalamarri, Bhongir fort.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which groundwater health problem is Nalgonda historically known for?* → **fluorosis** (dental & skeletal).
