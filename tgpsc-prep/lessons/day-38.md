## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Emergency Provisions (Art 352, 356, 360)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Part XVIII (`Art 352-360`) provides for **three kinds of emergency**.

**National Emergency (Art 352):** proclaimed on grounds of **war, external aggression, or armed rebellion** (the **44th Amendment, 1978** replaced "internal disturbance" with **"armed rebellion"** and required the **Cabinet's written advice**). Parliamentary approval is needed within **1 month** (special majority), and it continues **6 months at a time**.

| Effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Centre-State | Centre gets **executive/legislative power** over states |
| Fundamental Rights | `Art 19` **auto-suspended** (only on war/external aggression); **Art 20 & 21 NEVER suspended** (44th) |
| Lok Sabha term | May be **extended** by 1 year at a time |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — A **National Emergency has been declared three times**: `1962` (China war), `1971` (Pakistan war), and `1975` (the controversial "internal" emergency, Indira Gandhi). The **44th Amendment** ensured **Articles 20 and 21 can never be suspended**, even during emergency.

**President's Rule / State Emergency (Art 356):** on **failure of constitutional machinery** in a state (Governor's report or otherwise). Approval within **2 months**; maximum **3 years** (with conditions beyond 1 year). The **Bommai case (1994)** made it subject to **judicial review** and mandated a **floor test**.

**Financial Emergency (Art 360):** on a threat to **financial stability/credit** — approval within 2 months; it has **never been imposed**. Salaries can be reduced; money bills reserved.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Art 20 & 21 are never suspended** (post-44th). **Financial Emergency (Art 360) has never been used**. `Art 356` (President's Rule) is the most-used; **S.R. Bommai (1994)** curbed its misuse.

### 💡 Example
The **S.R. Bommai judgment (1994)** — requiring a **floor test** and allowing **judicial review** of Art 356 — is the landmark that curbed arbitrary dismissal of state governments.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Art 352** (National): war/external aggression/**armed rebellion**; declared **3 times**.
- **Art 20 & 21 never suspended** (44th Amendment).
- **Art 356** (President's Rule): Bommai case, floor test.
- **Art 360** (Financial): **never imposed**.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: The Telangana Armed Struggle (1946-51)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Telangana Armed Struggle (Telangana Rebellion), `1946–1951`** was a **peasant uprising** against the **feudal oppression** of the Nizam's **jagirdars and deshmukhs** — including **vetti (bonded/forced labour)** and illegal land seizures. It was led by the **Communist Party (CPI)** and the radical wing of the **Andhra Mahasabha**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The struggle is generally dated from **July `1946`**, sparked by the killing of **Doddi Komarayya** (the "**first martyr**") at **Kadavendi (Jangaon)**. It spread across **Nalgonda, Warangal, and Khammam**, where peasants formed **guerrilla squads (dalams)** and **village communes**, redistributed **~10 lakh acres** of land, and abolished **vetti**.

**Key figures:** **Ravi Narayana Reddy** (led the movement), **Puchalapalli Sundaraiah** (wrote *Telangana People's Struggle and Its Lessons*), **Baddam Yella Reddy, Chandra Rajeswara Rao, Arutla Ramachandra Reddy, Bhimreddy Narsimha Reddy**, and the woman icon **Chakali Ailamma** (who resisted a deshmukh at **Palakurthi**).

After **Operation Polo (1948)** integrated Hyderabad, the struggle continued against the Indian government until the **CPI withdrew it in `1951`**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Doddi Komarayya (1946, first martyr) → dalams & land redistribution → withdrawn 1951**". **Chakali Ailamma** (Palakurthi) is the woman symbol; **Sundaraiah** chronicled it.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The struggle began **before** Operation Polo (1946) and **continued after** it, ending in **1951**. **Chakali Ailamma's** resistance at Palakurthi is a landmark early episode. It targeted **vetti (forced labour)** and feudal landlords.

### 💡 Example
The **abolition of vetti** and the redistribution of land during the struggle became a template for later land-reform demands — linking the 1946-51 revolt to the modern Telangana movement's social-justice plank.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **1946-51**, led by **CPI** + radical **Andhra Mahasabha**.
- **Doddi Komarayya** — first martyr (Kadavendi, 1946).
- **Chakali Ailamma** — Palakurthi; **Ravi Narayana Reddy** led.
- Continued past **Operation Polo (1948)**, withdrawn **1951**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who is regarded as the first martyr of the Telangana Armed Struggle, and where?*
→ **Doddi Komarayya**, at **Kadavendi (Jangaon), 1946**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Partition of Bengal, Swadeshi & the Surat Split

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Partition of Bengal (1905):** announced by Viceroy **Lord Curzon**, it took effect on **`16 October 1905`**, splitting Bengal into a Hindu-majority west and a Muslim-majority **East Bengal & Assam** — widely seen as **"divide and rule."**

**Swadeshi & Boycott Movement (1905):** the anti-partition agitation grew into the **Swadeshi Movement** — **boycott of British goods**, promotion of Indian goods, **national education** (Bengal National College under **Aurobindo**), and swadeshi industries. **Tagore** composed songs (**"Amar Sonar Bangla"**) and started **Rakhi-bandhan** as a symbol of unity.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The Swadeshi era brought the **Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal)** — **Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak** ("**Swaraj is my birthright**"), and **Bipin Chandra Pal** — to the fore, alongside **Aurobindo Ghosh**. Their methods were **boycott, self-reliance, and passive resistance**.

**Surat Split (1907):** at the **Surat session**, the Congress **split** into **Moderates and Extremists** over leadership and methods. The two reunited only at the **Lucknow session (1916)**. The **Partition of Bengal was annulled in 1911** (and the capital shifted from **Calcutta to Delhi**). The **Muslim League** was founded in **1906 (Dhaka)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Partition 1905 → Swadeshi & Lal-Bal-Pal → Surat split 1907 → annulled 1911**". Tilak: "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Partition (1905, Curzon) was **annulled in 1911**; the **Surat split (1907)** divided Congress until **1916 (Lucknow reunion)**. **Lal-Bal-Pal** = Lajpat Rai, Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal (the Extremist trio).

### 💡 Example
The **Swadeshi movement's national education** effort (Bengal National College, with Aurobindo as principal) shows how the boycott spilled into **institution-building** — a constructive dimension beyond protest.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Partition of Bengal** — Curzon, effective **16 Oct 1905**; annulled **1911**.
- **Swadeshi/Boycott 1905**; **Lal-Bal-Pal** Extremists.
- **Surat Split 1907**; reunion **Lucknow 1916**.
- **Muslim League 1906** (Dhaka).

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*In which year and session did the Congress split into Moderates and Extremists, and when did they reunite?*
→ **Surat, 1907**; reunited at **Lucknow, 1916**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Medak-Sangareddy-Siddipet

![Telangana Regions Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Medak** region (erstwhile district now split into **Medak, Sangareddy, and Siddipet**) lies in the **north-west of the Hyderabad region**, drained by the **Manjira** (a Godavari tributary).

**Water & irrigation:** the **Singur dam** on the **Manjira** (Sangareddy) supplies drinking water to Hyderabad; **Kaleshwaram** reservoirs — **Mallanna Sagar, Kondapochamma Sagar, Ranganayaka Sagar** — lie in **Siddipet**.

**Heritage & institutions:**

| Site | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Medak Cathedral** | One of the **largest churches in Asia** (Gothic, built 1924) |
| **Medak Fort** | Kakatiya-era hill fort |
| **Komuravelli** (Siddipet) | **Mallanna temple** (Kurumas/shepherds' deity) |
| **ICRISAT** (Patancheru) | International crops research institute |
| **Pocharam** | Wildlife sanctuary & dam (Medak-Kamareddy) |

**Industry:** **Patancheru-Bollaram (Sangareddy)** is a major **pharma/industrial** belt (with pollution concerns); **Zaheerabad** hosts a **National Investment & Manufacturing Zone (NIMZ)**. **Siddipet** is known for **Gollabhama** handloom sarees.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Singur dam** (on the **Manjira**) is a key Hyderabad drinking-water source; **Siddipet** holds major **Kaleshwaram reservoirs** (Mallanna Sagar, Kondapochamma Sagar); **Medak Cathedral** is among **Asia's largest churches**; **ICRISAT** is at **Patancheru**. *(Verify latest capacities/figures.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Manjira (Singur) + Kaleshwaram reservoirs (Siddipet) + Medak Cathedral + ICRISAT**". Patancheru = pharma belt; Siddipet = Gollabhama sarees.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Singur** is on the **Manjira** (Sangareddy). The big **Kaleshwaram reservoirs** (Mallanna Sagar/Kondapochamma) are in **Siddipet**. **ICRISAT** and the **pharma belt** are at **Patancheru (Sangareddy)**. Medak Cathedral is a **church** (not a temple/fort).

### 💡 Example
The **Kaleshwaram reservoirs in Siddipet** (Mallanna Sagar etc.) transformed a drought-prone upland into an irrigated belt — a geography-plus-development point. *(Verify latest.)*

- **Singur dam** on **Manjira** (Sangareddy) — Hyderabad water.
- **Kaleshwaram reservoirs** (Mallanna Sagar, Kondapochamma) in **Siddipet**.
- **Medak Cathedral** — among Asia's largest churches; **ICRISAT** at Patancheru.
- **Siddipet** = Gollabhama sarees; **Zaheerabad** = NIMZ.
