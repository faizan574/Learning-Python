## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Governor, Chief Minister & State Legislature

### Deep Dive

**Governor (Art 153-162).** The constitutional head of a state (parallel to the President at the Union).
- **Appointment:** by the **President** (Art 155); holds office **during the President's pleasure** (Art 156); term 5 years. **Qualifications:** citizen, **35+**. One person may be **Governor of two or more states** (7th Amendment).
- **Powers:** executive (Art 154), legislative (summon/prorogue, assent/withhold/**reserve bills for the President**), and **discretionary** powers (Art 163) — e.g., reserving bills, appointing a CM in a hung house, **Art 356** report.

**Chief Minister & Council of Ministers (Art 163-164).** The **real executive** of the state.
- The **CM is appointed by the Governor** (leader of the Assembly majority); other ministers on the CM's advice.
- The **Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly** [Art 164]; a minister holds office during the Governor's pleasure. The **91st Amendment** caps the CoM at **≤ 15% of the Assembly** (min 12).

**State Legislature (Art 168-212).**
- **Unicameral** (Legislative Assembly only) or **bicameral** (Assembly + **Legislative Council/Vidhan Parishad**). **Telangana is bicameral** (Assembly + Council).
- **Legislative Council (Art 169):** can be **created or abolished** by Parliament on the state assembly's resolution (special majority). The Council is a **permanent** body (1/3 retire every 2 years); it has **limited power** (can only delay ordinary bills; no real say on money bills).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Governor: appointed by **President** (Art 155), **pleasure** tenure (Art 156); discretionary powers (Art 163). CM/CoM **collectively responsible to the Assembly** (Art 164); **91st Amdt** cap 15%. **Legislative Council** created/abolished under **Art 169**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Governor is **appointed (not elected)** and holds office at the **President's pleasure**. The CoM is responsible to the **Legislative Assembly** (lower house), not the Council. A Legislative **Council** can be **created/abolished** (unlike the Rajya Sabha).

💡 **Example:** A Governor **reserving a state bill for the President's assent** (Art 200/201) is a classic discretionary-power PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Governor: Art 153-162; appointed by President; discretionary (Art 163).
- CM/CoM: Art 163-164; responsible to Assembly; 91st Amdt 15% cap.
- Legislature: unicameral/bicameral; Telangana bicameral.
- Legislative Council: Art 169 (create/abolish); limited powers.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *To which house is the state Council of Ministers collectively responsible?* → the **Legislative Assembly**.

## 🗳️ Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: SIR (Special Intensive Revision) & Electoral-Roll Issues

### Deep Dive

**SIR = Special Intensive Revision** of electoral rolls by the **Election Commission of India (ECI)** — a drive to produce a **clean, accurate voter list** by **house-to-house (intensive) enumeration**, removing ineligible/duplicate/deceased entries and adding eligible new voters. *(Verify the latest SIR schedule/rounds and any state-specific status.)*

**Legal basis.** ECI's roll powers flow from **Article 324** (superintendence of elections), **Article 326** (adult suffrage — a citizen **≥ 18** years, ordinarily resident, not disqualified), and the **Representation of the People Act, 1950** with the **Registration of Electors Rules, 1960**. A revision can be **intensive** (fresh enumeration) or **summary** (update existing roll).

**Process & officials.** Draft roll → **claims and objections** window → disposal → final roll. **BLO** (Booth-Level Officer) verifies; **ERO** (Electoral Registration Officer) decides inclusions/deletions; appeals to **DEO/CEO**.

**The debate.** *In favour* — purges bogus/duplicate entries, improves accuracy. *Concerns* — risk of **wrongful deletion/disenfranchisement**, **documentation burden** on genuine voters, and **timing** close to elections; safeguards are adequate notice, the claims-objections process, and appeals.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — SIR = ECI's **intensive** roll revision (house-to-house); legal base **Art 324, 326 + RP Act 1950**; officials **BLO → ERO → DEO/CEO**; balance **roll purity vs wrongful deletion**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Exact **SIR schedule and figures change per cycle** — write **"verify latest official notification."** Universal adult franchise = **Art 326** (18 years, via the **61st Amendment, 1988**).

💡 **Example:** A voter who moved must file **Form 6** (new inclusion); failing to respond in the claims window risks deletion — the core disenfranchisement concern.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- SIR: ECI, intensive (house-to-house) revision.
- Legal base: Art 324, 326; RP Act 1950; Rules 1960.
- Officials: BLO → ERO → DEO/CEO; claims & objections.
- 18-year franchise: Art 326 (61st Amendment, 1988).

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Under which Article does the ECI have superintendence of elections?* → **Article 324**.

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Ikshvakus & Vishnukundins

### Deep Dive

**Ikshvakus (c. 3rd century CE).** Rose in the **Krishna valley** after the decline of the Satavahanas.
- **Capital: Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda)**; founder **Vasishthiputra Chamtamula (Santamula)**.
- Great **patrons of Buddhism** — the royal women (e.g., **Chamtisri**) funded the **mahachaityas/stupas at Nagarjunakonda** (a major Buddhist university-centre); the kings themselves performed **Vedic sacrifices (Ashvamedha)** (Brahmanical too).
- Nagarjunakonda's monuments were relocated when the Nagarjuna Sagar reservoir was built.

**Vishnukundins (c. 5th-6th century CE).** A major post-Ikshvaku dynasty ruling parts of **Telangana and coastal Andhra**.
- Capitals associated with **Indrapalanagara (Nalgonda region), Denduluru, Amaravati**.
- Notable rulers: **Govindavarman** and **Madhavavarman** (performed many Vedic sacrifices, **Ashvamedha**).
- Promoted **Brahmanism/Hindu revival** while also patronising **Buddhist cave architecture** (e.g., around the Krishna-Godavari belt). They bridge the gap between the Satavahana/Ikshvaku age and the Badami Chalukyas.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Ikshvakus**: capital **Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda)**; founder **Chamtamula**; Buddhist patronage (**Chamtisri**, Nagarjunakonda stupas). **Vishnukundins** (5th-6th c.): **Madhavavarman**, Vedic sacrifices, Hindu revival; capital **Indrapalanagara/Denduluru**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Ikshvakus = Nagarjunakonda (Buddhist mahachaityas)**; **Vishnukundins = Hindu revival (Madhavavarman, Ashvamedha)**. Don't confuse them with the later **Badami Chalukyas** or the Kakatiyas.

💡 **Example:** **Nagarjunakonda** (Ikshvaku Buddhist centre, relocated for Nagarjuna Sagar) is the classic Ikshvaku PYQ; **Madhavavarman's Vedic sacrifices** the Vishnukundin one.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Ikshvakus: Vijayapuri/Nagarjunakonda; Chamtamula; Buddhist (Chamtisri).
- Vishnukundins: 5th-6th c.; Madhavavarman, Govindavarman; Hindu revival.
- Ikshvakus Buddhist patrons; Vishnukundins Vedic sacrifices.
- Sequence: Satavahana → Ikshvaku → Vishnukundin → Badami Chalukya.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *What was the Ikshvaku capital?* → **Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda)**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Simon Commission, Nehru Report & the Lahore Session

### Deep Dive

**Simon Commission (1927-28).** A statutory commission to review the 1919 reforms — but **all-British (no Indian member)**. It was met with **"Simon Go Back"** protests nationwide; at the **Lahore** demonstration (1928), **Lala Lajpat Rai** was **injured in a lathi charge** and later **died** — galvanising the revolutionaries.

**Nehru Report (1928).** Indian leaders were challenged to produce an agreed constitution; an all-parties committee under **Motilal Nehru** drafted the **Nehru Report** — the **first major Indian attempt at a constitution**. It demanded **Dominion Status**, **rejected separate electorates** (favouring joint electorates with reserved seats), and listed **fundamental rights**. **Jinnah's "Fourteen Points" (1929)** were a response after the report was not accepted by the League; the younger leaders (Nehru, Bose) wanted **full independence**, not dominion status.

**Lahore Session (December 1929).** Under President **Jawaharlal Nehru**, the Congress adopted the **Purna Swaraj (complete independence) resolution**; it resolved to observe **26 January 1930 as the first Independence Day**, and authorised the launch of **Civil Disobedience**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Simon Commission (1927, all-British)** → "Simon Go Back", **Lajpat Rai** injured (Lahore, died). **Nehru Report (1928, Motilal Nehru)** — dominion status, **no separate electorates**. **Lahore Session (1929, Nehru)** — **Purna Swaraj**; 26 Jan 1930 Independence Day.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Simon Commission had no Indian member** (the grievance). The **Nehru Report (1928)** was by **Motilal Nehru** (not Jawaharlal). **Purna Swaraj** was declared at **Lahore (1929)**, and **26 Jan 1930** was the first Independence Day (later chosen as Republic Day).

💡 **Example:** The chain **Simon (1927) → Lajpat Rai's death → Nehru Report (1928) → Lahore Purna Swaraj (1929) → Civil Disobedience (1930)** is a very common sequence PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Simon Commission 1927: all-British; "Simon Go Back"; Lajpat Rai (Lahore).
- Nehru Report 1928: Motilal Nehru; dominion status; no separate electorates.
- Jinnah's 14 Points (1929).
- Lahore Session 1929 (J. Nehru): Purna Swaraj; 26 Jan 1930.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Medak-Sangareddy-Siddipet

### Deep Dive

The **Medak-Sangareddy-Siddipet** belt lies in **central/north-central Telangana**, near Hyderabad (*verify current district boundaries*).

**Rivers & irrigation.** The **Manjira** (a Godavari tributary) is the key river; **Singur Dam** (on the Manjira, Medak/Sangareddy — a Hyderabad drinking-water source) and **Nizam Sagar** (downstream, Manjira) are major reservoirs. The **Kaleshwaram** scheme created large reservoirs here — **Mallanna Sagar** and **Kondapochamma Sagar** (Siddipet) — key storage points.

**Industry.** **Sangareddy–Patancheru** is a **pharmaceutical/industrial** hub (historically with pollution concerns); **BHEL (Ramachandrapuram)** and **ICRISAT (Patancheru)** are nearby landmarks. **Siddipet** is known for **Gollabhama sarees**.

**Heritage & tourism.** **Medak Cathedral** — a **Gothic church, one of the largest in Asia/India** (built by Wesleyan missionaries); **Medak Fort**; **Pocharam Wildlife Sanctuary & lake**; **Edupayala** (Medak, Durga temple on the Manjira); **Komuravelli Mallanna** temple (Siddipet); **Kondapochamma** (Siddipet).

![Telangana regions base map — locate the Medak-Sangareddy-Siddipet belt on the Manjira near Hyderabad](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Belt on the **Manjira**; **Singur Dam** (Hyderabad water) & **Nizam Sagar**; Kaleshwaram reservoirs **Mallanna Sagar & Kondapochamma Sagar** (Siddipet); **Medak Cathedral** (Gothic, among Asia's largest); **Patancheru/BHEL/ICRISAT** (Sangareddy).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Singur & Nizam Sagar are on the Manjira**. **Mallanna Sagar/Kondapochamma Sagar** are **Kaleshwaram (Godavari-fed) reservoirs** in Siddipet — don't attribute them to the Manjira. **Medak Cathedral** is a **church**, not a temple.

💡 **Example:** **Medak Cathedral** (one of the largest churches in Asia) and **Singur Dam** (Hyderabad drinking water) are the belt's anchor heritage and water PYQs.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- River Manjira; Singur Dam (Hyderabad water), Nizam Sagar.
- Kaleshwaram reservoirs: Mallanna Sagar, Kondapochamma Sagar (Siddipet).
- Industry: Patancheru (pharma), BHEL, ICRISAT (Sangareddy).
- Medak Cathedral (Gothic); Siddipet Gollabhama sarees.
