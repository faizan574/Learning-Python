## 🏛️ Polity: Local Government — 73rd & 74th Amendments, Telangana Panchayat Raj Act

The **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts, 1992** (effective 1993) gave constitutional status to local self-government — the "third tier" of Indian federalism — realising the DPSP in `Art 40`.

**73rd Amendment — Panchayati Raj (rural).** Added **Part IX (`Art 243` to `243-O`)** and the **Eleventh Schedule** listing **29 subjects** for devolution. Key features:
- **Three-tier system** (village, intermediate, district) — the intermediate tier is optional for states with population under 20 lakh.
- **Gram Sabha** (`Art 243A`) as the foundational body of the village.
- **Compulsory elections** every **5 years**; if dissolved early, fresh polls within **6 months**.
- **Reservations**: SC/ST in proportion to population, and **one-third (minimum) for women** — Telangana has raised women's reservation to **50%**.
- **State Election Commission** (`Art 243K`) conducts the elections.
- **State Finance Commission** (`Art 243-I`) every 5 years to review panchayat finances.

**74th Amendment — Municipalities (urban).** Added **Part IXA (`Art 243P` to `243ZG`)** and the **Twelfth Schedule** with **18 subjects**. Three types of urban bodies: **Nagar Panchayat** (transitional area), **Municipal Council** (smaller town), **Municipal Corporation** (larger urban area). It created **Ward Committees**, the **District Planning Committee (DPC, `Art 243ZD`)** and the **Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC, `Art 243ZE`)**.

**Telangana Panchayat Raj Act, 2018** replaced the earlier 1994 Act — streamlining the three-tier structure for the state's reorganised districts, with strict provisions on gram-panchayat functioning, a focus on village development (e.g., Palle Pragathi), and **50% reservation for women**.

| Feature | 73rd (Rural) | 74th (Urban) |
|---|---|---|
| Part / Articles | IX / `243–243O` | IXA / `243P–243ZG` |
| Schedule | 11th (29 subjects) | 12th (18 subjects) |
| Bodies | Gram/Mandal/Zilla | Nagar Panchayat / Council / Corporation |
| Planning body | — | DPC & MPC |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **73rd = Part IX + 11th Schedule (29 subjects)**; **74th = Part IXA + 12th Schedule (18 subjects)**. Both mandate 5-year terms, SEC-run elections, and ≥1/3 women's reservation (Telangana: 50%).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**7-3 → Three-tier Rural, 11th, 29**; **7-4 → urban, 12th, 18**." (Rural first, lower schedule number, more subjects.)

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **29 subjects (11th Schedule)** go with the **73rd** (panchayats); **18 subjects (12th Schedule)** with the **74th** (municipalities). Swapping the schedule numbers/subject counts is the classic error. Also: women's reservation is a **minimum of one-third** nationally, not a ceiling.

💡 **Example:** A dispute over an early-dissolved gram panchayat must go to fresh elections within **6 months** — a constitutional guarantee under `Art 243E`.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Art 243-I = State Finance Commission; Art 243K = State Election Commission.
- DPC (243ZD) consolidates rural + urban district plans.
- Telangana PR Act 2018; women's reservation 50%.
- 11th Schedule inserted by 73rd; 12th by 74th.

## 🏗️ Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Budgets & Economic Survey Highlights

Telangana's annual **Budget** and the **Socio-Economic Outlook / Economic Survey** are recurring sources of current-affairs data. The discipline here: know the **structure and trends**, and treat every rupee figure as **"verify latest official figure"** since the numbers change each year.

**Budget basics.** The state presents an annual budget (a full budget, or a vote-on-account in an election year). It splits into **Revenue** and **Capital** accounts, with the **fiscal deficit** targeted around the FRBM limit (as a % of GSDP — **verify latest**) and the **revenue deficit/surplus** watched closely. Major expenditure heads are the **Six Guarantees / welfare schemes**, irrigation, agriculture (loan waiver, Rythu Bharosa), power subsidy, and education/health.

**Economy profile (structural facts, stable):**
- Telangana's economy is **services-led** — the services sector (IT, finance, real estate) contributes the largest share of GSVA, followed by industry, then agriculture.
- **Hyderabad** is the growth engine — IT/ITeS exports, pharma ("Genome Valley", Hyderabad Pharma City), and life sciences.
- Telangana's **per-capita income** is among the **highest** of Indian states (**verify latest figure and ranking**).

**Economic Survey highlights to track (verify each year's numbers):**
- **GSDP** size and real growth rate.
- **Per-capita income** vs national average.
- Sectoral growth (agriculture rebound from irrigation, services momentum).
- IT exports from Hyderabad; number of jobs.
- Social-sector spending share.

## 🏺 Telangana History: Kakatiyas — Administration, Economy, Society & Architecture

Beyond their political history, the **Kakatiyas (c. 1000–1323)** built an administrative and cultural order that defines Telangana's heritage.

**Administration — the Nayankara system.** The Kakatiyas divided the realm into provinces/territorial units and assigned land to **Nayakas** (military chiefs) in return for maintaining troops and remitting revenue — an early **feudal-military land-grant system** that later influenced Vijayanagara. The king was the apex; **Angaraksha/Padava** and a council assisted. This decentralised structure also carried the seeds of later fragmentation (the Velama/Nayaka chiefs).

**Economy — tank irrigation.** The Kakatiyas were master **tank (cheruvu) builders**, creating a chained network of reservoirs in the semi-arid Deccan — notably **Pakala Lake, Laknavaram Lake, and Ramappa Lake**. These "chain tanks" transformed agriculture and remain in use. Trade flourished through the **Motupalli port** (Ganapati Deva's safe-conduct charter); textiles and diamonds (Golconda region) were exported.

**Society & religion.** Society was broadly structured but relatively open — the dynasty itself rose from non-royal origins. They patronised **Shaivism** (Swayambhu/Shiva) while supporting Jainism and other faiths. **Telugu language and literature** flourished (e.g., **Palkuriki Somanatha**, **Tikkana** under feudatories).

**Architecture — the Kakatiya style.** A distinctive fusion marked by **star-shaped platforms, intricate carving, and floating "light" bricks**:
- **Ramappa Temple (Rudreshwara), Palampet (1213)** — built by **Recharla Rudra** under **Ganapati Deva**; inscribed as a **UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021**. Famous for **floating bricks** in the shikhara and Nandi.
- **Thousand Pillar Temple, Hanamkonda** — trikutalaya (three shrines), by Rudradeva (1163).
- **Warangal Fort** and the **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam (Warangal Gates)** — the ornamental gateways that form the **state emblem of Telangana**.
- **Perini Shivatandavam**, a vigorous Shaiva war-dance, was a Kakatiya-era tradition (revived by Nataraja Ramakrishna).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Ramappa Temple (Palampet)** = UNESCO World Heritage Site **2021**, built **1213** by Recharla Rudra under Ganapati Deva. The **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam** (Warangal gateway) is Telangana's **state emblem**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Tanks (Pakala/Laknavaram/Ramappa) + Temples (Ramappa/Thousand Pillar) + Thoranam (emblem) + Nayankara (land grants).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Ramappa Temple** is the **only temple in India named after its sculptor (Ramappa)**; it was built by **Recharla Rudra** (general), not the king directly. UNESCO inscription year is **2021**, construction **1213** — don't conflate them.

💡 **Example:** The "floating bricks" of Ramappa's shikhara (low-density, porous bricks) reduced the superstructure's weight — a notable engineering feat cited in its UNESCO recognition.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Chain tanks: Pakala, Laknavaram, Ramappa (Kakatiya irrigation).
- Ramappa UNESCO 2021; Warangal Kala Thoranam = state emblem.
- Perini Shivatandavam dance; Shaivism patronage.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Government of India Act 1935 & Provincial Ministries

The **Government of India Act, 1935** was the **longest Act ever passed by the British Parliament** and the most important constitutional document before independence — much of our present Constitution's structure derives from it.

**Background.** It grew out of the **Simon Commission report (1930)**, the **Round Table Conferences (1930–32)**, and the **White Paper (1933)**. Burma was separated from India (effective 1937).

**Key provisions:**
- **All-India Federation** proposed — uniting British provinces and princely states. It required accession by princely states and **never came into being** (the princes did not join).
- **Provincial Autonomy** replaced **dyarchy at the provincial level** — provinces became autonomous units with responsible government (ministers accountable to legislatures), though the **Governor retained discretionary/special powers**.
- **Dyarchy introduced at the Centre** (federal subjects split into reserved and transferred) — but this part was **never implemented** because the Federation didn't form.
- **Bicameral legislatures** in some provinces (e.g., Bengal, Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar, Assam).
- **Three lists**: **Federal, Provincial, Concurrent** — the direct ancestor of our **Seventh Schedule**.
- **Federal Court** established (**1937**) — forerunner of the Supreme Court.
- **Reserve Bank of India** provided for; franchise extended (~10% of population); **separate electorates extended** (to depressed classes, women, labour).
- Abolished the **Council of India**.

**Provincial elections & ministries (1937).** Elections under the Act were held in early 1937. The **Congress won majorities and formed ministries in 8 of 11 provinces** (later governing in a couple more). These Congress ministries **resigned in late 1939** in protest at India being declared a belligerent in **WWII** without consultation — the Muslim League observed this as **"Deliverance Day"**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — GoI Act 1935: **provincial autonomy** (dyarchy abolished in provinces, introduced at Centre but never enforced); **Federation never formed**; **Federal Court 1937**; basis of the **three lists / 7th Schedule**. Congress formed ministries in **8 provinces (1937)**, resigned **1939**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1935 = Provinces freed (autonomy), Centre's dyarchy stillborn, Federation never born, Court born (1937).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Dyarchy was **abolished in provinces** and **introduced at the Centre** by the 1935 Act — the **reverse** of the 1919 Act (which had dyarchy in provinces). The federal part never operated; only the **provincial** part did.

💡 **Example:** Our **Emergency provisions, federal scheme, office of Governor, and public service commissions** all trace structural roots to the 1935 Act.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- 1919 Act = dyarchy in provinces; 1935 Act = autonomy in provinces, dyarchy at Centre (unimplemented).
- Federal Court 1937; RBI; three lists.
- 1937 elections → Congress ministries in 8 provinces; resigned Oct–Nov 1939.
- Burma separated; Sind & Orissa created as provinces.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Telangana at a Glance — Location, Extent & Physiography

![Telangana regions map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

A top-down overview of the state — the foundation for every regional question.

**Formation & status.** Telangana became India's **29th state on 2 June 2014**, carved out of Andhra Pradesh under the **Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014**. **Hyderabad** is the capital.

**Location & extent.** Telangana lies on the **Deccan Plateau** in south-central India, roughly between **latitudes ~15°50′–19°55′ N** and **longitudes ~77°–81°20′ E** (**verify precise figures**). Its area is about **112,077 sq km** (~3.4% of India; roughly the **11th-largest** state — **verify ranking**). It is a **landlocked** state.

**Boundaries:** **Maharashtra** to the north/northwest, **Chhattisgarh** to the northeast, **Karnataka** to the west, and **Andhra Pradesh** to the south and east.

**Administrative divisions:** reorganised from the original **10 districts (2014)** to **31 (2016)** and currently **33 districts** (**verify latest count**), grouped for administration into divisions, mandals and gram panchayats.

**Physiography.** Telangana is almost entirely part of the **Deccan Plateau**, built mainly on ancient **Archaean granite-gneiss** (with some Gondwana and Deccan-trap patches in the north). The land generally **slopes from northwest to southeast**, draining toward the **Godavari** (north) and **Krishna** (south) river basins. Relief features include residual hills and the **Nallamala** ranges in the south. Elevation ranges broadly from ~100 m to over 900 m; **Doli Gutta** (near the Chhattisgarh border) is often cited as the **highest point** (**verify elevation**).
