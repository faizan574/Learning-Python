## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity (✓✓ Revision)
### 🎯 Revision: Amendment (Art 368) & Basic Structure

Continuing the **Polity revision**, today reconsolidates the **amendment process and Basic Structure doctrine** (originally Day 9).

### 📖 Concept

**Three amendment methods (Art 368):**

| Method | Majority | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Simple majority (outside 368) | ordinary | new states, citizenship |
| **Special majority** (368) | majority of total membership + 2/3 present & voting | FRs, DPSP, most |
| Special majority + **state ratification** | + ≥ half the states | federal features, Art 368 itself |

**Basic Structure journey:**
- **Shankari Prasad (1951) / Sajjan Singh (1965):** FRs amendable.
- **Golaknath (1967):** FRs NOT amendable.
- **24th Amendment (1971):** Parliament can amend any part.
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973):** can amend any part **but not the basic structure** (13-judge bench).
- **Minerva Mills (1980):** judicial review & FR-DPSP balance = basic features.

**Major amendments (recall):**
```
42nd (1976) = Mini-Constitution (Socialist/Secular/Integrity, FDs)
44th (1978) = undid 42nd; Property → 300A; "armed rebellion"
73rd/74th (1992) = Panchayats/Municipalities
86th (2002) = RTE (21A)
101st (2016) = GST ; 103rd (2019) = 10% EWS
```

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Art 368** = amendment power; **three methods** (simple / special / special+state ratification). **Basic Structure doctrine = Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** — amend anything except the basic structure. **42nd = Mini-Constitution; 44th = restorative.**

💡 **Example:** The **Kesavananda (1973)** verdict lets Parliament amend freely yet bars destroying the Constitution's "identity" (basic structure) — the balance that has governed every amendment since.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Basic Structure = Kesavananda (1973)**; Minerva Mills reinforced it.
- **State ratification** needed for **federal** provisions (half the states).
- **42/44/73/74/86/101/103** — know each by nickname/content.
- **Preamble is amendable** (42nd did) within basic-structure limits.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**368 = 3 methods; Kesavananda (1973) = basic structure. 42 broke it, 44 fixed it, 73-74 grassroots, 86 school, 101 GST, 103 EWS.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **States cannot initiate amendments** (only Parliament). **No joint sitting** for amendment bills. **Basic Structure came from Kesavananda (1973)** — not any written article.

**✅ Quick self-check:** Which case gave the Basic Structure doctrine, and which amendment is the "Mini-Constitution"?
*(Kesavananda Bharati, 1973; the 42nd Amendment, 1976.)*

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Telangana Temples, Monuments & Tourism Heritage

Telangana's **temples and monuments** span Chalukya, Kakatiya, Qutb Shahi and Asaf Jahi eras — a very high-yield **culture/tourism** topic.

### 📖 Concept

| Monument / Temple | Place | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Ramappa (Rudreshwara)** | Palampet, Mulugu | Kakatiya, 1213; **UNESCO 2021**; floating bricks |
| **Thousand Pillar Temple** | Hanamkonda | Kakatiya, 1163 (Rudradeva); trikutalaya |
| **Warangal Fort / Kirti Toranas** | Warangal | Kakatiya; **Kala Thoranam = state emblem** |
| **Bhadrachalam** | Bhadradri Kothagudem | Sita Ramachandra temple (Ramadasu); on Godavari |
| **Yadadri (Yadagirigutta)** | Yadadri Bhongir | Lakshmi Narasimha; rebuilt grandly |
| **Vemulawada** | Rajanna Sircilla | Raja Rajeswara temple ("Dakshina Kashi") |
| **Basara** | Nirmal | Gnana Saraswati temple (rare Saraswati temple) |
| **Alampur** | Jogulamba Gadwal | Nava Brahma temples (Badami Chalukya); Jogulamba Shakti Peetha |
| **Charminar** | Hyderabad | Qutb Shahi, 1591 (Md Quli Qutb Shah) |
| **Golconda Fort & Qutb Shahi Tombs** | Hyderabad | Qutb Shahi capital & royal necropolis |
| **Kuntala / Bogatha Falls** | Adilabad / Mulugu | Waterfalls (tourism) |

**UNESCO & emblem highlights:**
- **Ramappa Temple** — Telangana's **only UNESCO World Heritage Site** (inscribed **2021**).
- **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam** (Warangal Fort gateway) — the **Telangana state emblem** motif.

**Dynasty-architecture matching:**
- **Badami Chalukya:** Alampur Nava Brahma temples (7th c.).
- **Kakatiya:** Ramappa, Thousand Pillar, Warangal Fort (12-13th c.).
- **Qutb Shahi:** Charminar, Golconda, Qutb Shahi Tombs, Mecca Masjid (16-17th c.).
- **Asaf Jahi:** Chowmahalla, Falaknuma, Osmania University, High Court.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Ramappa (Palampet, 1213) = UNESCO 2021 (only one in TG)**; **Thousand Pillar (Hanamkonda, 1163)**; **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam = state emblem**; **Charminar/Golconda = Qutb Shahi**; **Alampur Nava Brahma = Badami Chalukya + Jogulamba Peetha**; **Basara = Saraswati temple**.

💡 **Example:** "Telangana's only UNESCO World Heritage Site, a Kakatiya temple with floating bricks" → **Ramappa (Palampet), 2021**. "Temple whose gateway is the state emblem" → **Warangal Fort's Kakatiya Kala Thoranam**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Ramappa = UNESCO 2021**; **Thousand Pillar = 1163 Hanamkonda**.
- **Kala Thoranam (Warangal) = state emblem**.
- **Alampur = Badami Chalukya + Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**.
- **Charminar (1591)/Golconda = Qutb Shahi**; **Basara = Saraswati**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Kakatiya: Ramappa (UNESCO), Thousand Pillar, Kala Thoranam (emblem). Chalukya: Alampur. Qutb Shahi: Charminar/Golconda. Saraswati: Basara. Rama: Bhadrachalam.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Ramappa (1213)** is the **UNESCO** site (not Thousand Pillar, 1163). **Alampur Nava Brahma = Badami Chalukya** (not Kakatiya). **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha = Alampur**; **Basara = rare Saraswati temple**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement (✓✓ Revision)
### 🎯 Revision: Gandhi's Arrival & Early Satyagrahas

Continuing the **INM revision**, today reconsolidates **Gandhi's early satyagrahas** (originally Day 7).

### 📖 Concept

**Background.** Gandhi returned from **South Africa (1915)**, where he developed **Satyagraha** (truth-force, non-violent resistance) during struggles against racial laws. **Gopal Krishna Gokhale** was his political mentor.

**Three early satyagrahas (1917-18):**

| Satyagraha | Year | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Champaran (Bihar)** | 1917 | **Indigo** planters' **tinkathia** system — Gandhi's **first satyagraha in India** |
| **Kheda (Gujarat)** | 1918 | Peasants' demand for **tax remission** after crop failure/plague |
| **Ahmedabad Mill Strike** | 1918 | **Textile workers'** wage dispute — Gandhi's first **hunger strike** |

- **Champaran (1917):** against the forced **indigo cultivation** (tinkathia — 3/20 of land); a **success** — the first application of satyagraha on Indian soil; associated with **Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad**.
- **Kheda (1918):** peasants couldn't pay land revenue; Gandhi + **Vallabhbhai Patel** led the no-tax campaign — partial success.
- **Ahmedabad (1918):** millworkers vs owners; Gandhi undertook his **first fast (hunger strike)** — arbitration gave workers a raise.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Gandhi returned in **1915** (mentor **Gokhale**); **Champaran (1917) = first satyagraha in India (indigo/tinkathia)**; **Kheda (1918) = peasant tax remission (with Patel)**; **Ahmedabad (1918) = mill strike, Gandhi's first hunger strike**.

💡 **Example:** **Champaran (1917)** was the **first satyagraha on Indian soil** — against the **tinkathia** indigo system — establishing Gandhi's method nationally and launching his Indian political career.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Gandhi returns 1915; Gokhale = mentor**.
- **Champaran 1917 = indigo/tinkathia (first satyagraha in India)**.
- **Kheda 1918 = tax remission (Patel)**.
- **Ahmedabad 1918 = mill strike (first hunger strike)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1915 return → Champaran (1917, indigo, first) → Kheda (1918, tax, Patel) → Ahmedabad (1918, mill strike, first fast).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Champaran (1917)** was the **first satyagraha in India** (indigo). **Kheda & Ahmedabad** were both **1918**. Ahmedabad involved Gandhi's **first hunger strike**. Patel rose to prominence in **Kheda**.

**✅ Quick self-check:** Which was Gandhi's first satyagraha in India and its cause, and where did he first use a hunger strike?
*(Champaran, 1917 — against the indigo tinkathia system; Ahmedabad mill strike, 1918.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Forests.** Telangana's forests are mainly **Southern Tropical Dry Deciduous** (teak, bamboo, terminalia); densest in the **north (Adilabad/Kumram Bheem)** and **east (Bhadradri/Mulugu)**. Forest cover ~**24% of area** (`verify latest ISFR figure`).

**Tiger reserves (2):**

| Tiger Reserve | Location | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Amrabad Tiger Reserve** | Nagarkurnool (Nallamala) | One of India's largest; Chenchu tribe; uranium controversy |
| **Kawal Tiger Reserve** | Adilabad (Kumram Bheem) | Northern teak forests; Godavari-Pranhita catchment |

**Major wildlife sanctuaries:**
- **Pakhal, Eturnagaram** (Warangal/Mulugu), **Kinnerasani** (Khammam), **Pranahita** (Adilabad/Kumram Bheem), **Kawal** (also WLS), **Manjira** (crocodiles, Sangareddy), **Pocharam** (Medak), **Shivaram, Sivaram** etc.
- **Manjira Wildlife Sanctuary** — famous for **muggers/crocodiles** and birds.
- **National Parks:** **Kasu Brahmananda Reddy (KBR), Mrugavani, Mahavir Harina Vanasthali** — all in/around Hyderabad.

**Conservation programmes:** **Haritha Haram** (afforestation), tiger conservation, urban lakes/biodiversity.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana has **2 tiger reserves: Amrabad (Nagarkurnool/Nallamala) & Kawal (Adilabad)**; forests are **dry deciduous (teak)**; **3 national parks around Hyderabad (KBR, Mrugavani, Mahavir Harina Vanasthali)**; **Manjira WLS = crocodiles**.

💡 **Example:** **Amrabad Tiger Reserve** (Nallamala) is both a tiger habitat and the site of the contested **uranium** proposal (Chenchu tribe, Krishna catchment) — linking wildlife to environmental issues (Day 20).

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- **National parks (3): KBR, Mrugavani, Mahavir Harina Vanasthali** (Hyderabad area).
- **Manjira WLS = crocodiles**; **Eturnagaram/Pakhal/Kinnerasani** sanctuaries.
- Forests = **dry deciduous (teak)**; **Haritha Haram** = afforestation.
