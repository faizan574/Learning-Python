## 🏛️ Polity: Citizenship (Articles 5–11, Citizenship Act, CAA)

**Part II (`Art 5–11`)** deals with citizenship at the Constitution's commencement; the detailed law is left to Parliament (the **Citizenship Act, 1955**). India has **single citizenship** (one national citizenship, unlike the dual citizenship of the USA).

**Articles 5–11.**
- **`Art 5`** — citizenship at commencement (domicile + born in India / parent born / ordinarily resident 5 years).
- **`Art 6` & `Art 7`** — rights of persons who **migrated to/from Pakistan**.
- **`Art 8`** — citizenship of **persons of Indian origin residing abroad**.
- **`Art 9`** — voluntarily acquiring **foreign citizenship → loss** of Indian citizenship.
- **`Art 10`** — continuance of citizenship rights.
- **`Art 11`** — **Parliament's power** to regulate citizenship by law (basis of the 1955 Act).

**Citizenship Act, 1955.** Provides **five ways to acquire** citizenship — **by birth, by descent, by registration, by naturalisation, and by incorporation of territory** — and ways to **lose** it (renunciation, termination, deprivation). It also governs **OCI (Overseas Citizen of India)** cards (not full citizenship, no voting rights).

**Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA), 2019.** Amended the 1955 Act to grant **eligibility for citizenship to non-Muslim minorities** (**Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, Christian**) who came from **Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan on or before 31 December 2014**, and reduced the **naturalisation residency requirement** for them (to about 5–6 years). It was controversial and widely debated.

| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| `Art 5` | citizenship at commencement |
| `Art 6/7` | migration to/from Pakistan |
| `Art 9` | foreign citizenship → loss |
| `Art 11` | Parliament regulates citizenship |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — India has **single citizenship**. `Art 5–11` cover commencement; the **Citizenship Act 1955** gives five modes (birth, descent, registration, naturalisation, incorporation). **CAA 2019** covers 6 non-Muslim communities from Pakistan/Bangladesh/Afghanistan who arrived by **31 Dec 2014**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — Modes: "**Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation (B-D-R-N-I).**" CAA cut-off = **31 Dec 2014**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — India has **single** citizenship (not dual). The CAA's cut-off is **31 December 2014** and it names **six** communities (Muslims not included) from **three** countries. `Art 11` (not the Constitution itself) empowers Parliament to make citizenship law.

💡 **Example:** An OCI cardholder (e.g., a person of Indian origin abroad) enjoys travel/work rights but **cannot vote or hold constitutional office** — OCI is not full citizenship.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Part II, Art 5–11; single citizenship.
- Citizenship Act 1955: 5 modes; OCI card.
- CAA 2019: 6 communities, 3 countries, cut-off 31 Dec 2014.
- Art 9: voluntary foreign citizenship → loss.

## 🏗️ Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Gruha Jyothi & Indiramma Indlu

Two more of the Six Guarantees: **free power** and **housing**. (Treat all amounts as **"verify latest official figure."**)

**Gruha Jyothi.** Provides **200 units of free electricity per month** to eligible households (domestic consumers meeting the criteria). It directly cuts household power bills and is one of the most widely availed guarantees. (Eligibility/consumption conditions apply — **verify latest**.)

**Indiramma Indlu.** The **housing guarantee** — construction of a **new house** (with financial assistance announced around **₹5 lakh per house** — **verify**) for eligible **houseless poor** families, plus house-site plots in some cases. It is the successor to the earlier **Double-Bedroom (2BHK) "Dignity Housing"** scheme, restructured to allow beneficiaries to build on their own plots with staged assistance. (Targets/number of houses — **verify latest official figure**.)

## 🏺 Telangana History: The Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–51)

The **Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–1951)** was a **communist-led peasant armed rebellion** against the Nizam's feudal order — one of the largest peasant uprisings in modern India.

**Causes.** The grievances were against the **feudal exploitation** of the **deshmukhs/jagirdars/doras** (landlords): forced labour (**vetti**), illegal exactions (**bhagela/bonded labour**), land-grabbing, and the Nizam's autocratic rule. The **Andhra Mahasabha's communist wing** and the **Communist Party** organised the peasants.

**Course.** The spark is traced to **Kadavendi village (Jangaon, Nalgonda)** where **Doddi Komarayya was martyred (July 1946)** resisting a landlord — igniting the movement. It spread across **Nalgonda, Warangal and Khammam**. Peasants formed **"gram rajyams" (village soviets/sanghams)**, abolished vetti, and **redistributed land** (an estimated **~10 lakh acres** seized/redistributed — **verify figure**) through guerrilla squads (**dalams**).

**Leaders.** **Ravi Narayan Reddy, Baddam Yella Reddy, Chandra Rajeswara Rao, Puchalapalli Sundarayya, Arutla Ramachandra Reddy & Arutla Kamala Devi, Mallu Swarajyam, Bhimreddy Narasimha Reddy** were prominent. Women participated in large numbers.

**End.** After the **1948 Police Action (Operation Polo)** integrated Hyderabad, the Indian Army also moved against the communists; the **CPI formally withdrew the armed struggle in 1951**. The movement forced major **land reforms** (abolition of jagirs, tenancy protections) and left a deep radical legacy in Telangana politics.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana Armed Struggle **1946–51**, communist/Andhra-Mahasabha-led against **vetti** and feudal landlords; sparked at **Kadavendi (Doddi Komarayya martyred, July 1946)**; formed **gram rajyams**, redistributed land; **withdrawn by the CPI in 1951** after Operation Polo.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1946 Kadavendi (Komarayya) → gram rajyams + land redistribution → 1948 Police Action → 1951 CPI withdraws.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The struggle (1946–51) targeted both the **Nizam's feudal landlords** and, after 1948, confronted the **Indian state**; it was **withdrawn in 1951** (not 1948). The spark was at **Kadavendi, Nalgonda (Doddi Komarayya)**.

💡 **Example:** The abolition of **vetti (forced labour)** and large-scale land redistribution during the struggle pressured post-1948 governments into **jagir abolition and tenancy reforms** in Hyderabad State.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- 1946–51; communist/Andhra Mahasabha-led; against vetti/feudalism.
- Kadavendi (Nalgonda) spark; Doddi Komarayya martyr (1946).
- Leaders: Ravi Narayan Reddy, Sundarayya, Mallu Swarajyam, etc.
- Withdrawn 1951; led to land reforms.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: INC Formation & the Moderate Era (1885–1905)

The **Indian National Congress (INC)** was founded in **1885**, inaugurating organised, all-India nationalism; its first phase (to 1905) was led by the **Moderates**.

**Formation (1885).** The INC was founded at **Bombay** (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College) in **December 1885**, largely through the efforts of **Allan Octavian Hume** (a retired British civil servant, its first General Secretary). Its **first president was Womesh Chandra (W.C.) Bonnerjee**, with **72 delegates** at the first session. **Badruddin Tyabji** became its **first Muslim president (1887)**.

**The "safety valve" theory** holds Hume intended the Congress as an outlet for discontent; nationalist historians stress Indian initiative instead.

**The Moderates (1885–1905).** Leaders included **Dadabhai Naoroji** (the "Grand Old Man of India," author of the **"Drain of Wealth" theory** in *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*), **Gopal Krishna Gokhale** (Gandhi's political guru), **Pherozeshah Mehta**, **Surendranath Banerjee**, **W.C. Bonnerjee**, and **M.G. Ranade**.

**Methods & demands.** The Moderates believed in **constitutional methods** — the **"3 Ps": petition, prayer, protest** — and faith in British fair play. Demands: **Indianisation of the services, expansion of legislative councils, reduction of military expenditure, protection of civil rights, and economic reforms** (critique of the drain). The Extremists later derided their approach as **"political mendicancy."**

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — INC founded **1885, Bombay**; **A.O. Hume** (founder/GS), **W.C. Bonnerjee** first president, **72 delegates**; **Badruddin Tyabji** first Muslim president (1887). Moderates (1885–1905): **Naoroji's Drain theory**, Gokhale, Mehta; methods = **petition, prayer, protest**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1885: Hume founds, Bonnerjee presides, 72 delegates, Bombay.**" Moderates = "**3 Ps + Drain theory (Naoroji).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **A.O. Hume = founder/General Secretary**; **W.C. Bonnerjee = first president** (don't swap). The **Drain of Wealth theory** is **Dadabhai Naoroji's**. Moderates used **constitutional** methods, criticised as "political mendicancy" by the Extremists.

💡 **Example:** Dadabhai Naoroji's **"Drain of Wealth"** critique — that Britain was economically draining India — gave the national movement a powerful **economic argument** that the Moderates pressed through petitions and speeches.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- 1885 Bombay; A.O. Hume (GS); W.C. Bonnerjee (1st president); 72 delegates.
- Badruddin Tyabji = first Muslim president (1887).
- Moderates: Naoroji (Drain theory), Gokhale, Mehta, S.N. Banerjee.
- Methods: petition/prayer/protest; "safety valve" theory.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Minerals & Energy

![Telangana regions map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

Telangana is mineral-rich (especially **coal** and building stone) and increasingly diverse in **power generation**.

**Minerals.**
- **Coal** — the state's most important mineral, mined by **Singareni Collieries Company Ltd (SCCL)** in the **Godavari valley coalfields** (**Ramagundam, Kothagudem, Bhadradri-Kothagudem, Mancherial, Yellandu, Bellampalli**). This is essentially the **only major coal source in South India**.
- **Limestone** — abundant (feeds a large **cement industry** — Nalgonda, Mancherial, etc.).
- **Granite** — famous **black and coloured granite** (Karimnagar, Khammam, Warangal belts), a major export.
- Also **dolomite, quartz, feldspar, laterite, clays**, and some **iron ore/manganese/bauxite** in patches (**verify specifics**).

**Energy.**
- **Thermal** — the backbone: **NTPC Ramagundam**, **Kothagudem (KTPS)**, **Bhadradri**, **Yadadri** thermal stations (coal-fired, near the coalfields).
- **Hydro** — **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam (left bank), Nizam Sagar, Lower Jurala, Pulichintala, Priyadarshini Jurala** power houses on the Krishna/Godavari.
- **Solar** — rapidly expanding (large solar parks); plus some wind/biomass. (Capacity/MW figures change — **verify latest official data**.)

| Resource | Location/Note |
|---|---|
| Coal (SCCL) | Godavari valley (Ramagundam, Kothagudem…) |
| Limestone | cement belt (Nalgonda, Mancherial) |
| Granite | Karimnagar/Khammam (export) |
| Thermal power | Ramagundam, KTPS, Bhadradri, Yadadri |
| Hydro power | Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Nizam Sagar |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Coal (Singareni/SCCL)** in the **Godavari valley** is Telangana's premier mineral (South India's main coal). **Granite** (Karimnagar/Khammam) and **limestone** (cement) are key. Power = **thermal (Ramagundam/KTPS/Bhadradri/Yadadri) + hydro (Nagarjuna Sagar/Srisailam) + growing solar**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Singareni coal in the Godavari valley; granite exports; thermal near the coal, hydro on the rivers.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Singareni coal is in the Godavari valley** (north/east), not the Krishna. It is the **only significant coalfield in South India**. Thermal plants cluster near the coal; hydro clusters on the dams.

💡 **Example:** **NTPC Ramagundam** sits beside the **Singareni (Godavari valley) coalfields** — a classic "pithead thermal plant" located near its fuel source.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Coal: SCCL, Godavari valley (Ramagundam, Kothagudem) — South India's main coal.
- Granite (Karimnagar/Khammam) export; limestone → cement.
- Thermal: Ramagundam, KTPS, Bhadradri, Yadadri.
- Hydro: Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Nizam Sagar; solar growing.
