## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Supreme Court & High Courts (Judicial Review, PIL, Collegium)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Supreme Court (Art `124-147`):** the apex court, established under `Art 124`. It has the **CJI + up to 33 other judges** (sanctioned strength **34**). Judges are appointed by the **President** through the **Collegium** (CJI + 4 senior-most judges) and **retire at 65**.

**Qualifications for an SC judge:** citizen of India, and either **5 years as a High Court judge**, or **10 years as a High Court advocate**, or a **distinguished jurist**.

**Jurisdiction:**

| Type | Article | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Original** | 131 | Centre-State & inter-state disputes |
| **Writ** | 32 | Enforcement of Fundamental Rights |
| **Appellate** | 132/133/134 | Constitutional, civil, criminal appeals |
| **Advisory** | 143 | President may seek the Court's opinion |
| **Court of Record** | 129 | Records + power to punish for contempt |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Judicial review** — the power to test the constitutionality of laws and executive actions — is part of the **basic structure**. **PIL (Public Interest Litigation)**, pioneered in the 1980s by Justices **P.N. Bhagwati** and **V.R. Krishna Iyer**, relaxed the rule of **locus standi** so any public-spirited person can move the court.

**Collegium system:** evolved through the **Three Judges Cases (1981, 1993, 1998)**; the **NJAC (99th Amendment, 2014)** was **struck down (2015)** as violating judicial independence.

**High Courts (Art `214-231`):** `Art 226` gives HCs **wider writ jurisdiction** (for FRs **and any other purpose**); HC judges retire at **62**. India currently has **25 High Courts** *(verify latest)*.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — SC judge retires at **65**, HC judge at **62**. **Art 226 (HC)** is **wider** than **Art 32 (SC)**. The **NJAC was struck down** — the collegium continues.

### 💡 Example
Through **PIL**, courts have enforced rights to a clean environment, food, and against bonded labour — an expansion of access to justice traceable to relaxed **locus standi**.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- SC: **34** judges, retire **65**; HC: retire **62**.
- **Collegium** (Three Judges Cases); **NJAC struck down 2015**.
- **PIL** — Bhagwati & Krishna Iyer; **Art 226 wider** than Art 32.
- **Advisory** jurisdiction = Art 143.

→ **65** (SC) and **62** (HC).

## 🏆 Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Caste Survey (SEEEPC) & the 42% BC Reservation

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **SEEEPC** (Comprehensive **Socio-Economic, Educational, Employment, Political and Caste** survey) was conducted across Telangana in **`2024`**, covering the state's population to generate caste-wise data for policy and reservation.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — On the basis of the caste survey, the Telangana government moved to raise **Backward Classes (BC) reservation to `42%`** in local bodies, education, and employment. This pushes total reservation **beyond the 50% ceiling** (with SC/ST/EWS), raising a **legal/constitutional question** (Indra Sawhney cap; possible Ninth Schedule protection). *(Verify the latest legislative and judicial status, and the exact survey findings, before the exam.)*

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — This is a **fast-moving current-affairs** topic. The **42% BC** figure, the survey's BC-percentage finding, and the bills' legal status all change — **verify latest official figure**. Do not state a final reservation percentage as settled law without checking.

### 💡 Example
Because total reservation crossing **50%** conflicts with the **Indra Sawhney (1992)** ceiling, states often seek **Ninth Schedule** protection or central concurrence — the same route used for earlier over-50% reservations. *(Verify current status.)*

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: The Mir Osman Ali Khan Era & Osmania University

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Mir Osman Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VII)** was the **seventh and last Nizam** of Hyderabad (r. `1911–1948`). Reputed to be among the **wealthiest people in the world**, he featured on the cover of **Time magazine (1937)**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — He founded **Osmania University in `1918`** — notable as the **first university in India to use Urdu as the medium of instruction** (with translation of texts). His reign built much of modern Hyderabad's public infrastructure.

**Modernisation & institutions** (the "Osmanian" public works): **Osmania General Hospital, the High Court, the Assembly (Town Hall), City College, Jubilee Hall, the State Bank (Hyderabad State Bank), State Railways, the Nizam Sagar dam**, and expanded electricity and roads. Hyderabad had its **own currency** (the Hyderabadi/Osmania Sicca Rupee) and an **airline (Deccan Airways)**.

**End of rule:** he refused to accede to India; after **Operation Polo (1948)** Hyderabad was integrated. He then served as the first **Rajpramukh** of Hyderabad State (1950-56). His family's collection underlies the famous **Salar Jung Museum** (Salar Jung III's collection).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Last Nizam, richest man, Osmania University 1918 (Urdu medium), Operation Polo 1948**". Own **currency + airline**; became first **Rajpramukh**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Osmania University (1918)** used **Urdu** as its medium — a distinctive fact. Mir Osman Ali Khan is the **7th (Asaf Jah VII)** and **last** Nizam — distinct from the founder **Asaf Jah I** and from **Salar Jung** (the earlier Diwan).

### 💡 Example
Hyderabad State's **own currency (the Osmania Sicca)** — the only princely state to issue its own paper currency during British rule — is a striking marker of the Nizam's autonomy.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Mir Osman Ali Khan** = 7th & last Nizam (1911-1948).
- **Osmania University 1918** — Urdu medium.
- Public works: Osmania Hospital, High Court, State Bank, Nizam Sagar.
- Own **currency & airline**; first **Rajpramukh** post-1948.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which university did the last Nizam found, in which year, and what was distinctive about it?*
→ **Osmania University (1918)** — first Indian university with **Urdu** as medium.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: The Revolt of 1857

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Revolt of 1857** — variously called the **First War of Independence, the Sepoy Mutiny, or the Great Rebellion** — was the first large-scale challenge to British rule.

**Spark:** the **greased cartridges** of the new Enfield rifle (rumoured beef/pork fat) offended sepoys. **Mangal Pandey** fired the first shot at **Barrackpore (29 March 1857)**; the revolt proper began at **Meerut on `10 May 1857`**, and spread to Delhi.

**Major centres and leaders:**

| Centre | Leader |
|---|---|
| **Delhi** | **Bahadur Shah Zafar** (symbolic head) |
| **Kanpur** | **Nana Saheb** (with Tantia Tope) |
| **Lucknow** | **Begum Hazrat Mahal** |
| **Jhansi** | **Rani Lakshmibai** |
| **Bihar (Jagdishpur)** | **Kunwar Singh** |

**Causes:** political (**Doctrine of Lapse**, annexation of **Awadh, 1856**), economic (drain of wealth, ruin of artisans), socio-religious (fear of conversion, reforms), and military (discrimination, the cartridge issue).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The revolt ended the **East India Company's rule**: the **Government of India Act 1858** transferred power to the **British Crown** (**Queen Victoria's Proclamation, 1858**), abandoned the **Doctrine of Lapse**, and reorganised the army. **Bahadur Shah Zafar** was exiled to **Rangoon**.

**Telangana/Hyderabad angle:** the **Hyderabad Residency** was attacked in **July 1857** by rebels led by **Turrebaz Khan** and **Maulvi Allauddin**, but the Nizam's Diwan **Salar Jung I** kept the state **loyal to the British**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Meerut 10 May 1857 → Delhi (Bahadur Shah) → Company out, Crown in (1858)**". Hyderabad: **Turrebaz Khan** attacked the Residency; **Salar Jung** stayed loyal.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Mangal Pandey** acted at **Barrackpore (29 March)**; the mass revolt began at **Meerut (10 May)**. The **Government of India Act 1858** (not 1857) ended Company rule. **Awadh** annexation (1856, Dalhousie) was a key immediate cause.

### 💡 Example
The abandonment of the **Doctrine of Lapse** and Queen Victoria's promise of religious non-interference (1858 Proclamation) were direct British responses to the grievances that fuelled 1857.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Mangal Pandey** — Barrackpore, 29 Mar 1857; revolt begins **Meerut, 10 May 1857**.
- Leaders: Delhi (Bahadur Shah), Kanpur (Nana Saheb), Jhansi (Lakshmibai), Lucknow (Hazrat Mahal).
- **GoI Act 1858** → Crown rule; Doctrine of Lapse dropped.
- Hyderabad: **Turrebaz Khan** (Residency attack); Salar Jung loyal.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Where and on what date did the mass revolt of 1857 begin?*
→ At **Meerut**, on **`10 May 1857`**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Karimnagar-Jagtial

![Telangana Regions Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Karimnagar** region (erstwhile district now split into **Karimnagar, Jagtial, Peddapalli, and Rajanna Sircilla**) lies in the **northern Godavari basin**.

**Rivers & irrigation:** the **Godavari** and its tributary the **Maner (Manair)** drain the area; the **Lower Manair Dam (LMD)** is at Karimnagar, and the **Sri Ram Sagar Project (SRSP)** and **Kaleshwaram** project's barrages influence the region.

**Economy:**

| Sector | Highlight |
|---|---|
| **Granite** | **Karimnagar granite** (black/exported) — a major mineral export |
| **Coal & Power** | **Ramagundam (Peddapalli)** — Singareni coal, **NTPC Ramagundam** thermal plant, revived **RFCL fertiliser** |
| **Textiles** | **Sircilla** — a major powerloom/handloom textile town |
| **Silver filigree** | **Karimnagar filigree** (GI craft) |

**Heritage:** **Vemulawada** (Rajanna Sircilla) — **Sri Raja Rajeswara Swamy temple** ("Dakshina Kashi"); **Kondagattu** (Anjaneya temple, Jagtial); **Dharmapuri** (Narasimha temple, on the Godavari, Jagtial); **Elgandal Fort** (Karimnagar); and **Kotilingala** (Jagtial) — the early **Satavahana** coin site.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Karimnagar** is famous for **granite** and **silver filigree** (a GI craft), **Ramagundam** for **coal + NTPC power**, and **Sircilla** as a **textile town**. **Vemulawada** is a major pilgrimage centre ("Dakshina Kashi"). *(Verify latest project/output figures.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Granite, coal-power, textiles, temples**": Karimnagar granite/filigree, **Ramagundam NTPC**, **Sircilla** looms, and **Vemulawada/Kondagattu** temples. **Kotilingala = Satavahana coins**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Ramagundam** (Peddapalli) hosts both **Singareni coal** and the **NTPC** super thermal plant. **Vemulawada** is in **Rajanna Sircilla**; **Kotilingala** (Satavahana) is in **Jagtial**. The **Lower Manair Dam** is on the **Maner**, a Godavari tributary.

### 💡 Example
**Ramagundam's** coal-to-power-to-fertiliser cluster (Singareni + NTPC + RFCL) is a textbook "resource-based industrial complex" — a strong geography-plus-economy answer. *(Verify latest.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Karimnagar granite** & **silver filigree** (GI).
- **Ramagundam** (Peddapalli) — Singareni coal + **NTPC** + RFCL.
- **Sircilla** = textiles; **Vemulawada** = temple town.
- **Lower Manair Dam** (Maner); **Kotilingala** (Jagtial) = Satavahana.
