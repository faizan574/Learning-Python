## 🏛️ Polity: Local Government — 73rd & 74th Amendments

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts (1992, effective 1993)** gave **constitutional status** to local self-government — the "third tier" of federalism.

**73rd Amendment — Panchayati Raj (rural).** Added **Part IX (Art 243-243O)** and the **11th Schedule (29 subjects)**.
- **Three-tier structure**: Gram Panchayat (village), Mandal/Panchayat Samiti (intermediate), Zilla Parishad (district) — intermediate tier optional for states with population < 20 lakh.
- **Gram Sabha** — the village assembly (Art 243A).
- **Elections** every 5 years by the **State Election Commission (SEC)**.
- **Reservations** — SC/ST (proportional) and **1/3 for women** (many states, incl. Telangana, raised to **50%**).
- **State Finance Commission (Art 243-I)** every 5 years; the **District Planning Committee (Art 243ZD)**.
- Follows the **Balwant Rai Mehta (1957)** and **Ashok Mehta (1978)** committee legacy.

**74th Amendment — Municipalities (urban).** Added **Part IXA (Art 243P-243ZG)** and the **12th Schedule (18 subjects)**.
- Three types: **Nagar Panchayat** (transitional area), **Municipal Council** (small urban), **Municipal Corporation** (large urban).
- **Wards Committees**, **Metropolitan/District Planning Committees**, SEC elections, reservations (incl. women).

**Telangana Panchayat Raj Act, 2018** — the state's law updating PR institutions (new panchayats/mandals after district reorganisation, terms, etc.).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **73rd Amdt** (Part IX, Art 243-243O, 11th Schedule, 29 subjects) — 3-tier PR, Gram Sabha, SEC, 1/3 women. **74th Amdt** (Part IXA, Art 243P-243ZG, 12th Schedule, 18 subjects) — municipalities. Both 1992/1993. **Telangana PR Act 2018**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **73rd = rural (Part IX, 11th Schedule, 29 subjects)**; **74th = urban (Part IXA, 12th Schedule, 18 subjects)**. Elections by the **State Election Commission** (not the central ECI). Women's reservation = **1/3 constitutionally** (Telangana 50%).

### 💡 Example

*Q: Which schedule lists the 29 subjects for Panchayats?* → The **11th Schedule** (added by the 73rd Amendment).

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- 73rd: Part IX, Art 243, 11th Schedule (29), rural, 3-tier.
- 74th: Part IXA, 12th Schedule (18), urban.
- SEC conducts elections; State Finance Commission (243-I).
- Committees: Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta.

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Gruha Jyothi & Indiramma Indlu (Round 5)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Two guarantees covering **household energy and housing** — a recap (verify latest):

1. **Gruha Jyothi** — **free electricity up to 200 units/month** to eligible domestic households; launched 2024. Households within 200 units pay nothing; the state reimburses DISCOMs for the consumed units. Targets reducing the power-bill burden for low/middle-income families. (Consumption above 200 units in a month typically makes the whole bill payable — verify slab condition.)

2. **Indiramma Indlu (Indiramma Housing)** — financial assistance (~**₹5 lakh/unit**, verify) to eligible poor/houseless families to **build a house**; revives the "Indiramma" housing brand; each constituency gets a target number of houses (verify latest allocation). Also aids those in dilapidated homes.

**Context & comparisons:** Gruha Jyothi complements the earlier free-power for farmers (agricultural pumpsets — a long-standing Telangana policy); Indiramma Indlu overlaps with the central **PM Awas Yojana (PMAY-G/U)**. Delivery via **Praja Palana** enrolment and DISCOM/household targeting.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Gruha Jyothi** = free power up to **200 units/month** (state reimburses DISCOMs). **Indiramma Indlu** = housing assistance (~₹5 lakh/unit, verify) for the poor. Both under the Six Guarantees; overlap with central schemes (free power / PM Awas Yojana). Verify figures.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Gruha Jyothi = 200 units** free power (domestic); **Indiramma Indlu = housing**. Don't swap. Indiramma Indlu overlaps with **PM Awas Yojana**. Above-200-unit consumption may forfeit the free benefit (verify slab rule).

## 🏰 Telangana History: Mir Osman Ali Khan Era — Modernisation & Osmania University

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Mir Osman Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VII, r. 1911-1948)** was the **last Nizam** and, at his peak, among the **richest men in the world** (Golconda diamond wealth, vast estates). His reign saw major **modernisation** of Hyderabad State alongside autocratic rule.

**Modernisation & institutions:**
- **Osmania University (1918)** — founded by his firman; notably taught in **Urdu** (the first Indian university with an Indian-language medium at that scale), with a translation bureau (Dar-ul-Tarjuma) rendering texts into Urdu.
- **Infrastructure** — **Osmania General Hospital, High Court, Assembly (Town Hall/Public Gardens), Nizam's State Railway, State Bank of Hyderabad (1941)**, Begumpet airport, City College.
- **Flood control & water** — after the great **Musi floods of 1908** (thousands died), he commissioned engineer **Sir M. Visvesvaraya**'s advice; built **Osman Sagar (Gandipet, 1920)** and **Himayat Sagar** reservoirs on the Musi to control floods and supply water.
- **Currency** — the **Hyderabadi/Osmania Sicca rupee** (Hyderabad had its own currency until the 1950s).
- **Philanthropy/endowments** — donations to institutions (including contributions to national causes and the National Defence Fund later).

**Political tensions:** his reign also saw the **Telangana Armed Struggle (1946-51)**, the **Razakars** (Kasim Razvi), and finally **Operation Polo (Sept 1948)**, after which Hyderabad acceded to India (he became **Rajpramukh** of Hyderabad State 1950-56).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Mir Osman Ali Khan (VII, 1911-1948, last Nizam, immensely wealthy): **Osmania University (1918, Urdu medium)**, **Osman Sagar/Himayat Sagar** (post-1908 Musi flood), Osmania Hospital/High Court, **State Bank of Hyderabad (1941)**, own currency. Ended by **Operation Polo 1948**; later **Rajpramukh**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Osmania University = 1918** (Urdu medium). **Osman Sagar (1920)** & Himayat Sagar were built after the **1908 Musi floods**. Mir Osman Ali Khan = **7th (last)** Nizam; he became **Rajpramukh** (1950-56) after accession.

### 💡 Example

*Q: What prompted the building of Osman Sagar and Himayat Sagar reservoirs?* → The catastrophic **Musi floods of 1908** (flood control + water supply for Hyderabad).

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Mir Osman Ali Khan: last Nizam (1911-48), very wealthy.
- Osmania University 1918 (Urdu medium).
- Osman Sagar/Himayat Sagar (after 1908 Musi floods).
- State Bank of Hyderabad 1941; own currency; Rajpramukh (1950-56).

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Non-Cooperation Movement & Swarajists

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)** was the **first nationwide mass movement** under Gandhi, launched at the **Nagpur Congress session (Dec 1920)** and merged with the **Khilafat** cause.

**Programme:** withdrawal of cooperation from the British —
- **Boycott** of government schools/colleges, courts, councils, foreign goods, titles and honours.
- **Constructive**: national schools (Jamia Millia, Kashi Vidyapith, Gujarat Vidyapith), **khadi/charkha**, promotion of Hindu-Muslim unity and removal of untouchability.
- Goal: **Swaraj within a year** (Gandhi's promise).

**Spread & impact:** mass participation (students, lawyers like C. R. Das, Motilal Nehru gave up practice); it shook British authority. But it turned violent at **Chauri Chaura (5 Feb 1922, UP)** — a mob burned a police station killing 22 policemen. Gandhi, committed to non-violence, **suspended the movement (12 Feb 1922)** — a decision criticised by many.

**Aftermath — the Swarajists.** Gandhi's suspension and subsequent arrest led to a split. **C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru** formed the **Swaraj Party (1923)** advocating **council entry** (contesting elections to the legislative councils to obstruct from within), against the "No-Changers" (Rajendra Prasad, Sardar Patel, C. Rajagopalachari) who wanted to continue constructive Gandhian work outside the councils. Swarajists won seats and were effective in the Central Legislative Assembly (e.g., defeating budget demands), though the party declined after Das's death (1925).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **NCM 1920-22** (Nagpur session, merged Khilafat; boycott schools/courts/councils/foreign goods; swaraj in a year). Suspended after **Chauri Chaura (5 Feb 1922)** → **12 Feb 1922**. **Swaraj Party (1923)** — C. R. Das & Motilal Nehru (council entry) vs No-Changers.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — NCM was **suspended** (not defeated) after **Chauri Chaura (5 Feb 1922)**; the suspension resolution was **12 Feb 1922**. **Swarajists (C. R. Das, Motilal Nehru)** wanted **council entry**; **No-Changers** opposed it. NCM merged with **Khilafat**.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Why did Gandhi suspend the Non-Cooperation Movement?* → After the **Chauri Chaura** incident (5 Feb 1922), where a mob turned violent — against his principle of **non-violence**.

- Boycott: schools, courts, councils, foreign goods, titles.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Environmental Issues (Musi Pollution, Urban Lakes, Uranium/Nallamala)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana faces several **environmental challenges**, especially around rapid urbanisation and resource extraction.

**Musi river pollution.** The **Musi** (Krishna tributary) through Hyderabad is heavily polluted by **untreated sewage and industrial effluent** — one of India's most polluted urban rivers. The **Musi Riverfront Development/Rejuvenation** project aims at sewage interception, STPs, and riverfront redevelopment (with rehabilitation of encroachers — a sensitive issue).

**Urban lakes & encroachment.** Hyderabad's historic tank/lake system (**Hussain Sagar, Osman Sagar, Himayat Sagar**, and hundreds of smaller cheruvus) faces **encroachment, sewage inflow, and shrinkage**. **Hussain Sagar** is polluted (immersion, effluent); **HYDRAA (2024)** was set up partly to protect **lake Full Tank Levels (FTL) and buffer zones** from encroachment (demolitions). Urban **flooding** (e.g., 2020) is worsened by lost water bodies.

**Uranium mining — Nallamala.** Proposals to mine **uranium in the Nallamala forest** (Amrabad Tiger Reserve area, Nagarkurnool) have been **strongly opposed** on ecological and tribal (Chenchu) grounds — risk to the tiger reserve, the **Krishna river catchment (Srisailam)**, and biodiversity. (Verify current status of exploration/ban.)

**Other issues:** **fluoride** contamination (Nalgonda — addressed by Mission Bhagiratha); **industrial pollution** (Patancheru/Bollaram — pharma effluent); **air quality** in Hyderabad; **deforestation** vs Haritha Haram afforestation (Telangana ku Haritha Haram — massive tree-planting programme).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Musi** = severely polluted (sewage/effluent) → rejuvenation project. **Urban lakes** (Hussain Sagar etc.) encroached/polluted → **HYDRAA** protects FTL/buffers. **Uranium in Nallamala** (Amrabad, Chenchu, Krishna catchment) opposed. Also fluoride (Nalgonda), Patancheru effluent, **Haritha Haram** afforestation.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Musi = Krishna tributary**, heavily polluted (rejuvenation project). **Uranium mining opposition** is in the **Nallamala/Amrabad** (tiger reserve, Chenchu tribe, Srisailam catchment). **HYDRAA** protects lakes/buffer zones; **Haritha Haram** = afforestation drive.
