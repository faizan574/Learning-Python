## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 Schedules, Key Articles Quick-Revision & Landmark Cases

The final Polity topic ties the map together: the **12 Schedules**, high-frequency **articles**, and the **landmark Supreme Court cases**.

### 📖 Concept

**The 12 Schedules (memorise the list):**

| Schedule | Content |
|---|---|
| **1st** | States & Union Territories |
| **2nd** | Salaries/emoluments of officials (President, Governors, judges…) |
| **3rd** | Forms of oaths & affirmations |
| **4th** | Allocation of **Rajya Sabha** seats to states/UTs |
| **5th** | Administration of **Scheduled Areas & Tribes** |
| **6th** | Tribal areas of **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram** |
| **7th** | Union / State / Concurrent Lists |
| **8th** | **22 official languages** |
| **9th** | Land-reform laws (protected; but post-**1973** judicially reviewable — I.R. Coelho) |
| **10th** | **Anti-defection** law |
| **11th** | Panchayats (29 subjects) |
| **12th** | Municipalities (18 subjects) |

**High-frequency articles:** `14` (equality), `19` (freedoms), `21` (life & liberty), `21A` (education), `32` (writs/SC), `226` (writs/HC), `44` (UCC), `50` (separation of judiciary), `72/161` (pardon), `112/202` (budget), `280` (FC), `356` (President's Rule), `368` (amendment).

**Landmark cases (the greatest hits):**

| Case | Year | Held |
|---|---|---|
| **A. K. Gopalan** | 1950 | narrow view of Art 21 (procedure established by law) |
| **Golaknath** | 1967 | FRs cannot be amended |
| **Kesavananda Bharati** | 1973 | **Basic Structure** doctrine |
| **Maneka Gandhi** | 1978 | **due process**; Art 14, 19, 21 interlinked; "procedure must be fair, just, reasonable" |
| **Minerva Mills** | 1980 | judicial review & FR-DPSP balance are basic features |
| **S. R. Bommai** | 1994 | Art 356 justiciable; secularism basic feature |
| **I. R. Coelho** | 2007 | 9th Schedule laws (post-1973) subject to basic-structure review |
| **Puttaswamy** | 2017 | **Right to Privacy** a fundamental right (part of Art 21) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Schedules: 8th = 22 languages, 9th = land reforms, 10th = anti-defection, 11th = panchayats, 12th = municipalities.** Landmark cases: **Kesavananda (basic structure, 1973), Maneka Gandhi (due process, 1978), Puttaswamy (privacy, 2017)**.

💡 **Example:** **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** transformed Art 21 — "procedure established by law" must now be **fair, just and reasonable** (due process), linking Articles 14, 19 and 21 into a "golden triangle." It's the most-cited rights case.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Schedule numbers ↔ content** (esp. 8/9/10/11/12).
- **Kesavananda (1973), Maneka (1978), Puttaswamy (2017)** — the three most important.
- **10th Schedule = anti-defection (52nd Amendment, 1985)**.
- **8th Schedule = 22 languages** (Telugu, Urdu included).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Schedules 8-9-10-11-12 = Languages, Land reforms, Anti-defection, Panchayats, Municipalities.** Cases: Kesavananda (basic structure), Maneka (due process), Puttaswamy (privacy)."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **10th Schedule = anti-defection** (not 9th, which is land reforms). **Puttaswamy (2017)** made privacy a fundamental right under **Art 21**. The **8th Schedule has 22 languages** (verify latest if any addition).

### 🎯 Revision Deep-Dive: Caste Survey (SEEEPC) & 42% BC Reservation

Continuing the **revision pass**, today consolidates the **caste survey and BC reservation** issue (originally Day 7). *(Verify all latest figures/legal status before the exam.)*

### 📖 Concept

**The Caste Survey (SEEEPC).**
- The government conducted a comprehensive **Social, Economic, Employment, Education, Political & Caste (SEEEPC)** survey (a "caste census") of households across Telangana (**2024** — verify).
- Aim: enumerate caste-wise population and socio-economic status to **rationalise reservations and welfare** — the empirical basis for enhanced **BC (Backward Classes) reservation**.
- Data built on the **Praja Palana** enrolment base.

**42% BC Reservation.**
- Based on the survey (which reportedly found **BCs to be a large majority** of the population — verify figure), the government moved to raise **BC reservation to 42%** in education, employment and **local bodies**.
- **Legal hurdle — the 50% ceiling:** the Supreme Court's **Indra Sawhney (1992)** judgment caps total reservation at **50%** (barring exceptional circumstances). Raising BC to 42% (plus SC ~15%, ST ~10%) **exceeds 50%**, so it needs either a constitutional route (like the **Tamil Nadu 69%** model via the **9th Schedule**) or central/legal backing. *(Verify current legal/legislative status.)*

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **SEEEPC caste survey (2024)** provides the data for raising **BC reservation to 42%** in Telangana; this **breaches the 50% ceiling (Indra Sawhney, 1992)**, so it needs a constitutional/legal route (9th Schedule/central approval). **Verify latest legal status.**

💡 **Example:** Telangana's 42% BC quota mirrors **Tamil Nadu's 69%** reservation, which survives by being placed in the **9th Schedule** — the exam links the **50% ceiling (Indra Sawhney)** to how states exceed it.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **SEEEPC = the caste survey** (2024); basis for BC reservation.
- **42% BC reservation** proposal → breaches **50% ceiling**.
- **Indra Sawhney (1992) = 50% reservation cap**.
- **Tamil Nadu 69% (9th Schedule)** = the precedent for exceeding 50%.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**SEEEPC survey → 42% BC quota → breaches the 50% cap (Indra Sawhney 1992) → needs 9th-Schedule/central route (like TN 69%).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **50% reservation ceiling is from Indra Sawhney (1992)**. Raising BC to 42% pushes the total past 50%, so implementation depends on the **legal/constitutional route** — a **current-affairs** matter; **verify the latest official status**.

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Telangana Folk & Performing Arts

Telangana's **folk and performing arts** — from the martial Perini dance to storytelling traditions — are a rich source of **culture** questions.

### 📖 Concept

| Art form | Type | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Perini Shivatandavam** | Martial/temple dance | Kakatiya-era warrior dance to Lord Shiva; revived by **Nataraja Ramakrishna** |
| **Oggu Katha** | Ballad storytelling | Narrates Mallanna/Beerappa/Yellamma legends; Kuruma/Yadava community |
| **Burra Katha** | Ballad storytelling | Trio with **tambura + gummeta (burra)**; social/historical themes |
| **Chindu Bhagavatam / Yakshaganam** | Dance-drama | Madiga community; mythological plays |
| **Gussadi** | Tribal dance | **Raj Gonds (Adilabad)**, Dandari-Gussadi festival (Diwali/Dussehra) |
| **Dappu** | Percussion dance | Drum (dappu) dance, energetic, community festivals |
| **Kolatam** | Stick dance | Group dance with sticks (like dandiya) |
| **Golla Suddulu** | Ballad | Yadava (Golla) narrative songs of Mallanna/Katamaraju |
| **Garelu / Bonalu / Bathukamma songs** | Folk songs | Festival music |

**Perini Shivatandavam** — a vigorous **martial dance** performed by men before battle in Kakatiya times, dedicated to **Shiva (Nataraja)**; nearly extinct, **revived by Nataraja Ramakrishna** in the 20th century. Depicted in the **Ramappa temple** sculptures.

**Oggu Katha & Burra Katha** — **oral storytelling/ballad** traditions. **Oggu Katha** (Kuruma/Yadava priests) narrates the tales of folk deities **Mallanna, Beerappa, Yellamma**. **Burra Katha** uses a lead singer with a **tambura** and two co-performers with **gummeta (burra) drums**, covering mythological, historical and social-reform themes (used in the freedom & Telangana movements).

**Chindu Bhagavatam / Yakshaganam** — folk **dance-drama** (Madiga community) enacting mythological stories.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Perini Shivatandavam** = Kakatiya martial dance to Shiva (revived by **Nataraja Ramakrishna**); **Oggu Katha** & **Burra Katha** = ballad storytelling (Burra Katha uses tambura + gummeta); **Gussadi** = Raj Gond tribal dance (Adilabad); **Dappu, Kolatam** = percussion/stick dances.

💡 **Example:** "A martial temple dance of the Kakatiya era, dedicated to Shiva and revived by Nataraja Ramakrishna" → **Perini Shivatandavam** — a signature Telangana culture answer.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Perini Shivatandavam = Kakatiya, Shiva, Nataraja Ramakrishna revival**.
- **Burra Katha = tambura + gummeta (burra)**; social/historical ballads.
- **Oggu Katha = Mallanna/Yellamma tales (Kuruma/Yadava)**.
- **Gussadi = Raj Gonds (Adilabad); Dappu = drum; Kolatam = sticks**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Perini (martial, Shiva, Ramakrishna) + Oggu/Burra Katha (ballads) + Gussadi (Gonds) + Dappu (drum) + Kolatam (sticks) + Golla Suddulu (Yadava songs).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Perini Shivatandavam** was **revived by Nataraja Ramakrishna** (Kakatiya-era origin). **Burra Katha's** instrument is the **burra/gummeta** (with a tambura). **Gussadi** is a **Raj Gond (Adilabad)** dance, not a coastal form.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement (✓✓ Revision)
### 🎯 Revision: Partition of Bengal, Swadeshi & the Extremists

Continuing the **INM revision**, today reconsolidates the **1905 Partition of Bengal, the Swadeshi Movement, and the Extremist phase** (originally Day 4).

### 📖 Concept

**Partition of Bengal (1905).** **Lord Curzon** partitioned Bengal (effective **16 October 1905**), ostensibly for administrative efficiency but really to **divide Hindus and Muslims** and weaken nationalism. It triggered massive protest.

**Swadeshi & Boycott Movement (1905-08).**
- **Swadeshi** (use Indian goods) + **Boycott** (of British goods, institutions).
- Methods: **boycott of foreign cloth, national education (Bengal National College), swadeshi enterprises, samitis, songs (Tagore's "Amar Sonar Bangla")**.
- Spread beyond Bengal; leaders: **Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghosh**.

**The Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal).**
- **Lala Lajpat Rai (Punjab), Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Maharashtra), Bipin Chandra Pal (Bengal)** — the assertive nationalists.
- **Tilak:** "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**"; used **Ganapati & Shivaji festivals** to mobilise; papers **Kesari & Maratha**.
- **Methods vs Moderates:** self-reliance, passive resistance, **swaraj as the goal** (vs Moderate petitions).

**Surat Split (1907).** The Congress split at **Surat** into **Moderates and Extremists** over the pace/methods (boycott, swaraj). The party reunited only in **1916 (Lucknow)**.

**Morley-Minto / Indian Councils Act 1909.** Response to unrest; introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** (communal representation) — "seeds of partition."

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Partition of Bengal (Curzon, 1905)** sparked the **Swadeshi & Boycott Movement**; the **Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal)** demanded **swaraj** (Tilak: "Swaraj is my birthright"). The Congress **split at Surat (1907)**; the **1909 Morley-Minto Act** brought **separate electorates**.

💡 **Example:** **Tilak's** slogan "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**" captured the Extremist shift from **petitioning** (Moderates) to **demanding** self-rule — a turning point that the Swadeshi movement energised.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Partition of Bengal — Curzon — 16 Oct 1905** (annulled 1911).
- **Swadeshi & Boycott (1905-08)**; national education, samitis.
- **Lal-Bal-Pal**; Tilak's "Swaraj is my birthright"; Kesari & Maratha.
- **Surat Split 1907**; **Morley-Minto 1909 = separate electorates**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Curzon partitions Bengal (1905) → Swadeshi/Boycott → Lal-Bal-Pal demand swaraj → Surat Split (1907) → Morley-Minto (1909, separate electorates).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Partition of Bengal was **1905 (Curzon)**, **annulled in 1911**. The **Surat Split (1907)** divided Moderates & Extremists; the **1909 Act (Morley-Minto)** introduced **separate electorates** (not the 1919 Act).

### 📖 Concept

**Location & extent.**
- Telangana lies on the **Deccan Plateau** in **south-central India**; formed **2 June 2014** (29th state).
- Borders: **Maharashtra & Chhattisgarh (N), Andhra Pradesh (S & E), Karnataka (W)**.
- **33 districts** (verify latest); capital **Hyderabad**. Largely **semi-arid**, elevation ~**300-700 m**.

**Physiography.**
- Part of the **Archaean granite–gneiss** Deccan plateau; **Deccan Trap basalt** in the north (Adilabad → black soils); hills — **Nallamala, Amrabad plateau (S), Satmala/northern hills**.
- Slopes generally **north-west to south-east** toward the rivers.

**River systems (the core):**

**1. Godavari system (northern):**
```
Godavari — enters from Maharashtra; major Telangana river
Tributaries: Pranhita (Wardha + Wainganga), Manjira, Kinnerasani, Kadem, Taliperu, Sabari
Key projects: Sri Ram Sagar (Pochampad), Kaleshwaram, Nizam Sagar (Manjira), Singur
Bhadrachalam is on the Godavari
```

**2. Krishna system (southern):**
```
Krishna — flows along the southern border
Tributaries: Tungabhadra, Bhima, Musi, Dindi, Halia
Key projects: Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Jurala (Priyadarshini), Palamuru-Rangareddy, Kalvakurthy
```

**Godavari > Krishna** in Telangana's share/basin area. Both rivers flow **east/southeast to the Bay of Bengal**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana = **Deccan Plateau, 29th state (2014), bordered by Maharashtra/Chhattisgarh/AP/Karnataka**. Two rivers: **Godavari (N; Pranhita, Manjira; SRSP, Kaleshwaram)** and **Krishna (S; Tungabhadra, Bhima, Musi; Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam)**. Godavari's share is larger.

💡 **Example:** **Kaleshwaram** lifts the **Godavari**; **Palamuru-Rangareddy** lifts the **Krishna (Srisailam)** — the two mega lift schemes map onto the two river systems (northern vs southern).

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Borders: Maha/Chhattisgarh (N), AP (S/E), Karnataka (W)**.
