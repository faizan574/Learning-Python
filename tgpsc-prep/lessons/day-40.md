## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Constitutional Bodies (EC, UPSC/TGPSC, CAG, AG, SC/ST/BC Commissions)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Constitutional bodies** are created directly by the Constitution.

| Body | Article | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Election Commission** | **324** | Conducts elections to Parliament, State legislatures, President & VP |
| **UPSC** | **315-323** | Recruits for All-India & central services |
| **State PSC (TGPSC)** | **315** | State-service recruitment (members removed by **President**) |
| **CAG** | **148** | Audits Union & State accounts — "guardian of the public purse" |
| **Attorney-General** | **76** | Highest law officer of the **Union** (states have an **Advocate-General, Art 165**) |
| **Finance Commission** | **280** | Tax devolution & grants |

**Commissions for weaker sections:** **National Commission for SCs (Art 338)**, **National Commission for STs (Art 338A, 89th Amendment 2003)**, and **National Commission for Backward Classes (Art 338B, 102nd Amendment 2018)** — the NCBC gained **constitutional status** only in 2018.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **CAG (Art 148)** — appointed by the President, removable only like a **Supreme Court judge** — audits all government accounts and reports to the **PAC** through the President/Governor. The **NCBC** became a **constitutional body** via the **102nd Amendment (2018)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **State PSC members** are appointed by the **Governor** but can be **removed only by the President** (a key safeguard of independence). **NITI Aayog, NHRC, CIC, and Lokpal are NON-constitutional/statutory** bodies — don't list them as constitutional.

### 💡 Example
The **CAG's audit reports**, examined by the **Public Accounts Committee**, are the backbone of parliamentary financial accountability — Ambedkar called the CAG one of the most important officers under the Constitution.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **EC = 324; UPSC/State PSC = 315; CAG = 148; AG = 76**.
- **State PSC**: appointed by Governor, **removed by President**.
- **SC (338), ST (338A/89th), BC (338B/102nd, 2018)** commissions.
- **NITI Aayog/NHRC/CIC/Lokpal = non-constitutional**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who can remove a State Public Service Commission member, and under whose appointment do they serve?*
→ Appointed by the **Governor**, but removable only by the **President**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Kaleshwaram** lifts Godavari water via three barrages — **Medigadda (Lakshmi), Annaram (Saraswati), Sundilla (Parvathi)** — into reservoirs like **Mid Manair, Mallanna Sagar, and Kondapochamma Sagar**. ## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Merger, the Gentlemen's Agreement & its Violations

### 📖 Deep Dive

When the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** merged **Telangana (Hyderabad State)** with **Andhra State** to form **Andhra Pradesh (1 Nov 1956)**, the **Fazl Ali Commission (SRC)** had actually recommended keeping **Telangana separate** (as Hyderabad State) until at least `1961`, with an option to merge later. The political decision overrode this.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — To allay Telangana's fears, the **Gentlemen's Agreement (`20 February 1956`)** was signed between Telangana and Andhra leaders (including **Burgula Ramakrishna Rao** and **Bezawada Gopala Reddy**). Its safeguards included: **Telangana's surplus revenue for Telangana**, **Mulki (domicile) rules** for jobs, a **Telangana Regional Committee**, reserved **local educational admissions**, and a **Deputy CM** convention.

**Violations:** over the years, **Mulki rules were flouted** (non-locals given jobs), **Telangana's surplus revenue was diverted**, and the safeguards were poorly implemented. This breach of trust directly fuelled the **1969 "Jai Telangana" agitation** and, ultimately, the demand for a **separate state**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**SRC (Fazl Ali) suggested separate → merged anyway (1956) → Gentlemen's Agreement safeguards → violated → 1969 agitation**". Safeguards: **revenue, Mulki jobs, Regional Committee**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Fazl Ali Commission favoured a separate Telangana** initially; the merger was a **political** decision. The **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** safeguards — especially **Mulki rules** — and their **violation** are the historical root of the statehood movement.

### 💡 Example
The repeated **violation of Mulki (jobs-for-locals) rules** is the single most-cited grievance that turned the Gentlemen's Agreement's breach into the **1969 agitation** — a direct 1956-to-1969 causal link.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **SRA 1956** → AP (1 Nov 1956); **Fazl Ali (SRC)** favoured separate Telangana.
- **Gentlemen's Agreement (20 Feb 1956)** — Mulki rules, revenue, Regional Committee.
- **Violations** → **1969 Jai Telangana agitation**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*What did the Fazl Ali Commission recommend for Telangana, and what agreement provided safeguards on merger?*
→ It favoured keeping **Telangana separate** (until ~1961); the **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** gave safeguards.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Morley-Minto, Montagu-Chelmsford, Lucknow Pact & Home Rule

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909):** expanded the legislative councils and — most controversially — introduced **separate electorates for Muslims**. Viceroy **Minto** is thus called the **"Father of the communal electorate."**

**Home Rule Leagues (1916):** two leagues demanded **self-government** on the Irish model — **Tilak's League** (April 1916, Maharashtra; "**Home Rule is my birthright**") and **Annie Besant's League** (September 1916, Madras).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Lucknow Pact (`1916`)** achieved **Congress-Muslim League unity** (Congress accepted separate electorates) **and** the reunion of Congress **Moderates and Extremists**. **Jinnah**, who brokered it, was hailed as the "**Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity**."

**Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act, 1919):** introduced **dyarchy in the provinces** (subjects split into **transferred** and **reserved**), a **bicameral** central legislature, and an expanded franchise — following the **Montagu Declaration (1917)** promising **responsible government** as the goal.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Morley-Minto 1909 (separate electorates) → Lucknow Pact 1916 (unity + Home Rule era) → Montagu-Chelmsford 1919 (dyarchy)**". Tilak & Besant = **Home Rule Leagues**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Separate electorates** came with **Morley-Minto (1909)**; **dyarchy** with **Montagu-Chelmsford (1919)**. The **Lucknow Pact (1916)** is where Congress **accepted** separate electorates for unity. Don't swap 1909 and 1919.

### 💡 Example
The **1917 Montagu Declaration** — promising "responsible government" — was the first explicit British commitment toward Indian self-rule, setting the stage for the 1919 Act and later demands.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Morley-Minto 1909** — separate electorates (Minto = father of communal electorate).
- **Home Rule Leagues 1916** — Tilak & Annie Besant.
- **Lucknow Pact 1916** — Congress-League unity; Moderate-Extremist reunion.
- **Montagu-Chelmsford 1919** — **dyarchy** in provinces; Montagu Declaration 1917.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which reforms introduced separate electorates, and which introduced dyarchy?*
→ **Morley-Minto (1909)** = separate electorates; **Montagu-Chelmsford (1919)** = dyarchy.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Environmental Issues (Musi, Urban Lakes, Uranium/Nallamala)

![Telangana Rivers Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana faces several pressing **environmental challenges**, concentrated around **Hyderabad and the Nallamala forest**.

| Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Musi pollution** | The **Musi** (Krishna tributary) is heavily polluted by sewage and industrial effluents through Hyderabad → the **Musi Riverfront rejuvenation** aims to clean it |
| **Urban lakes** | **Hussain Sagar** (and many city lakes) suffer **pollution, encroachment, and shrinkage**; **HYDRAA** enforces **FTL/buffer** protection |
| **Uranium / Nallamala** | Proposed **uranium mining** in the **Nallamala (Amrabad)** forest was opposed over **Krishna-water contamination**, **Chenchu tribal displacement**, and forest loss |
| **Industrial/fluoride** | **Patancheru-Nacharam** industrial pollution; **fluoride/fluorosis** in Nalgonda groundwater |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The proposed **uranium exploration/mining in the Nallamala forest (Amrabad Tiger Reserve)** — one of India's largest tiger reserves and home to the **Chenchu** tribe — was strongly opposed and shelved because it threatened the **Krishna river (Srisailam reservoir)** catchment and tribal habitat. *(Verify the latest status.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Musi & lakes (Hyderabad) + uranium/Nallamala (Amrabad) + fluoride (Nalgonda)**": the state's environmental hotspots. HYDRAA protects **lake FTL**; Musi rejuvenation cleans the river.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Nallamala uranium** issue centres on protecting the **Krishna/Srisailam** water source and the **Chenchu** tribe in the **Amrabad Tiger Reserve**. **Musi** is a **Krishna** tributary (pollution flows downstream). Nalgonda's issue is **fluoride**; Patancheru's is **industrial** pollution.

### 💡 Example
The campaign against **Nallamala uranium mining** — balancing energy needs against the **Krishna drinking-water source** for two states and tribal rights — is a textbook **development-vs-environment** debate. *(Verify current status.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Musi** (Krishna tributary) — Hyderabad pollution → rejuvenation.
- **Urban lakes** (Hussain Sagar) — encroachment/pollution; **HYDRAA/FTL**.
- **Nallamala uranium** (Amrabad) — Krishna contamination + Chenchu displacement.
- **Nalgonda fluoride**; **Patancheru** industrial pollution.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Why was uranium mining in the Nallamala forest opposed?*
→ Risk of **Krishna (Srisailam) water contamination**, **Chenchu tribal displacement**, and forest destruction in the **Amrabad Tiger Reserve**.
