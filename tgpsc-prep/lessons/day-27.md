## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Making of the Constitution & Constituent Assembly

### 📖 Deep Dive

The demand for a **Constituent Assembly** framed by Indians was first raised by **M.N. Roy** in `1934`; the Indian National Congress made it an official demand in `1935`; and it was finally conceded through the **Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946**. The Assembly was thus **not** directly elected by universal adult franchise — its members were **indirectly elected** by the members of the **Provincial Legislative Assemblies**, with seats allocated roughly by population (one seat per ~10 lakh people).

Total strength was fixed at **`389`** — `292` from British provinces, `93` from princely states, and `4` from chief commissioners' provinces (Delhi, Ajmer-Merwara, Coorg, British Baluchistan). After Partition, the strength fell to **`299`**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — First meeting: **`9 December 1946`**. **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** (oldest member) was the *temporary/provisional* president; **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was elected **permanent President** on `11 December 1946`. **B.N. Rau** was the constitutional advisor.

The **Objective Resolution**, laying down the philosophy and guiding principles, was moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru** on `13 December 1946` and adopted on `22 January 1947`. It later inspired the **Preamble**.

**Key committees:**

| Committee | Chairman |
|---|---|
| Drafting Committee (7 members, set up `29 Aug 1947`) | **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** |
| Union Powers Committee | Jawaharlal Nehru |
| Union Constitution Committee | Jawaharlal Nehru |
| Provincial Constitution Committee | Vallabhbhai Patel |
| Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights & Minorities | Vallabhbhai Patel |
| Rules of Procedure Committee | Rajendra Prasad |

The Constitution was **adopted on `26 November 1949`** (now celebrated as **Samvidhan Divas / Constitution Day**) and **came into force on `26 January 1950`** — the date deliberately chosen to honour the **Purna Swaraj Day** of `26 January 1930`.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**2-11-18**": the Assembly took **2 years, 11 months, 18 days** across **`11` sessions**, at a cost of about **₹64 lakh**. At adoption the Constitution had **`395` articles, `22` parts, `8` schedules**.

### 💡 Example
In `2015`, the Government declared `26 November` as **Constitution Day** to mark the 125th birth anniversary of Dr. Ambedkar — a favourite "current-affairs-meets-static" TGPSC linkage.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Provisional President = **Sinha**; Permanent President = **Rajendra Prasad**; Drafting Committee Chair = **Ambedkar**.
- Adopted `26 Nov 1949`; enforced `26 Jan 1950`.
- Objective Resolution → Nehru → became the Preamble.
- Members **signed** the Constitution in the last session on `24 January 1950`.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Pre-Satavahana Telangana (Megaliths, Kotilingala, Asmaka)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Before the Satavahanas rose to imperial power, Telangana passed through a long **Megalithic (Iron Age)** phase, roughly **`1000 BCE` to the early centuries CE**. "Megalith" means *large stone*, and the term covers the stone-built burial and commemorative monuments of these iron-using communities.

**Types of megaliths:** *cairn circles* (stone circles over burials), *cists* (stone-slab boxes), *dolmens* (table-like slabs), *menhirs* (standing stones), and *sarcophagi*. Grave goods — iron tools, black-and-red ware pottery, beads — signal an agrarian, iron-working society.

**Key early sites in Telangana** (mostly in the **Karimnagar–Jagtial–Godavari belt**):

| Site | District (region) | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Kotilingala** | Jagtial/Karimnagar (on the Godavari) | Punch-marked & **pre-Satavahana local coins**; considered an **early Satavahana capital/mint** region |
| **Dhulikatta** | Karimnagar | Early **Buddhist stupa** with sculpted casing slabs |
| **Peddabankur** | Karimnagar | Early-historic **urban settlement**, ring-wells, coins |
| **Kondapur** | Sangareddy/Medak | Satavahana-era town — coins, beads, terracotta |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Kotilingala**, on the banks of the **Godavari**, yielded coins of early/local rulers (such as **Gobada, Samagopa**) and of the earliest Satavahana ruler **Simuka**, which is why many scholars treat it as an **early nucleus of Satavahana power**.

**Asmaka (Assaka) Mahajanapada:** Of the **16 Mahajanapadas** of the `6th century BCE`, **Asmaka** was the **only one located in the south (the Deccan)**, on the banks of the **Godavari**. Its capital is given as **Potali / Potana / Podana**, often identified with **Bodhan** in present-day Nizamabad district.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Asmaka is the **southernmost / only Deccan** Mahajanapada — a very common one-line question. Do not confuse **Potali (Asmaka's capital)** with **Pratishthana / Paithan** (the later Satavahana capital, in Maharashtra).

### 💡 Example
The presence of **Simuka's coins at Kotilingala** is often cited as archaeological evidence that the Satavahanas may have **originated in the Telangana Godavari belt** rather than only in western Deccan — a favourite analytical point.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Megalithic period ≈ **Iron Age**, black-and-red ware.
- **Kotilingala** = pre-Satavahana coins, early capital/mint.
- **Dhulikatta** = Buddhist stupa; **Peddabankur/Kondapur** = early towns.
- **Asmaka** = only southern Mahajanapada, on the Godavari, capital **Potali (Bodhan)**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check

→ **Asmaka (Assaka)**, on the **Godavari**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Simon Commission, Nehru Report & Lahore Session

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Simon Commission (1927).** The **Government of India Act 1919** (Montagu-Chelmsford reforms) had promised a review after ten years. The Conservative British government, fearing a Labour victory, appointed the **Indian Statutory Commission** in **`November 1927`**, headed by **Sir John Simon**. All **`7` members were British** — the "**all-white commission**" — with **no Indian**, an insult to the principle of self-determination.

It landed at Bombay on **`3 February 1928`** and was met with **black flags and "Simon Go Back"** everywhere. During the Lahore protest on `30 October 1928`, **Lala Lajpat Rai** was injured in a **lathi charge** and died on **`17 November 1928`** — his death later avenged by the revolutionaries (Saunders killing).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The Simon Commission was boycotted by almost all parties because it had **no Indian member**. Secretary of State **Lord Birkenhead** taunted Indians to produce an agreed constitution of their own — a challenge the **Nehru Report** answered.

**Nehru Report (1928).** An All-Parties Conference committee, chaired by **Motilal Nehru** (with **Jawaharlal Nehru** as secretary), drafted India's first constitutional blueprint by Indians. Its main features:
- **Dominion Status** (not full independence) as the goal.
- **Rejection of separate electorates**; joint electorates with reserved seats.
- A detailed set of **Fundamental Rights**.
- Responsible government, linguistic provinces.

It was opposed by **Jinnah** (who framed his **Fourteen Points** in `1929`) and by younger leaders like **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Subhas Chandra Bose**, who wanted **complete independence**, not dominion status.

**Lahore Session (December 1929).** Presided over by **Jawaharlal Nehru**, the Congress passed the historic **Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence)** resolution.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — On the banks of the **Ravi at Lahore**, the tricolour of independence was hoisted, and **`26 January 1930`** was fixed as the **first Independence Day (Purna Swaraj Day)** — celebrated across India before the Congress launched **Civil Disobedience**.

### 💡 Example
The choice of **`26 January`** as later the date of the Constitution's commencement (`1950`) directly commemorates this **1930 Purna Swaraj Day** — linking Subject 1 and Subject 4 today.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Simon = **all-British**, appointed `1927`, arrived `3 Feb 1928`, boycotted.
- Lajpat Rai died `17 Nov 1928` (Simon protest injuries).
- Nehru Report `1928` → **Dominion Status**, chaired by **Motilal Nehru**.
- Lahore `1929` → **Purna Swaraj**, presided by **Jawaharlal Nehru**; `26 Jan 1930` first Independence Day.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Rivers — The Krishna System in Depth

![Telangana Rivers Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Krishna** is the **second-largest river of peninsular India** (after the Godavari). It **rises at Mahabaleshwar** in the **Western Ghats of Maharashtra** (about `1,337 m` elevation) and flows for roughly **`1,400 km`** across **Maharashtra → Karnataka → Telangana → Andhra Pradesh**, emptying into the **Bay of Bengal** near **Hamsaladeevi** (AP). *(Verify precise length/discharge figures from official sources.)*

**In Telangana:** the Krishna enters along the **Mahabubnagar–Narayanpet** stretch, forming part of the state boundary with Karnataka and later Andhra Pradesh, and cuts through the **Nallamala** hills at Srisailam.

**Major projects on the Krishna in Telangana:**

| Project | Location | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Priyadarshini Jurala (PJP)** | Mahabubnagar/Jogulamba-Gadwal | **First major project** on the Krishna as it enters Telangana |
| **Srisailam** | Nagarkurnool (Telangana–AP shared) | Large hydro-electric + storage dam on the Nallamala gorge |
| **Nagarjuna Sagar** | Nalgonda (shared with AP) | One of the **world's largest masonry dams**; huge left/right canals |

**Major tributaries (Krishna basin) relevant to Telangana:**

| Tributary | Confluence / note |
|---|---|
| **Tungabhadra** | Joins the Krishna at **Alampur (Kudali/Sangameshwara)**, Jogulamba-Gadwal |
| **Bhima** | Joins in Karnataka (basin extends to Telangana lift schemes) |
| **Musi** | Rises near **Anantagiri (Vikarabad)**, flows through **Hyderabad** (Osman Sagar & Himayat Sagar reservoirs), joins the Krishna at **Wadapally**, Nalgonda |
| **Dindi, Halia, Paleru, Munneru** | Minor Telangana tributaries feeding the Krishna |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Lift Irrigation Schemes** in the Palamuru (Mahabubnagar) region draw from the Krishna: **Kalwakurthy, Nettempadu, Bhima, Koilsagar**, and the mega **Palamuru-Rangareddy LIS** — designed to lift Krishna/Srisailam water to drought-prone uplands.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Ma-Ka-Te-A**": the Krishna's state journey — **Ma**harashtra → **Ka**rnataka → **Te**langana → **A**ndhra Pradesh. Its biggest tributary overall is the **Tungabhadra**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Water sharing on the Krishna is governed by the **Krishna Water Disputes Tribunal** — **KWDT-I (Bachawat)** and **KWDT-II (Brijesh Kumar)**. Don't confuse the tribunal names with the Godavari (GWDT).

### 💡 Example
The **Musi**, though it flows through Hyderabad and is the focus of the *Musi Riverfront* project (Subject 2 today), is technically a **tributary of the Krishna** — a neat cross-subject linkage examiners love.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Source: **Mahabaleshwar** (Maharashtra); mouth: **Bay of Bengal** (Hamsaladeevi).
- **Jurala** = first major project in Telangana; **Nagarjuna Sagar** = masonry dam, Nalgonda.
- **Tungabhadra** joins at **Alampur**; **Musi** joins at **Wadapally**.
- Tribunals: **Bachawat (KWDT-I)**, **Brijesh Kumar (KWDT-II)**.
