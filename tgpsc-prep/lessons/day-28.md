## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Citizenship (Art 5-11, Citizenship Act 1955 & CAA)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Citizenship is dealt with in **Part II, Articles `5–11`** of the Constitution. India has **single citizenship** for the whole country (unlike the USA's dual federal/state citizenship). The Constitution only identified who were citizens **at its commencement** (`26 Jan 1950`) and left the rest to Parliament.

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Art 5** | Citizenship at commencement — domicile + (born in India / either parent born in India / ordinary residence for 5 years) |
| **Art 6** | Rights of persons who **migrated from Pakistan** (cut-off `19 July 1948` for registration) |
| **Art 7** | Rights of persons who **migrated to Pakistan** but returned under a permit |
| **Art 8** | Citizenship of **persons of Indian origin residing abroad** |
| **Art 9** | Voluntarily acquiring **foreign citizenship = loss** of Indian citizenship (no dual citizenship) |
| **Art 10** | Continuance of citizenship rights |
| **Art 11** | **Parliament may regulate** citizenship by law → the **Citizenship Act, 1955** |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Citizenship Act, `1955`** provides five ways to **acquire** citizenship: by **birth, descent, registration, naturalisation,** and **incorporation of territory**; and loss by **renunciation, termination,** and **deprivation**.

**Key amendments:** `1986`, `2003` (introduced **OCI** and the idea of a National Register of Citizens), `2005`, `2015`, and the **Citizenship (Amendment) Act, `2019` (CAA)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **CAA 2019** fast-tracks citizenship for **six non-Muslim communities** (Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, Christian) who fled **Pakistan, Bangladesh, or Afghanistan** and entered India **on or before `31 December 2014`**, cutting the naturalisation residency requirement from `11` to `5` years for them. The **CAA Rules were notified in `March 2024`**. *(Verify latest implementation details.)*

**OCI (Overseas Citizen of India)** is **not** dual citizenship — an OCI cardholder **cannot vote, hold constitutional office, or buy agricultural land**.

### 💡 Example
A person born in India, whose parents both hold foreign citizenship and one is an illegal migrant, is **not** a citizen by birth — the `2003` amendment tightened the "birth" route, showing why current rules matter.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Citizenship = **Part II, Art 5-11**; **single** citizenship.
- Acquisition/loss governed by the **Citizenship Act 1955** (Art 11).
- CAA `2019`: cut-off `31 Dec 2014`; 3 countries; 6 communities; Rules `2024`.
- **OCI ≠ dual citizenship**; no voting/constitutional office.

→ **Article 11**; the **Citizenship Act, 1955**.

## 🏆 Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Central Schemes Active in Telangana

### 📖 Deep Dive

TGPSC frequently tests **Centre-vs-State scheme overlaps** — where a Union scheme and a Telangana scheme address the same need. Know the flagship central schemes, their core benefit, and their state counterpart.

| Central scheme | Core benefit | Telangana counterpart / overlap |
|---|---|---|
| **PM-KISAN** | `₹6,000`/year (3 × ₹2,000) to landholding farmers | **Rythu Bharosa / Rythu Bandhu** (state investment support) |
| **Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY)** | Health cover up to `₹5 lakh`/family/year | **Rajiv Aarogyasri** (state health scheme) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **PM-KISAN** gives **`₹6,000` per year** in **three equal instalments**; **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY** offers **`₹5 lakh`** annual family health cover. ## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: The Ikshvakus & the Vishnukundins

### 📖 Deep Dive

After the Satavahanas declined (mid-3rd century CE), the eastern Deccan and Krishna valley passed to the **Ikshvakus**, and later Telangana came under the **Vishnukundins**.

**Ikshvakus (c. 3rd–4th century CE).** The founder was **Vasishthiputra Sri Chamtamula (Santamula I)**. Their capital was **Vijayapuri**, at **Nagarjunakonda (Sriparvata)** on the Krishna. They performed **Vedic sacrifices** (ashvamedha, agnihotra), yet their reign is famous as a **golden age of Buddhism** — the great **mahastupa, viharas and the university** at Nagarjunakonda were built largely through the patronage of **royal women** (e.g., **Chamtisiri**), even though the kings themselves were Brahmanical. Our knowledge comes from the **Nagarjunakonda and Jaggayyapeta inscriptions**. The dynasty was overthrown by the **Pallavas**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Under the **Ikshvakus**, **Nagarjunakonda (Vijayapuri)** became a major **Buddhist centre**; the kings performed Vedic sacrifices while the **women of the royal house** endowed the Buddhist monuments.

**Vishnukundins (c. 5th–6th century CE).** They ruled parts of **Telangana** and coastal Andhra, initially as feudatories linked to the **Vakatakas**. Their early capital is identified as **Indrapalanagara** (associated with **Tummalagudem**, Nalgonda region, from copper-plate inscriptions), later shifting to **Denduluru/Amarapura** in the Andhra deltas. The greatest ruler was **Madhavavarman I**, credited with performing **many ashvamedhas** and bearing grand titles. They patronised both **Saivism and Buddhism**. The dynasty fell to **Pulakeshin II of the Badami (Western) Chalukyas**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**I before V**": **I**kshvakus (Nagarjunakonda, Buddhism, 3rd–4th c.) came **before** the **V**ishnukundins (Indrapalanagara, Madhavavarman, 5th–6th c.), who were then ended by the **Chalukyas**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Nagarjunakonda's Buddhist glory belongs to the **Ikshvakus**, not the Satavahanas (though Amaravati/Dharanikota is Satavahana-linked). And the Vishnukundin capital **Indrapalanagara (Tummalagudem)** places them firmly in **Telangana** history.

### 💡 Example
The **Nagarjunakonda inscriptions** (Prakrit, Brahmi) recording endowments by Ikshvaku queens are a favourite source-based question — they show Buddhism thriving under a Brahmanical dynasty.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Ikshvakus**: founder **Chamtamula**, capital **Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda)**, Buddhist patronage by royal women, ended by **Pallavas**.
- **Vishnukundins**: greatest ruler **Madhavavarman I**, capital **Indrapalanagara (Tummalagudem, Nalgonda)**, ended by **Chalukyas (Pulakeshin II)**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Which dynasty made Nagarjunakonda (Vijayapuri) its capital and a great Buddhist centre?*
→ The **Ikshvakus**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Civil Disobedience, Round Tables & Poona Pact

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM), 1930.** After the Lahore Purna Swaraj resolution, Gandhi issued **eleven demands** to Viceroy **Irwin** and, when refused, launched the **Dandi Salt March**: from **Sabarmati Ashram on `12 March 1930`**, walking about **`385 km` (240 miles)** with **`78` followers** to **Dandi**, where he **broke the salt law on `6 April 1930`**. The movement spread into **no-tax campaigns, boycott of foreign cloth and liquor, and forest-law violations**, with mass participation of **women**.

**Round Table Conferences (London):**

| Conference | Date | Congress |
|---|---|---|
| **First RTC** | Nov 1930 – Jan 1931 | **Boycotted** (leaders jailed) |
| **Second RTC** | Sep – Dec 1931 | **Gandhi attended** (sole Congress rep) — deadlock on communal issue |
| **Third RTC** | Nov – Dec 1932 | Congress **absent** |

**Gandhi-Irwin Pact (`5 March 1931`):** Congress agreed to attend the Second RTC and suspend CDM; the government agreed to release political prisoners and allow salt-making for personal use.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Communal Award** (`16 August 1932`) by British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** granted **separate electorates** to the **Depressed Classes**. Gandhi opposed it with a **fast unto death at Yerwada jail**, leading to the **Poona Pact**.

**Poona Pact (`24 September 1932`)** — between **Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar**: it **replaced separate electorates** for the Depressed Classes with **reserved seats within the general (joint) electorate**, raising their reserved seats in the provincial legislatures (from `71` to **`147`**) and giving them `18%` of central seats.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Salt → Talk → Award → Pact**": Salt March (1930) → Round Table talks (1930-32) → Communal Award (Aug 1932) → **Poona Pact (Sep 1932)**.

### 💡 Example
The **Poona Pact** is why India adopted **reserved seats (joint electorate)** rather than **separate electorates** for Scheduled Castes — a direct link to later constitutional reservation policy.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Dandi March: **12 Mar 1930**, `385 km`, `78` followers; salt law broken **6 Apr 1930**.
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact**: `5 Mar 1931` (Delhi Pact).
- **Communal Award**: `16 Aug 1932`, Ramsay MacDonald.
- **Poona Pact**: `24 Sep 1932`, Gandhi–Ambedkar; reserved seats in joint electorate.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*What did the Poona Pact replace, and between whom was it signed?*
→ It replaced **separate electorates** for the Depressed Classes with **reserved seats in a joint electorate**; signed between **Gandhi and Ambedkar** (`1932`).

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Soils & Agriculture

![Telangana Regions Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana's agriculture rests on its **soils, monsoon seasons, and irrigation**. The plateau's soils fall into a few broad classes:

| Soil type | Local name | Where / suited to |
|---|---|---|
| **Red soils** | *Chalka* | Most **widespread**; sandy-loam uplands — coarse grains, groundnut, pulses |
| **Black soils** | *Regur / black cotton* | Adilabad, Khammam, Warangal belts — **cotton**, jowar, oilseeds |
| **Laterite soils** | — | High-rainfall patches — plantation/horticulture |
| **Alluvial soils** | — | River valleys (Godavari, Krishna) — paddy, sugarcane |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Red (chalka) soils** cover the **largest area** of Telangana, while **black (regur) cotton soils** dominate the northern (Adilabad) and some western belts. *(Verify exact area percentages from the latest state statistics.)*

**Cropping seasons:**

```
Kharif (Vaanakalam) : June–October  (monsoon)  → paddy, cotton, maize, redgram
Rabi   (Yasangi)     : October–March (winter)   → paddy, maize, groundnut, jowar
```

**Major crops:** **Paddy (rice)** is the dominant crop — expanded hugely after **Kaleshwaram** and other lift schemes, making Telangana a leading rice producer *(verify latest rank/output)*. Others: **maize, cotton, red gram (pulses), groundnut, soybean, sugarcane, chilli, turmeric, tobacco**.

**Horticulture:** **mango** (Khammam, Nalgonda, Jagtial), **sweet orange/mosambi** (Nalgonda), **turmeric** (Nizamabad), **chillies** (Khammam, Warangal), **grapes** (Rangareddy), and a growing **oil palm** push in irrigated districts.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Red is broad, Black is cotton**": red *chalka* soils are the most extensive; black *regur* soils grow cotton. Seasons: **Vaanakalam (Kharif) = monsoon**, **Yasangi (Rabi) = winter**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Do not confuse the **local season names**: **Vaanakalam = Kharif** (monsoon) and **Yasangi = Rabi** (winter) — a common regional-terminology question.

### 💡 Example
The surge in **paddy** cultivation after **Kaleshwaram** irrigation links Subject 5 (agriculture) with the irrigation topics — examiners love this "water → crop" cause-effect chain. *(Verify latest paddy area/production figures.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Red (chalka)** soils = most widespread; **black (regur)** = cotton (Adilabad).
- Seasons: **Kharif = Vaanakalam** (Jun-Oct); **Rabi = Yasangi** (Oct-Mar).
- **Paddy** = dominant crop (post-Kaleshwaram boom — verify latest).
- Horticulture hubs: mango (Khammam), turmeric (Nizamabad), chilli (Khammam/Warangal).
