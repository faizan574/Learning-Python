## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 Non-Constitutional (Statutory/Executive) Bodies: NITI Aayog, NHRC, CIC, Lokpal

**Non-constitutional bodies** are created by an **executive resolution** or an **Act of Parliament**, not by the Constitution itself. Know each one's basis, composition and role.

### 📖 Concept

| Body | Basis | Head/Composition | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NITI Aayog** | Executive resolution (**2015**) | PM = Chairperson; CEO; Governing Council (CMs) | Policy think-tank (replaced **Planning Commission**) |
| **NHRC** | Protection of Human Rights Act, **1993** | Chairperson (ex-CJI/SC judge) + members | Human-rights protection |
| **CIC** | RTI Act, **2005** | Chief Information Commissioner + ICs | Information (RTI) appeals |
| **Lokpal** | Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act, **2013** | Chairperson + up to 8 members | Anti-corruption ombudsman (Centre) |
| **CVC** | CVC Act, 2003 (statutory) | Central Vigilance Commissioner | Vigilance/anti-corruption |
| **CBI** | DSPE Act, 1946 | Director | Investigation agency |

**NITI Aayog (2015).** **National Institution for Transforming India** — an **advisory think-tank** (no fund-allocation power, unlike the old Planning Commission). **PM chairs**; has a **Governing Council** (all CMs + Lt. Governors), a **CEO**, vice-chairperson, and members. Promotes **cooperative & competitive federalism**.

**NHRC (1993).** Statutory watchdog for human rights; **Chairperson** must have been a **Chief Justice or a judge of the Supreme Court** (broadened by 2019 amendment); investigates violations, recommends (its recommendations are **advisory**, not binding). State Human Rights Commissions parallel it.

**CIC (2005).** Apex body under the **RTI Act**; hears second appeals; the CIC and ICs are appointed by the President on a committee's recommendation.

**Lokpal (2013).** The **anti-corruption ombudsman** at the Union level (**Lokayuktas** in states); can inquire into allegations against public functionaries incl. the **PM (with safeguards)**, ministers, MPs, officials. **Chairperson** = ex-CJI/SC judge or eminent person.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **NITI Aayog (2015, executive resolution)** replaced the Planning Commission — a **think-tank with no allocation power**. **NHRC (1993), CIC (RTI 2005), Lokpal (2013)** are **statutory** bodies. **NHRC recommendations are advisory** (not binding).

💡 **Example:** Unlike the old **Planning Commission** (which allocated Plan funds to states), **NITI Aayog only advises** — fund transfers now flow through the **Finance Commission** and central schemes. That shift from "allocator" to "advisor" is a favourite exam contrast.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **NITI Aayog = 2015, executive resolution, PM chair, no funds** (replaced Planning Commission, itself non-constitutional).
- **NHRC = 1993** (Chairperson ex-CJI/SC judge); recommendations **advisory**.
- **CIC = RTI Act 2005**; **Lokpal = 2013** (ombudsman).
- **Constitutional vs non-constitutional:** FC/EC/CAG/UPSC are constitutional; these are **not**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**NITI (2015, think-tank, no money) — NHRC (1993) — CIC (RTI 2005) — Lokpal (2013).** All statutory/executive, none in the Constitution."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **NITI Aayog has NO power to allocate funds** (advisory only) — the Planning Commission did. **NHRC recommendations are not binding**. The **Planning Commission was also non-constitutional** (a common trap — it was never a constitutional body).

*(NITI Aayog, 2015 (executive resolution); no — NHRC recommendations are advisory.)*

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Telangana Festivals & Jataras

Telangana's living culture is expressed in its **festivals and tribal jataras** — Bathukamma, Bonalu, and the great Medaram gathering. These are high-yield **culture** questions.

### 📖 Concept

| Festival / Jatara | When / Where | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Bathukamma** | Sept–Oct (Ashwayuja), 9 days ending Saddula Bathukamma | **State festival**; floral arrangement worshipping Goddess **Gauri**; women's festival |
| **Bonalu** | Aashada (Jul–Aug); Golconda, Secunderabad (Ujjaini Mahankali), Lal Darwaza | Thanksgiving to Goddess **Mahakali**; "Bonam" (offering), Pothuraju, Rangam prophecy |
| **Sammakka-Saralamma (Medaram) Jatara** | Biennial, Medaram (Mulugu) | **Asia's largest tribal congregation**; Koya tribe; honours Sammakka & Saralamma |
| **Nagoba Jatara** | Keslapur (Adilabad), Pushya (Jan) | Mesram clan of **Raj Gonds**; serpent deity Nagoba |
| **Peddagattu / Komuravelli (Lingamanthula) Jatara** | Nalgonda / Siddipet | Folk deity Mallanna (Shaivite) |
| **Sadar (Saddula)** | Post-Diwali, Hyderabad | **Yadav (Golla/Kuruma)** community; buffalo festival |
| **Moharram / Peerla Panduga** | Islamic month of Muharram | Observed across communities (composite culture) |

**Bathukamma** — the floral festival (stacked seasonal flowers in a conical arrangement), celebrated by women over nine days, ending with **Saddula Bathukamma**; declared the **state festival**; a symbol of Telangana identity and ecology.

**Bonalu** — a **Mahakali** thanksgiving festival; women carry decorated pots (**Bonam**) of rice/jaggery/curd; features **Pothuraju** (male protector), and the **Rangam** (a woman possessed by the goddess delivers prophecies) at **Ujjaini Mahankali (Secunderabad)**, **Golconda**, and **Lal Darwaza (Old City)**.

**Sammakka-Saralamma Jatara (Medaram)** — held **biennially** at **Medaram (Mulugu district)**; honours the Koya tribal mother-daughter warriors **Sammakka and Saralamma** who resisted the Kakatiyas; draws **crores of pilgrims** — Asia's largest tribal fair.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Bathukamma = state floral festival (Gauri, women, 9 days)**; **Bonalu = Mahakali thanksgiving (Bonam, Pothuraju, Rangam)**; **Medaram Sammakka-Saralamma Jatara = Asia's largest tribal congregation (Koya, biennial, Mulugu)**; **Nagoba Jatara = Raj Gonds, Keslapur (Adilabad)**.

💡 **Example:** If a question mentions "**Asia's largest tribal fair, held every two years, honouring a mother and daughter**," the answer is the **Medaram Sammakka-Saralamma Jatara** — a near-certain culture question.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Bathukamma = state festival, Goddess Gauri, floral, women, 9 days**.
- **Bonalu = Mahakali; Ujjaini/Golconda/Lal Darwaza; Bonam, Pothuraju, Rangam**.
- **Medaram = Koya, biennial, Mulugu, Sammakka & Saralamma**.
- **Nagoba (Keslapur, Raj Gonds), Sadar (Yadavs, buffalo)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Bathukamma (flowers, state fest, Gauri) + Bonalu (pots, Mahakali) + Medaram (tribal, biennial, Koya) + Nagoba (Gonds, serpent) + Sadar (Yadavs, buffaloes).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Bathukamma** worships **Gauri** (floral); **Bonalu** worships **Mahakali** (pots) — don't swap. **Medaram** is **biennial** (every 2 years) in **Mulugu**; **Nagoba** is the **Raj Gond** serpent jatara at **Keslapur (Adilabad)**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement (✓✓ Revision)
### 🎯 Revision: INC Formation & the Moderate Era (1885–1905)

Continuing the **INM revision**, today reconsolidates the **founding of the Congress and the Moderate phase** (originally Day 3).

### 📖 Concept

**Foundation (1885).** The **Indian National Congress** was founded in **December 1885** at **Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College)** by **A. O. Hume** with prominent Indians; **72 delegates**; first president **W. C. Bonnerjee**. The **"safety valve"** theory (Hume intended it to defuse discontent) is debated by historians.

**The Moderates (1885–1905).** Early leaders: **Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjee, W. C. Bonnerjee, Badruddin Tyabji, R. C. Dutt**.

**Ideology & methods:**
- Faith in **British sense of justice** and constitutional means — the **"3 P's": Petitions, Prayers, Protests** ("political mendicancy," Extremists later scoffed).
- **Prayer-and-petition** politics; annual sessions, memoranda, press.

**Demands:**
- **Constitutional/political:** expansion of legislative councils, more Indians in councils, **Indianisation of the civil services** (simultaneous ICS exams in India), separation of judiciary from executive.
- **Economic (the strongest):** the **"drain of wealth"** theory (**Naoroji's *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India***; **R. C. Dutt's *Economic History of India***); reduction of **military expenditure** and land revenue.
- **Administrative & civil rights:** freedom of speech/press.

**Achievements & limits.**
- Achieved: the **Indian Councils Act 1892** (limited council expansion); a powerful **economic critique**; created a national platform and political awakening.
- Limits: **narrow social base** (educated elite), slow methods → disillusionment fed the **Extremist** rise after **1905 (Partition of Bengal)**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **INC founded Dec 1885, Bombay, A. O. Hume, first president W. C. Bonnerjee, 72 delegates**. **Moderates (1885-1905): petitions & prayers (3 P's)**, key weapon the **drain-of-wealth theory (Naoroji, R. C. Dutt)**; achieved the **Indian Councils Act 1892**.

💡 **Example:** The Moderates' faith in petitions produced modest gains (the **1892 Act**) but their **economic critique (drain theory)** had a far deeper, lasting impact — exposing the exploitative nature of colonial rule.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **1885, Bombay, Hume, Bonnerjee, 72 delegates; "safety valve"**.
- **Moderates: 3 P's; Naoroji "Grand Old Man" & drain theory; R. C. Dutt**.
- **Indian Councils Act 1892** = main constitutional gain.
- Moderate phase **1885-1905**; Extremists rise after the **1905 Partition of Bengal**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1885 (Hume/Bonnerjee/Bombay/72) → Moderates: petitions + prayers + drain theory (Naoroji) → Indian Councils Act 1892 → 1905 Partition ends the phase.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — First INC president = **W. C. Bonnerjee** (founder = **A. O. Hume**) — don't conflate. The **drain-of-wealth theory** is **Naoroji's** (with R. C. Dutt's economic history). Moderate methods = **constitutional/petitions**, not agitation.

### 🎯 Environmental Issues: Musi Pollution, Urban Lakes & Uranium/Nallamala

Telangana's development brings environmental stress — the **polluted Musi**, **shrinking urban lakes**, and the contested **uranium mining** proposal in the **Nallamala** forest.

**🗺️ Reference map — Telangana regions (Musi through Hyderabad; Nallamala in the south):**

![Telangana regions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

*(If the map doesn't display in email, open the lesson on GitHub — the SVG is at `tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg`.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Musi river pollution.**
- The **Musi** (a Krishna tributary) flows through **Hyderabad**; downstream it is heavily polluted by **untreated sewage and industrial effluents** — a chronic problem.
- The government's **Musi Riverfront Development / rejuvenation** project (with **STPs — sewage treatment plants**) aims to clean and revive it (linked to Day 9). *(Verify latest project status.)*

**Urban lakes.**
- Hyderabad historically had **hundreds of interconnected tanks/lakes** (Qutb Shahi cascade system). **Encroachment, sewage inflow and eutrophication** have shrunk/degraded them — **Hussain Sagar** (notorious pollution), **Osman Sagar/Himayat Sagar** (drinking-water lakes needing protection).
- **HYDRAA** (Day 9/13) was set up partly to **protect lakes and FTL (Full Tank Level) zones** from encroachment.

**Uranium mining — Nallamala / Amrabad.**
- Proposed **uranium exploration/mining** in the **Nallamala forest (Amrabad Tiger Reserve, Nagarkurnool)** by the **Atomic Minerals Directorate / UCIL** raised strong **environmental & tribal (Chenchu)** opposition — fears of contaminating the **Krishna/Nagarjuna Sagar** catchment and displacing the **Chenchu** tribe.
- The proposal has been **contested/paused** amid protests. *(Verify current status.)*

**Other issues:** **fluoride** (Nalgonda — Day 17), **industrial pollution** (Patancheru — Day 18), **deforestation** (countered by **Haritha Haram**), **air/solid-waste** in cities.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Musi** = sewage/effluent pollution (rejuvenation + STPs); **urban lakes** (Hussain Sagar etc.) degraded by encroachment/sewage (**HYDRAA** protects FTLs); **uranium mining in Nallamala/Amrabad** opposed over the **Krishna catchment & Chenchu tribe** — contested.

💡 **Example:** The proposed **Nallamala uranium mining** pits energy needs against the **Amrabad Tiger Reserve**, the **Chenchu** tribe, and the **Krishna/Nagarjuna Sagar** water source — the classic development-vs-environment question for Telangana.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Musi = sewage/industrial pollution**; rejuvenation via **STPs**.
- **Hussain Sagar** = polluted city lake; **HYDRAA** protects **FTL/lakes**.
- **Nallamala/Amrabad uranium** = opposed (Chenchu tribe + Krishna catchment).
- Links: **fluoride (Nalgonda), Patancheru industrial pollution, Haritha Haram (afforestation)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Musi (sewage), Hussain Sagar (lake pollution, HYDRAA saves lakes), Nallamala uranium (Chenchu + Krishna catchment opposition).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **uranium proposal is in the Nallamala/Amrabad (Nagarkurnool)** — opposed for threatening the **Chenchu tribe** and the **Krishna/Nagarjuna Sagar** water source. **HYDRAA** protects lake **FTL** zones.
