## 🏛️ Polity: Emergency Provisions (Art 352, 356, 360)

The emergency provisions in **Part XVIII (Articles 352–360)** are borrowed largely from the **Government of India Act 1935** and the **Weimar Constitution of Germany**. They let the Centre convert the normally federal structure into a **unitary** one during crises — which Dr. Ambedkar defended as the Constitution's safety valve.

**National Emergency — `Art 352`.** Proclaimed when the security of India is threatened by **war, external aggression, or armed rebellion**. The term "armed rebellion" replaced "internal disturbance" via the **44th Amendment Act, 1978** — a direct response to the misuse during 1975. The President can proclaim it only on the **written advice of the Cabinet** (not just the PM). Parliamentary approval is needed within **one month** by a **special majority**; once approved it runs **six months** and can be extended indefinitely in six-month blocks. During its operation, `Art 358` automatically suspends `Art 19` (only for war/external aggression), and `Art 359` lets the President suspend enforcement of other FRs — **except `Art 20` and `Art 21`**, a protection added by the 44th Amendment.

**President's Rule — `Art 356`.** Imposed on failure of constitutional machinery in a state. Approved by **simple majority** within two months; initially **six months**, extendable up to a maximum of **three years** (beyond one year only under strict conditions). The **S.R. Bommai case (1994)** is the landmark: it made the proclamation subject to **judicial review** and held that the floor of the Assembly — not the Governor's subjective report — is the test of majority.

**Financial Emergency — `Art 360`.** Proclaimed if the financial stability or credit of India is threatened. Approved by simple majority within two months; once approved it operates **indefinitely** with no repeat approval needed. It has **never been invoked** in India.

| Type | Article | Ground | Approval majority | Max duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| National | `352` | War / external aggression / armed rebellion | Special | Indefinite (6-month blocks) |
| President's Rule | `356` | Failure of constitutional machinery | Simple | 3 years max |
| Financial | `360` | Threat to financial stability | Simple | Indefinite |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — National Emergency has been declared **thrice**: `1962` (China war), `1971` (Pakistan war), and `1975` (internal disturbance — the controversial one). `Art 360` has **never** been used.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**3-5-6-0**": Art **35**2 National, Art **356** State, Art **360** Financial — ascending numbers, ascending rarity (3 times / many times / never).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Only `Art 20` (protection in respect of conviction) and `Art 21` (life & liberty) can **never** be suspended, even during a National Emergency. The 44th Amendment also made the Cabinet's **written** advice mandatory.

💡 **Example:** In the **S.R. Bommai (1994)** judgment, the Supreme Court restored dismissed state governments and ruled that secularism is part of the basic structure — curbing arbitrary use of `Art 356`.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- 44th Amendment (1978): "armed rebellion", written Cabinet advice, Art 20 & 21 sacrosanct, Art 359 narrowed.
- 42nd Amendment (1976): had allowed emergency in a *part* of India.
- Bommai case → judicial review + floor test.
- 38th Amendment had made the proclamation immune from judicial review (later reversed).

## 🏗️ Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Praja Palana, Praja Vani & Administrative Changes

After the December 2023 government change, a flagship **grievance-redressal and welfare-enrolment** drive was launched to operationalise the poll promises.

**Praja Palana (Dec 2023–Jan 2024).** A mass application-collection programme where citizens submitted a single combined form to enrol for the **Six Guarantees**. Applications were received at ward/village level over several weeks; the state reported crores of applications (**verify the latest official figure before the exam**). It served as the enrolment backbone for schemes like Mahalakshmi, Gruha Jyothi, Rythu Bharosa and Indiramma Indlu.

**Praja Vani.** A weekly grievance-redressal platform at the Secretariat and district/collectorate level where the public directly submits petitions to the administration — positioned as the successor to the earlier grievance mechanism, emphasising direct citizen access to officials.

**Administrative & symbolic changes.** The new government introduced several renamings and symbolic resets:
- **State Secretariat** named **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar Telangana State Secretariat**; a tall **Ambedkar statue** (125 ft) stands near Hussain Sagar.
- Revival emphasis on **Telangana Formation Day / Praja Palana Dinotsavam** framing.
- Scheme-delivery shifted toward direct benefit transfer and the single-window Praja Palana database.
- (Several district/institution renamings and emblem-usage decisions were revisited — **verify the latest official notifications** as these evolve.)

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Praja Palana** = one-stop application drive for the Six Guarantees (Dec 2023–Jan 2024); **Praja Vani** = recurring weekly public-grievance redressal day. Don't confuse the two.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Palana = Plan/enrol** (one-time mass forms), **Vani = Voice** (ongoing grievances)."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Exact application counts, dates of specific GO numbers, and renaming notifications change; the discipline is to recall the **concept and purpose**, and mark figures as "verify latest official figure."

## 🏺 Telangana History: Kakatiyas — Political History

The **Kakatiyas (c. 1000–1323 CE)**, with their capital at **Orugallu (Warangal)**, were the first dynasty to bring most of the Telugu-speaking region — and Telangana's core — under a single, cohesive rule. They began as **feudatories of the Western (Kalyani) Chalukyas** and rose to sovereign power.

**Early rulers.** **Beta Raja I** and **Prola Raja I** were early chiefs. **Prola Raja II (c. 1116–1157)** asserted independence from the declining Kalyani Chalukyas. His son **Rudradeva (Prataparudra I)** (c. 1158–1195) formally declared independence, issued the **Anumakonda (Hanamkonda) Thousand Pillar Temple** inscription (1163) — a key source — and expanded the kingdom.

**Ganapati Deva (c. 1199–1262)** was the greatest Kakatiya ruler and had the **longest reign**. He unified the Telugu lands, promoted trade (the **Motupalli port** and his **Abhaya Shasana / safe-conduct charter** encouraging foreign merchants), and extended authority to the coast. Having no son, he was succeeded by his daughter.

**Rani Rudrama Devi (c. 1262–1289)** was one of the few women to rule in medieval India. She took the male title **"Rudradeva Maharaja,"** repulsed the **Yadavas (Seunas) of Devagiri**, and was praised by the Venetian traveller **Marco Polo** (who visited the kingdom around her period) for her just governance. She likely died fighting the **Kayastha chief Ambadeva**.

**Prataparudra II (c. 1289–1323)**, her grandson, was the last sovereign Kakatiya. He faced repeated invasions from the **Delhi Sultanate**: **Malik Kafur** (under Alauddin Khalji, c. 1310) extracted tribute, and finally **Ulugh Khan (Muhammad bin Tughlaq)** captured Warangal in **1323**, ending the dynasty. Prataparudra II died en route to Delhi.

| Ruler | Period (approx.) | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| Prola Raja II | 1116–1157 | asserted independence |
| Rudradeva | 1158–1195 | Thousand Pillar Temple, 1000-pillar inscription |
| Ganapati Deva | 1199–1262 | greatest/longest reign, Motupalli charter |
| Rudrama Devi | 1262–1289 | woman ruler, repulsed Yadavas, Marco Polo |
| Prataparudra II | 1289–1323 | last ruler, fell to Delhi Sultanate (1323) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Warangal fell to **Ulugh Khan in 1323 CE**, ending Kakatiya sovereignty. **Rudrama Devi** ruled under the title **Rudradeva Maharaja** and was noted by **Marco Polo**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Prola → Rudradeva → Ganapati → Rudrama → Prataparudra**" = "**PRGRP**" — the five that matter. Ganapati = Greatest; Rudrama = Rani; Prataparudra = Pralaya (the fall).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Thousand Pillar Temple** (Hanamkonda) is linked to **Rudradeva (1163)**, while the **Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple** (UNESCO site, 1213) belongs to **Ganapati Deva's** general **Recharla Rudra**. Don't swap them.

💡 **Example:** The **Motupalli Abhaya Shasana** of Ganapati Deva guaranteed safety to foreign traders — an early example of state promotion of maritime commerce.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Capital Orugallu/Warangal; feudatories of Kalyani Chalukyas originally.
- Rudrama Devi = rare woman ruler; adopted son Prataparudra II succeeded.
- 1323 = fall of Warangal to Delhi Sultanate (Tughlaq).
- Patron deity: Swayambhu (Shiva); emblem associations with Kakati goddess.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Revolutionary Movement Phase II (Bhagat Singh, Azad, Surya Sen)

The second wave of revolutionary activity (**c. 1924–1934**) was more organised, ideologically sharper (socialist leanings), and emerged partly out of disillusionment after the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation (1922).

**Hindustan Republican Association (HRA), 1924** — founded in Kanpur by **Ramprasad Bismil, Sachindranath Sanyal, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee**. Its famous action was the **Kakori Conspiracy (9 August 1925)** — looting a train carrying government money near Kakori (UP). **Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Roshan Singh, and Rajendra Lahiri** were executed (1927).

**Reorganisation → HSRA, 1928.** At **Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi (Sept 1928)**, under **Chandrashekhar Azad**, the group was renamed the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)** — adding "Socialist" to reflect its new ideology (influenced by **Bhagat Singh** and **Bhagwati Charan Vohra**).

**Key actions:**
- **Saunders murder (Dec 1928, Lahore):** Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Azad killed J.P. Saunders to avenge the death of **Lala Lajpat Rai** (from a lathi-charge during the Simon Commission protest).
- **Central Assembly bomb (8 April 1929):** **Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt** threw non-lethal bombs in the Assembly and courted arrest — slogan **"Inquilab Zindabad"** — "to make the deaf hear."
- **Lahore Conspiracy Case:** Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev were hanged on **23 March 1931** (observed as Shaheed Diwas).
- **Chandrashekhar Azad** died fighting police at **Alfred Park, Allahabad (27 Feb 1931)** — true to his vow never to be captured alive.

**Bengal (Surya Sen):** **Surya Sen ("Masterda")** led the **Chittagong Armoury Raid (18 April 1930)**, with revolutionaries including **Pritilata Waddedar** and **Kalpana Dutt**. Surya Sen was captured and executed in **1934**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — HRA (1924) → **HSRA (1928)** added "Socialist". Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev hanged **23 March 1931**; Chittagong Armoury Raid led by **Surya Sen, 1930**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Kakori(25) → Saunders/Assembly(28–29) → Hangings(31) → Chittagong(30)**." Azad's park & Bhagat's gallows both fall in **1931**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Central Assembly bomb (1929)** was deliberately **non-lethal** — the aim was arrest and publicity, not killing. And it was **Saunders**, not Scott (the intended target), who was shot in Lahore.

💡 **Example:** "Inquilab Zindabad" (Long Live the Revolution), popularised by Bhagat Singh in the Assembly action, became a lasting slogan of the freedom movement.

- HRA founders: Bismil, Sanyal, Chatterjee; Kakori 1925.
- HSRA 1928 at Feroz Shah Kotla under Azad.
- Pritilata Waddedar — first woman martyr of the armed movement (Chittagong).
- Bhagat Singh's "Why I am an Atheist"; trial writings.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Environmental Issues — Musi Pollution, Urban Lakes & Uranium/Nallamala

![Telangana rivers map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_rivers.svg)

Telangana's environmental challenges cluster around its **urban water bodies**, the **Musi river**, and the ecologically sensitive **Nallamala forest**.

**Musi river pollution.** The **Musi** (a tributary of the Krishna) flows through Hyderabad and has become one of the state's most polluted stretches — untreated sewage and industrial effluent from the city degrade water quality downstream, affecting agriculture and health. The government launched the **Musi Riverfront Development / rejuvenation** project, involving relocation of encroachments within the river's **Full Tank Level (FTL) and buffer zones**, sewage treatment plants (STPs), and riverfront redevelopment (status and figures **verify latest official**).

**Urban lakes crisis.** Hyderabad's historic lakes — **Hussain Sagar, Osman Sagar (Gandipet), Himayat Sagar, and hundreds of smaller cheruvu (tanks)** — face **encroachment, sewage inflow, and shrinkage**. **Hussain Sagar** is notably polluted; restoration has been attempted under lake-protection and **HYDRAA** (Hyderabad Disaster Response and Assets Protection Agency) actions targeting FTL/buffer-zone encroachments. Osman Sagar and Himayat Sagar are protected drinking-water catchments under GO 111 (its status has been debated — **verify latest**).

**Uranium mining & Nallamala.** The **Nallamala forest** (spanning the Krishna belt, part of the **Amrabad Tiger Reserve**) has been the site of proposed **uranium exploration/mining** by the Atomic Minerals Directorate / UCIL. This has drawn strong opposition over threats to the **Krishna river catchment, the tiger reserve, and the Chenchu tribal community** — a recurring environment-vs-development flashpoint in exam-relevant current affairs.

| Issue | Location | Core concern |
|---|---|---|
| Musi pollution | Hyderabad (Krishna tributary) | sewage/effluent; rejuvenation project |
| Urban lakes | Hussain Sagar, Osman Sagar, Himayat Sagar | encroachment, sewage, shrinkage |
| Uranium/Nallamala | Amrabad TR, Krishna belt | tiger reserve + Chenchu tribe + Krishna catchment |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Musi** is a tributary of the **Krishna**. **Amrabad Tiger Reserve** (Nallamala) is one of India's largest tiger reserves and the focus of the anti-uranium-mining concern. **Osman Sagar & Himayat Sagar** are Hyderabad's protected drinking-water reservoirs (GO 111 catchment).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Musi → Krishna; Nallamala → Amrabad tigers + Chenchus; Lakes → Sagars (Hussain/Osman/Himayat).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Osman Sagar is on the **Musi**, Himayat Sagar on the **Esi** (a Musi tributary) — both supply Hyderabad's drinking water. Don't confuse them with Hussain Sagar, which is an urban recreational lake on a Musi tributary (not a drinking source).

💡 **Example:** HYDRAA demolitions targeting construction within lake FTL/buffer zones illustrate the enforcement side of Hyderabad's urban-water protection.
