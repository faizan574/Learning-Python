## 🏛️ Polity: President & Vice-President

The **President** (`Art 52–62`) is the constitutional **head of state** and part of the Union Executive; the **Vice-President** (`Art 63–71`) is the second-highest office and **ex-officio Rajya Sabha Chairman**.

**President — election.** Elected **indirectly** by an **electoral college** of elected MPs and elected MLAs (incl. Delhi & Puducherry), by the system of **proportional representation with a single transferable vote (STV)** and **secret ballot**. The **value of votes** is weighted to maintain uniformity between states and parity between Union & states. **Qualifications:** citizen, 35+, qualified for Lok Sabha membership, not holding an office of profit. **Term:** 5 years; eligible for re-election. **Oath** administered by the **CJI**.

**President — powers.** Executive (appoints PM, Governors, judges, CAG, etc.), legislative (summons/prorogues Parliament, assents to bills, `Art 123` ordinances, nominates members), financial (money bills need prior recommendation), judicial (`Art 72` **pardoning power**), and emergency (`Art 352/356/360`). The President acts on the **aid and advice** of the Council of Ministers (`Art 74`); the **42nd/44th amendments** made advice binding but allow **one reconsideration**.

**President — impeachment.** Removed only for **"violation of the Constitution"** by **impeachment** (`Art 61`) — a quasi-judicial process; charge framed by either House (**1/4 members' notice, 14 days**), passed by **2/3 of total membership** of that House, investigated and passed likewise by the other House. **Nominated members participate** (unlike a Presidential election).

**Vice-President.** Elected by an **electoral college of both Houses of Parliament** (elected **and nominated** members) by STV/secret ballot — **no state legislatures**. Qualifications: 35+, qualified for Rajya Sabha. He is the **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**. Removed by a **Rajya Sabha resolution (effective majority)** agreed to by the Lok Sabha — **no formal "grounds"** needed.

| Office | Electoral college | Removal |
|---|---|---|
| President | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (STV) | Impeachment (Art 61) |
| Vice-President | Both Houses (elected + nominated) | RS resolution + LS agreement |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — President elected by **elected MPs + elected MLAs** (STV, secret ballot); **impeached** for violation of the Constitution (`Art 61`, 2/3 majority). Vice-President elected by **both Houses only** (incl. nominated), is **ex-officio RS Chairman**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**President = MPs + MLAs (STV), impeachment; VP = Parliament only, RS Chairman.**" VP election has **no MLAs**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — In the **President's election**, only **elected** members vote (no nominated MPs, no MLCs); in **impeachment**, **nominated members DO participate**. The VP's electoral college **excludes state legislatures**. Pardoning power = `Art 72` (President), `Art 161` (Governor, narrower).

💡 **Example:** A bill returned by the President for reconsideration (`Art 111`) must be assented if passed again by Parliament — a **suspensive veto** (not for money bills/amendments).

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Impeachment Art 61 (violation of Constitution); nominated members vote.
- VP: Art 63-71; both Houses; ex-officio RS Chairman; no MLAs.
- Oath: President by CJI; VP by President.

## 🤝 Schemes: Telangana Budget & Economic Survey Highlights

Budget and Economic Survey questions test **fiscal structure and headline indicators** — treat **all specific numbers as "verify latest official figure"** since they change every year.

**Budget structure.** Telangana's annual budget (presented by the Finance Minister) has **revenue** and **capital** accounts, with large shares to **welfare (Six Guarantees)**, irrigation, agriculture, education and health. The state relies on **own tax revenue (GST/SGST, stamps & registration, excise, motor vehicle tax)**, **own non-tax revenue**, **central transfers (share of central taxes + grants)**, and **borrowings**. Key fiscal indicators (verify current year's figures): **Fiscal Deficit (target ≤3% of GSDP under FRBM, with flexibility)**, **Revenue Deficit/Surplus**, and **outstanding debt as % of GSDP**.

**Economic Survey highlights.** The Telangana **Socio-Economic Outlook** reports **GSDP growth, per-capita income** (among the highest in India — verify), **sectoral shares** (services-dominated, strong IT & pharma; agriculture's share declining but employment-heavy), and **human-development indicators**. Hyderabad contributes a large share of GSDP.

**Key fiscal concepts (for MCQs):**
- **FRBM Act:** caps fiscal deficit and debt; promotes fiscal discipline.
- **GSDP:** Gross State Domestic Product (state's output).
- **Devolution:** share of central taxes to states per **Finance Commission** formula.
- **Cess/grants-in-aid:** central transfers outside the divisible pool.

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| Own revenue | SGST, stamps, excise, MV tax |
| Central transfers | tax devolution + grants |
| Deficits | fiscal (≤3% GSDP target), revenue |
| Survey | GSDP growth, per-capita income, sector shares |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana's budget relies on **own tax revenue (SGST, excise, stamps)** + **central transfers** + **borrowings**; **FRBM** targets fiscal deficit **≤3% of GSDP** (with flexibility). The state is **services-led (IT/pharma)** with **high per-capita income** — verify current figures.

## 🏺 History: Telangana Festivals & Jataras

Telangana's cultural identity is vivid in its **festivals and tribal jataras** — frequently asked in TGPSC for their **deities, locations and distinctive practices**.

**Bathukamma** — the **state festival**, a nine-day floral festival celebrated by women in **Sharad Ritu (Sept–Oct, around Dussehra)**, stacking seasonal flowers in conical tiers around **Gauri (Goddess Maha Gauri)**; it ends on **Saddula Bathukamma**. A symbol of Telangana identity and ecology.

**Bonalu** — a **folk festival** honouring **Mahankali/Grama Devatha (village goddess)**, celebrated in **Ashada Masam (July–Aug)**, with women carrying **bonam** (decorated pots of rice/jaggery) to temples (**Golconda, Secunderabad Ujjaini Mahankali, Lal Darwaza**); features **Potharaju** and **Rangam (oracle)**.

**Sammakka-Saralamma (Medaram) Jatara** — Asia's largest tribal fair, a **biennial** gathering at **Medaram (Mulugu district)** honouring the **Koya tribal goddesses Sammakka and Saralamma (Sarakka)**; millions attend. (Verify next occurrence year.)

**Other jataras:** **Nagoba Jatara** (Keslapur, Adilabad — Mesram clan of Gonds, snake god); **Peddagattu/Lingamantula Jatara** (Suryapet); **Komuravelli Mallanna**; **Sadar** (Yadav buffalo festival, Hyderabad, post-Diwali); **Moharram/Peerla Panduga**; **Edupayala** (Medak).

| Festival | Deity / Focus | When/Where |
|---|---|---|
| Bathukamma | Gauri; floral (state festival) | Sept–Oct; statewide |
| Bonalu | Mahankali (village goddess) | Ashada; Hyderabad/Golconda |
| Medaram Jatara | Sammakka-Saralamma (Koya) | Biennial; Medaram (Mulugu) |
| Nagoba Jatara | Snake god (Gond Mesram) | Keslapur (Adilabad) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Bathukamma** = state floral festival (Gauri, Sept–Oct); **Bonalu** = Mahankali (Ashada); **Medaram (Sammakka-Saralamma) Jatara** = biennial Koya tribal fair at **Medaram (Mulugu)** — Asia's largest tribal gathering.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Bathukamma = flowers/Gauri (state festival); Bonalu = pots/Mahankali; Medaram = Sammakka-Saralamma (Koya, biennial); Nagoba = Gond snake god.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Medaram Jatara is biennial** (every 2 years), in **Mulugu** district, honouring **Koya** goddesses. **Bonalu = Ashada**; **Bathukamma = Sharad/Dussehra season**. Nagoba is **Gond (Mesram clan)** at Keslapur.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"Asia's largest tribal jatara"* → **Medaram (Sammakka-Saralamma)**; *"state floral festival"* → **Bathukamma**.

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Simon Commission, Nehru Report & Lahore Session

The late 1920s saw a new constitutional push: the **Simon Commission (1927)**, the Indian response via the **Nehru Report (1928)**, and the declaration of **Purna Swaraj at Lahore (1929)**.

**Simon Commission (1927).** An **all-white** statutory commission (chaired by **Sir John Simon**) to review the 1919 reforms — **no Indian member**. India erupted in protest ("**Simon Go Back**") with **black-flag** demonstrations. At Lahore (1928), a lathi-charge injured **Lala Lajpat Rai**, who died soon after — avenged later by Bhagat Singh & associates (Saunders' killing).

**Nehru Report (1928).** In response to the Secretary of State's challenge to produce an agreed constitution, an **All-Parties Conference** committee under **Motilal Nehru** (with Tej Bahadur Sapru, Subhas Bose, etc.) drafted the **Nehru Report** — the **first major Indian attempt** at a constitution. It proposed **Dominion Status**, **fundamental rights**, **responsible government**, **joint electorates** (with reserved seats), and **linguistic provinces** — but **rejected separate electorates**, which Jinnah opposed (his **"Fourteen Points," 1929**).

**Lahore Session (Dec 1929).** Under **Jawaharlal Nehru**'s presidency, Congress declared **"Purna Swaraj" (Complete Independence)** as the goal, fixed **26 January 1930** as the **first Independence Day** (hence Republic Day date later), and authorised the **Civil Disobedience Movement**. The tricolour was unfurled on the Ravi's banks.

| Event | Year | Highlight |
|---|---|---|
| Simon Commission | 1927 | All-white; "Simon Go Back"; Lajpat Rai injured |
| Nehru Report | 1928 | Motilal; Dominion Status, joint electorates |
| Lahore Session | 1929 | Purna Swaraj; 26 Jan 1930; J. Nehru president |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Simon Commission (1927)** = all-white, "Simon Go Back"; **Nehru Report (1928, Motilal Nehru)** = first Indian constitution draft (Dominion Status, joint electorates, no separate electorates); **Lahore 1929 (J. Nehru)** declared **Purna Swaraj**, 26 Jan 1930.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1927 Simon (white, go back) → 1928 Nehru Report (Motilal, Dominion) → 1929 Lahore (Purna Swaraj, 26 Jan 1930, J. Nehru).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Nehru Report = Motilal Nehru (1928)**; **Lahore session president = Jawaharlal Nehru (1929)** — don't confuse the two Nehrus. Nehru Report favoured **Dominion Status** (not full independence) & **joint electorates**; Lahore raised the bar to **Purna Swaraj**.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"first Independence Day declared"* → **26 Jan 1930 (Lahore)**; *"first Indian-drafted constitution"* → **Nehru Report 1928**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Simon Commission 1927: John Simon, all-white, "Simon Go Back," Lajpat Rai.
- Nehru Report 1928: Motilal Nehru; Dominion Status, joint electorates, FRs.
- Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929) — Muslim League's response.
- Lahore 1929: J. Nehru, Purna Swaraj, 26 Jan 1930 first Independence Day.

## 🗺️ Geography: Area Focus — Karimnagar-Jagtial

The **Karimnagar-Jagtial** belt in **north-central Telangana** is a fertile **Godavari-basin** granite region, historically the heartland of agrarian prosperity, the **granite industry**, and the Naxalite movement's early strongholds.

**Location & physiography.** Part of old composite Karimnagar (now **Karimnagar, Jagtial, Peddapalli, Rajanna-Sircilla, Jayashankar-Bhupalpally** partly; verify). It lies in the **Godavari basin** (the **Godavari** and tributaries **Maneru, Manair**), on Archean **granite/gneiss** terrain. The **Lower Manair Dam (LMD)** near Karimnagar and the **Sri Ram Sagar/Kaleshwaram** canal network irrigate the belt.

**Resources & economy.** Famous for **granite** (Karimnagar black/"Tan Brown" granite is exported worldwide), **cotton, paddy, maize**, and **poultry**. **Sircilla** is a major **powerloom textile** town (Rajanna-Sircilla). The **Ramagundam** area (Peddapalli) has the **NTPC Ramagundam thermal plant** and the **Ramagundam Fertilizers (RFCL)** — major energy/industry nodes (Singareni coal nearby).

**Heritage & sites.** **Karimnagar silver filigree** (GI craft); **Elgandal Fort**, **Dharmapuri** (Narasimha temple on the Godavari), **Vemulawada** (Sri Raja Rajeshwara Swamy temple — "Dakshina Kashi," Rajanna-Sircilla), **Kondagattu** (Anjaneya temple, Jagtial), and ancient **Jagtial/Kotilingala** (early Satavahana site) in the belt.
