## 🏛️ Polity: DPSP (Art 36-51) & Fundamental Duties (51A)

The **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)** in **Part IV (`Art 36–51`)** are non-justiciable guidelines for governance, borrowed from the **Irish Constitution**. **Fundamental Duties** in **Part IVA (`Art 51A`)** list citizens' obligations.

**Nature of DPSP.** Not enforceable by courts (`Art 37`) but "**fundamental in the governance**" — the state should apply them in law-making. They aim at a **welfare state** and **social/economic democracy**. Classified into:
- **Socialistic:** `Art 38` (welfare, reduce inequalities), `Art 39` (adequate means of livelihood, equal pay, no concentration of wealth), `Art 39A` (free legal aid), `Art 41-43` (work, living wage, social security), `Art 43A` (workers' participation).
- **Gandhian:** `Art 40` (village panchayats), `Art 43` (cottage industries), `Art 46` (SC/ST/weaker sections), `Art 47` (prohibition, nutrition), `Art 48` (cattle/cow protection).
- **Liberal-intellectual:** `Art 44` (**Uniform Civil Code**), `Art 45` (early childhood care, now <6 after 86th Amd), `Art 48` (modern agriculture), `Art 48A` (environment), `Art 49` (monuments), `Art 50` (separation of judiciary from executive), `Art 51` (international peace).

**DPSP vs FRs.** FRs are **justiciable** (Part III), DPSP **non-justiciable** (Part IV). In conflicts, courts evolved from *Champakam* (FRs prevail) to *Minerva Mills* — **balance/harmony** between the two is part of the **basic structure**.

**Fundamental Duties (`Art 51A`).** Added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** on the **Swaran Singh Committee** recommendation; originally **10**, an **11th** (education for child 6–14) added by the **86th Amendment (2002)**. They include respecting the Constitution, flag & anthem, cherishing freedom-struggle ideals, protecting sovereignty, promoting harmony, preserving heritage, protecting environment, developing scientific temper, safeguarding public property, striving for excellence, and (11th) providing education. **Non-justiciable** but a constitutional reminder.

| Part | Articles | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Part IV | `Art 36-51` | DPSP (non-justiciable) |
| Part IVA | `Art 51A` | Fundamental Duties (11) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — DPSP (Part IV, Art 36-51) from **Ireland**, **non-justiciable** (`Art 37`); **UCC = Art 44**, **village panchayats = Art 40**, **environment = Art 48A**. Fundamental Duties (`Art 51A`) added by **42nd Amendment (1976)** (Swaran Singh Committee); **11th duty** by **86th Amendment**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**DPSP = non-justiciable goals (Irish); FRs = justiciable rights.**" Duties: 10 in 1976 (42nd) + 1 in 2002 (86th) = **11**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — DPSP from **Ireland** (not USA). **UCC = Art 44** (often confused with Art 48 cow protection). Fundamental Duties are **non-justiciable** and were added by the **42nd (1976)**, not in the original Constitution. *Minerva Mills* established FR-DPSP **balance** as basic structure.

💡 **Example:** A directive to organise **village panchayats** (`Art 40`) underpinned the **73rd Amendment**; `Art 48A`/`51A(g)` on environment are cited in green-bench judgments.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- UCC (44), panchayats (40), environment (48A), judiciary separation (50).
- Fundamental Duties: 42nd Amd (1976), Swaran Singh; 11th by 86th (2002).
- Minerva Mills: FR-DPSP balance = basic structure.

## 🤝 Schemes: Musi Rejuvenation, HYDRAA & Urban Governance

The present Telangana government has made **urban environmental governance** a priority — the **Musi River rejuvenation** project and the creation of **HYDRAA** to protect lakes and public land from encroachment.

**Musi Riverfront rejuvenation.** The **Musi** (a Krishna tributary flowing through Hyderabad) is heavily polluted by untreated sewage. The project aims at **sewage treatment (STPs), riverbed clean-up, relocation of encroachers, beautification and a riverfront development** on the Sabarmati model. It involves resettlement of riverbank dwellers (into **Indiramma Indlu/2BHK** housing) and is a large, multi-year effort. (Verify cost, STP capacity and timelines from official sources.)

**HYDRAA.** The **Hyderabad Disaster Response and Assets Protection Agency** — set up in **2024** to protect **lakes (Full Tank Level/FTL & buffer zones), parks, nalas and government land** from illegal encroachment, and to strengthen **urban disaster response** (flooding). It gained prominence for **demolition drives** against structures encroaching on lake beds/FTL. (Verify the exact jurisdiction and legal framework.)

**Urban governance context.** These fit a wider push on **GHMC/ORR-region planning**, flood management (after recurring Hyderabad floods), and lake protection — intersecting with the **Revenue/Irrigation** departments and courts on encroachment.

## 🏺 History: Statehood Movement 2001–2014 (TRS, JACs, Sri Krishna Committee)

The final phase of Telangana's long struggle — the **2001–2014 statehood movement** — culminated in the creation of **India's 29th state** on **2 June 2014**.

**Birth of the TRS.** **K. Chandrashekar Rao (KCR)** founded the **Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS)** on **27 April 2001**, with the single-point agenda of a separate Telangana state (capital Hyderabad). It built on the accumulated grievances — water, funds, jobs (G.O. 610), and identity.

**Mobilisation.** The movement used **Joint Action Committees (JACs)** — the **Telangana JAC (TJAC, 2009)** led by **Prof. M. Kodandaram** united parties, employees, students and intellectuals. Agitations included **Sakala Janula Samme (general strike, 2011)**, **Million March**, **Sagara Haram**, road/rail blockades, and emotive student mobilisation (several self-immolations). Cultural assertion (Bathukamma, Gaddar's songs, dhoom-dham) fuelled the sentiment.

**Political trigger & committee.** KCR's **fast-unto-death (Nov–Dec 2009)** forced the Centre's hand: on **9 December 2009**, Home Minister **P. Chidambaram** announced the process of forming Telangana — but it was put on hold amid Andhra backlash. The **Sri Krishna Committee (B.N. Srikrishna, Feb 2010; report Dec 2010)** examined the demand and offered **six options** (ranging from status quo to bifurcation with Hyderabad as a separate UT/joint capital).

**Formation.** After prolonged politics, the **Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014** was passed; **Telangana was formed on 2 June 2014** with **Hyderabad as the common capital for 10 years**. KCR became the **first Chief Minister**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **TRS founded 27 April 2001 (KCR)**; **TJAC (2009, Kodandaram)**; KCR's **2009 fast → 9 Dec 2009 Chidambaram announcement**; **Sri Krishna Committee (2010, six options)**; **AP Reorganisation Act 2014 → Telangana formed 2 June 2014** (Hyderabad common capital 10 yrs).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**2001 TRS → 2009 fast + Dec-9 announcement → 2010 Sri Krishna (6 options) → 2 June 2014 Telangana.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Sri Krishna Committee = B.N. Srikrishna (2010)**; don't confuse with the **Sri Krishna Commission** (Mumbai riots). Formation date **2 June 2014**; the **9 December 2009** announcement is a separate, earlier milestone. TRS founding = **2001** (renamed **BRS** in 2022).

💡 **Example:** Pair *"committee on Telangana demand (2010)"* → **Sri Krishna Committee**; *"date Telangana was formed"* → **2 June 2014**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- TRS: 27 April 2001, KCR; TJAC 2009, Kodandaram.
- KCR fast Nov–Dec 2009; Chidambaram announcement 9 Dec 2009.
- Sri Krishna Committee (B.N. Srikrishna), 2010, six options.
- AP Reorganisation Act 2014; Telangana 2 June 2014; Hyderabad common capital 10 yrs.

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh & Khilafat

The year **1919** marked a turning point: repressive legislation, a massacre, and a pan-Islamic cause combined to launch Gandhi into **mass national leadership**.

**Rowlatt Act (1919).** Officially the **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act**, it empowered the government to **detain without trial** ("no vakil, no appeal, no daleel"). Gandhi launched the **Rowlatt Satyagraha** — his first **all-India** agitation (hartals, fasting). Protests turned violent in places; Gandhi called it a "**Himalayan blunder**" where people were unprepared for non-violence.

**Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919, Amritsar).** During **Baisakhi**, a peaceful crowd (protesting the Rowlatt Act and the arrest of leaders **Satyapal & Saifuddin Kitchlew**) gathered at the enclosed **Jallianwala Bagh**. **General Dyer** ordered firing without warning, blocking the only exit; hundreds were killed (official ~379; many more by other counts — verify). **Rabindranath Tagore** renounced his **knighthood**; **Michael O'Dwyer** (Punjab Lt-Governor) backed Dyer. The **Hunter Commission** later censured Dyer. (Udham Singh assassinated O'Dwyer in 1940.)

**Khilafat Movement (1919–1924).** Indian Muslims protested the harsh treatment of the **Ottoman Caliph (Khalifa)** after WWI (Treaty of Sèvres). Led by the **Ali Brothers (Maulana Mohammad Ali & Shaukat Ali)**, Maulana Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan. Gandhi saw it as a chance for **Hindu-Muslim unity** and linked it with the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)**. The movement waned after **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk abolished the Caliphate (1924)**.

| Event | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Rowlatt Act | 1919 | Detention without trial; first all-India satyagraha |
| Jallianwala Bagh | 13 Apr 1919 | Dyer's firing, Amritsar; Tagore returns knighthood |
| Khilafat | 1919–24 | Ali Brothers; linked to Non-Cooperation |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Rowlatt Act (1919)** = detention without trial → first **all-India satyagraha**; **Jallianwala Bagh, 13 April 1919** (Dyer, Amritsar, Baisakhi) → Tagore renounced knighthood, Hunter Commission; **Khilafat (1919-24, Ali Brothers)** linked with **Non-Cooperation**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Rowlatt (no trial) → Jallianwala (13 Apr 1919, Dyer) → Khilafat (Ali Brothers) → Non-Cooperation (1920).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Dyer** ordered the firing; **O'Dwyer** was the Lt-Governor (assassinated by Udham Singh 1940) — don't swap. Jallianwala date = **13 April 1919**. Khilafat concerns the **Ottoman Caliph**, abolished by **Atatürk (1924)**.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"renounced knighthood after Jallianwala"* → **Rabindranath Tagore**; *"leaders of the Khilafat movement"* → **Ali Brothers**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Rowlatt Act 1919 (Anarchical & Revolutionary Crimes Act); Himalayan blunder.
- Jallianwala Bagh 13 Apr 1919, Dyer; Satyapal & Kitchlew; Hunter Commission; Tagore.
- Khilafat 1919-24; Ali Brothers, Azad; linked to Non-Cooperation; Caliphate abolished 1924.

## 🗺️ Geography: Area Focus — Adilabad & the Northern Gond Belt

**Adilabad**, Telangana's **northernmost region**, is a **forested, tribal (Gond/Kolam) and mineral-rich** plateau bordering Maharashtra — distinctive for its black soils, teak forests and Gond culture.

**Location & physiography.** Part of the old composite Adilabad district (now split into **Adilabad, Nirmal, Mancherial, Kumram Bheem-Asifabad**; verify). It lies on the **Deccan trap/Gondwana** terrain in the **Godavari basin** (the Godavari and its tributary **Pranhita, Penganga, Kadam** drain it). It has the state's **highest forest cover** and a relatively **cooler, higher-rainfall** climate.

**Resources & economy.** Rich in **coal** (**Singareni Collieries** — Mancherial/Bellampalli belt), **limestone** (cement plants), and forests (**teak, bamboo**). **Kaghaznagar** had a paper mill; **cement** industries cluster here. Cotton ("white gold") is a major crop; the region saw farmer distress historically.

**Tribal culture & heritage.** Home to the **Gond** and **Kolam** tribes; the freedom fighter **Komaram Bheem** ("**Jal, Jangal, Jameen**") hailed from here (district Kumram Bheem-Asifabad named after him). The **Nagoba Jatara** (Keslapur, Mesram clan of Gonds) and **Gussadi dance** (Dandari festival) are notable. The **Kadam** and **Sri Ram Sagar (Pochampad)** projects serve irrigation; **Kuntala, Pochera, Gayatri** waterfalls (on the Kadam/Godavari tributaries) and the **Kawal Tiger Reserve** (Jannaram) and **Pranahita WLS** are in the belt.
