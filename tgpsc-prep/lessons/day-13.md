## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 Parliament: Composition, Sessions, Bills, Budget & Committees

Parliament (`Art 79`) = **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**. It is the supreme legislative organ of the Union.

### 📖 Concept

**Composition.**

| House | Max strength | Term | Members |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) | **550** (530 states + 20 UTs) | 5 years (dissolvable) | Directly elected; `verify current 543` |
| **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States) | **250** (238 elected + **12 nominated**) | Permanent (1/3 retire every 2 yrs); 6-yr member term | Elected by state MLAs (STV); nominated for art/science/literature/social service |

*(The anti-defection **91st/erstwhile provisions** and the abolition of Anglo-Indian nomination by the **104th Amendment (2020)** are worth noting.)*

**Sessions.** Three usual sessions: **Budget (Feb–May), Monsoon (Jul–Sep), Winter (Nov–Dec)**. The **President summons** each House; the gap between two sessions **cannot exceed 6 months** (`Art 85`). **Prorogation** ends a session; **dissolution** (only Lok Sabha) ends the House. Key terms: **Quorum = 1/10** of total members; **Question Hour, Zero Hour** (not in rules), **Adjournment, Prorogation**.

**Types of bills:**
- **Ordinary bill** — either House; deadlock resolved by **joint sitting** (`Art 108`).
- **Money bill** (`Art 110`) — **only Lok Sabha** (on Speaker's certification); Rajya Sabha can only **delay 14 days / recommend**; no joint sitting.
- **Financial bill**, **Constitutional Amendment bill** (`Art 368`).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — A **Money Bill (Art 110)** can be introduced **only in the Lok Sabha** on the **Speaker's certification**; the Rajya Sabha can withhold it for a maximum of **14 days** and only **recommend** changes — **no joint sitting** applies to money bills.

**Budget (`Art 112` — Annual Financial Statement).** Stages: presentation → general discussion → **scrutiny by Departmental Standing Committees** → **demands for grants** (voting, only Lok Sabha) → **Appropriation Bill** (withdraw from Consolidated Fund) → **Finance Bill** (taxes). Key devices: **Cut Motions** (Policy/Economy/Token), **Vote on Account**, **Guillotine**, **Supplementary/Excess grants**.

**Parliamentary Committees:**
- **Financial committees:** **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** (chaired by Opposition; audits CAG report), **Estimates Committee** (only Lok Sabha members), **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)**.
- **Standing Committees** (Departmental, 24), **Ad hoc** committees, **Business Advisory, Rules, Privileges** committees.

💡 **Example:** When the CAG's audit report reveals wasteful expenditure, it is examined by the **Public Accounts Committee** — famously chaired by a **member of the Opposition** — the classic accountability mechanism.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Art 79** (Parliament), **Art 85** (sessions/6-month rule), **Art 108** (joint sitting), **Art 110** (money bill), **Art 112** (budget).
- **PAC = Opposition chair; Estimates Committee = only Lok Sabha (30 members)**.
- **Joint sitting** presided over by the **Lok Sabha Speaker**; never for money/constitutional-amendment bills.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**79 = Parliament, 85 = 6-month gap, 108 = joint sitting, 110 = money bill (LS only), 112 = budget.** PAC audits, Estimates estimates, COPU checks PSUs."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Joint sitting (Art 108) does NOT apply to Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills** — only to ordinary bills. And a Money Bill is introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, never the Rajya Sabha.

*(Only the Lok Sabha; the Rajya Sabha can delay it a maximum of 14 days.)*

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 The Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–1951)

The **Telangana Rebellion / Sayudha Poratam** was a **peasant-Communist armed revolt** against the **Nizam, the Razakars and the feudal (jagirdar/deshmukh) order** — one of India's largest peasant uprisings.

### 📖 Concept

**Causes.** Extreme feudal oppression: **vetti (forced/bonded labour)**, illegal exactions, land-grabbing by **deshmukhs/doras (landlords)**, and the Nizam's autocratic, communal rule (backed by the **Razakars** under **Kasim Razvi**).

**Course.**
- Led by the **Communist-wing Andhra Mahasabha** and the **Communist Party**; the spark is traditionally traced to **Kadavendi (Jangaon)** in **1946**, where **Doddi Komaraiah** was killed resisting a landlord — igniting the revolt.
- Peasants formed **village sanghams** and **guerrilla dalams (squads)**; at its height the movement controlled **~3,000 villages** across **Nalgonda, Warangal, Bhongir** and Khammam, redistributing land and abolishing vetti. *(Verify exact village count.)*
- Key leaders: **Ravi Narayana Reddy** (a towering figure; polled more votes than Nehru in 1952), **Baddam Yella Reddy**, **Chandra Rajeswara Rao**, **Arutla Ramachandra Reddy & Arutla Kamala Devi**, **Mallu Swarajyam**, **Chakali Ailamma** (the woman who defied a landlord over her land at Palakurthi — an iconic symbol).

**Climax & end.**
- **Operation Polo (Sept 1948)** — the Indian Army's **Police Action** ended the Nizam's rule and merged Hyderabad into India.
- The Communists continued the armed struggle even after 1948 (now against the Indian state's handling), but **withdrew it in 1951** on the party's decision (partly after the **Vinoba Bhave Bhoodan** movement began, and central promises of land reform).

**Outcomes.** Abolition of **vetti** and **jagirdari**; major impetus to **land reforms**; deep imprint on Telangana's political consciousness — later feeding the **statehood** sentiment.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Period | **1946–1951** |
| Against | Nizam, Razakars, deshmukhs/jagirdars, vetti |
| Led by | Communist Party / Andhra Mahasabha (radical wing) |
| Spark | **Kadavendi (1946), Doddi Komaraiah** |
| Icons | Ravi Narayana Reddy, Chakali Ailamma, Mallu Swarajyam |
| End | Withdrawn **1951** (after Operation Polo 1948) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Telangana Armed Struggle (1946–51)** was a **Communist-led peasant revolt** against the Nizam and feudal **vetti**; sparked at **Kadavendi (1946)** with **Doddi Komaraiah's** death, it controlled thousands of villages and was **withdrawn in 1951**.

💡 **Example:** **Chakali Ailamma's** refusal to surrender her land to a landlord at **Palakurthi** became the movement's rallying symbol — the peasant's defiance of feudal power. Expect a direct question on her.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement
### 🎯 The Government of India Act 1935 & Provincial Ministries

The **GoI Act 1935** was the **longest Act** passed by the British Parliament and the **direct source** of much of India's Constitution.

### 📖 Concept

**Background.** Product of the **Simon Report, Round Table Conferences, and the White Paper (1933)**. It had **321 sections and 10 schedules**.

**Key features:**
1. **All-India Federation** — proposed a federation of **British provinces + princely states**. It **never came into being** (princes didn't accede).
2. **Provincial Autonomy** — abolished **dyarchy in the provinces** (introduced by 1919 Act) and gave provinces **autonomous** self-government with responsible ministries; **dyarchy shifted to the Centre**.
3. **Dyarchy at the Centre** — federal subjects split into **Reserved** (Governor-General with advisers) and **Transferred** (ministers) — but this **never operated** (federation didn't form).
4. **Bicameral legislatures** in some provinces; expanded but **communal (separate) electorates** retained and extended.
5. **Federal Court (1937)** established (forerunner of the Supreme Court).
6. **RBI**, **Federal Public Service Commission / Provincial PSCs**, abolition of the **Council of India**.
7. Franchise extended to ~**10%** of the population (`verify`).

**Provincial elections & ministries (1937).** Elections held under the Act in **1937**; the **Congress won majorities** and formed ministries in **most provinces (8 of 11)**. The Congress ministries **resigned in 1939** (protesting India's inclusion in WWII without consultation) — the League observed this as **"Deliverance Day."**

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **GoI Act 1935** gave **provincial autonomy** (ended provincial dyarchy) and proposed an **All-India Federation** (never formed) with **dyarchy at the Centre** (never operated). Under it, **1937 provincial elections** brought **Congress ministries** in most provinces; they **resigned in 1939**.

💡 **Example:** The Constitution borrowed heavily from this Act — **federal scheme, office of Governor, emergency provisions, PSCs, Federal Court** — which is why the 1935 Act is called the **"skeleton" of the Indian Constitution**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Provincial autonomy** (ended provincial dyarchy); **federation proposed but never formed**; **centre dyarchy never operated**.
- **Federal Court 1937; RBI; extended communal electorates**.
- **1937 elections → Congress ministries in 8/11 provinces → resigned 1939** (WWII).
- Constitution's borrowings: **emergency, federal scheme, Governor, PSCs**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1935: provinces get autonomy (dyarchy dies there, moves to Centre), a federation is drawn but never built, and the RBI + Federal Court arrive.** 1937 Congress ministries → quit 1939."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Two "never happened" facts: the **All-India Federation never came into being**, and **dyarchy at the Centre never operated** — only **provincial autonomy** was actually implemented. Don't say the federation functioned.

**✅ Quick self-check:** What did the 1935 Act actually implement (vs propose), and what happened in 1937 and 1939?
*(Implemented provincial autonomy; the federation/central dyarchy never operated. 1937: Congress ministries in most provinces; 1939: they resigned over WWII.)*

## 🗺️ Subject 5: Telangana Geography (with Map)
### 🎯 Area Focus: Adilabad & the Northern Gond Belt

**Adilabad** — Telangana's northernmost frontier — is teak forests, the Gond and Kolam tribes, black cotton soil, and the Godavari's northern reach.

**🗺️ Reference map — Telangana regions (Adilabad & the northern Gond belt in the far north):**

![Telangana regions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

*(If the map doesn't display in email, open the lesson on GitHub — the SVG is at `tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg`.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Location & administration.** The undivided Adilabad district (northernmost, bordering **Maharashtra** to the north/west and **Chhattisgarh** to the northeast) was reorganised (2016) into **Adilabad, Nirmal, Mancherial, and Komaram Bheem Asifabad** (`verify exact list`).

**Physiography & geology.**
- Northern hills of the **Deccan plateau**; part is covered by **Deccan Trap basalt** (unlike the granite of southern Telangana), giving **black cotton soil (regur)** ideal for **cotton** — Adilabad is a major **cotton** belt ("white gold").
- Also **Gondwana coal-bearing rocks** in the **Pranhita–Godavari valley** — the **Singareni** coalfields (**Mancherial, Bellampalli, Ramagundam** belt).
- Drained by the **Godavari** and its tributaries **Penganga, Wardha, Pranhita, Kadem**.

**Forests & tribes (the "Gond belt").**
- Among Telangana's **densest forests** — **teak (Tectona)**, bamboo; **Kawal Tiger Reserve** (Adilabad) and **Pranahita Wildlife Sanctuary**.
- Home to the **Gonds, Kolams, Naikpods, Pardhans, Thotis** tribes; strong **Adivasi culture** — **Gussadi dance** (Dandari-Gussadi festival of the Raj Gonds, associated with **Diwali/Dussehra**), Kolam crafts.
- **Komaram Bheem** (Day 12) and **Ramji Gond** are the region's tribal icons; **Jodeghat** memorial (Komaram Bheem Asifabad).

**Heritage & sites:**
- **Basara** (Nirmal) — **Gnana Saraswati Temple**, one of India's few Saraswati temples (on the Godavari).
- **Nirmal** — famous **Nirmal toys and paintings** (GI craft).
- **Kuntala, Pochera, Gayatri** waterfalls (Adilabad/Nirmal) — the state's tallest falls (**Kuntala** on the Kadem).
- **Kadem project**, **SRSP** reach; **Ramagundam** (NTPC super-thermal plant + fertiliser).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Adilabad's north has **Deccan basalt → black cotton soil (cotton belt)** and **Gondwana coal (Singareni: Mancherial/Ramagundam)**; it holds **Kawal Tiger Reserve**, dense **teak forests**, the **Gond/Kolam** tribes (**Gussadi dance**), **Basara Saraswati temple**, and **Kuntala falls**.

💡 **Example:** "Geology + culture of Adilabad" → **basalt/black-cotton-soil + Singareni coal** (geology) with **Gond tribes/Gussadi + Basara + Nirmal toys** (culture) — the classic area-focus pairing, and it explains why **cotton** and **coal/thermal power** cluster here.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Black cotton soil (regur)** from **Deccan Trap** → **cotton**; **Singareni coal** → **Ramagundam** thermal.
- **Kawal Tiger Reserve** (Adilabad); **Kuntala Falls** (tallest in Telangana, on the Kadem).
- **Basara Gnana Saraswati Temple** (Nirmal); **Nirmal toys/paintings** (GI).
- Tribes: **Gond, Kolam, Naikpod**; **Gussadi/Dandari** festival; icon **Komaram Bheem**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Adilabad = basalt + black cotton (white gold) + Singareni coal; teak forests & Kawal tigers; Gonds' Gussadi; Basara Saraswati; Kuntala falls.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Adilabad's soil is **black cotton (regur) from Deccan basalt**, **not** the red granite soil of southern Telangana (Mahabubnagar). And **Basara** hosts a rare **Saraswati** temple (not Shiva/Shakti). Verify the reorganised district list and falls heights.
