## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 The Prime Minister & the Council of Ministers

The President is the nominal head; the **Prime Minister** is the **real executive** head who leads the **Council of Ministers (CoM)** — the engine of parliamentary government.

### 📖 Concept

**Appointment (`Art 75`).** The PM is **appointed by the President**. By convention, the President invites the **leader of the majority party** (or coalition) in the **Lok Sabha**. A person **not a member of either House** can become PM but must get elected within **6 months**. The PM can be a member of **either House** (Rajya Sabha PMs: Indira Gandhi, Deval Gowda's era, Manmohan Singh).

**Council of Ministers (`Art 74, 75`).**
- `Art 74`: There **shall be** a CoM **headed by the PM** to aid and advise the President; advice is **binding** (42nd/44th Amendments) — President can return it **once** for reconsideration.
- Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President** (i.e., effectively the PM); the CoM is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (`Art 75(3)`) — a **no-confidence** motion against the CoM, if passed, brings down the whole government.
- **Individual responsibility** (`Art 75(2)`): a minister holds office during the President's pleasure.
- **91st Amendment (2003):** total size of CoM (incl. PM) **≤ 15% of Lok Sabha** strength; a defector is disqualified from being a minister.

**Ranks of ministers:** **Cabinet Ministers** (head major ministries, part of Cabinet), **Ministers of State (MoS)** (independent charge or attached), and **Deputy Ministers**. The **Cabinet** is a smaller core body; the **Kitchen Cabinet** is an informal inner circle.

| Body | Basis | Members |
|---|---|---|
| Council of Ministers | `Art 74/75` | All ministers (Cabinet + MoS + Deputy) |
| Cabinet | Convention (given constitutional mention by 44th Amdt in Art 352) | Senior-most ministers only |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (`Art 75(3)`); the **91st Amendment** caps the CoM at **15% of Lok Sabha** strength. The PM is **appointed** by the President (not elected by Parliament).

**Powers/role of the PM:** leader of the CoM (allocates portfolios, can recommend dismissal), links President and Cabinet, advises the President on key appointments (recommends dissolution of Lok Sabha), chairs **NITI Aayog**, leads in Parliament, and is chief spokesperson of the government.

**Death/resignation of the PM** dissolves the entire CoM. If the PM dies, the CoM cannot continue; the President appoints a new PM.

💡 **Example:** When a no-confidence motion is passed against a single unpopular minister's policy, the **whole Council** must resign — because responsibility is **collective**, not individual, to the House. That's the essence of the Westminster system India adopted.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Art 74** = there shall be a CoM to aid & advise; **Art 75** = appointment, responsibility, oath.
- **91st Amendment (2003):** ≤ **15%** of Lok Sabha; anti-defection link.
- CoM is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha only** (not Rajya Sabha).
- A non-member can be PM/minister for max **6 months** (`verify` in the specific question).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**74 = advice (binding), 75 = appointment + collective responsibility, 91 = 15% cap.** PM appointed, not elected; CoM answers to the Lok Sabha."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Collective responsibility is to the **Lok Sabha**, NOT the Rajya Sabha (the RS can't remove the government). And the **15% cap** (91st Amdt) is on the **whole CoM including the PM**, computed on **Lok Sabha** strength.

*(The Lok Sabha; ≤ 15% of Lok Sabha strength, per the 91st Amendment.)*

## 🏛️ Subject 2: Telangana Schemes & Important Incidents
### 🎯 Irrigation Policy: Kaleshwaram Commission & Palamuru-Rangareddy LIS Status

Telangana's identity is bound to **lift irrigation** — carrying Godavari and Krishna water onto its plateau. Two projects dominate current affairs: **Kaleshwaram** (and its judicial commission) and **Palamuru-Rangareddy**.

### 📖 Concept

**Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Scheme (KLIS)** — on the **Godavari** at **Medigadda (Lakshmi barrage)**, Jayashankar Bhupalpally district.
- World's largest multi-stage lift irrigation project; lifts Godavari water through a chain of **barrages (Medigadda, Annaram, Sundilla)**, pump houses, tunnels and reservoirs (**Mid Manair, Mallanna Sagar** etc.).
- **The incident:** in **October 2023**, piers of the **Medigadda (Lakshmi) barrage sank/were damaged**, raising questions on design and quality.
- **Kaleshwaram Commission:** the state government (post-2023) appointed a **judicial commission** (headed by a retired High Court judge — **Justice P. C. Ghose**) to probe the planning, execution and cost of KLIS. *(Verify the commission's latest findings/status before the exam.)*
- The **National Dam Safety Authority (NDSA)** also examined the Medigadda failure.

**Palamuru-Rangareddy Lift Irrigation Scheme (PRLIS)** — on the **Krishna**, drawing from **Srisailam** backwaters to irrigate the drought-prone **Palamuru (Mahabubnagar) – Rangareddy – Nalgonda** belt.
- A mega lift scheme (huge pumping heads) intended to provide irrigation + drinking water to southern Telangana.
- **Inter-state angle:** objections from Andhra Pradesh and questions on **Krishna water allocation** (the **Krishna Water Disputes Tribunal / apportionment** issue) and environmental clearance. *(Verify current commissioning status and allocated TMC before the exam.)*

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Kaleshwaram (Godavari)** — largest multi-stage lift scheme; its **Medigadda barrage** piers sank in **Oct 2023**, triggering a **judicial commission (Justice P. C. Ghose)** and NDSA review. **Palamuru-Rangareddy (Krishna, from Srisailam)** waters southern Telangana amid inter-state allocation disputes.

**Policy themes to know:** Telangana's shift to **lift irrigation** (because rivers flow at lower elevation than the cultivable plateau), reliance on **barrage + pump + reservoir** chains, the debate over **cost/financial burden and re-engineering**, and **inter-state river water sharing** (Godavari & Krishna tribunals, the **Krishna River Management Board / Godavari RMB**).

💡 **Example:** The Medigadda pier subsidence is the textbook "important incident" — pair it with the **Kaleshwaram Commission** (who probes) and the **NDSA** (dam safety) for a complete answer.

- **KRMB/GRMB** = Krishna & Godavari River Management Boards.

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Tribal & Peasant Revolts: Ramji Gond & Komaram Bheem

Before the organised Armed Struggle, **tribal (Adivasi) revolts** against the Nizam and forest/land exploitation lit the flame — most iconically **Ramji Gond** and **Komaram Bheem** ("**Jal, Jangal, Jameen**").

### 📖 Concept

**Ramji Gond (Ramji Gond's revolt, ~1857–1860).**
- A **Gond leader** from the **Adilabad/Nirmal** region who rebelled against the **Nizam** and the **British** around the time of the **1857 revolt**.
- Led Gond and **Rohilla** fighters against oppressive rule and forest exploitation.
- Captured and **hanged (1860)** at **Nirmal** — tradition holds that **~1,000 rebels were hanged on a large banyan tree**, remembered as **"Veyyi Urula Marri" (the banyan of a thousand nooses)** near Nirmal. *(Verify exact numbers/dates.)*

**Komaram Bheem (1901–1940).**
- A **Gond tribal leader** from **Adilabad** (Sankepalli/Jodeghat area) who led an armed resistance against the **Nizam's / landlords' (jagirdars')** exploitation and forest laws.
- Coined/popularised the slogan **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"** (Water, Forest, Land) — the tribal right over natural resources.
- Fought for the tribal **gutti** (podu/shifting cultivation) lands around **Babejhari and Jodeghat**; killed in an encounter with the Nizam's police at **Jodeghat on ~ 27 October 1940** (Ashwayuja Pournami). *(Verify date.)*
- His revolt prompted the Nizam to commission the anthropologist **Christoph von Fürer-Haimendorf** to study tribal conditions, leading to some **tribal protection measures**.
- Commemorated widely; **Komaram Bheem Asifabad** district is named after him; **Jodeghat** has a memorial.

| Leader | Region | Cause | Fate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ramji Gond** | Adilabad/Nirmal | Anti-Nizam/British, forest & land | Hanged at Nirmal, **1860** |
| **Komaram Bheem** | Adilabad (Jodeghat) | "Jal Jangal Jameen", tribal land | Killed at Jodeghat, **1940** |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Komaram Bheem (1901–1940)** gave the slogan **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"** and died at **Jodeghat (1940)** fighting the Nizam; his revolt led to **Fürer-Haimendorf's** tribal study. **Ramji Gond** was hanged at **Nirmal (1860)** ("thousand-noose banyan").

💡 **Example:** "Jal, Jangal, Jameen" → **Komaram Bheem**; "thousand nooses banyan at Nirmal" → **Ramji Gond**. These two mnemonics answer most one-mark questions.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Komaram Bheem: slogan Jal-Jangal-Jameen; Jodeghat; 1940; Fürer-Haimendorf** aftermath; **Komaram Bheem Asifabad** district.
- **Ramji Gond: Gond+Rohilla; Nirmal; 1860; Veyyi Urula Marri.**
- Both are **Gond tribal** leaders from **Adilabad** region.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Ramji (1857-60, Nirmal, thousand-noose banyan) → Komaram Bheem (1940, Jodeghat, Jal-Jangal-Jameen).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Komaram Bheem was NOT part of the 1857 revolt** (he lived 1901–1940); **Ramji Gond** is the one linked to the **1857** era. Don't merge the two. Verify exact dates/figures (the "1000 hanged" is traditional).

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement
### 🎯 Revolutionary Movement Phase II (Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekar Azad, Surya Sen)

Alongside Gandhian mass movements, a **revolutionary** stream used armed action. **Phase II (late 1920s–early 1930s)** is defined by the **HSRA** and the **Chittagong** uprising.

### 📖 Concept

**Hindustan Republican Association (HRA, 1924) → HSRA (1928).**
- **HRA** founded 1924 (Ramprasad Bismil, Sachindranath Sanyal, Jogesh Chatterjee).
- **Kakori Conspiracy (Aug 1925):** HRA revolutionaries looted a train carrying government money at **Kakori** (near Lucknow). **Ramprasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Roshan Singh, Rajendra Lahiri** were **hanged (1927)**.
- Reorganised in **1928** at **Feroz Shah Kotla (Delhi)** as the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)** — adding a **socialist** goal — under **Chandrashekar Azad**, with **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru, Bhagwati Charan Vohra**.

**Key actions:**
- **Saunders killing (Dec 1928, Lahore):** to avenge **Lala Lajpat Rai's** death (from the anti-Simon lathi charge), Bhagat Singh & Rajguru shot police officer **J. P. Saunders**.
- **Central Assembly bomb (8 April 1929):** **Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt** threw (non-lethal) bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly and courted arrest, shouting **"Inquilab Zindabad"**, to "make the deaf hear" — protesting the Public Safety & Trade Disputes Bills.
- **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru hanged on 23 March 1931** (Lahore) — **Shaheed Diwas**.
- **Chandrashekar Azad** died fighting police at **Alfred Park, Allahabad (27 Feb 1931)** — never captured alive ("Azad").

**Chittagong Armoury Raid (April 1930):** led by **Surya Sen (Masterda)** in Bengal — revolutionaries seized the **Chittagong armoury** and declared a provisional government. Notable women revolutionaries **Pritilata Waddedar** and **Kalpana Dutt** participated. Surya Sen was **hanged in 1934**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **HSRA (1928, Chandrashekar Azad)** carried out the **Saunders killing (1928)** and the **Central Assembly bomb (8 April 1929, Bhagat Singh & B. K. Dutt)**. **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru** were **hanged on 23 March 1931**. **Surya Sen** led the **Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930)**.

💡 **Example:** The Assembly bomb was **deliberately non-lethal** — the aim was **arrest and a public trial platform** ("propaganda by deed"), not assassination. That distinction (protest vs killing) is a common exam nuance.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **HRA (1924) → Kakori (1925) → HSRA (1928)**.
- **Saunders killing 1928** (avenging Lajpat Rai); **Assembly bomb 8 April 1929** (Bhagat Singh + B. K. Dutt).
- **23 March 1931** — Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru hanged.
- **Surya Sen — Chittagong Armoury Raid, 1930**; **Pritilata Waddedar**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Kakori (1925) → HSRA (1928) → Saunders (1928) → Assembly bomb (1929) → hanged 23 March 1931.** Azad stayed free to the end; Surya Sen took Chittagong."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Central Assembly bomb (1929)** was thrown by **Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt** (not Rajguru); Bhagat Singh was hanged for the **Saunders killing** case (Lahore Conspiracy), not the Assembly bomb (for which he got life). Keep the two cases distinct.

**✅ Quick self-check:** Who led the Chittagong Armoury Raid, and in which year were Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru hanged?
*(Surya Sen, 1930; hanged 23 March 1931.)*

## 🗺️ Subject 5: Telangana Geography (with Map)
### 🎯 Area Focus: Warangal & the Kakatiya Belt

**Warangal** (Orugallu) — the **Kakatiya capital** — anchors north-central Telangana: granite uplands, tank irrigation, and a dense heritage of temples and forts.

**🗺️ Reference map — Telangana regions (Warangal & Kakatiya belt in the north-central zone):**

![Telangana regions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

*(If the map doesn't display in email, open the lesson on GitHub — the SVG is at `tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg`.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Location & administration.** The old Warangal district was reorganised (2016) into **Warangal (Urban → Hanamkonda + Warangal), Jangaon, Jayashankar Bhupalpally, Mahabubabad, Mulugu** (`verify exact list`). Warangal is Telangana's **second-largest city** and a major regional hub.

**Physiography & geology.** Part of the **Archaean granite–gneiss** Deccan plateau; **rolling granite uplands** with characteristic **tors/boulders**; **red and some black soils**. Drained by the **Godavari tributaries** (e.g., **Akeru, Munneru**). The area is famous for **tank (kunta) irrigation** — the **Kakatiya-era chain tanks**.

**Water & irrigation.** The **Kakatiyas built great tanks** — **Pakhal, Ramappa, Laknavaram, Ghanpur** — cascading tank systems still in use. Modern schemes: **Devadula (J. Chokka Rao) Lift Irrigation Scheme** (Godavari lift to Warangal uplands) and **SRSP** canals; **Mission Kakatiya** (tank restoration programme) is named for this heritage.

**Cultural & heritage crown (the "Kakatiya belt"):**
- **Warangal Fort (Orugallu)** — Kakatiya capital; the iconic **Kirti Toranas (Kakatiya Kala Thoranam)** — the state **emblem** motif.
- **Thousand Pillar Temple (Rudreshwara/Hanamkonda, 1163 CE)** — Kakatiya trikutalaya (three shrines), built by **Rudradeva**.
- **Ramappa Temple (Palampet, Mulugu, 1213 CE)** — built by **Recharla Rudra** (general of **Ganapati Deva**); **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021)** — famed for **floating (lightweight) bricks** and **Nandi**, dedicated to **Ramalingeswara (Shiva)**.
- **Bhadrakali Temple** (Warangal); **Laknavaram** suspension-bridge lake (tourism); **Pakhal & Eturnagaram Wildlife Sanctuary** (Mulugu).
- **Medaram (Mulugu)** — **Sammakka-Saralamma Jatara**, Asia's largest tribal congregation (biennial).
- **Handloom:** the region is linked to weaving traditions; **Warangal durries/carpets** are a GI product.
