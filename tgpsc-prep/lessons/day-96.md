## 🏛️ Polity: Prime Minister & Council of Ministers

The **real executive** of the Union is the **Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister** (`Art 74, 75`); the President is the nominal head who acts on their advice.

**Prime Minister (`Art 75`).** Appointed by the **President** — by convention, the **leader of the majority party** in the Lok Sabha. No specific qualifications beyond being an MP (or becoming one within 6 months); can be from **either House**. The PM is the **head of government**, **chairman of NITI Aayog**, principal link between President and Cabinet, and leader of the Lok Sabha. **Powers:** recommends ministers, allocates portfolios, chairs the Cabinet, advises the President on dissolution of Lok Sabha, etc.

**Council of Ministers (`Art 74`).** Aids and advises the President (`Art 74(1)` — advice binding, one reconsideration). Three categories: **Cabinet Ministers** (top, head major ministries, form the Cabinet), **Ministers of State** (independent charge or attached), and **Deputy Ministers**. The **Cabinet** is the inner ring that directs policy.

**Key provisions.**
- `Art 75(1A)` (**91st Amendment, 2003**): total ministers ≤ **15% of Lok Sabha** strength.
- `Art 75(1B)`: a **disqualified defector** cannot be a minister.
- `Art 75(3)`: Council is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (cornerstone of parliamentary government) — a no-confidence vote against the Council forces resignation.
- `Art 75(2)`: ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President**.
- `Art 78`: PM's duties to furnish information to the President.
- **Individual responsibility** (`Art 75(2)`) and the **oath** administered by the President.

**Collective vs individual responsibility.** **Collective** = the whole Council sinks or swims with Lok Sabha confidence ("**all for one**"). **Individual** = a minister answerable to the PM/President for their department.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — PM appointed by President (majority leader); **Council collectively responsible to Lok Sabha** (`Art 75(3)`). **91st Amendment (2003)**: ministers ≤ **15% of LS**. Advice under `Art 74` is **binding** (one reconsideration).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**PM = majority leader (Art 75); Council ≤15% LS (91st Amd); collectively responsible to Lok Sabha.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Collective responsibility is to the **Lok Sabha** (not Rajya Sabha). The **15% cap** came via the **91st Amendment (2003)**. A PM can be a **Rajya Sabha** member. `Art 74` advice is binding (42nd/44th), with **one** reconsideration.

💡 **Example:** If the Lok Sabha passes a **no-confidence motion**, the **entire** Council of Ministers must resign — collective responsibility in action.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Council: Cabinet/MoS/Deputy; Art 74 binding advice.
- 91st Amendment: ministers ≤15% of LS; anti-defection bar.
- Collective responsibility (Art 75(3)) to Lok Sabha.

## 🤝 Schemes: Irrigation Policy — Kaleshwaram & Palamuru-Rangareddy

Telangana's irrigation strategy centres on **lift irrigation** of Godavari and Krishna water to drought-prone uplands — headlined by **Kaleshwaram (KLIS)** and **Palamuru-Rangareddy (PRLIS)**.

**Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Scheme (KLIS).** On the **Godavari** (at the Pranhita-Godavari confluence near **Medigadda, Kaleshwaram**, Jayashankar-Bhupalpally), it is described as one of the world's largest **multi-stage lift irrigation** projects, lifting water through barrages (**Medigadda, Annaram, Sundilla**), pump houses and reservoirs (e.g., **Mallannasagar**) to irrigate large areas across many districts. It has faced **structural issues at the Medigadda barrage (pier sinking)**, now under a **judicial commission (the Ghose Commission)** and NDSA scrutiny. (Verify current status, capacity and cost.)

**Palamuru-Rangareddy LIS (PRLIS).** On the **Krishna** (Srisailam backwaters), it lifts water to the **drought-prone Palamuru (Mahabubnagar)-Rangareddy** uplands via reservoirs, addressing the historically dry south. (Verify stage/status — it has been in phased commissioning.)

**Policy context.** Earlier projects: **Sri Ram Sagar (SRSP/Pochampad)**, **Nagarjunasagar** (shared), **Jurala**, **Kadam**, and **Mission Kakatiya** (minor tank restoration). The thrust is **lift schemes** (because much of Telangana's terrain is above river level). Inter-state water sharing (Krishna via **KRMB**, Godavari via **GRMB**) and tribunal allocations constrain usage. (All figures — ayacut, TMC, cost — are **verify latest official figure**.)

| Project | River | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Kaleshwaram (KLIS) | Godavari | Multi-stage lift; Medigadda barrage |
| Palamuru-Rangareddy | Krishna | Drought-prone south uplands |
| SRSP/Jurala/Kadam | Godavari/Krishna | Older reservoirs |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Kaleshwaram (KLIS)** = Godavari multi-stage lift (Medigadda-Annaram-Sundilla barrages; Medigadda pier-sinking under judicial commission). **Palamuru-Rangareddy (PRLIS)** = Krishna lift to drought-prone south. Verify all capacity/cost figures.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Kaleshwaram = Godavari lift (Medigadda); Palamuru-Rangareddy = Krishna lift (south). Lift schemes because terrain is above the rivers.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Kaleshwaram is on the Godavari**; **Palamuru-Rangareddy on the Krishna** — don't swap rivers. Medigadda barrage issues are **current & litigated** — write "verify latest official figure/status." KRMB (Krishna) vs GRMB (Godavari) river boards.

## 🏺 History: Telangana Folk & Performing Arts

Telangana's **folk and performing arts** are a rich cultural layer — frequently tested for the **art form, community and region**.

**Perini Shivatandavam** — an ancient, vigorous **male warrior dance** dedicated to **Shiva**, revived from the **Kakatiya** era (associated with Ramappa temple sculptures); re-popularised by **Nataraja Ramakrishna**.

**Oggu Katha** — a ballad/storytelling folk form by the **Oggu/Kuruma-Golla** community praising **Mallanna, Beerappa, Yellamma**; narrative with song and percussion.

**Burra Katha** — a narrative art with a lead performer (with a **tambura**) and two co-performers (with **gummeta/dakki** drums); used for social/political messaging (e.g., during the freedom and Telangana movements).

**Chindu Bhagavatam / Yakshaganam** — dance-drama (Chindu Madigas); **Gussadi** — Gond tribal dance (Dandari festival, Adilabad); **Dappu** — a powerful drum/folk dance; **Kolatam** — stick dance; **Golla Suddulu** — Yadava ballads; **Veedhi Bhagavatham**, **Chekka Bhajana**, **Bonalu Potharaju** performances.

| Art form | Nature / Community |
|---|---|
| Perini Shivatandavam | Male warrior dance (Shiva; Kakatiya) |
| Oggu Katha | Ballad (Oggu/Kuruma-Golla; Mallanna) |
| Burra Katha | Narrative (tambura + gummeta) |
| Gussadi | Gond tribal dance (Dandari) |
| Dappu / Kolatam | Drum dance / stick dance |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Perini Shivatandavam** = Kakatiya-era male warrior dance (Shiva), revived by **Nataraja Ramakrishna**; **Oggu Katha** = Oggu-community ballad (Mallanna); **Burra Katha** = tambura + two gummeta drums narrative; **Gussadi** = Gond dance (Dandari, Adilabad).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Perini = warrior/Shiva (Kakatiya); Oggu Katha = Mallanna ballad; Burra Katha = tambura storytelling; Gussadi = Gond; Dappu = drum.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Perini Shivatandavam** revived by **Nataraja Ramakrishna**; it is a **male** dance (warrior). **Gussadi** is a **Gond (Adilabad)** tribal dance (Dandari festival). Burra Katha's lead instrument is the **tambura** (co-performers use gummeta/dakki).

💡 **Example:** Pair *"Kakatiya-era warrior dance of Shiva"* → **Perini Shivatandavam**; *"Gond tribal dance in the Dandari festival"* → **Gussadi**.

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Civil Disobedience, Round Tables & Poona Pact

The early 1930s saw Gandhi's **Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM)**, the **Round Table Conferences**, and the **Poona Pact** on separate electorates for the depressed classes.

**Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34).** Launched with the **Dandi Salt March (12 March – 6 April 1930)** — Gandhi walked ~**240 miles** from **Sabarmati to Dandi** to break the **salt law**. CDM involved **breaking laws** (not just non-cooperation): salt satyagraha, boycott of foreign cloth, no-tax campaigns, forest-law defiance. Mass participation (women prominently). The government responded with mass arrests.

**Round Table Conferences (London).**
- **First RTC (1930–31):** Congress **boycotted** (leaders jailed).
- **Second RTC (1931):** Gandhi attended (after the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact, March 1931**, which suspended CDM); deadlock over minorities/separate electorates.
- **Third RTC (1932):** Congress absent; led to the **Government of India Act 1935**.

**Communal Award & Poona Pact (1932).** British PM **Ramsay MacDonald's Communal Award (Aug 1932)** granted **separate electorates to the Depressed Classes (Dalits)**. Gandhi, opposing separation of Dalits from the Hindu fold, began a **fast unto death** in **Yerwada jail**. The **Poona Pact (24/25 Sept 1932)** between **Gandhi and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** replaced separate electorates with **reserved seats within the general (joint) electorate** for the Depressed Classes (increased number of reserved seats).

| Event | Year | Highlight |
|---|---|---|
| Dandi March / CDM | 1930 | Salt law broken; Sabarmati→Dandi |
| Gandhi-Irwin Pact | 1931 | CDM suspended; 2nd RTC |
| Communal Award | 1932 | Separate electorates for Dalits |
| Poona Pact | 1932 | Gandhi-Ambedkar; reserved seats (joint electorate) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **CDM (1930)** began with the **Dandi Salt March**; **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)** → 2nd RTC; **Communal Award (1932, MacDonald)** gave Dalits separate electorates; **Poona Pact (1932, Gandhi-Ambedkar)** replaced them with **reserved seats in a joint electorate**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Dandi 1930 (salt) → Gandhi-Irwin 1931 → 2nd RTC → Communal Award 1932 → Poona Pact (Gandhi-Ambedkar, reserved seats).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Poona Pact (1932)** replaced **separate electorates** (Communal Award) with **reserved seats in a joint/general electorate** — it did **not** abolish reservation. **Gandhi-Irwin Pact = 1931** (suspended CDM for 2nd RTC). Dandi March started **12 March 1930**.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"pact between Gandhi and Ambedkar, 1932"* → **Poona Pact**; *"event starting Civil Disobedience"* → **Dandi Salt March**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- CDM 1930: Dandi March (Sabarmati→Dandi, salt law), no-tax, boycott.
- Gandhi-Irwin Pact 1931 → 2nd RTC (Gandhi attends).
- Communal Award 1932 (MacDonald) — separate electorates for Depressed Classes.
- Poona Pact 1932 (Gandhi-Ambedkar) — reserved seats, joint electorate.

## 🗺️ Geography: Area Focus — Khammam-Bhadradri

The **Khammam-Bhadradri (Kothagudem)** region in **south-eastern Telangana** is a **forested, coal-and-river** belt on the **Godavari**, bordering Andhra Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, with strong tribal (Koya) presence.

**Location & physiography.** Part of old composite Khammam (now **Khammam** and **Bhadradri-Kothagudem**; verify). The **Godavari** forms its eastern/south-eastern boundary (and the famous **Bhadrachalam** temple town on its banks). Terrain includes the **Eastern Ghats fringe**, dense forests, and fertile river plains. It is drained by the **Godavari** and tributaries (**Kinnerasani**, **Munneru**).

**Resources & economy.** **Coal** — the **Singareni Collieries** have major mines at **Kothagudem, Yellandu, Manuguru**; the **Kothagudem Thermal Power Station (KTPS)** is a key power plant. Other: the **Bhadrachalam paper mill (ITC)**, forests (teak, bamboo, tendu), and **paddy/chilli/cotton** agriculture. **Kinnerasani** reservoir/sanctuary.

**Heritage & sites.** **Bhadrachalam** (Sri Sita Ramachandra Swamy temple — major Vaishnava pilgrimage, Rama); **Kinnerasani Wildlife Sanctuary** (deer park); **Parnasala** (Rama's exile site, legend); tribal Koya culture; the **Papikondala/Papi Hills** gorge on the Godavari (shared with AP). Historically part of the Bhadrachalam/Godavari agency areas.
