## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Fundamental Rights — Articles 12, 13 & 14

### Deep Dive

**Article 12 — Definition of "State".** Fundamental Rights (FRs) are mostly enforceable **against the State**, so Art 12 defines it broadly to include:
- the **Government and Parliament of India** (executive + legislature of the Union),
- the **Government and Legislature of each State**,
- **all local authorities** (municipalities, panchayats, etc.), and
- **"other authorities"** — statutory & non-statutory bodies performing governmental/public functions (interpreted expansively by the courts to include instrumentalities/agencies of the State).

**Article 13 — Judicial review.** Any **law inconsistent with FRs is void**. Art 13 covers **pre-constitutional** laws (void to the extent of inconsistency — **Doctrine of Eclipse**) and **post-constitutional** laws (void ab initio). "**Law**" includes ordinances, orders, bye-laws, rules, regulations, notifications, customs and usages. Key doctrines: **severability**, **eclipse**, and **no waiver** of FRs. (Art 13 is the textual basis of **judicial review**.)

**Article 14 — Equality.** "The State shall not deny to any person **equality before the law** or the **equal protection of the laws**."
- **Equality before law** — a *negative* concept (British origin, Dicey's Rule of Law): no one is above the law.
- **Equal protection of laws** — a *positive* concept (American origin): like should be treated alike.
- Allows **reasonable classification** (intelligible differentia + rational nexus to the objective) but **forbids class legislation**.
- Modern test: **non-arbitrariness** (*E.P. Royappa*, *Maneka Gandhi*) — arbitrary action violates Art 14.
- Exceptions: **Art 361** immunity for President/Governor; diplomatic immunities.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Art 12** defines "State" (incl. "other authorities"); **Art 13** makes inconsistent laws void (**judicial review**, eclipse/severability); **Art 14** = **equality before law** (negative) + **equal protection** (positive), permitting **reasonable classification**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Art 14 forbids **class legislation** but **permits reasonable classification**. FRs **cannot be waived**. "Equality before law" is **British/negative**, "equal protection" is **American/positive** — don't swap.

💡 **Example:** A law giving special maternity benefits to women is valid under Art 14 as **reasonable classification** (intelligible differentia + rational nexus), not discrimination.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Art 12: State = Union/State govt+legislature + local + other authorities.
- Art 13: void if inconsistent; eclipse (pre-), void ab initio (post-); no waiver.
- Art 14: equality before law (negative) + equal protection (positive).
- Reasonable classification test; non-arbitrariness (Maneka Gandhi).

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which article is the basis of judicial review of laws violating FRs?* → **Article 13**.

## 🗳️ Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Irrigation Policy — Kaleshwaram Commission & Palamuru-Rangareddy Status

### Deep Dive

**Kaleshwaram (KLIS) inquiry.** The **Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Scheme** on the Godavari — with its **Medigadda (Laxmi), Annaram and Sundilla** barrages — became the subject of scrutiny after **structural distress at the Medigadda barrage** (pier sinking/damage reported around **October 2023**). Following this:
- A **Commission of Inquiry** (headed by a retired High Court/Supreme Court judge — **Justice P.C. Ghose**) was constituted by the state (2024) to examine **design, construction, cost escalation and decision-making**.
- The **National Dam Safety Authority (NDSA)** examined the barrage failures and recommended remedial measures.
*(Verify the commission's latest findings, terms of reference and status before the exam.)*

**Palamuru-Rangareddy LIS.** A major **Krishna-basin lift scheme** to carry water from the **Srisailam** reservoir backwaters to the drought-prone **Palamuru (Mahabubnagar)–Rangareddy** region. It is being **commissioned in phases**; *verify the latest completion/commissioning status and inter-state (Krishna water-sharing) clearances.*

**Policy themes:** dam safety, cost-benefit and design audits, inter-state river-water disputes (Krishna & Godavari tribunals/boards — **KRMB, GRMB**), and the shift from gravity to **lift** irrigation.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Kaleshwaram** inquiry (Justice **P.C. Ghose** commission, 2024) followed **Medigadda** barrage distress (Oct 2023); the **NDSA** examined dam safety. **Palamuru-Rangareddy** lifts **Krishna (Srisailam)** water to Mahabubnagar-Rangareddy.

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Statehood Movement 2001-2014 (TRS, JACs, Sri Krishna Committee)

### Deep Dive

**Founding of TRS.** The **Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS)** was formed on **`27 April 2001`** by **K. Chandrashekar Rao (KCR)** with the single-point agenda of a **separate Telangana state**, reviving the dormant demand.

**Escalation (2009-2011).**
- **KCR's fast-unto-death (Nov 2009)** brought a breakthrough: on **`9 December 2009`**, Union Home Minister **P. Chidambaram** announced that the **process of forming Telangana would be initiated** — but it was **put on hold on `23 December 2009`** after protests in the Andhra region.
- The **Sri Krishna Committee** (Justice **B.N. Srikrishna**) was constituted in **Feb 2010**; its report (**Dec 2010**) gave **six options** ranging from status quo to bifurcation.
- Mass mobilisation followed: the **Telangana Joint Action Committee (JAC)** under **Prof. M. Kodandaram**, the **"Million March" (2011)**, **Sagara Haram**, and the **Sakala Janula Samme (general strike, 2011)**.

**Culmination (2013-2014).**
- On **`30 July 2013`**, the Congress Working Committee/UPA resolved to create Telangana.
- The **Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014** was passed by Parliament (**Feb 2014**), and **Telangana was formed as the 29th state on `2 June 2014`**, with **KCR as the first Chief Minister**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **TRS founded `27 Apr 2001`** (KCR). **`9 Dec 2009`** Telangana process announced; **Sri Krishna Committee** (2010, six options); **AP Reorganisation Act 2014** → **Telangana on `2 June 2014`**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **9 Dec 2009** announcement was **put on hold on 23 Dec 2009** — a two-step sequence often confused. The **Sri Krishna Committee (2010)** gave **six options**, not a single recommendation.

💡 **Example:** The **Sakala Janula Samme (2011)** — a 42-day general strike including Singareni coal workers and RTC — is a classic "mass-mobilisation" PYQ of the movement.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- TRS: 27 Apr 2001 (KCR).
- 9 Dec 2009 announcement → 23 Dec 2009 hold.
- Sri Krishna Committee (Feb 2010; report Dec 2010, six options).
- Telangana JAC (Kodandaram); AP Reorg. Act 2014; state on 2 June 2014.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Who headed the committee that examined the Telangana demand in 2010?* → **Justice B.N. Srikrishna**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Integration of Princely States & Making of the Republic

### Deep Dive

**The problem.** At Independence there were about **`565` princely states**, freed from **British paramountcy** by the **Indian Independence Act 1947** and technically free to join India, Pakistan, or stay independent.

**Patel & Menon.** **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (as Home Minister, the "**Iron Man**") with **V.P. Menon** (Secretary, States Department) persuaded rulers to sign the **Instrument of Accession** (ceding **defence, external affairs, communications**). By **`15 August 1947`**, almost all had acceded — a remarkable peaceful integration.

**The three hard cases:**
- **Junagadh** — the ruler acceded to Pakistan; a **plebiscite (1948)** confirmed the people's wish to join **India**.
- **Hyderabad** — the Nizam held out; integrated by **"Operation Polo" (Sept 1948)**.
- **Jammu & Kashmir** — the ruler signed the **Instrument of Accession (Oct 1947)** to India after a Pakistani tribal invasion.

**Democratisation.** Accession was followed by **integration/merger** — states were merged into provinces or formed into unions, and later reorganised on a **linguistic basis (SRA 1956)**.

**Making of the Republic.** The **Constitution was adopted on `26 Nov 1949`** and **came into force on `26 January 1950`**, when India became a **sovereign democratic republic** with **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** as its first President.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **~565 states**; **Patel + V.P. Menon**; **Instrument of Accession** (defence, external affairs, communications). Hard cases: **Junagadh (plebiscite), Hyderabad (Operation Polo 1948), Kashmir (accession Oct 1947)**. Republic on **`26 Jan 1950`**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Instrument of Accession** ceded only **three subjects** (defence, external affairs, communications). **Junagadh** joined India via **plebiscite**, **Hyderabad** via **police action** — different routes.

💡 **Example:** **Sardar Patel's** peaceful integration of ~565 states (contrasted with the three exceptions) is a very common "one-liner" and short-note PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- ~565 states; paramountcy lapsed (Independence Act 1947).
- Patel + V.P. Menon; Instrument of Accession (3 subjects).
- Junagadh (plebiscite), Hyderabad (Operation Polo 1948), Kashmir (Oct 1947).
- Republic: Constitution in force **26 Jan 1950**; first President Rajendra Prasad.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which three subjects did the Instrument of Accession transfer to the Union?* → **defence, external affairs, and communications**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Minerals & Energy (Coal, Limestone, Granite, Power Plants)

### Deep Dive

**Minerals.** Telangana is rich in several minerals:
- **Coal** — the state's most important mineral, mined by **Singareni Collieries Company Ltd (SCCL)** in the **Godavari Valley Coalfield** (**Ramagundam, Kothagudem, Bhupalpally, Manuguru, Yellandu, Bellampalli**). *(Verify latest production figures.)*
- **Limestone** — abundant, feeding a large **cement** industry (Nalgonda, Adilabad, Suryapet belts).
- **Granite & dolerite** — high-quality **black/coloured granite** (Karimnagar, Warangal, Khammam) — a major **export**.
- Others: **bauxite, mica, quartz, feldspar, dolomite, barytes, clays**.

**Energy / power plants.**
- **Thermal (coal):** **Ramagundam (NTPC)**, **Kothagudem (KTPS)**, **Bhupalpally**, and the new **Yadadri (Damaracherla)** plant — mostly near the coalfields. *(Verify capacities and commissioning.)*
- **Hydro:** **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam (left bank), Priyadarshini Jurala, Lower Jurala, Pulichintala** (Krishna basin), and pumped-storage components.
- **Renewables:** growing **solar** capacity (solar parks) and some wind/biomass.

The state's **coal-thermal** base plus **hydro** and rising **solar** form its energy mix; power demand is driven by **agriculture (free farm power), industry and Hyderabad's IT/urban load**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Coal** = top mineral, mined by **Singareni (SCCL)** in the **Godavari Valley Coalfield**. Thermal: **Ramagundam (NTPC), Kothagudem, Yadadri**. Hydro: **Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Jurala**. Limestone → **cement**; **granite** → export.
