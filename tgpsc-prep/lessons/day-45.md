## 🏛️ Polity: The Preamble & Its Keywords

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Preamble** is the introductory statement of the Constitution embodying its **ideals and philosophy**. It is based on the **Objectives Resolution** drafted by Nehru (moved `13 Dec 1946`). It reads: *"We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic**..."* securing to all citizens **Justice** (social, economic, political), **Liberty** (of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship), **Equality** (of status and opportunity), and promoting **Fraternity** (assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the nation).

The **`42nd Amendment Act, 1976`** added three words: **Socialist, Secular** and (changed "unity of the Nation" to) **"unity and integrity"**. The Preamble declares the date of adoption as **`26 November 1949`**.

Key doctrines:
- **Source of authority** — "We, the People of India" → the Constitution derives from the people (popular sovereignty).
- **Nature of state** — Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic (**"Republic"** = elected head of state, no hereditary ruler).
- **Objectives** — Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity (mnemonic **JLEF**; note the *order* — Justice first).

**Is the Preamble part of the Constitution?** In **Berubari Union (1960)** the SC held it was **not** part of the Constitution. In **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** the SC reversed this, holding the Preamble **is** a part and can be amended under Art 368 **without damaging the basic structure**. In **LIC of India (1995)** the SC reaffirmed it is a part. The Preamble is **non-justiciable** (not enforceable in court) and is **not a source of power/prohibition**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Preamble source = **"We, the People"**; ideals **JLEF**; **42nd Amendment (1976)** added **Socialist, Secular, Integrity**. **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** → Preamble is part of the Constitution and amendable (within basic structure).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Liberty** in the Preamble does **not** include "liberty of life" (that's Art 21); it lists thought, expression, belief, faith, worship. And the Preamble is **non-justiciable**.

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Rythu Bharosa, Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa & the ₹2 Lakh Loan Waiver

### 📖 Deep Dive

Three interlinked farmer-welfare measures under the Six Guarantees framework:

1. **Rythu Bharosa** — investment-support (per-acre) assistance to farmers, the successor to the earlier Rythu Bandhu model. The manifesto committed **`₹15,000` per acre per year** to farmers and a separate support to **tenant/landless** cultivators (*verify latest per-acre figure, eligibility ceiling, and rollout status before the exam, as implementation details have evolved*).
2. **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** — assistance for **landless agricultural labourers** (manifesto figure around **`₹12,000`/year** per family — *verify latest official figure*), addressing the gap that per-acre schemes leave for those who own no land.
3. **₹2 Lakh Crop Loan Waiver** — waiver of crop loans **up to `₹2,00,000`** per eligible farmer family, implemented in **2024** (rolled out in phases/slabs — *verify latest coverage figures and number of beneficiaries before the exam*).

Together these target **owner-cultivators (Rythu Bharosa)**, **landless labour (Atmiya Bharosa)**, and **debt relief (loan waiver)** — a three-pronged approach. Contrast with **PM-KISAN** (central, `₹6,000`/year flat), which stacks on top for eligible farmers.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Rythu Bharosa = per-acre investment support (manifesto `₹15,000`/acre/yr, **verify**); Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa = landless-labour support (`₹12,000`/yr, **verify**); **`₹2 lakh` crop-loan waiver** implemented **2024**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Don't confuse **Rythu Bharosa** (Telangana, per-acre) with **PM-KISAN** (central, flat `₹6,000`/yr) or the older **Rythu Bandhu**. Always write "verify latest figure" for the exact per-acre amount.

## 🏰 Telangana History: Festivals & Jataras

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana's living heritage centres on distinctive festivals and tribal *jataras*:

- **Bathukamma** — the **state festival**, a floral celebration by women over **nine days** ending on **Saddula Bathukamma** (just before Dussehra, in Ashwayuja month). Flowers are stacked in a conical *gopuram* shape; celebrates goddess Gauri and the onset of a good harvest.
- **Bonalu** — a folk festival honouring **Mahankali**, celebrated in **Ashada** (Jul-Aug), prominent in **Hyderabad, Secunderabad (Ujjaini Mahankali), and Golconda**; women carry *bonam* (offering pots).
- **Sammakka-Saralamma (Medaram) Jatara** — Asia's **largest tribal fair**, held **biennially** (once every two years) at **Medaram, Mulugu district**, honouring the **Koya** tribal goddesses Sammakka and Saralamma (Saaralamma); draws crores of pilgrims.
- **Nagoba Jatara** — a **Gond/Mesram clan** tribal festival at **Keslapur, Indervelli (Adilabad)**, worshipping the serpent god **Nagoba**; features the *Keslapur* rituals and the **Praja Darbar**.
- **Peddagattu (Komuravelli) Jatara** — honouring **Lingamanthula Swamy**; and **Sadar** — a Yadav community buffalo festival in Hyderabad after Diwali.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Bathukamma** = state festival (9 days, floral, women). **Medaram (Sammakka-Saralamma)** = largest tribal jatara, **biennial**, **Koya** tribe, Mulugu. **Bonalu** in Ashada; **Nagoba Jatara** = Gonds, Keslapur (Adilabad).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Medaram Jatara is **biennial** (every 2 years), not annual. Bathukamma is the **state festival**; don't confuse it with Bonalu (Mahankali worship).

### 💡 Example

*Q: Which is Asia's largest tribal jatara and which tribe/goddesses does it honour?* → **Sammakka-Saralamma (Medaram) Jatara**, honouring the **Koya** goddesses Sammakka & Saralamma.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Bathukamma** — Ashwayuja, 9 days, ends Saddula Bathukamma; state festival.
- **Bonalu** — Ashada; Ujjaini Mahankali (Secunderabad).
- **Medaram** — biennial, Mulugu; Koya tribe.
- **Nagoba Jatara** — Mesram Gonds, Keslapur, Adilabad/Indervelli.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Civil Disobedience, Round Tables & Poona Pact

### 📖 Deep Dive

Authorised by the Lahore session (1929), Gandhi launched the **Civil Disobedience Movement** with the **Dandi Salt March** — from **Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi**, `241` miles over **12 March – 6 April `1930`**, breaking the **salt law**. Unlike Non-Cooperation (which withdrew participation), Civil Disobedience actively **broke specific laws** (salt, forest, tax). It saw wide participation including women; the **North-West Frontier** movement was led by **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** (the "Frontier Gandhi") and his **Khudai Khidmatgars (Red Shirts)**.

The **First Round Table Conference (1930-31, London)** was boycotted by the Congress. The **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (`5 March 1931`)** suspended the movement; Gandhi attended the **Second Round Table Conference (1931)** as the sole Congress representative, but it failed over the communal question. Civil Disobedience was **resumed (1932)** after Gandhi's return and Irwin's successor **Willingdon** cracked down.

The **Communal Award (Aug 1932)** by British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** granted **separate electorates** to the **Depressed Classes (Dalits)**. Gandhi opposed this (fearing it would divide Hindu society) and undertook a **fast unto death** in Yerawada jail. The resulting **Poona Pact (`24 September 1932`)** between **Gandhi and B. R. Ambedkar** replaced separate electorates with **reserved seats** for the Depressed Classes within the general (joint) electorate, and increased their number of reserved seats.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Dandi March **12 Mar–6 Apr 1930** (salt law). **Gandhi-Irwin Pact 5 Mar 1931** → 2nd RTC. **Communal Award 1932** (MacDonald, separate electorates for Depressed Classes) → **Poona Pact `24 Sep 1932`** (Gandhi–Ambedkar): reserved seats within joint electorate.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Poona Pact** replaced separate electorates with **reserved seats** (joint electorate) — it did **not** abolish reservation, it changed its form. The **2nd** RTC (not 1st) was the one Gandhi attended.

### 💡 Example

*Q: What did the Poona Pact (1932) achieve?* → It replaced the **separate electorates** for Depressed Classes (Communal Award) with **reserved seats** in a **joint** electorate, following Gandhi's fast.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Dandi/Salt Satyagraha = start of Civil Disobedience (1930).
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact** (Delhi Pact) 1931 → 2nd RTC.
- **3 Round Table Conferences**: 1930-31, 1931, 1932.
- Communal Award by **Ramsay MacDonald**; Poona Pact = **Gandhi + Ambedkar**.
- Frontier Gandhi = **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** (Red Shirts).

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Soils & Agriculture

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana's agriculture rests on its **soil mosaic**. The dominant types:

| Soil | Where | Suited crops |
|---|---|---|
| **Red soils (chalka)** | most of Telangana (~48%+) | coarse cereals, groundnut, pulses, castor |
| **Black soils (regur)** | Adilabad, parts of the north/west | cotton, jowar, oilseeds (moisture-retentive) |
| **Laterite soils** | scattered high-rainfall pockets | plantation/coarse crops |
| **Alluvial soils** | river valleys (Godavari, Krishna) | paddy, sugarcane, vegetables |

**Cropping seasons.**

- **Kharif (Vanakalam)** — sown Jun-Jul with the SW monsoon, harvested Oct-Nov: **rice (paddy), cotton, maize, soybean, redgram (tur)**.
- **Rabi (Yasangi)** — sown Oct-Nov, harvested Feb-Mar, relying on irrigation/residual moisture: **rice, maize, groundnut, bengal gram, sunflower**.

**Rice (paddy)** is the state's principal food crop and its area has expanded sharply with lift-irrigation (Kaleshwaram) — Telangana has become a leading **rice-contributing** state to the central pool (*verify latest procurement figures*). **Cotton** is the main commercial crop (Adilabad black-soil belt = "white gold" region). Other important crops: **maize, redgram, chilli** (Khammam/Warangal), **turmeric** (Nizamabad), **groundnut** (Mahabubnagar). Horticulture (mango, sweet orange/*batayi* in Nalgonda, vegetables around cities) is growing.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Red soils** dominate Telangana; **black (regur)** soils in the **Adilabad/cotton** belt. **Kharif = Vanakalam** (monsoon; paddy, cotton), **Rabi = Yasangi** (irrigated; paddy, gram). **Rice** = principal crop; **cotton** = main commercial crop.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — In Telangana usage, **Vanakalam = Kharif** and **Yasangi = Rabi** — a favourite matching question. Black soil (not red) is best for **cotton**.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Which soil dominates Telangana and which is ideal for cotton?* → **Red soils (chalka)** dominate; **black regur soils** (Adilabad belt) are ideal for cotton.
