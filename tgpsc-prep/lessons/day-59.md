## 🏛️ Polity: Emergency Provisions (Art 352, 356, 360)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Part XVIII (Art 352-360)** provides three types of emergency — a "unitary" shift during crises. Borrowed conceptually from the German (Weimar) idea, but with safeguards after the 44th Amendment.

**1. National Emergency (Art 352).** Declared on grounds of **war, external aggression, or armed rebellion** (the term "internal disturbance" was replaced by "armed rebellion" by the **44th Amendment, 1978**).
- Proclamation needs **written Cabinet advice** to the President; must be **approved by both Houses within 1 month** (special majority); then lasts **6 months**, extendable indefinitely (6 months at a time).
- Effects: Centre gets power over State List (states become quasi-unitary); **Art 19 freedoms** can be suspended (only during **war/external aggression**, not armed rebellion — 44th Amendment); other FRs' enforcement suspended by a separate order (Art 359), **except Art 20 & 21** (44th Amendment). Lok Sabha term can be extended.
- Declared in **1962 (China), 1971 (Pakistan), 1975 (internal — controversial)**.

**2. President's Rule / State Emergency (Art 356).** On failure of constitutional machinery in a state (Governor's report or otherwise).
- Approved by Parliament within **2 months**; lasts **6 months**, extendable up to a **maximum of 3 years** (with conditions). State executive taken over by the Centre; Assembly dissolved/suspended.
- **Bommai (1994)** — subject to **judicial review**; floor test; secularism as basic structure.

**3. Financial Emergency (Art 360).** On a threat to India's financial stability/credit.
- Approved within **2 months**; no maximum duration. The Centre can direct states on financial matters, reduce salaries (incl. judges). **Never been imposed.**

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Art 352 (National — war/external aggression/**armed rebellion**; approval **1 month**, special majority; **Art 20 & 21 never suspended** — 44th Amdt). Art 356 (President's Rule — approval **2 months**, max **3 years**; Bommai checks). Art 360 (Financial — never used).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — "**Internal disturbance**" → replaced by "**armed rebellion**" (44th Amdt). **Art 20 & 21 cannot be suspended** even in emergency. National Emergency approval = **1 month** (special majority); President's Rule/Financial = **2 months**. Financial Emergency (Art 360) has **never** been declared.

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Rythu Bharosa & the ₹2-Lakh Loan Waiver (Round 5)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Rythu Bharosa** (with **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** and the **crop-loan waiver**) is the farmer-welfare guarantee — a recap with focus (verify latest):

1. **Rythu Bharosa** — per-acre **investment support** to farmers (successor to Rythu Bandhu); the manifesto committed **₹15,000/acre/year** (verify latest figure, eligibility ceiling, and whether tenant/landless are covered separately).
2. **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** — annual support (manifesto ~**₹12,000/year**) for **landless agricultural labour** families — filling the gap that per-acre schemes leave.
3. **₹2-Lakh Crop-Loan Waiver** — waiver of crop loans **up to ₹2,00,000** per eligible farmer family, implemented in **2024** (in phases/slabs; verify coverage and beneficiary numbers).

**Policy context:** Telangana pioneered **direct per-acre farmer support** (Rythu Bandhu, 2018); Rythu Bharosa continues and enhances it. Related: **Rythu Bima** (farmers' life-insurance, ₹5 lakh cover — a TRS-era scheme, verify continuation), and central **PM-KISAN** (₹6,000/yr) which stacks on top. Debates: fiscal cost, tenant-farmer coverage, and linkage to actual cultivation.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Rythu Bharosa** (per-acre investment support, manifesto ₹15,000/acre/yr — verify) + **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** (landless labour ~₹12,000/yr) + **₹2-lakh crop-loan waiver (2024)**. ## 🏰 Telangana History: The Asaf Jahis (Nizams), 1724-1948

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Asaf Jahi dynasty (Nizams of Hyderabad)** ruled the largest and richest princely state from **1724 to 1948**.

**Founding & key rulers:**
- **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I (Mir Qamaruddin)** — a Mughal governor of the Deccan who became **effectively independent in 1724** (after the Battle of Shakar Kheda), founding the dynasty; capital eventually **Hyderabad**.
- **Subsidiary Alliance (1798)** — **Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II)** was the **first Indian ruler to sign** Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance, accepting British troops and a Resident, and surrendering foreign relations — making Hyderabad a **loyal British ally**.
- **Salar Jung I (Mir Turab Ali Khan)** — the famous **Prime Minister (Diwan)** under Asaf Jah V (Afzal-ud-Daula) and VI; introduced **modernising reforms** (administration, revenue, railways, postal, judiciary, education) in the mid-19th century.
- **Mir Mahbub Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VI)** and **Mir Osman Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VII, r. 1911-1948)** — the last Nizam, once among the world's richest; founded **Osmania University (1918)**, the **Nizam's railways, Osmania General Hospital, High Court, State Bank of Hyderabad**; used **Osmania Sicca** currency. His reign ended with **Operation Polo (1948)**.

**Administration & currency:** the state had its own **currency (Hyderabadi/Osmania rupee)**, army, railways, and **Urdu as the official language** (Osmania University taught in Urdu). Persianate high culture; a composite Deccani society.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Asaf Jahis (1724-1948): founder **Asaf Jah I (1724)**; **Subsidiary Alliance 1798** (Asaf Jah II, first to sign); **Salar Jung I** reforms; **Mir Osman Ali Khan** (VII, last; **Osmania University 1918**, richest). Own currency (Osmania rupee), Urdu official.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Asaf Jah I** founded the dynasty (1724); **Asaf Jah II (Nizam Ali Khan)** signed the **Subsidiary Alliance (1798, first Indian ruler)**; **Salar Jung I** was the reforming **Diwan**; **Mir Osman Ali Khan (VII)** was the last (Osmania University). Don't mix them.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh & Khilafat

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Rowlatt Act (1919).** The **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act** allowed detention **without trial** ("no vakil, no dalil, no appeal"). Gandhi launched the **Rowlatt Satyagraha** (his first all-India political action, a hartal on **6 April 1919**).

**Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919, Amritsar).** During Baisakhi, a peaceful crowd protesting the Rowlatt Act and the arrest of leaders (Dr. Satyapal & Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew) gathered at Jallianwala Bagh. **General Reginald Dyer** ordered troops to fire without warning, blocking the only exit — hundreds killed (official ~379; actual far higher). **Michael O'Dwyer** (Punjab Lt. Governor) backed it. Aftermath: **Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood**; **Gandhi returned the Kaisar-i-Hind medal**; the **Hunter Commission** inquiry censured Dyer mildly. (Udham Singh later assassinated O'Dwyer in London, 1940.)

**Khilafat Movement (1919-1924).** Indian Muslims protested the harsh treatment of the **Ottoman Caliph (Khalifa)** by the Allies after WWI (Treaty of Sèvres). Led by the **Ali Brothers (Muhammad Ali & Shaukat Ali), Maulana Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan**. Gandhi supported it to forge **Hindu-Muslim unity** and **merged it with the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)** — the high point of communal unity. The movement collapsed after **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk abolished the Caliphate (1924)**.

These events (Rowlatt → Jallianwala → Khilafat) set the stage for the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)**, the first true mass movement.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Rowlatt Act 1919** (detention without trial) → hartal 6 Apr 1919. **Jallianwala Bagh 13 Apr 1919** (Dyer; Tagore renounced knighthood). **Khilafat (1919-24)** — Ali Brothers, Ottoman Caliph; merged with **Non-Cooperation (1920)**; ended when Atatürk abolished the Caliphate (1924).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Dyer** ordered the firing; **O'Dwyer** was the Punjab Lt. Governor who endorsed it (don't confuse them). **Rowlatt = detention without trial**; Jallianwala on **13 April 1919** (Baisakhi). Khilafat merged with Non-Cooperation (1920).

### 💡 Example

*Q: Who ordered the firing at Jallianwala Bagh, and who renounced his knighthood in protest?* → **General Dyer** ordered it; **Rabindranath Tagore** renounced his knighthood.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Hyderabad & Rangareddy Urban Region

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Hyderabad-Rangareddy-Medchal** urban region is Telangana's economic core — the **capital, largest city, and a national metropolis**.

**Physiography:** on the **Deccan plateau** (~500 m elevation), granite terrain (the famous **Hyderabad rocks/boulders**); the **Musi river** (Krishna tributary) bisects the old city; **lakes** — **Hussain Sagar** (1563), **Osman Sagar (Gandipet)** & **Himayat Sagar** (on the Musi, drinking-water sources), and many tanks (now under encroachment pressure — HYDRAA).

**Administration:** **GHMC** (Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation); districts **Hyderabad, Medchal-Malkajgiri, Rangareddy** (and Sangareddy fringe). Hyderabad is the **common capital** of Telangana and (until 2024) residuary AP.

**Economy:**
- **IT/ITES** — **HITEC City, Gachibowli, Financial District** (Cyberabad); global tech HQs.
- **Pharma/biotech** — **Genome Valley**, Hyderabad Pharma City (planned), "Vaccine/Bulk-Drug Capital."
- **Aerospace/defence** — Adibatla; DRDO/HAL/BDL/ECIL/DLRL.
- Services, real estate, tourism.

**Infrastructure:** **Outer Ring Road (ORR ~158 km)**, planned **Regional Ring Road (RRR)**, **Hyderabad Metro Rail**, **MMTS**, **RGIA (Shamshabad) airport**; proposed **Future City** (Fourth City) to the south.

**Heritage & culture:** **Charminar, Golconda Fort, Qutb Shahi Tombs, Chowmahalla & Falaknuma Palaces, Mecca Masjid, Salar Jung Museum, Birla Mandir, Ramoji Film City**; Deccani/Ganga-Jamuni culture, Hyderabadi biryani/haleem (GI).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Hyderabad-Rangareddy: capital & metropolis on the Deccan plateau; **Musi** river; lakes **Hussain/Osman/Himayat Sagar**; **GHMC**; IT (HITEC City), pharma (Genome Valley); **ORR ~158 km**, Metro, RGIA; heritage (Charminar/Golconda/Salar Jung Museum).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Osman Sagar (Gandipet)** & **Himayat Sagar** (on the Musi) are Hyderabad's **drinking-water** reservoirs; **Hussain Sagar** (1563) is the city-centre lake. **Salar Jung Museum** (one of the world's largest one-man collections) is here. GHMC governs the city.
