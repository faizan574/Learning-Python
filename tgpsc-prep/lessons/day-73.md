## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Amendment (Art 368) & Basic Structure

### Deep Dive

**Article 368** lays down the procedure to amend the Constitution. There are **three amendment routes**:
1. **Simple majority** (outside Art 368) — e.g., creation of new states, citizenship, salaries (treated as ordinary legislation).
2. **Special majority** (Art 368) — majority of the **total membership** of each House **and** **two-thirds of members present and voting**.
3. **Special majority + ratification by half the states** — for **federal provisions** (election of President, distribution of powers, 7th Schedule, representation of states, Art 368 itself).

**Basic Structure Doctrine.** In **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**, the Supreme Court held that Parliament can amend **any** part of the Constitution **but cannot destroy its "basic structure."** Recognised elements include: **supremacy of the Constitution, rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review, federalism, secularism, free & fair elections, the sovereign-democratic-republic character, and the balance between FRs and DPSPs**. (The precise list is **open-ended**, decided case by case.)

**Landmark amendments to know:**

| Amendment | Year | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | 1951 | Added **9th Schedule**, restrictions on free speech |
| **42nd** | 1976 | "**Mini-Constitution**" — Socialist/Secular, FDs, DPSP priority |
| **44th** | 1978 | Right to property removed (→ 300A); emergency safeguards |
| **73rd/74th** | 1992 | **Panchayats / Municipalities** |
| **86th** | 2002 | **Right to Education (21A)** |
| **101st** | 2016 | **GST** |
| **103rd** | 2019 | **EWS** 10% reservation |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Art 368: **special majority** (total membership + 2/3 present-voting); federal parts need **state ratification (half)**. **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** → **basic structure** limit on amending power.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Basic-structure review means a **constitutional amendment can be struck down** (e.g., parts of the 42nd in *Minerva Mills*). "Simple-majority" changes (new states, citizenship) are **not** Art 368 amendments. The basic-structure list is **not exhaustive**.

💡 **Example:** *Minerva Mills (1980)* struck down parts of the **42nd Amendment** for violating the FR-DPSP balance and judicial review — basic structure in action.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Three routes: simple / special / special + state ratification.
- Special majority = total membership + 2/3 present & voting.
- Kesavananda (1973): basic structure; Minerva Mills (1980) applied it.
- Key amendments: 1st, 42nd, 44th, 73rd/74th, 86th, 101st, 103rd.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which case established the basic structure doctrine?* → **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**.

## 🗳️ Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Rythu Bharosa, Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa & the ₹2-Lakh Loan Waiver

### Deep Dive

The **farmer guarantee** has three strands (*verify all amounts/eligibility against the latest official notification*):

- **Rythu Bharosa** — a **per-acre investment-support** payment to farmers each crop season (the successor to the earlier **Rythu Bandhu**). The government extended discussion to cover **tenant farmers** and limit support to **cultivable land** (verify the per-acre amount and land conditions).
- **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** — support for **landless agricultural labour** families (who own no land and so miss per-acre schemes) — an income-support measure (verify amount).
- **₹2-lakh crop-loan waiver** — a **one-time waiver** of eligible farm crop loans up to **₹2 lakh** per farm family, implemented in phases (verify coverage/criteria and completion status).

**Significance.** Together these target the **three farming groups** — landowning cultivators (Rythu Bharosa), **tenants/landless labour** (Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa), and **indebted farmers** (loan waiver) — a comprehensive agrarian-support package.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Rythu Bharosa** = per-acre investment support (successor to Rythu Bandhu); **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** = support for **landless agri-labour**; **₹2-lakh** one-time **crop-loan waiver**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Amounts, per-acre rates, land/tenant conditions, and waiver phases **change** — write **"verify latest official figure."** Rythu Bharosa targets **landowners**; **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** targets the **landless** — don't conflate them.

💡 **Example:** A **tenant farmer** without land would look to **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa**, while a **landowning cultivator** gets **Rythu Bharosa** — the distinction is a likely PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Rythu Bharosa: per-acre investment support (← Rythu Bandhu).
- Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa: landless agri-labour support.
- ₹2-lakh one-time crop-loan waiver (phased).
- Covers landowners + landless + indebted farmers; verify amounts.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which scheme supports landless agricultural labour?* → **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa**.

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Literature & Language

### Deep Dive

**Classical Telugu (Telangana roots):**
- **Palkuriki Somanatha** (13th c.) — a pioneer of the **dwipada** metre; wrote **Basava Purana, Panditaradhya Charitra** (Veerashaiva literature).
- **Bammera Potana** (15th c.) — rendered the **Srimad Bhagavatam** into Telugu (**Andhra Mahabhagavatam**); born in the Telangana region.

**The language/identity dimension.** **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** — editor of **Golconda Patrika**, author of **"Andhrula Sanghika Charitra"** (social history) and compiler of **Telangana poets' anthology (Golconda Kavula Sanchika)**; a key figure of the **Andhra Mahasabha**/awakening. The **distinct Telangana dialect** gained literary dignity through such works.

**Modern & movement poets:**
- **Dasarathi Krishnamacharya** — poet of the anti-Nizam struggle ("**Agni Dhara**").
- **Kaloji Narayana Rao** — "**Praja Kavi**" (people's poet), champion of the **Telangana dialect**; his birthday is **Telangana Language Day**.
- **C. Narayana Reddy (Cinare)** — **Jnanpith awardee** (*Viswambhara*); versatile poet/lyricist.
- **Gaddar** and **Suddala Hanmanthu** — **balladeer-activists** of the people's movements.
- **Vignana Sarvaswam** — the Telugu **encyclopedia** project (knowledge in the people's language).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Palkuriki Somanatha** (dwipada, Basava Purana); **Potana** (Bhagavatam). **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** (Golconda Patrika, Andhrula Sanghika Charitra). **Cinare** = Jnanpith; **Kaloji** = Praja Kavi (Telangana Language Day).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **C. Narayana Reddy (Cinare)** won the **Jnanpith**; **Kaloji** is the **Praja Kavi** (don't swap). **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** → **Golconda Patrika** (a cultural-awakening PYQ anchor).

💡 **Example:** **Kaloji Narayana Rao's** birthday observed as **Telangana Language Day**, and **Cinare's Jnanpith** for *Viswambhara*, are frequent literature PYQs.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Classical: Palkuriki Somanatha (dwipada), Potana (Bhagavatam).
- Suravaram Pratapa Reddy: Golconda Patrika, Andhrula Sanghika Charitra.
- Dasarathi (Agni Dhara), Kaloji (Praja Kavi), Cinare (Jnanpith).
- Gaddar/Suddala (ballads); Vignana Sarvaswam.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Who is called the "Praja Kavi" of Telangana?* → **Kaloji Narayana Rao**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Revolutionary Movement Phase I (Bengal, Punjab, Abroad)

### Deep Dive

The **early revolutionary movement (c. 1900-1917)** arose alongside the Swadeshi upsurge, favouring **individual heroism, assassinations of unpopular officials, and armed conspiracy**.

**Bengal:**
- **Anushilan Samiti** and **Jugantar** — secret societies; **Barindra Kumar Ghosh, Aurobindo** linked.
- **Khudiram Bose & Prafulla Chaki** — the **Muzaffarpur bombing (1908)** (targeting Magistrate Kingsford); **Alipore Conspiracy Case**.

**Maharashtra:**
- **Chapekar brothers** — assassinated Rand (plague commissioner), **1897** (Pune).
- **V.D. Savarkar** — **Abhinav Bharat**.

**Punjab:**
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** and **Ajit Singh** — agrarian/anti-colonial agitation (deported 1907).

**Abroad:**
- **India House, London** — **Shyamji Krishna Varma**; **Madan Lal Dhingra** (assassinated Curzon-Wyllie, 1909).
- **Madame Bhikaji Cama** — **unfurled an early version of the Indian flag at Stuttgart (1907)**.
- **Ghadar Party (1913, San Francisco)** — **Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, Kartar Singh Sarabha**; planned the **1915 Ghadar (mutiny) attempt**.
- **Berlin Committee** (WWI); **Komagata Maru** incident (1914).
- **Delhi Conspiracy / Delhi-Lahore (1912)** — **Rash Behari Bose** (bomb on Viceroy Hardinge).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Bengal: **Anushilan/Jugantar**, **Khudiram Bose** (Muzaffarpur 1908). Abroad: **Ghadar Party (1913, San Francisco, Har Dayal)**; **Madame Cama** (flag at Stuttgart 1907); **India House** (Shyamji Krishna Varma, Dhingra).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Ghadar Party** was founded **abroad (San Francisco, 1913)**, not in India. **Madame Cama** raised the flag at **Stuttgart (1907)**. Distinguish **Phase I (1900-17, Bengal/Punjab/abroad)** from **Phase II (1920s, HSRA/Bhagat Singh)**.

💡 **Example:** **Khudiram Bose's** hanging (1908) and the **Ghadar movement** are the two anchor PYQs of early revolutionary nationalism.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Bengal: Anushilan Samiti, Jugantar; Khudiram Bose/Prafulla Chaki (1908).
- Chapekar brothers (1897); Savarkar (Abhinav Bharat).
- Abroad: India House (Shyamji Krishna Varma, Dhingra); Madame Cama (Stuttgart 1907).
- Ghadar Party (1913, SF, Har Dayal); Rash Behari Bose (Delhi 1912).

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Adilabad & the Northern Gond Belt

### Deep Dive

The **Adilabad** region — the **northernmost** part of Telangana — spans **Adilabad, Kumram Bheem Asifabad, Mancherial and Nirmal** districts (*verify current boundaries*). It is the state's most **forested, tribal and mineral-rich** frontier.

**Physiography & forests.** Hilly, part of the **Satmala/Sahyadri-parvatam** ranges and the **Godavari basin**; **dense teak forests**. Rivers: the **Godavari** and its tributaries **Pranahita, Penganga, Kadam, Peddavagu** — with the **Kadam** and **Sripada Yellampalli** projects.

**Tribes & culture.** Home to the **Gond and Kolam** tribes; the **Raj Gonds** of **Keslapur** hold the **Nagoba Jatara**, and the **Gussadi dance** features in the **Dandari** festival. **Komaram Bheem** (Gond rebel against the Nizam, "**Jal, Jangal, Zameen**") is the region's iconic figure — a district (**Kumram Bheem Asifabad**) is named after him. **Nirmal** is famed for **toys & paintings**.

**Natural landmarks.** **Kawal Tiger Reserve** (Jannaram), **Pranahita Wildlife Sanctuary**; waterfalls — **Kuntala (tallest in Telangana), Pochera, Gayatri**; **Basar** (Nirmal) — the **Gnana Saraswati Temple** on the Godavari.

**Minerals.** **Coal** (Mancherial/Bellampalli belt, Singareni) and **limestone/cement**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Adilabad belt = northernmost, **teak forests**, **Gond/Kolam** tribes; **Komaram Bheem** ("Jal Jangal Zameen"); **Kawal Tiger Reserve**; **Kuntala** (tallest waterfall); **Basar** Saraswati temple; coal (Mancherial/Bellampalli).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Kuntala** is the **tallest waterfall** in Telangana; **Basar** has the **Gnana Saraswati** temple (one of few Saraswati temples). **Komaram Bheem** (Gond, Nizam era) ≠ **Komuravelli Mallanna** (deity) — keep names distinct.

💡 **Example:** **Komaram Bheem's "Jal, Jangal, Zameen"** slogan and the **Nagoba Jatara (Keslapur)** are the anchor Adilabad PYQs.

- Kawal Tiger Reserve; Kuntala/Pochera waterfalls; Basar Saraswati temple.
