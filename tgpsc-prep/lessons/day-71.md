## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Fundamental Rights — Articles 23-30 & Article 32 (Writs)

### Deep Dive

**Right against Exploitation (Art 23-24):**
- **Art 23** — prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar (forced labour), and other similar forced labour**. (Compulsory service for **public purposes**, e.g., military conscription, is allowed.)
- **Art 24** — prohibits employment of **children below 14** in **factories, mines, or hazardous work**.

**Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25-28):**
- **Art 25** — freedom of **conscience** and free profession, practice, propagation of religion (subject to public order, morality, health).
- **Art 26** — freedom to **manage religious affairs** / institutions.
- **Art 27** — **no tax** for promotion of a particular religion.
- **Art 28** — **no religious instruction** in wholly State-funded institutions.

**Cultural & Educational Rights (Art 29-30):**
- **Art 29** — protects the **language, script, culture** of any section; no denial of admission to State-aided institutions on grounds of religion/race/caste/language.
- **Art 30** — **minorities' right to establish and administer** educational institutions.

**Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32).** Ambedkar called Art 32 the **"heart and soul of the Constitution"** — the right to move the **Supreme Court** directly for enforcement of FRs. The Court (and High Courts under **Art 226**) can issue **five writs**:

| Writ | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | produce a detained person ("have the body") |
| **Mandamus** | command a public authority to do its duty |
| **Prohibition** | stop a lower court exceeding jurisdiction (preventive) |
| **Certiorari** | quash a lower court's order (curative) |
| **Quo Warranto** | question a person's right to a public office |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Art 32** (writs; SC) = "heart and soul"; **Art 226** gives **HCs wider** writ power (for FRs *and* other legal rights). Five writs: **Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Art 226 (HC) is wider than Art 32 (SC)** — HCs issue writs for **any legal right**, not just FRs. **Art 32 itself is a Fundamental Right** and cannot be ordinarily suspended (except during emergency, subject to Art 20/21 safeguards).

💡 **Example:** A person unlawfully detained files a **Habeas Corpus** petition; a public officer refusing a statutory duty is compelled by **Mandamus** — classic writ illustrations.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Art 23 (forced labour/trafficking), Art 24 (child labour <14).
- Art 25-28 religion; Art 29-30 minorities.
- Art 32 "heart and soul" (SC); Art 226 (HC, wider).
- Five writs and their functions.

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Folk & Performing Arts

### Deep Dive

Telangana's **folk and performing arts** are a rich exam area (culture):

- **Perini Shivatandavam** — an ancient **warrior/vigorous dance** linked to the **Kakatiya** era (performed before warriors went to battle); **revived by Nataraja Ramakrishna**. Also called *Perini Thandavam*.
- **Oggu Katha** — a **ballad/storytelling** tradition of the **Yadava/Kuruma** communities, narrating **Mallanna, Beerappa, Yellamma**; uses the *oggu* (drum).
- **Burra Katha** — a three-person **narrative** form: a main performer with a **tambura** and two co-performers; used for social/political themes.
- **Chindu Bhagavatam & Yakshaganam** — theatrical/dance-drama traditions.
- **Gussadi** — a **Gond tribal dance** of **Adilabad**, performed during the **Dandari** festival (feathered headgear).
- **Dappu** — an energetic **drum dance** (Nizamabad and elsewhere).
- **Kolatam** — a **stick dance** (group, rhythmic).
- **Golla Suddulu** — ballads of the **Golla (Yadava)** community.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Perini Shivatandavam** = Kakatiya-era warrior dance, revived by **Nataraja Ramakrishna**. **Oggu Katha** (Yadava/Kuruma ballad, Mallanna/Beerappa); **Burra Katha** (tambura, 3 performers); **Gussadi** = Gond dance (Adilabad, Dandari).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Perini** is a **warrior dance** (Kakatiya), **not** a story-telling form. **Gussadi is a Gond (Adilabad) tribal dance**; **Oggu Katha is a Yadava/Kuruma ballad** — keep the art-community pairings straight.

💡 **Example:** "Which dance form of the Kakatiya period was revived by Nataraja Ramakrishna?" → **Perini Shivatandavam** — a very common culture PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Perini Shivatandavam: warrior dance, Kakatiya, Nataraja Ramakrishna.
- Oggu Katha: Yadava/Kuruma ballad (Mallanna/Beerappa).
- Burra Katha: tambura + 2; Gussadi: Gond, Adilabad, Dandari.
- Dappu (drum), Kolatam (sticks), Golla Suddulu (ballad).

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Who revived the Perini Shivatandavam?* → **Nataraja Ramakrishna**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: INC Formation & the Moderate Era (1885-1905)

### Deep Dive

**Formation of the INC.** The **Indian National Congress** was founded in **1885**; **A.O. Hume** (a retired British civil servant) was the key organiser. The **first session** was held in **Bombay** (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College) with **72 delegates**; **W.C. Bonnerjee** was the **first president**.

**The Moderates (1885-1905).** The early Congress was led by **Moderates** — **Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjee, Badruddin Tyabji** (first Muslim president), **Dinshaw Wacha**, etc.

**Methods & ideology.**
- Faith in **constitutional agitation** — the **"3 Ps": Prayer, Petition, Protest**; petitions, memoranda, resolutions.
- Belief in **British sense of justice** and gradual reform.

**Demands:**
- **Legislative reforms** — more elected Indians in councils.
- **Indianisation of the civil services** (simultaneous ICS exams in India).
- **Reduction of military/home-charges expenditure**; separation of judiciary from executive.
- **Economic critique** — the **"Drain of Wealth"** theory (**Dadabhai Naoroji**, *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*); R.C. Dutt's economic history.

**Achievements & limits.** The Moderates won the **Indian Councils Act, 1892** (enlarged councils, limited indirect election) and, crucially, built a **national platform** and an **economic critique** of colonial rule. Critics ("Extremists") later dismissed their methods as **"political mendicancy."**

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — INC founded **1885** (A.O. Hume; first president **W.C. Bonnerjee**, Bombay, 72 delegates). Moderates used **prayer-petition-protest**; **Naoroji's Drain Theory**; won the **Indian Councils Act 1892**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **A.O. Hume** was the **organiser**; **W.C. Bonnerjee** the **first president** — don't swap. **Badruddin Tyabji** was the **first Muslim president** (1887). The Moderate era is **1885-1905** (up to the Swadeshi/Surat shift).

💡 **Example:** **Dadabhai Naoroji's "Drain of Wealth"** (*Poverty and Un-British Rule*) is the standard Moderate economic-critique PYQ, alongside the **Grand Old Man of India** epithet.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- INC 1885; Hume (organiser), Bonnerjee (first president), Bombay, 72 delegates.
- Moderates: Naoroji, Gokhale, Mehta, S.N. Banerjee, Tyabji.
- Methods: 3 Ps; Drain Theory (Naoroji).
- Indian Councils Act 1892.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Who was the first president of the INC?* → **W.C. Bonnerjee** (1885, Bombay).

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Mahabubnagar / Palamuru

### Deep Dive

**Mahabubnagar (Palamuru)** is a large, historically **drought-prone** region of **southern Telangana**, later reorganised into several districts (**Mahabubnagar, Nagarkurnool, Wanaparthy, Jogulamba Gadwal, Narayanpet** — *verify current district boundaries*).

**Physiography & geology.** It lies on the **Deccan plateau's** ancient **Archaean granite-gneiss** terrain, with **red/chalka soils** and a **rain-shadow, semi-arid climate** — hence chronic drought and historic **labour migration ("Palamuru labour")**.

**Rivers & irrigation.** The **Krishna** and its tributaries (**Tungabhadra, Dindi**) border/cross the region. Key projects: **Priyadarshini Jurala (Jurala)** — the **first major Krishna project in Telangana** — **Rajolibanda Diversion Scheme (RDS)**, **Koilsagar**, and the flagship **Palamuru-Rangareddy Lift Irrigation Scheme** designed to drought-proof the region.

**Cultural & heritage highlights:**
- **Alampur** (Jogulamba Gadwal) — the **Navabrahma temples** (Badami Chalukya architecture) on the Tungabhadra, and the **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**.
- **Pillalamarri** — a centuries-old **giant banyan tree** (a tourist landmark near Mahabubnagar).
- **Gadwal** — famous **Gadwal sarees** (GI) and the historic **Gadwal fort/samsthanam**; **Wanaparthy** and **Kollapur** were notable **samsthanams (estates)**.

![Mahabubnagar / Palamuru district map — locate the Krishna-Tungabhadra belt and the Jurala / Palamuru-Rangareddy projects](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/mahabubnagar.svg)

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Palamuru = drought-prone SW Telangana on **Archaean granite**; **Jurala** = first major **Krishna** project in TS; **Alampur** = Navabrahma temples + **Jogulamba Shakti Peetha**; **Gadwal sarees** (GI); **Pillalamarri** banyan.
