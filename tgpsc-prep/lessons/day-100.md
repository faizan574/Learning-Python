## 🏛️ Polity: Centre-State Relations & Federalism

India is a **federation with a unitary bias** ("union of states," `Art 1`). Centre-state relations fall into three heads: **legislative (`Art 245–255`), administrative (`Art 256–263`), and financial (`Art 268–293`)**.

**Legislative relations.** The **Seventh Schedule** divides subjects into **three lists**:
- **Union List** (~98 subjects; defence, foreign affairs, atomic energy, railways) — Parliament.
- **State List** (~59; police, public health, agriculture, land) — State legislatures.
- **Concurrent List** (~52; criminal law, marriage, education, forests) — both; **central law prevails** on conflict (`Art 254`), **repugnancy**.
**Residuary powers** (`Art 248`) → **Parliament**. Parliament can legislate on State subjects in special cases (`Art 249` RS resolution, `Art 250` emergency, `Art 252` two states' request, `Art 253` treaties).

**Administrative relations.** States must comply with central laws (`Art 256`); the Centre can give directions (`Art 257`); **all-India services (IAS/IPS)**; inter-state councils (`Art 263`), **inter-state water disputes** (`Art 262`).

**Financial relations.** **GST** (101st Amendment, 2016) — the **GST Council** (`Art 279A`) a federal body (Union FM + state FMs) decides rates; **Finance Commission** (`Art 280`) — every 5 years, recommends **tax devolution** (vertical: Centre→states; horizontal: among states) and grants-in-aid. The **16th Finance Commission** is the current one (verify its award period/recommendations).

**Nature of Indian federalism.** Called **"quasi-federal"** (K.C. Wheare: "federal in form, unitary in spirit") / **"cooperative federalism"**. Unitary tilt: single Constitution/citizenship, strong Centre in emergency, All-India Services, Governor, residuary powers with Centre.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Seventh Schedule: **Union/State/Concurrent** lists; residuary → **Parliament** (`Art 248`); concurrent conflict → **central law prevails** (`Art 254`). **GST Council** (`Art 279A`, 101st Amd); **Finance Commission** (`Art 280`) every 5 yrs for **tax devolution**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**3 lists (Union/State/Concurrent); residuary = Centre; GST Council 279A; Finance Commission 280 (devolution).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Residuary powers = Centre** (`Art 248`) — unlike the US. **Finance Commission (Art 280)** ≠ **GST Council (Art 279A)**. In concurrent conflict, **central law prevails** (Art 254). Inter-state **water disputes** = `Art 262` (can bar SC jurisdiction).

💡 **Example:** GST rate changes are decided by the **GST Council** (cooperative federalism); the share of taxes to states is set by the **Finance Commission**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Art 249/250/252/253 — Parliament on state subjects; Art 254 repugnancy.
- GST Council Art 279A (101st Amd); Finance Commission Art 280 (5-yr).
- Quasi-federal / cooperative federalism; Art 262 water disputes.

## 🤝 Schemes: Mahalakshmi

**Mahalakshmi** is the **women-centric** guarantee among Telangana's Six Guarantees — a bundle of free travel, subsidised LPG and direct cash support. (Treat amounts as **verify latest official figure**.)

**Components.**
1. **Free bus travel for women** — free rides for women/girls in TGSRTC **ordinary/express (non-luxury)** buses within the state (from Dec 2023). Hugely popular; large ridership increase (verify numbers).
2. **₹500 LPG cylinder** — subsidised cooking-gas refill for eligible (white-ration-card) households.
3. **₹2,500 per month** to eligible women (the direct-benefit cash component; rollout/eligibility — verify current status).

**Significance.** Mahalakshmi targets **women's mobility, household energy, and income** — a flagship welfare promise with high fiscal weight (free travel reimbursed to TGSRTC; LPG subsidy). It intersects with central schemes (**Ujjwala** LPG) and is delivered via the **Praja Palana** enrolment. Debates centre on **fiscal sustainability** and **TGSRTC reimbursement**.

## 🏺 History: Telangana Cuisine, Attire, Tribes & Cultural Symbols

The living heritage of Telangana — **food, dress, tribes and state symbols** — completes the cultural syllabus.

**Cuisine.** Staples: **jowar/bajra (millets) roti (sajja/jonna rotte)**, rice; spicy, tangy flavours. Signature dishes: **Hyderabadi Biryani & Haleem** (Ramzan special, a GI tag), **Sakinalu, Garijelu, Sarva Pindi (Ginnappa), Pachi Pulusu, Golichina Mamsam**. Telangana cuisine is distinct from coastal Andhra (more millets, less coconut).

**Attire & tribes.** Traditional dress; **Banjara/Lambada** embroidery and mirror-work; the **Gond, Koya, Chenchu, Kolam, Lambada/Banjara, Pardhan** are major tribes. **Chenchu** (Nallamala forest, PVTG — Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group), **Kolam** & **Thoti** also PVTGs; **Gond** (Adilabad) with Gussadi/Dandari.

**State cultural symbols (Telangana).** (Verify any that changed.)
- **State festival:** Bathukamma
- **State bird:** Palapitta (Indian Roller)
- **State animal:** Jinka (Deer/Blackbuck — verify; spotted deer)
- **State tree:** Jammi (Prosopis/Shami)
- **State flower:** Tangedu (Tanner's Cassia)
- **State song:** "Jaya Jaya He Telangana" (by Ande Sri)
- **Emblem:** features the **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam** & Charminar.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Cuisine: **millet rottis**, **Hyderabadi Biryani/Haleem (GI)**, Sakinalu, Sarva Pindi. Tribes: **Gond, Koya, Chenchu (PVTG), Lambada/Banjara, Kolam**. Symbols: festival **Bathukamma**, bird **Palapitta**, flower **Tangedu**, tree **Jammi**, song by **Ande Sri**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Bathukamma (festival), Palapitta (bird), Tangedu (flower), Jammi (tree); Chenchu/Kolam = PVTG; Haleem = GI.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **State flower = Tangedu**, **state bird = Palapitta (Indian Roller)**, **state tree = Jammi** — commonly swapped. **Chenchu, Kolam, Thoti** are **PVTGs**. **Hyderabadi Haleem** has a **GI tag**. Verify any symbol said to have changed.

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Subhas Bose, the INA & the RIN Revolt

The final push toward independence included the **armed nationalism** of **Subhas Chandra Bose's INA** and the **1946 naval mutiny**.

**Subhas Chandra Bose.** Twice Congress President (**1938 Haripura, 1939 Tripuri**); resigned after differences with Gandhi and formed the **Forward Bloc (1939)**. He **escaped to Germany (1941)** and then to Southeast Asia.

**Indian National Army (INA / Azad Hind Fauj).** First formed by **Mohan Singh (1942)** from Indian POWs in Southeast Asia (Japanese support); revived and led by **Bose (1943)**, who took charge in **Singapore** and set up the **Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind, Oct 1943)**. Slogans: **"Dilli Chalo,"** "**Jai Hind**," "**Give me blood, I will give you freedom**." INA regiments (incl. the **Rani Jhansi Regiment** of women, led by **Lakshmi Sahgal**) fought alongside Japan in **Imphal-Kohima (1944)**. After Japan's defeat, the INA collapsed; Bose reportedly died in an **air crash (Aug 1945, Taihoku/Taipei)** — contested.

**INA Trials (Red Fort, 1945–46).** The public trial of INA officers (**Shah Nawaz Khan, P.K. Sahgal, G.S. Dhillon**) sparked huge sympathy; Congress defended them (Nehru, Bhulabhai Desai).

**RIN Revolt (Feb 1946).** The **Royal Indian Navy ratings** mutinied at **Bombay** (HMIS Talwar) over pay, food and racial discrimination, spreading to other ports — a sign that even the armed forces were slipping from British control, hastening the decision to quit India.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Bose: Congress Pres (1938/39), **Forward Bloc (1939)**; **INA** (Mohan Singh 1942 → Bose 1943, Singapore; Azad Hind govt Oct 1943; "Dilli Chalo"/"Jai Hind"); **INA Trials Red Fort 1945-46**; **RIN Revolt Feb 1946 (Bombay)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Bose: Forward Bloc 1939 → INA 1943 (Dilli Chalo, Azad Hind) → INA Trials 1945-46 → RIN Revolt Feb 1946.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — INA **first formed by Mohan Singh (1942)**, **revived by Bose (1943)**. **Azad Hind** provisional govt = **Singapore, Oct 1943**. **RIN Revolt = Feb 1946 (naval ratings, Bombay)**. Rani Jhansi Regiment led by **Lakshmi Sahgal**.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"Give me blood..."* → **Subhas Bose**; *"1946 naval mutiny"* → **RIN Revolt**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Bose: 1938/39 Congress Pres; Forward Bloc 1939; escape 1941.
- INA: Mohan Singh 1942; Bose 1943 Singapore; Azad Hind Oct 1943; Rani Jhansi Regt (Lakshmi Sahgal).
- Imphal-Kohima 1944; INA Trials Red Fort 1945-46.
- RIN Revolt Feb 1946 (Bombay, HMIS Talwar).

## 🗺️ Geography: Environmental Issues — Musi Pollution, Urban Lakes & Uranium/Nallamala

Telangana's environmental challenges span **river/lake pollution, mining threats, and forest conservation** — closing the geography syllabus.

**Musi river pollution.** The **Musi** (Krishna tributary through Hyderabad) is heavily polluted by **untreated domestic sewage and industrial effluents** downstream of the city; it affects irrigation/health in downstream villages. The **Musi Riverfront rejuvenation** project (STPs, clean-up, relocation) addresses it.

**Urban lakes.** Hyderabad's lakes (**Hussain Sagar, Osman Sagar, Himayat Sagar** and hundreds of minor tanks) face **encroachment, sewage inflow and eutrophication**. **Hussain Sagar** is notably polluted (industrial + sewage). Protection drives via **HYDRAA** (FTL/buffer zones) and lake-restoration.

**Uranium mining threat — Nallamala.** Proposed **uranium exploration/mining in the Nallamala forest (Amrabad Tiger Reserve area)** raised concerns over the **Chenchu (PVTG) tribals, Krishna river catchment (Srisailam reservoir) contamination, and biodiversity**. Widespread opposition led to the proposals being **shelved/contested** (verify current status).

**Other issues.** **Fluoride** groundwater (Nalgonda — addressed by Mission Bhagiratha); **deforestation/mining** (coal in Singareni belt); **industrial pollution** (Patancheru-Bollaram); **urban air quality**; the **Haritha Haram** afforestation programme (Telanganaku Haritha Haram).
