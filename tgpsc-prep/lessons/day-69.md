## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Fundamental Rights — Articles 15-18 (incl. Reservations)

### Deep Dive

**Article 15 — Prohibition of discrimination.** The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds **only** of **religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth** (or any of them). Enabling clauses:
- **15(3)** — special provisions for **women and children**.
- **15(4)** — special provisions for **SEBCs, SCs and STs** (added by the **1st Amendment, 1951**).
- **15(5)** — reservation in **educational institutions incl. private** (except minority) — **93rd Amendment (2005)**.
- **15(6)** — **EWS** (up to 10%) — **103rd Amendment (2019)**.

**Article 16 — Equality of opportunity in public employment.**
- **16(4)** — reservation for **backward classes** inadequately represented.
- **16(4A)** — reservation in **promotion** for SC/ST (77th Amendment).
- **16(4B)** — **carry-forward** of unfilled vacancies (81st Amendment).
- **16(6)** — **EWS** reservation (103rd Amendment).

**Article 17 — Abolition of untouchability** — its practice in any form is forbidden and punishable (**Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955**).

**Article 18 — Abolition of titles** — the State cannot confer titles (except **military and academic** distinctions); citizens cannot accept foreign titles. **Bharat Ratna / Padma awards are not "titles"** (upheld in *Balaji Raghavan* case) and cannot be used as suffixes/prefixes.

**Reservation jurisprudence:** *Indra Sawhney (Mandal, 1992)* — **50% ceiling**, **creamy-layer** exclusion for OBCs, and no reservation in promotions (later enabled by 16(4A)). The **EWS 10%** (103rd Amendment) was upheld in *Janhit Abhiyan (2022)*.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **15(4)** = 1st Amendment (1951); **15(5)** = 93rd (2005); **EWS 15(6)/16(6)** = 103rd (2019). *Indra Sawhney (1992)*: **50% cap + creamy layer**. Art 18: Bharat Ratna/Padma are **not titles**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Art 15 says discrimination on these grounds **"only"** — a combination with other valid grounds may be allowed. Reservation's **50% ceiling** comes from *Indra Sawhney*; **EWS** is **over and above** and was upheld separately.

💡 **Example:** Free/subsidised education for women under Art 15(3), and OBC reservation with **creamy-layer** exclusion under 15(4)/16(4), are standard illustrations of "enabling" provisions.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Art 15: grounds = religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth ("only").
- 15(4) 1st Amdt; 15(5) 93rd; EWS 103rd (2019).
- Art 16(4A) promotion, 16(4B) carry-forward.
- Indra Sawhney: 50% cap, creamy layer; Art 17 untouchability; Art 18 titles.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *What ceiling on total reservation did Indra Sawhney lay down?* → **50%** (barring extraordinary circumstances).

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Formation of Telangana & After (2014-present)

**Political trajectory (2014-present):**
- **2014-2018:** first TRS government; welfare and irrigation focus (**Kaleshwaram, Mission Bhagiratha, Rythu Bandhu, Kalyana Lakshmi**, etc.).
- **2018:** early assembly elections; **TRS returned** to power.
- **2019 (verify):** the party was renamed **Bharat Rashtra Samithi (BRS)** to signal a national role.
- **December 2023:** the **Congress won** the assembly elections; **A. Revanth Reddy** became Chief Minister, ending nearly a decade of TRS/BRS rule.

**Key institutions/decisions:** new **districts reorganisation** (expanding from 10 to many more districts), a new **Secretariat** (named after Dr. B.R. Ambedkar), and flagship welfare/irrigation programmes across both governments. *(Verify exact district counts, dates, and scheme names.)*

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Telangana formed **`2 June 2014`** (29th state); **KCR (TRS)** first CM; **TRS → BRS** rename (2019, verify); **Congress win Dec 2023**, **Revanth Reddy** CM.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: The Revolt of 1857

### Deep Dive

The **Revolt of 1857** — the first major uprising against the English East India Company — began at **Meerut on `10 May 1857`** and spread across north and central India.

**Causes:**
- **Political** — annexations via the **Doctrine of Lapse** (Dalhousie) and the annexation of **Awadh (1856)**.
- **Economic** — ruin of artisans, heavy land revenue, drain of wealth.
- **Socio-religious** — fear of interference with religion/customs (e.g., social reforms, missionary activity).
- **Military** — discrimination against sepoys; the **immediate spark** was the **greased cartridge (Enfield rifle)** rumour (cow/pig fat).

**Outbreak.** **Mangal Pandey** (Barrackpore, **29 March 1857**) was an early flashpoint; the full mutiny erupted at **Meerut (10 May 1857)**, and the sepoys marched to **Delhi**, proclaiming the Mughal **Bahadur Shah Zafar (II)** as symbolic emperor.

**Major centres & leaders:**

| Centre | Leader(s) |
|---|---|
| Delhi | Bahadur Shah II; **Bakht Khan** |
| Kanpur | **Nana Saheb**; Tantia Tope |
| Lucknow | **Begum Hazrat Mahal** |
| Jhansi | **Rani Lakshmibai** |
| Bareilly | Khan Bahadur Khan |
| Bihar (Arrah) | **Kunwar Singh** |
| Faizabad | Maulvi Ahmadullah |

**Suppression & aftermath.** The revolt was **crushed by 1858**. Its most important consequence was the **Government of India Act, 1858** — **abolishing Company rule** and transferring power to the **British Crown**; the **Queen's Proclamation (1858)** promised non-interference and equal treatment.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Began **Meerut `10 May 1857`**; immediate cause the **greased cartridge**; symbolic head **Bahadur Shah II**. Result: **GoI Act 1858** ended Company rule → **Crown rule** + **Queen's Proclamation**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Mangal Pandey (29 Mar 1857)** was an early incident; the **revolt proper began at Meerut (10 May 1857)**. The revolt **transferred power to the Crown**, it did **not** grant self-rule.

💡 **Example:** Matching **centre-to-leader** (Kanpur → Nana Saheb/Tantia Tope, Jhansi → Lakshmibai, Lucknow → Hazrat Mahal) is a very common 1857 PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Causes: political/economic/socio-religious/military; spark = greased cartridge.
- Meerut 10 May 1857; Bahadur Shah II at Delhi.
- Leaders by centre (table above).
- GoI Act 1858 → Crown rule; Queen's Proclamation.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which Act ended East India Company rule after 1857?* → the **Government of India Act, 1858**.
