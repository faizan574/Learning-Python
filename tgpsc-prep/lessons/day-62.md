## 🏛️ Polity: Non-Constitutional (Statutory/Executive) Bodies

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Non-constitutional bodies** are created by an **Act of Parliament (statutory)** or by an **executive resolution** — not directly by the Constitution.

**NITI Aayog (2015)** — a **non-statutory** (executive resolution) **think-tank**, replacing the **Planning Commission (1950)**. Chaired by the **PM**; a **Governing Council** of all CMs/LG; promotes **cooperative & competitive federalism**; **no financial-allocation power** (unlike the old Planning Commission). Has a **CEO** and Vice-Chairperson.

**NHRC (1993)** — the **National Human Rights Commission**, a **statutory** body under the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**. Chairperson (a retired CJI or SC judge) + members; **recommendatory** powers (can inquire, recommend, approach courts) — a "watchdog," not enforcement. Term/age revised by amendments.

**CIC (2005)** — the **Central Information Commission**, statutory under the **Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005**; hears second appeals/complaints on information access; State Information Commissions parallel it.

**Lokpal (2013/operational later)** — anti-corruption **ombudsman** at the Union level under the **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013**; inquires into corruption complaints against public functionaries (incl. PM with safeguards). States have **Lokayuktas**.

**CVC (1964, statutory 2003)** — **Central Vigilance Commission**, apex vigilance body (Santhanam Committee origin; statutory status after *Vineet Narain*).

**Key distinction:** these derive authority from **statute/executive order**, so Parliament can alter/abolish them by ordinary law — unlike constitutional bodies (EC, CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **NITI Aayog (2015, executive, think-tank, no funds power)** replaced Planning Commission. **NHRC (1993, statutory, recommendatory)**. **CIC (RTI 2005)**. **Lokpal (2013, ombudsman)**. **CVC (statutory 2003)**. All **non-constitutional** (statute/executive).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **NITI Aayog is non-statutory** (executive resolution) and has **no fund-allocation** power. **NHRC/Lokpal/CIC/CVC are statutory** (by law), not constitutional. Constitutional bodies (EC, CAG, UPSC, FC, SC/ST/BC) are the contrast.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Which body replaced the Planning Commission, and is it statutory?* → **NITI Aayog (2015)** — it is **non-statutory** (created by executive resolution).

### 🎯 Exam Pointers

- NHRC 1993 (statutory, recommendatory); CIC (RTI 2005).
- Lokpal 2013 (ombudsman); Lokayukta in states.
- All non-constitutional (vs EC/CAG/UPSC/FC).

## 🏰 Telangana History: Tribal & Peasant Revolts (Ramji Gond, Komaram Bheem)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana's tribal belts saw sustained revolts against the Nizam's feudal/forest exploitation and later against landlords.

**Ramji Gond (1857-1860, Adilabad).** A **Gond leader** who led an armed rebellion against the Nizam and the British (during the 1857 uprising era), resisting oppressive taxation and forest/land encroachment on tribal territory. He was captured and hanged (~1860), reportedly at **Nirmal** (the "Veyyi Urula Marri" — the banyan where many rebels were hanged, is associated with this).

**Komaram Bheem (1901-1940, Adilabad).** The iconic **Gond freedom fighter** who fought the **Nizam's regime** and its landlords/forest officials for tribal rights. His rallying cry **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"** (water, forest, land) demanded tribal control over natural resources. He organised Gonds in the **Jodeghat** area; the Nizam's forces killed him in an encounter at **Jodeghat (1940)**. He became a symbol of Adivasi self-rule (and his story influenced later autonomy/Fifth Schedule debates). Widely commemorated (Kumram Bheem Asifabad district is named after him).

**Peasant/other movements:** the broader **Telangana Peasant Armed Struggle (1946-51)** (next in the syllabus) drew on this legacy of agrarian/tribal resistance (vetti/bonded labour, illegal exactions by deshmukhs/jagirdars).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Ramji Gond** (Adilabad, 1857-60, anti-Nizam/British, hanged ~1860). **Komaram Bheem** (Gond, 1901-1940; **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"**; killed at **Jodeghat 1940**; district Kumram Bheem Asifabad named after him). Both from the **Adilabad Gond** belt.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Komaram Bheem's slogan = "Jal, Jangal, Jameen"** (killed at **Jodeghat, 1940**). **Ramji Gond** (1857-60) is earlier. Both are **Gond** leaders of the **Adilabad** region — distinct figures, distinct periods.

### 💡 Example

*Q: What was Komaram Bheem's rallying cry, and where was he killed?* → **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"**; killed at **Jodeghat (1940)**.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Civil Disobedience, Round Tables & Poona Pact (Round 4)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-34).** Authorised at Lahore (1929), Gandhi launched it with the **Dandi Salt March** (Sabarmati → Dandi, `241 miles`, **12 March – 6 April 1930**), breaking the **salt law**. Unlike Non-Cooperation (withdrawal), CDM actively **broke laws** (salt, forest, tax); wide participation incl. women; the **North-West Frontier** wing led by **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** (Khudai Khidmatgars / Red Shirts).

**Round Table Conferences (London):**
- **First RTC (1930-31)** — Congress **boycotted** it.
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931)** — suspended CDM; Gandhi attended the **Second RTC (1931)** as sole Congress representative, but it failed over the **communal question**.
- CDM **resumed (1932)** under Willingdon's repression; **Third RTC (1932)** — Congress absent.

**Communal Award & Poona Pact.** The **Communal Award (Aug 1932)** by PM **Ramsay MacDonald** granted **separate electorates to the Depressed Classes (Dalits)**. Gandhi opposed it (fearing division of Hindu society) with a **fast unto death** in Yerawada jail. The **Poona Pact (24 September 1932)** between **Gandhi and B. R. Ambedkar** replaced separate electorates with **reserved seats** for the Depressed Classes within a **joint (general) electorate**, and increased their reserved seats.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — CDM 1930 (Dandi/salt, 12 Mar–6 Apr); **Gandhi-Irwin Pact 5 Mar 1931** → 2nd RTC; **Communal Award 1932** (MacDonald, separate electorates for Depressed Classes) → **Poona Pact 24 Sep 1932** (Gandhi–Ambedkar): reserved seats, joint electorate. NWFP: Ghaffar Khan (Red Shirts).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Poona Pact** replaced separate electorates with **reserved seats** (joint electorate) — it didn't abolish reservation. Gandhi attended the **2nd** RTC (not 1st/3rd). Communal Award by **Ramsay MacDonald**.

### 💡 Example

*Q: What did the Poona Pact (1932) achieve?* → Replaced **separate electorates** for the Depressed Classes with **reserved seats** in a **joint electorate** (after Gandhi's fast).

- Gandhi-Irwin Pact 1931 → 2nd RTC.
- 3 RTCs (1930-31, 1931, 1932).
- Communal Award (MacDonald) → Poona Pact (Gandhi + Ambedkar), 24 Sep 1932.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Rivers — The Godavari System (Round 4)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Godavari** is Telangana's **largest river** (~**79%** of the state's drainage) — the "Dakshina Ganga" (Ganga of the South), India's second-longest river.

**Course & entry.** Rises at **Trimbak (Nashik, Maharashtra)**; enters Telangana in the **north-west (Nizamabad/Kandakurthy)**, flows generally **south-east** across the northern districts, and exits toward Andhra Pradesh (Bhadrachalam) to the Bay of Bengal.

**Major tributaries (in/bordering Telangana):**
- **Left bank:** **Pranahita** (formed by Wardha + Wainganga + Penganga; the largest Godavari tributary), **Indravati**, **Sabari** — join in the north-east/east.
- **Right bank:** **Manjira** (through Nizamabad/Medak; Nizam Sagar, Singur dams), **Manair** (Karimnagar; Lower/Mid Manair), **Kinnerasani** (Khammam).
- **Kandakurthy (Nizamabad)** — the **Triveni Sangamam** where the Godavari meets Manjira & Haridra.

**Major projects on the Godavari system:**
- **Sri Ram Sagar Project (SRSP / Pochampad)** — Nizamabad; major multipurpose.
- **Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Project (KLIS)** — Medigadda (Godavari-Pranahita confluence), reservoirs Mid Manair/Mallanna Sagar/Kondapochamma.
- **Nizam Sagar** (Manjira), **Singur** (Manjira), **Kadem**, **Sripada Yellampally**, **Devadula LIS**, **Kanti Velugu**.

**Significance:** irrigation backbone of northern Telangana; **Basara** (Saraswati temple on the Godavari), **Bhadrachalam** (Rama temple), and the **Godavari Pushkaralu** (12-yearly festival) are cultural landmarks.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Godavari** = largest river (~79% drainage), "Dakshina Ganga"; rises **Trimbak (Nashik)**; enters Telangana at **Kandakurthy (Nizamabad, Triveni Sangamam)**. Tributaries: **Pranahita (largest), Indravati, Sabari (left); Manjira, Manair, Kinnerasani (right)**. Projects: **SRSP/Pochampad, Kaleshwaram, Nizam Sagar**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Pranahita** is the **largest Godavari tributary** (Wardha+Wainganga+Penganga). Godavari rises at **Trimbak/Nashik** (Maharashtra), not in Telangana. **Manjira** (right bank) hosts Nizam Sagar/Singur; **Kaleshwaram is on the Godavari** at the Pranahita confluence.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Which is the largest tributary of the Godavari, and where does the Godavari enter Telangana?* → **Pranahita** (largest tributary); enters at **Kandakurthy (Nizamabad)** — the Triveni Sangamam.
