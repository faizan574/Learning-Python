## 🏛️ Polity: Fundamental Rights — Articles 23-30 & Article 32 (Writs)

These complete the FR chapter: the **Right against Exploitation (23-24)**, **Right to Freedom of Religion (25-28)**, **Cultural & Educational Rights (29-30)**, and the **Right to Constitutional Remedies (32)** — called by Ambedkar the "**heart and soul of the Constitution**."

**Right against Exploitation.** `Art 23` — prohibits **traffic in human beings, begar and forced labour** (basis of the Bonded Labour System Abolition Act). `Art 24` — prohibits employment of **children below 14** in factories, mines or hazardous work.

**Right to Freedom of Religion.** `Art 25` — freedom of **conscience & free profession, practice, propagation** of religion (subject to public order, morality, health). `Art 26` — freedom to **manage religious affairs**. `Art 27` — no **compulsory taxes** for promotion of a religion. `Art 28` — no **religious instruction** in wholly state-funded institutions.

**Cultural & Educational Rights.** `Art 29` — protection of **language, script, culture** of any section; no denial of admission on grounds of religion/race/caste/language. `Art 30` — right of **minorities (religious & linguistic)** to **establish and administer** educational institutions.

**Right to Constitutional Remedies.** `Art 32` — the right to move the **Supreme Court directly** for enforcement of FRs; the SC can issue **writs**. (High Courts have wider writ power under `Art 226`.) The five writs:
- **Habeas Corpus** ("have the body") — against illegal detention.
- **Mandamus** ("we command") — directs a public authority to do its duty.
- **Prohibition** — stops a lower court exceeding jurisdiction (issued *before*).
- **Certiorari** — quashes a lower court's order (issued *after*).
- **Quo-Warranto** — questions a person's title to a public office.

| Article | Right |
|---|---|
| `Art 23-24` | Against exploitation (forced labour; child labour) |
| `Art 25-28` | Freedom of religion |
| `Art 29-30` | Cultural & educational (minorities) |
| `Art 32` | Constitutional remedies (writs) |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — `Art 32` is the "**heart and soul**" (Ambedkar) — direct SC access for FR enforcement via **5 writs**. `Art 32` is itself a **Fundamental Right** and cannot be suspended except during Emergency (not for Art 20/21).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — Writs = "**Ha-Ma-Pro-Cert-Quo**": Habeas corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-warranto. Religion = **25 profess, 26 manage, 27 no tax, 28 no instruction**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — `Art 32` (SC) is a **FR**; `Art 226` (HC) is **not** a FR but is **wider** (covers FRs *and* legal rights). **Prohibition = before** judgment (stops), **Certiorari = after** (quashes). Mandamus **cannot** be issued against the President/Governor or a private individual (except statutory duty).

💡 **Example:** A person illegally detained files for **Habeas Corpus**; a municipal body refusing to perform a statutory duty is compelled by **Mandamus**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Art 23 forced labour/begar; Art 24 child labour <14 (hazardous).
- Art 25-28 religion (profess/manage/no-tax/no-instruction); Art 29-30 minorities.
- Art 32: 5 writs; heart & soul; vs Art 226 (wider, HC).
- Prohibition (before) vs Certiorari (after); Habeas corpus even against private persons.

## 🤝 Schemes: SIR (Special Intensive Revision) & Electoral Roll Issues

The **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of electoral rolls is a current-affairs topic — an **Election Commission of India (ECI)** exercise to thoroughly verify and update the voter list, which became a live political issue in Telangana and nationally.

**What is SIR?** The ECI periodically revises electoral rolls. A **Special Intensive Revision** is a more thorough, **house-to-house verification** drive (as opposed to the routine Summary Revision), aimed at **removing ineligible/duplicate/dead entries** and adding eligible new voters — ensuring a clean, accurate roll before elections. (Verify the exact scope, reference date and states covered in the current round from ECI notifications.)

**Why it became contentious.** Opposition parties alleged that intensive revisions could lead to **wrongful deletions** of genuine voters (especially the poor, migrants, minorities) if documentation demands are high, while the ECI maintains it improves roll integrity. Debates centre on **documentation requirements, timelines, and the burden of proof** on voters. In Telangana, roll accuracy intersects with the caste survey data and local-body elections. (Treat specific numbers/claims as **verify latest** — this is evolving and litigated.)

| Aspect | Note |
|---|---|
| Authority | Election Commission of India (ECI) |
| Nature | Intensive house-to-house roll verification |
| Goal | Remove ineligible/duplicate; add eligible |
| Controversy | Risk of wrongful deletions vs roll purity |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **SIR = Special Intensive Revision** of electoral rolls by the **ECI** — a deep, house-to-house verification to clean the voter list; politically contested over **possible wrongful deletions**. Verify current scope/reference date.

## 🏺 History: Telangana 1970–2000 — TPS, Naxalism Phase & Identity Politics

The three decades between the **1969 agitation** and the **2001 statehood movement** shaped Telangana's political consciousness through **regional parties, radical-left (Naxalite) activity**, and a slow build-up of **sub-regional identity**.

**Aftermath of 1969.** The **Telangana Praja Samithi (TPS)** won big in **1971** but merged with the **Congress**; the **Six-Point Formula (1973)** and **Article 371D (1975)** institutionalised local safeguards (zones, cadres, Administrative Tribunal). The **jai Andhra** agitation (1972, coastal Andhra) and the Mulki court rulings formed the backdrop.

**Naxalite/radical-left phase.** From the **Srikakulam** origins (late 1960s) the **Naxalite movement** spread into Telangana districts — especially **Karimnagar, Warangal, Adilabad, Khammam** (the "North Telangana" belt). The **People's War Group (PWG)**, founded by **Kondapalli Seetharamaiah (1980)**, became a major force, drawing on agrarian discontent, feudal landlordism (dora/vetti) and tribal land alienation. This radical politics deeply influenced Telangana's cultural expression (revolutionary poetry, Gaddar's songs).

**Identity & cultural assertion.** Writers, poets and intellectuals (Kaloji, Dasarathi, **Gaddar**, Jana Natya Mandali) kept the distinct **Telangana identity** alive — dialect, culture, grievances over water, funds and jobs. The **1985 "Mulki/Presidential Order" G.O. 610 (1985)** on local employment violations later became a key grievance document in the statehood movement.

**Political churn.** The rise of the **Telugu Desam Party (TDP, 1982, NTR)** reshaped state politics; Telangana sentiments simmered within both Congress and TDP, setting the stage for the **TRS (2001)**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — 1970–2000: **TPS merges with Congress**; **Six-Point Formula (1973)/Art 371D (1975)**; **Naxalite/PWG (Kondapalli Seetharamaiah, 1980)** in North Telangana; **G.O. 610 (1985)** on local-jobs violations; cultural identity kept alive (Gaddar) — paving the way for **TRS (2001)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**69 agitation → 73 Six-Point/371D → 80 PWG (North Telangana) → 85 G.O. 610 → 2001 TRS.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **G.O. 610 (1985)** addressed violations of local-employment (Presidential Order) rules — a frequently tested date. The **PWG** (1980, K. Seetharamaiah) is distinct from the original **Srikakulam** struggle. Don't confuse the **1969** movement with this **1970–2000** phase.

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Gandhi's Arrival & Early Satyagrahas (Champaran, Kheda, Ahmedabad)

Returning from South Africa in **1915**, **M.K. Gandhi** spent a year observing India (on Gokhale's advice) before leading three **local satyagrahas (1917–1918)** that established his method and leadership.

**Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar) — his first in India.** Indigo planters forced peasants under the **tinkathia system** (grow indigo on 3/20 of land). Invited by **Raj Kumar Shukla**, Gandhi investigated, defied an order to leave, and secured a **committee** (Gandhi a member) that abolished tinkathia and part-refunded illegal dues. First use of **satyagraha** in India.

**Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918, Gujarat).** A dispute between mill owners and workers over the **plague bonus**. Gandhi advised workers to strike and went on his **first hunger fast** to sustain their resolve; a tribunal awarded workers a **35% wage rise**. (Here Gandhi's associate **Anasuya Behn** was prominent.)

**Kheda Satyagraha (1918, Gujarat).** Crop failure; peasants couldn't pay land revenue but the government refused remission. Gandhi (with **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**, **Indulal Yagnik**) led a **no-tax campaign**; government quietly conceded that revenue be collected only from those who could pay. This brought **Patel** into the national movement.

| Satyagraha | Year | Issue / Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Champaran | 1917 | Indigo/tinkathia abolished (first in India) |
| Ahmedabad | 1918 | Mill workers' wages (Gandhi's first fast) → 35% rise |
| Kheda | 1918 | No-tax (revenue remission); Patel emerges |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Champaran (1917, Bihar)** was Gandhi's **first satyagraha in India** (indigo/tinkathia). **Ahmedabad (1918)** saw his **first hunger fast** (mill workers → 35% rise); **Kheda (1918)** was a **no-tax** campaign bringing in **Sardar Patel**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Champaran (indigo, 1917) → Ahmedabad (mill fast, 1918) → Kheda (no-tax, Patel, 1918).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Champaran = indigo (Bihar)**; **Kheda = revenue/no-tax (Gujarat)** — don't swap. Gandhi's **first fast** was at **Ahmedabad (1918)**. **Raj Kumar Shukla** invited Gandhi to Champaran.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"first Gandhi satyagraha in India"* → **Champaran 1917**; *"brought Sardar Patel into the movement"* → **Kheda 1918**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Champaran 1917: Raj Kumar Shukla, tinkathia, first satyagraha.
- Ahmedabad 1918: mill workers, plague bonus, first fast, 35% rise, Anasuya Behn.
- Kheda 1918: no-tax, revenue remission, Patel & Indulal Yagnik.
- Gandhi returned to India in 1915 (on Gokhale's advice).

## 🗺️ Geography: Area Focus — Warangal & the Kakatiya Belt

**Warangal** (historic **Orugallu/Ekasila Nagaram**) in **north-central/eastern Telangana** was the **Kakatiya capital** and remains a major urban, agrarian and heritage hub on the Deccan plateau.

**Location & physiography.** Part of the old composite Warangal district (now split into **Hanumakonda, Warangal, Jangaon, Mahabubabad, Jayashankar-Bhupalpally, Mulugu**; verify the exact carve-up). It lies in the **Godavari basin**, with undulating plains, granitic hills and numerous **Kakatiya-era tanks (cheruvu)** — a notable **chain-tank irrigation** legacy.

**Water & agriculture.** The region relies on **tank irrigation** (Kakatiya tanks like **Pakhal, Laknavaram, Ramappa lakes**) and canals; **Mission Kakatiya** revived minor irrigation tanks. Crops include **paddy, cotton, maize, chillies**; Warangal hosts a large **agricultural market (enumalagadda/enumamula market)** and was a cotton/handloom centre.

**Heritage (Kakatiya belt).** UNESCO-listed **Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple** at Palampet (13th c.); the **Thousand Pillar Temple (Hanumakonda)**; the **Warangal Fort** with its iconic **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam** (the state emblem's arch); **Bhadrakali temple**; and **Laknavaram/Pakhal** lakes. Forests and the **Eturnagaram Wildlife Sanctuary** (Mulugu) lie in the belt.
