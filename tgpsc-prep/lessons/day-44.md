## 🏛️ Polity: Making of the Constitution & Constituent Assembly

### 📖 Deep Dive

The Constituent Assembly (CA) was constituted under the **Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946**, *not* by universal adult franchise — members were **indirectly elected** by the provincial legislative assemblies (themselves elected on a limited franchise), with seats allotted roughly **one per million** population and divided among Muslims, Sikhs and "General" categories. Total planned strength was `389` (296 British-India + 93 princely states); after Partition it reduced to `299`.

The Assembly first met on **`9 December 1946`**; **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** was the *temporary* (provisional) chairman as the eldest member, and **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was elected permanent President on `11 December 1946`. The **Objectives Resolution**, moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru** on `13 December 1946`, laid down the philosophy later crystallised as the **Preamble**.

The **Drafting Committee** (set up `29 August 1947`) had **7 members** under **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar** as Chairman. Key functionaries: **B. N. Rau** was the Constitutional Advisor who prepared the initial draft; **H. V. R. Iyengar** was Secretary; **Prem Behari Narain Raizada** was the calligrapher who handwrote the Constitution.

The Assembly worked for **`2 years, 11 months, 18 days`**, held **11 sessions**, and the Constitution was **adopted on `26 November 1949`** (celebrated as **Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas**) and **came into force on `26 January 1950`** — a date chosen to honour the 1930 Purna Swaraj declaration.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Adopted `26 Nov 1949`; enforced `26 Jan 1950`. Drafting Committee = `7` members under `Ambedkar`. Objectives Resolution moved by Nehru on `13 Dec 1946`.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Members were **indirectly elected** by provincial assemblies, **NOT** directly by the people. Also, the CA was **not** created by the Indian Independence Act 1947 — it predates it (Cabinet Mission, 1946).

Certain **provisions came into force immediately on `26 Nov 1949`** (citizenship, elections, provisional parliament, Arts 5-9, 60, 324, 366-393); the *rest* on 26 Jan 1950. Some important committees: Union Powers & Union Constitution Committees (Nehru), Provincial Constitution Committee (Patel), and the Drafting Committee (Ambedkar).

### 💡 Example

*Q: Who acted as the provisional/temporary chairman of the Constituent Assembly?* → **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** (as eldest member), before Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Cabinet Mission `1946` → CA scheme; **not** adult franchise, **not** the 1947 Act.
- First sitting `9 Dec 1946`; the Muslim League **boycotted** it.
- Draft published Feb 1948; ~`2000` amendments discussed.
- Total sittings ≈ `165` days across `11` sessions.

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Mahalakshmi (Free Travel, ₹500 LPG, Women's Support)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Mahalakshmi** is the first of the Congress government's **Six Guarantees** (launched after the Dec 2023 win), aimed at women's economic empowerment. It bundles three components:

1. **Free bus travel for women** in Telangana State Road Transport (TGSRTC) ordinary & express services — launched **`9 December 2023`** (the first guarantee operationalised). Women (and transgender persons) travel free in **Pallevelugu, Express and Metro Express** categories within the state using a **zero-fare pink ticket**; **not** valid on premium (AC/Garuda/Rajadhani) services.
2. **`₹500` LPG cylinder** for eligible households.
3. **Monthly financial assistance** to eligible women (announced as `₹2,500`/month under the manifesto — *verify latest official rollout status and figure before the exam*, as phased implementation and eligibility criteria evolve).

The **free-travel** component alone has driven a very large number of zero-fare journeys and a substantial reimbursement outgo to TGSRTC (*verify latest official cumulative figures*). Administratively, the state reimburses the corporation for the fare value of pink tickets.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Mahalakshmi = **free bus travel + ₹500 LPG + monthly cash support**. Free travel launched **`9 Dec 2023`** on TGSRTC ordinary/express/metro (not premium AC services).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Free travel does **not** apply to AC/luxury (Garuda/Rajadhani) services, and applies **within Telangana** only. Don't confuse the ₹500 LPG (Mahalakshmi) with any central LPG subsidy.

**Exam framing:** Mahalakshmi is usually paired conceptually with women-centric welfare (compare with other states' free-travel models). Focus on the **launch date**, the **three components**, and the **category of buses covered**.

## 🏰 Telangana History: Formation of Telangana & After (2014-Present)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014** received Presidential assent on **`1 March 2014`**, and **Telangana was formed as India's `29th` state on `2 June 2014`** (celebrated as **Telangana Formation Day**). **K. Chandrashekar Rao (KCR)** became the **first Chief Minister**; **E. S. L. Narasimhan** served as the common Governor for both Telangana and residuary Andhra Pradesh in the transition.

Telangana was carved from **10 districts** of the erstwhile combined state. **Hyderabad** was designated the **common capital** for both states for a period **up to `10 years`** (i.e., until 2 June 2024) under the Act. The state later undertook a major **reorganisation of districts** (2016 onwards), expanding from 10 to **33 districts** (*verify current count before exam, as boundaries have been adjusted*).

Key post-formation milestones (verify latest specifics before exam):
- **`2014`** — first TRS (now **BRS**) government under KCR.
- **`2018`** — early dissolution of the assembly; TRS returned to power (Dec 2018).
- **`2023` (Nov/Dec)** — Assembly elections; **Indian National Congress** won and **A. Revanth Reddy** became Chief Minister (*first Congress CM of Telangana*), ending ~10 years of TRS/BRS rule.

Flagship programmes across this decade include **Mission Kakatiya** (minor irrigation/tank restoration), **Mission Bhagiratha** (piped drinking water), **Rythu Bandhu** (investment support, TRS era) and the **Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Project**. Under the new government, the **Six Guarantees** (Mahalakshmi, Rythu Bharosa, Gruha Jyothi, Indiramma Indlu, Yuva Vikasam, Cheyutha) frame welfare policy.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Formed **`2 June 2014`** as the **29th** state; **AP Reorganisation Act 2014** (assent `1 Mar 2014`); **KCR** first CM; Hyderabad common capital for up to `10 years`.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Telangana was the **29th** state (not 28th). Formation Day is **2 June**, distinct from the Act's assent date (1 March 2014).

*Q: Who became Telangana's first Chief Minister and which party formed the government in 2023?* → **KCR (TRS) in 2014; Congress under Revanth Reddy in 2023.**

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Simon Commission, Nehru Report & Lahore Session

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Simon Commission** (formally the Indian Statutory Commission) was appointed in **`1927`** to review the working of the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms (Govt of India Act 1919). It was boycotted nationwide because it was **all-white** (`7` British members, no Indian) — the slogan **"Simon Go Back"** spread. It arrived in India on **`3 February 1928`**. During a Lahore protest, **Lala Lajpat Rai** was injured in a lathi charge (Oct 1928) and died soon after (`17 Nov 1928`), an event the revolutionaries later avenged (Saunders killing).

In response to the Secretary of State's challenge to produce an agreed constitution, an All-Parties Conference drafted the **Nehru Report (`1928`)**, chaired by **Motilal Nehru**. Its key recommendations: **Dominion Status** as the goal, **rejection of separate electorates**, a **bill of fundamental rights**, linguistic provinces, and responsible government. It was opposed by **Jinnah** (who countered with his **Fourteen Points, 1929**) and by younger leaders like **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Subhas Bose**, who wanted **complete independence**, not dominion status.

At the **Lahore Session of the INC (December `1929`)**, presided over by **Jawaharlal Nehru**, the Congress adopted **Purna Swaraj (complete independence)** as its goal. It resolved that **`26 January 1930`** be observed as the **first Independence Day (Purna Swaraj Day)**, and authorised the launch of **Civil Disobedience**. On `31 Dec 1929`, the tricolour was unfurled on the banks of the Ravi.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Simon Commission `1927` (all-white, "Simon Go Back"); Nehru Report `1928` (Motilal Nehru, Dominion Status); Lahore Session `1929` → **Purna Swaraj**; first Independence Day `26 Jan 1930`.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Nehru Report** was chaired by **Motilal** Nehru (not Jawaharlal). It sought **Dominion Status**, whereas the **Lahore Session (1929)** demanded **complete independence** — a key contrast.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Why was the Simon Commission boycotted?* → Because it had **no Indian member** (all-white), denying Indians a role in reviewing their own constitutional future.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Simon appointed 1927; reached India **3 Feb 1928**.
- Lajpat Rai injured (Lahore lathi charge) → died 17 Nov 1928.
- Nehru Report 1928: no separate electorates, fundamental rights, Dominion Status.
- Jinnah's **Fourteen Points** (1929) = response to Nehru Report.
- Lahore 1929: J. Nehru president; Purna Swaraj; 26 Jan 1930 Independence Day.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Climate, Rainfall Patterns & Droughts

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana has a **tropical, semi-arid to sub-humid** climate with **three seasons**: hot summer (Mar-May, temperatures often crossing `40°C`, peaking in the northern/Adilabad and Ramagundam belts), the **southwest monsoon** (Jun-Sep) that delivers the **bulk (~80%)** of annual rainfall, and a mild winter (Dec-Feb). Some northeast-monsoon (Oct-Nov) rain reaches the southern districts.

Average annual rainfall is around **`900–950 mm`** (*verify latest official normal*), but it is **unevenly distributed**: the **northern districts (Adilabad, Kumram Bheem, Nirmal)** receive more (`1000+ mm`), while the **southern/western Palamuru (Mahabubnagar) region** is comparatively **rain-shadow / drought-prone** (`600–700 mm`). This gradient — wetter north, drier south-west — is the state's defining rainfall pattern.

**Drought vulnerability** is highest in the erstwhile **Mahabubnagar, Nalgonda and parts of Rangareddy** districts, driven by low/erratic rainfall, high evaporation, hard-rock (granitic-gneiss) terrain with poor groundwater retention, and historical fluoride/quality issues (Nalgonda). Deficient or delayed monsoons produce **kharif** stress; the state has responded with large **lift-irrigation** projects (Kaleshwaram, Palamuru-Rangareddy) and **Mission Kakatiya** tank restoration to buffer rainfall variability.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — SW monsoon (Jun-Sep) gives ~**80%** of rainfall; state average ~**`900-950 mm`** (verify). **North wetter (1000+ mm), south-west drier (600-700 mm)** — Palamuru/Nalgonda most drought-prone.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **southwest** monsoon (not northeast) is the primary rain source for Telangana. The **north** (Adilabad belt) is the wettest, not the south.

### 💡 Example

*Q: Which region of Telangana is most drought-prone and why?* → The **south-western Palamuru (Mahabubnagar) belt**, due to rain-shadow location, erratic low rainfall, and hard-rock terrain with poor groundwater.
