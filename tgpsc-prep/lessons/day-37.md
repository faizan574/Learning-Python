## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Centre-State Relations & Federalism

### 📖 Deep Dive

India is a **"Union of States" (Art 1)** — a **quasi-federal** system (federal structure with a **unitary bias**). Centre-State relations fall in three groups:

**Legislative (Art 245-255):** the **Seventh Schedule** divides subjects into three lists —

| List | Subjects | Legislated by |
|---|---|---|
| **Union List** | ~100 | Parliament |
| **State List** | ~61 | State legislatures |
| **Concurrent List** | ~52 | Both (Union prevails on conflict, **Art 254**) |

**Residuary powers** rest with the **Union (Art 248)**. Parliament can legislate on State subjects in special cases (`Art 249` Rajya Sabha resolution, `250` emergency, `252` states' consent, `253` international agreements).

**Administrative (Art 256-263):** includes the **Inter-State Council (Art 263)**.

**Financial (Art 264-293):**

| Body | Article | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Finance Commission** | **280** | Every 5 years; recommends **tax devolution** & grants-in-aid |
| **GST Council** | **279A** (101st Amendment, 2016) | Chaired by **Union FM** + state FMs; recommends GST rates |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — In the **GST Council**, the **Centre has 1/3 and states together 2/3** of the votes, and every decision needs a **3/4 majority**. The **Finance Commission (Art 280)** — a constitutional body appointed by the President every five years — governs **vertical & horizontal tax devolution**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Residuary powers** are with the **Union (Art 248)** — unlike the USA (states). On a Concurrent-List conflict, **Union law prevails (Art 254)**. The GST Council is `Art 279A` (**101st Amendment, 2016**).

### 💡 Example
The GST Council's **weighted-voting** design (states 2/3, 3/4 needed) is a rare **cooperative-federalism** institution — neither Centre nor states can act alone, a favourite analytical MCQ.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **7th Schedule** = 3 lists; **residuary → Union (Art 248)**.
- **Art 254** = Union prevails on Concurrent conflict.
- **Finance Commission = Art 280** (5-yearly); **GST Council = Art 279A** (101st Amdt).
- India = **quasi-federal**, "Union of States."

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Tribal & Peasant Revolts — Ramji Gond & Komaram Bheem

### 📖 Deep Dive

Telangana's tribal belts (especially **Adilabad**) produced heroic revolts against feudal and colonial exploitation.

**Ramji Gond** (mid-19th century): a **Gond leader of Adilabad** who rose against the **Nizam and the British** around the time of the **1857 upheaval**, resisting oppressive taxation and forest control. He was captured and **hanged near Nirmal** — tradition holds that around a **thousand rebels were hanged on a banyan tree** ("Nirmal Marri").

**Komaram Bheem** (`1901–1940`): the iconic **Gond leader** of Adilabad who led a rebellion against the **Nizam (Asaf Jah VII)** over **land and forest rights** (podu cultivation), with the enduring slogan **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"** (water, forest, land). He waged a guerrilla struggle around **Jodeghat/Babejhari** and **died in a police encounter on `2 September 1940`** at Jodeghat.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Komaram Bheem** (`1901-1940`) raised the cry **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"** for tribal rights and fell at **Jodeghat (1940)**; the anthropologist **Christoph von Fürer-Haimendorf** later studied and championed the Gonds. **Ramji Gond** was the earlier (1857-era) Gond rebel hanged near **Nirmal**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Ramji Gond (1857, Nirmal) → Komaram Bheem (Jodeghat, 1940, Jal-Jangal-Jameen)**". Both are **Gond** leaders of **Adilabad**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Komaram Bheem's slogan is **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"**; he died at **Jodeghat (1940)**. **Komaram Bheem Asifabad** district is named after him. Ramji Gond belongs to the **1857 era**, not the 20th century.

### 💡 Example
The naming of a district **Komaram Bheem Asifabad** and the state's celebration of his memory show how a tribal revolt entered mainstream Telangana identity — a static-to-current linkage.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Ramji Gond** — Gond rebel, 1857 era, hanged near **Nirmal**.
- **Komaram Bheem** — 1901-1940, **"Jal Jangal Jameen"**, died **Jodeghat 1940**.
- Both **Gond** leaders of **Adilabad**; Haimendorf studied the Gonds.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*What was Komaram Bheem's slogan, and where did he die?*
→ **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"**; he died at **Jodeghat (1940)**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: INC Formation & the Moderate Era (1885-1905)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Indian National Congress (INC)** was founded on **`28 December 1885`** at **Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay**, by **A.O. Hume** (a retired British civil servant), with **72 delegates**; its first president was **W.C. Bonnerjee**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The INC's early phase (`1885-1905`) was led by the **Moderates** — **Dadabhai Naoroji** ("Grand Old Man of India," **drain theory**, *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*), **Gopal Krishna Gokhale** (Gandhi's political guru; founded the **Servants of India Society, 1905**), **Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjee**, and **R.C. Dutt**.

**Methods:** the Moderates believed in **constitutional agitation** — petitions, resolutions, and appeals ("the 3 P's: prayer, petition, protest") — and had **faith in British justice**. Extremists later derided this as "**political mendicancy**."

**Achievements:** they exposed the **economic drain**, built **national consciousness**, and won the **Indian Councils Act, 1892** (expanded legislative councils). **Dadabhai Naoroji** became the **first Indian in the British House of Commons (1892)** and was INC president three times.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Hume founds INC 1885 (Bombay, Bonnerjee president); Moderates 1885-1905 (Naoroji drain theory, Gokhale)**". Method = **constitutional (3 P's)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — INC founder = **A.O. Hume**; first president = **W.C. Bonnerjee** (not Naoroji). The **"drain of wealth"** theory is **Dadabhai Naoroji's**. The Moderate era ends around **1905** (Partition of Bengal → Extremist rise).

### 💡 Example
**Naoroji's drain theory** — that Britain was systematically draining India's wealth — gave the freedom movement its most powerful **economic critique**, later echoed by R.C. Dutt and used by the Extremists.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- INC founded **28 Dec 1885**, Bombay; **A.O. Hume**; 1st president **W.C. Bonnerjee**.
- **Moderates (1885-1905)**: Naoroji, Gokhale, Mehta, S.N. Banerjee.
- **Drain theory** = Dadabhai Naoroji; **Servants of India Society (1905)** = Gokhale.
- Method: **constitutional (3 P's)**; achievement: **Indian Councils Act 1892**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who founded the INC, and who was its first president?*
→ Founded by **A.O. Hume**; first president **W.C. Bonnerjee** (1885).

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Nalgonda-Suryapet

![Telangana Regions Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Nalgonda** region (erstwhile district now split into **Nalgonda, Suryapet, and Yadadri Bhuvanagiri**) lies in the **south**, drained by the **Krishna** and **Musi**.

**Water — the Krishna:** the **Nagarjuna Sagar dam** (Nalgonda-Guntur border) is one of the **world's largest masonry dams**, with vast left/right canals; nearby **Nagarjunakonda** (a submerged **Buddhist** site) has a museum on an island in the reservoir.

**Environmental issue — fluoride:** Nalgonda is historically infamous for **high fluoride in groundwater** causing **fluorosis** — a problem addressed by **Mission Bhagiratha** (safe piped water). **Uranium** deposits in the **Nallamala (Peddagattu)** area are a mining controversy.

**Heritage & tourism:**

| Site | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Yadagirigutta (Yadadri)** | **Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy temple** (redeveloped) |
| **Bhongir (Bhuvanagiri) Fort** | Monolithic **rock fort** |
| **Kolanupaka (Kulpakji)** | Ancient **Jain temple** |
| **Phanigiri** | **Buddhist** heritage site (Suryapet) |
| **Pillalamarri (Suryapet)** | **Kakatiya-era temples** |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Nagarjuna Sagar** (on the Krishna) is a giant **masonry dam**; **Nalgonda** is known for the **fluoride/fluorosis** groundwater problem; **Yadadri (Yadagirigutta)** hosts the redeveloped **Lakshmi Narasimha** temple. *(Verify latest project/figures.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Nagarjuna Sagar + fluoride + Yadadri**": the great Krishna dam, the groundwater fluoride issue, and the Narasimha temple. **Bhongir = rock fort**; **Kolanupaka = Jain temple**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Nagarjunakonda** (Buddhist, Ikshvaku capital) is now **submerged** under Nagarjuna Sagar (artefacts moved to an island museum). Nalgonda's signature environmental issue is **fluoride** (not salinity). **Bhongir/Bhuvanagiri** is a monolithic **rock** fort.

### 💡 Example
The **fluoride-to-Mission-Bhagiratha** story — piped Krishna/Godavari water replacing fluoride-laden groundwater — is a strong "environmental problem → policy response" answer. *(Verify latest coverage.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Nagarjuna Sagar** (Krishna) — masonry dam; **Nagarjunakonda** submerged (Buddhist).
- **Nalgonda** = **fluoride/fluorosis** issue → Mission Bhagiratha.
- **Yadadri** = Lakshmi Narasimha temple; **Bhongir** = rock fort.
- **Kolanupaka** = Jain; **Phanigiri** = Buddhist (Suryapet).
