## 🏛️ Polity: Emergency Provisions (Art 352, 356, 360)

**Part XVIII (`Art 352–360`)** gives the Centre extraordinary powers to meet abnormal situations. Inspired partly by the Weimar Constitution and the Government of India Act 1935, these provisions let the federal system convert to a **unitary** one when needed — Dr Ambedkar called them a "safety valve."

**National Emergency (`Art 352`).** Grounds: **war, external aggression, or armed rebellion**. The phrase "internal disturbance" was replaced by **"armed rebellion"** by the **44th Amendment (1978)** after the 1975 experience. The President proclaims it only on the **written recommendation of the Cabinet** (44th Amd safeguard). Parliamentary approval is needed within **one month**, by a **special majority**; once approved it runs **six months** and can be extended **indefinitely, six months at a time**. Effects: the Centre may legislate on **State List** subjects; the **Lok Sabha's term** can be extended one year at a time; **`Art 358`** automatically suspends **`Art 19`** (only in an **external** emergency, i.e. war/aggression, not armed rebellion); **`Art 359`** lets the President suspend enforcement of other Fundamental Rights — **but `Art 20` and `Art 21` can never be suspended** (44th Amd). Declared **three times**: **1962** (China), **1971** (Pakistan), and **1975** (the controversial "internal disturbance" emergency).

**President's Rule / State Emergency (`Art 356`).** Imposed on **failure of constitutional machinery** in a State (also `Art 365` — failure to comply with central directions). Parliamentary approval within **two months**; runs **six months**, extendable up to a **maximum of three years** (beyond one year only if a National Emergency is in force **and** the EC certifies elections cannot be held). The **S.R. Bommai case (1994)** made it subject to **judicial review**, held that the majority must be tested on the **floor of the House**, and that secularism is part of the basic structure. The most-misused provision historically.

**Financial Emergency (`Art 360`).** Grounds: a threat to the **financial stability or credit** of India. Approval within **two months**; remains in force indefinitely until revoked. The Centre can reduce salaries (including of judges) and reserve state money bills. It has **never been proclaimed**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — `Art 352` National (war/external aggression/**armed rebellion**; approval **1 month**, special majority; `Art 358` suspends `Art 19` in external emergency; `Art 20` & `21` never suspendable). `Art 356` President's Rule (approval **2 months**, max **3 years**; *S.R. Bommai 1994* → judicial review + floor test). `Art 360` Financial (**never imposed**).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**352 = Nation, 356 = State (President's Rule), 360 = Finance.** 44th Amd: armed rebellion, Cabinet in writing, Art 20 & 21 sacred."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — National Emergency approval is **1 month** (not 2); President's Rule and Financial Emergency are **2 months**. `Art 358` (auto-suspend Art 19) applies **only to external** emergency; `Art 359` never touches **Art 20 & 21**. Financial Emergency (`Art 360`) has **never** been used.

💡 **Example:** In 1975 the emergency was proclaimed on grounds of "internal disturbance"; the 44th Amendment (1978) changed this to "armed rebellion" so such an emergency cannot again rest on vague internal unrest.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- `Art 358` vs `Art 359`: 358 auto-suspends Art 19 (external only); 359 suspends enforcement of others — **not Art 20, 21**.
- `Art 356`: 2-month approval; max 3 years; *S.R. Bommai (1994)*.
- `Art 360`: financial stability/credit; never imposed.
- Three national emergencies: 1962, 1971, 1975.

## 🏗️ Schemes: Rythu Bharosa, Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa & the ₹2 Lakh Loan Waiver

Agriculture support is the backbone of Telangana's welfare architecture. Under the current government three interlocking schemes target **landholding farmers, landless labourers, and the debt burden**.

**Rythu Bharosa (farmer investment support).** A per-acre, per-year investment-support transfer to farmers, succeeding the earlier **Rythu Bandhu**. It provides crop-investment assistance directly into farmers' accounts ahead of the cropping seasons (kharif/rabi). The headline promised rate was **₹12,000 per acre per year** (the exact rolled-out amount, eligible land categories such as whether non-cultivable/real-estate land is excluded, and the per-season split — *verify latest official figure*). The policy aim is to reduce dependence on informal credit and ensure timely inputs (seed, fertiliser).

**Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa (for landless agricultural labourers).** Because Rythu Bharosa reaches only **landowners**, this scheme targets **landless agricultural labour families** — a group long excluded from investment-support transfers. It promises an **annual payment** to each eligible landless labour household (the amount announced was around **₹12,000/year**; *verify latest official figure and eligibility/coverage*). This recognises that the rural poorest often own no land yet depend wholly on farm work.

**The ₹2 lakh crop-loan waiver.** A one-time waiver of **crop loans up to ₹2 lakh per farmer family**, implemented in **2024** to relieve farm indebtedness. Loans were waived on a graded/phased basis (by loan-amount slabs) and credited to eligible Rythu accounts so farmers could re-access institutional credit. (Total number of beneficiary families and amount disbursed — *verify latest official figure*.)

Together these form part of the government's **Six Guarantees** agenda: Rythu Bharosa featured under the agriculture guarantee, with the loan waiver as a distinct one-time relief. The schemes are DBT-based (direct benefit transfer) and linked to land records (Dharani/revised land database) and ration/eligibility data.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Rythu Bharosa** = per-acre investment support to **landowning farmers** (promised ₹12,000/acre/yr — *verify*). **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa** = annual support to **landless agricultural-labour families** (promised ~₹12,000/yr — *verify*). **Loan waiver** = crop loans up to **₹2 lakh/family**, implemented **2024**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Bharosa = land owner; Atmiya Bharosa = landless labourer; ₹2 lakh = loan waiver.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Rythu Bharosa supports **landholders**; the **landless** are covered by **Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa**, not Rythu Bharosa. The **₹2 lakh** figure is the **loan-waiver** cap, *not* the annual investment support. All specific rupee figures and coverage numbers are recent and must be **verified against the latest official data** before the exam.

💡 **Example:** A family that owns 2 acres receives Rythu Bharosa on its land; a neighbouring family that works others' fields but owns no land receives Indiramma Atmiya Bharosa instead — the two schemes are complementary, not overlapping.

## 🏺 History: The Satavahanas

The **Satavahanas** were the **first major indigenous dynasty** of the Deccan and the earliest great power associated with Telangana, bridging the gap after the Mauryas (roughly **2nd century BCE to 2nd–3rd century CE** — *verify precise dates*). They are called the **Andhras** in the Puranas.

**Origins & capitals.** The founder was **Simuka**. **Kotilingala** (in present Jagtial/Karimnagar region, Telangana) was an early power centre and mint — coins of early rulers were found here, underlining Telangana's role in the dynasty's rise. Later the political centre shifted west to **Pratishthana (Paithan)** on the Godavari, with **Amaravati/Dharanikota** important in the east.

**Great rulers.** **Satakarni I** expanded power and performed Vedic sacrifices (Ashvamedha). The greatest ruler was **Gautamiputra Satakarni**, who defeated the **Shaka** ruler Nahapana and revived Satavahana power; his achievements are recorded in the **Nasik Prashasti** composed by his mother **Gautami Balasri**. His successor **Vasishthiputra Pulumavi** consolidated the east. A distinctive feature is **matronymic names** (Gautami-putra, Vasishthi-putra) — rulers named after their mothers, suggesting the status of women, though succession remained **patrilineal**.

**Administration & society.** The realm was divided into **aharas** (districts) under **amatyas**; villages headed by a **gramika**. The Satavahanas pioneered the **land-grant** system — granting tax-free land (to Brahmins and Buddhist monks) with fiscal/administrative immunities, a precursor to later feudal practice.

**Religion & culture.** The Satavahanas were **Brahmanical** (performed Vedic yajnas, patronised the varna order) **yet generously patronised Buddhism** — the **Amaravati stupa** and cave monasteries (Nasik, Karle) flourished under them. Language of administration was **Prakrit**, in **Brahmi** script. King **Hala** authored the **Gatha Saptasati (Gaha Sattasai)** — a celebrated anthology of ~700 Prakrit love/folk verses.

**Economy.** Flourishing **Indo-Roman trade** (ports on the west coast; Roman gold flowed in); they issued **lead, potin, copper and silver coins** often depicting ships. Guilds (**shrenis**) and craft specialisation thrived.

**Decline.** After Pulumavi and Yajna Sri Satakarni the empire fragmented; successors in Telangana included the **Ikshvakus** (at Nagarjunakonda) and later the **Vishnukundins**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Founder **Simuka**; early mint **Kotilingala** (Telangana); greatest ruler **Gautamiputra Satakarni** (defeated Shaka Nahapana; **Nasik Prashasti** by **Gautami Balasri**); **matronymic** names; **Prakrit**/Brahmi; King **Hala** → **Gatha Saptasati**; **Amaravati stupa**; Indo-Roman trade.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Simuka starts, Kotilingala mints, Gautamiputra beats Shakas (Nasik by Gautami Balasri), Hala writes Gatha Saptasati.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Matronymic names (named after **mother**) ≠ matrilineal succession (still patrilineal). The **Nasik Prashasti** was composed by Gautamiputra's **mother Gautami Balasri**, not himself. **Hala** (not Gautamiputra) wrote the Gatha Saptasati. Satavahanas were **Brahmanical** but **patronised Buddhism** — both, not either.

💡 **Example:** The lead coins and inscriptions at **Kotilingala** (Karimnagar/Jagtial) are often cited to argue the Satavahanas originated in or near **Telangana**, not only coastal Andhra.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Gautamiputra Satakarni — Nasik Prashasti (Gautami Balasri); defeated Nahapana (Shaka).
- Hala — Gatha Saptasati (Prakrit, ~700 verses).
- Matronymic naming; land grants begin; Amaravati stupa; Indo-Roman trade; lead/potin coins.
- Succeeded in Telangana by Ikshvakus → Vishnukundins.

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Cabinet Mission to Independence & Partition

The final phase (1946–47) saw constitutional negotiations collapse into **Partition**.

**Cabinet Mission (1946).** Attlee's Labour government sent three ministers — **Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary of State), **Stafford Cripps**, and **A.V. Alexander** — to settle the transfer of power. The Mission **rejected the demand for a full sovereign Pakistan** and instead proposed (May 1946) a **three-tier federation**: a weak central union (handling defence, foreign affairs, communications), **provinces**, and an intermediate layer of **three Groups** of provinces (Group A = Hindu-majority; Groups B & C = Muslim-majority NW and NE). It also provided for a **Constituent Assembly** to be indirectly elected by provincial assemblies, and an **interim government**. Both the Congress and the League first accepted but **interpreted the "grouping" clause differently**, and the plan unravelled.

**Interim Government & Direct Action.** An **Interim Government** was formed in **September 1946** under **Jawaharlal Nehru**; the Muslim League initially stayed out, then joined. Earlier, the League had declared **16 August 1946 as "Direct Action Day,"** triggering the horrific **Great Calcutta Killings** and communal violence that spread (Noakhali, Bihar), hardening positions toward partition.

**Mountbatten & the June 3 Plan (1947).** **Lord Mountbatten**, the last Viceroy, concluded that partition was unavoidable. The **3 June 1947 (Mountbatten) Plan** proposed partitioning British India into **India and Pakistan**, with Bengal and Punjab to be divided, a referendum in NWFP and Sylhet, and **dominion status** for both.

**Indian Independence Act 1947.** The British Parliament passed the **Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947)**, which created the two **dominions** of **India and Pakistan** from **15 August 1947**, ended British paramountcy over princely states (leaving them to accede), and empowered the Constituent Assemblies as legislatures. The **Radcliffe Line** (Sir Cyril Radcliffe) demarcated the boundaries — announced after independence, provoking massive migration and violence. **Pakistan became independent on 14 August, India on 15 August 1947.**

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Cabinet Mission 1946** (Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander) rejected Pakistan, proposed a **3-tier grouping** federation + Constituent Assembly. **Direct Action Day = 16 Aug 1946**. Interim Govt **Sept 1946** (Nehru). **Mountbatten/June 3 Plan 1947** → **Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947)** → **dominions from 15 Aug 1947**; boundaries by the **Radcliffe Line**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Cabinet Mission (3 members, 3 tiers) → Direct Action (16 Aug 1946) → Interim Govt (Nehru) → June 3 Plan → Independence Act (18 July) → 15 Aug 1947 (Radcliffe Line).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Cabinet Mission **rejected** a sovereign Pakistan (it proposed grouping, not partition). **Direct Action Day = 16 Aug 1946** (not 1947). Independence was granted as **dominion status**, not immediate full republic. Pakistan's independence day is **14 Aug**, India's **15 Aug 1947**.

💡 **Example:** The deadlock over the Cabinet Mission's "grouping" clause — Congress said provinces could opt out, the League said grouping was compulsory — is a classic exam illustration of why the plan failed and partition followed.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Three-tier plan; Constituent Assembly indirectly elected; grouping dispute.
- Direct Action Day 16 Aug 1946 → Great Calcutta Killings.
- Interim Government Sept 1946 (Nehru as de facto PM).
- June 3 Plan (Mountbatten); Indian Independence Act 18 July 1947; Radcliffe Line; dominion status; 15 Aug 1947.

## 🗺️ Geography: Telangana at a Glance — Location, Extent & Physiography

![Map of Telangana's three regions and districts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

**Formation.** Telangana became the **29th state** of India on **2 June 2014**, carved out of the erstwhile Andhra Pradesh, with **Hyderabad** as its capital (shared as joint capital with AP for a transition period, now up). It is the youngest state of peninsular India.

**Location & extent.** Telangana lies on the **Deccan Plateau** in **south-central India**, roughly between latitudes **~15.8°N and ~19.9°N** and longitudes **~77°E and ~81.3°E** (*verify precise extent*). It is a completely **landlocked** state. Its approximate area is **~112,077 sq km** (*verify latest official figure*), making it a medium-sized state. Administrative divisions: **33 districts** (*verify latest count, as districts have been reorganised*).

**Boundaries.** Telangana is bordered by **Maharashtra** to the north and north-west, **Chhattisgarh** to the north-east, **Andhra Pradesh** to the east and south, and **Karnataka** to the west. The **Godavari** and **Krishna** are the two great rivers; the land broadly slopes from **north-west to south-east** (toward the Bay of Bengal drainage via these rivers).

**Physiography.** The state is part of the **Deccan Plateau** (Deccan Traps / Archaean granitic-gneiss terrain), with **general elevation ~300–600 m** above sea level. Key physiographic features:
- **Undulating plateau** of ancient crystalline rocks (granites, gneisses) — hence granite quarrying and rocky outcrops around Hyderabad.
- **Residual hills** and remnants of the **Eastern Ghats** in the east/south-east (Khammam, Bhadradri belts).
- **River valleys** of the Godavari (north) and Krishna (south) and their tributaries dissect the plateau.
- Highest points lie in the forested hill tracts of the north and south-east (*verify specific peak/elevation*).

**Regions.** Culturally/administratively the area is often described in terms of regions around **Hyderabad-Deccan core, the northern Telangana (Godavari belt), and southern Telangana (Krishna/Palamuru belt)** — see the map above for the district spread.
