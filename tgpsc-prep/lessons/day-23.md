## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity (✓✓ Revision)
### 🎯 Revision: Fundamental Rights (Part III)

Continuing the **Polity revision**, today reconsolidates the **Fundamental Rights** (Art 12-35) — the enforceable core of citizens' liberties.

### 📖 Concept

**Six categories of Fundamental Rights (after Right to Property was removed):**

| Right | Articles | Key content |
|---|---|---|
| **Right to Equality** | `14-18` | Equality before law (14); no discrimination (15); equal opportunity (16); abolition of untouchability (17), titles (18) |
| **Right to Freedom** | `19-22` | Six freedoms (19); protection re: offences (20); life & liberty (21); education 21A; preventive detention (22) |
| **Right against Exploitation** | `23-24` | No trafficking/forced labour (23); no child labour < 14 in hazardous work (24) |
| **Right to Freedom of Religion** | `25-28` | Freedom of conscience & religion |
| **Cultural & Educational Rights** | `29-30` | Minorities' rights to conserve culture & run institutions |
| **Right to Constitutional Remedies** | `32` | Writs (heart & soul — Ambedkar) |

**Key points:**
- **Art 12** defines "State"; **Art 13** = laws inconsistent with FRs are **void** (judicial review basis).
- **Art 14** applies to **all persons**; **Art 15, 16, 19** to **citizens only**.
- **Five writs (Art 32/226):** Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto.
- **Art 20 & 21 cannot be suspended** even during emergency (44th Amdt).
- **Right to Property** removed as FR by the **44th Amendment (1978)** → now a **legal right (Art 300A)**.
- **Art 21A** (Right to Education) added by **86th Amendment**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Six FRs: **Equality (14-18), Freedom (19-22), Against Exploitation (23-24), Religion (25-28), Cultural/Educational (29-30), Constitutional Remedies (32)**. **Art 32 = heart & soul** (writs); **Art 20 & 21 never suspendable**; **Right to Property is now only a legal right (300A)**.

💡 **Example:** A **Habeas Corpus** writ ("produce the body") protects personal liberty against illegal detention — the most powerful of the five writs, available under **Art 32 (SC)** and **Art 226 (HC)**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Art 14 = all persons; Art 15/16/19 = citizens only**.
- **Five writs**; Art 32 = heart & soul.
- **Art 20 & 21 non-suspendable**; **Property = legal right (300A, 44th Amdt)**.
- **Art 17 (untouchability), 18 (titles), 23-24 (exploitation), 21A (education)**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Equality (14-18), Freedom (19-22), anti-Exploitation (23-24), Religion (25-28), Cultural (29-30), Remedies (32).** 20 & 21 untouchable; property demoted (300A)."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Right to Property is NOT a Fundamental Right** (removed by 44th Amdt; now Art 300A legal right). **Art 15, 16, 19 apply to citizens only** (Art 14 to all persons). **Art 20 & 21 cannot be suspended** even in emergency.

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 Telangana Literature & Language

Telangana's **literary heritage** spans classical Telugu, movement poetry, and a distinct **dialect** — a rich source of culture questions.

### 📖 Concept

**Classical & medieval Telugu (Telangana contributions):**
- **Palkuriki Somanatha** (13th c.) — pioneer of **Telugu dwipada** & Veerashaiva literature; wrote **Basava Purana, Panditaradhya Charitra**.
- **Bammera Potana** (15th c.) — from **Bammera (Warangal)**; wrote the **Telugu Bhagavatam (Andhra Maha Bhagavatamu)** — a devotional classic.
- **Gona Budda Reddy** — **Ranganatha Ramayanam** (dwipada Ramayana).
- **Marana, Mallinatha** and others in the Kakatiya-era literary flowering.

**Modern & movement poets (Telangana identity/movement):**
- **Dasarathi Krishnamacharya** — revolutionary poet against Nizam's rule ("**Na Telangana koti ratanala veena**"); Sahitya Akademi awardee.
- **Kaloji Narayana Rao** — "**Praja Kavi**" (people's poet); his birthday (**9 Sep**) is **Telangana Language Day (Bhasha Dinotsavam)**; fought for Telangana identity.
- **C. Narayana Reddy (Cinare)** — **Jnanpith** awardee (*Viswambhara*); prolific poet-lyricist.
- **Gaddar** — revolutionary balladeer of the Telangana movement (Jana Natya Mandali).
- **Suddala Hanmanthu** — Telangana Armed Struggle poet ("**Palletturi Pillagada**").
- **Vattikota Alwar Swamy** — first Telangana novelist (**Prajala Manishi**); library movement.

**Language & knowledge:**
- **Telangana dialect (Telangana Telugu)** — distinct vocabulary, idiom; asserted as a marker of identity (vs the "standard/coastal" dialect).
- **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** — edited **"Golconda Kavula Sanchika"** (answering the taunt that Telangana had no poets) and compiled **"Andhrula Sanghika Charitra"** (Kendra Sahitya Akademi award).
- **Vignana Sarvaswam** — a Telugu **encyclopaedia** effort (knowledge dissemination; linked to the Vignana Chandrika Mandali/Komarraju Lakshmana Rao).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Classical: **Palkuriki Somanatha (dwipada), Bammera Potana (Telugu Bhagavatam)**. Movement/modern: **Dasarathi, Kaloji ("Praja Kavi", Language Day 9 Sep), C. Narayana Reddy (Jnanpith), Gaddar, Suddala, Vattikota Alwar Swamy**. **Telangana dialect** = identity marker; **Vignana Sarvaswam** = encyclopaedia.

💡 **Example:** **Kaloji Narayana Rao's** birthday (**9 September**) is observed as **Telangana Language Day** — a direct one-mark culture question linking the "people's poet" to Telugu identity.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Bammera Potana → Telugu Bhagavatam (from Warangal)**.
- **Palkuriki Somanatha → dwipada / Basava Purana**.
- **Kaloji = Praja Kavi; 9 Sep = Language Day**; **C. Narayana Reddy = Jnanpith**.
- **Dasarathi, Gaddar, Suddala = movement poets**; **Vattikota Alwar Swamy = first novelist**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Potana (Bhagavatam), Somanatha (dwipada); movement: Dasarathi, Kaloji (Praja Kavi, 9 Sep), Cinare (Jnanpith), Gaddar, Suddala; Vattikota = first novelist.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Bammera Potana** (Telugu Bhagavatam) was from **Bammera, Warangal** (Telangana). **C. Narayana Reddy (Cinare)** won the **Jnanpith**; **Kaloji** is the **Praja Kavi** whose birthday is **Telangana Language Day (9 Sep)**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement (✓✓ Revision)
### 🎯 Revision: Morley-Minto & Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms; Lucknow Pact; Home Rule

Continuing the **INM revision**, today reconsolidates the **1909-1919 constitutional reforms and wartime nationalism** (originally Day 6).

### 📖 Concept

**Morley-Minto Reforms / Indian Councils Act 1909.**
- Expanded legislative councils; introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** ("communal representation" — seeds of partition).
- Indians got a limited elected element but **no real power** (advisory).

**Lucknow Pact (1916).**
- **Congress-Muslim League unity pact** (Tilak & Jinnah instrumental); Congress **accepted separate electorates** for the sake of joint demand for self-government.
- Also marked **Congress reunion** (Moderates + Extremists, after the 1907 Surat split).

**Home Rule Movement (1916).**
- Two Home Rule Leagues: **Tilak's** (April 1916, Maharashtra) and **Annie Besant's** (Sept 1916, Madras).
- Demanded **self-government (Home Rule)** within the Empire; energised nationalism during WWI; Besant became **Congress President (1917)**.

**Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms / Government of India Act 1919.**
- **Dyarchy in the provinces** — subjects split into **Reserved** (Governor + executive council) and **Transferred** (Indian ministers responsible to legislature).
- **Bicameral central legislature**; expanded (but communal) franchise; **Secretary of State**; a **Public Service Commission** (1926).
- Montagu's **August 1917 Declaration** promised "**responsible government**" gradually.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Morley-Minto (1909) = separate electorates**; **Lucknow Pact (1916) = Congress-League unity + Congress reunion**; **Home Rule (1916) = Tilak & Annie Besant**; **Montagu-Chelmsford / GoI Act 1919 = dyarchy in provinces** (Reserved/Transferred subjects).

💡 **Example:** The **1919 Act's dyarchy** split provincial subjects into **Transferred** (to Indian ministers) and **Reserved** (with the Governor) — a halfway house to responsible government, later abolished by the 1935 Act.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **1909 Morley-Minto = separate electorates**.
- **Lucknow Pact 1916 = Congress-League pact + reunion**.
- **Home Rule 1916 = Tilak + Annie Besant**.
- **1919 Montford = provincial dyarchy** (Reserved/Transferred).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1909 Morley-Minto (separate electorates) → 1916 Lucknow Pact (unity) + Home Rule (Tilak/Besant) → 1919 Montford (provincial dyarchy).**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Dyarchy was in the PROVINCES** under the **1919 Act** (Montagu-Chelmsford), later shifted to the Centre by the 1935 Act. **Separate electorates** came with **Morley-Minto (1909)**. The **Lucknow Pact (1916)** is Congress-League unity.

### 📖 Concept

**On the Godavari system:**

|---|---|---|

**On the Krishna system:**

| Project | River | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Nagarjuna Sagar** | Krishna | One of the largest masonry dams; canals to Nalgonda/Khammam |
| **Srisailam** | Krishna | Hydro + source for lift schemes |

**Types:** **Major** (large dams/command), **Medium**, **Minor** (tanks), and **Lift Irrigation Schemes (LIS)** — pumping water uphill (essential because rivers flow below the plateau). Telangana pioneered **mega lift schemes** (Kaleshwaram, Palamuru-Rangareddy).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Godavari: Kaleshwaram (largest lift, Medigadda), SRSP/Pochampad, Nizam Sagar (Manjira), Devadula.** **Krishna: Nagarjuna Sagar, Srisailam, Jurala (first entering TG), Palamuru-Rangareddy (lift from Srisailam), Kalvakurthy.** Lift schemes dominate (rivers flow below the plateau).

💡 **Example:** **Kaleshwaram (Godavari)** and **Palamuru-Rangareddy (Krishna)** are the two mega **lift** schemes — one per river system — remedying the geographic problem that Telangana's rivers flow **below** its cultivable plateau.
