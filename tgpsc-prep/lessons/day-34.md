## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Parliament — Composition, Sessions, Bills, Budget & Committees

### 📖 Deep Dive

The Union **Parliament** = **President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha** (`Art 79`).

| House | Composition |
|---|---|
| **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States) | Max **250** (238 elected by state/UT assemblies + **12 nominated**); **permanent** house, **1/3 retire every 2 years**, 6-year terms; chaired by the **Vice-President** |
| **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) | Max **552** (530 states + 20 UTs); **5-year** term; presided by the **Speaker** |

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **104th Amendment (2019)** abolished the **nomination of two Anglo-Indians** to the Lok Sabha. The Rajya Sabha's **12 nominated** members (for art, science, literature, social service) remain.

**Sessions:** Budget (Feb-May), Monsoon (Jul-Sep), Winter (Nov-Dec). The gap between two sessions must **not exceed 6 months**. **Quorum = 1/10** of the House.

**Bills:**

| Bill type | Key feature |
|---|---|
| **Ordinary** | Both Houses equal; deadlock resolved by **joint sitting (Art 108)**, presided by the **Speaker** |
| **Money Bill (Art 110)** | Introduced only in **Lok Sabha**; **Rajya Sabha can only recommend** (within 14 days); **Speaker certifies** |
| **Constitutional Amendment (Art 368)** | Special majority; **no joint sitting** |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — On a **Money Bill**, the **Lok Sabha is supreme** — the Rajya Sabha has only 14 days to make (non-binding) recommendations, and the **Speaker's certification** that a bill is a Money Bill is **final**.

**Budget (Art 112 — Annual Financial Statement):** stages are presentation → general discussion → **voting on Demands for Grants (Lok Sabha only)** → **Appropriation Bill** → **Finance Bill**. Cut motions (policy/economy/token) and the **Vote on Account** are part of the process.

**Committees:** **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** (22 members, chaired by an **Opposition** member), **Estimates Committee** (30, **Lok Sabha only**), **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)**, and **24 Departmentally-Related Standing Committees**.

### 💡 Example
Only the **Lok Sabha** votes on Demands for Grants and can reject/reduce them via **cut motions** — a direct expression of its control over the "power of the purse."

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Rajya Sabha: **250** max, **12 nominated**, VP chairs; Lok Sabha: **552** max, Speaker.
- **104th Amendment** ended Anglo-Indian nomination.
- **Money Bill (Art 110)** — LS supreme; **joint sitting (Art 108)** for ordinary bills.
- **PAC** chaired by Opposition; **Estimates Committee** LS-only.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*On a Money Bill, how long can the Rajya Sabha retain it, and are its recommendations binding?*
→ Up to **14 days**, and its recommendations are **not binding**.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: The Asaf Jahis (1724-1911) — Salar Jung & Subsidiary Alliance

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Asaf Jahi dynasty** (the **Nizams of Hyderabad**) was founded in **`1724`** by **Mir Qamaruddin Khan (Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I)**, a Mughal viceroy of the Deccan who became effectively independent as Mughal power declined. The capital shifted from Aurangabad to **Hyderabad**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II)** was the **first Indian ruler to sign the Subsidiary Alliance** with **Lord Wellesley** in **`1798`**. Under its terms the Nizam maintained British troops and, in `1800`, **ceded territories** (the "Ceded Districts"/Rayalaseema) to the British.

**Salar Jung I (Mir Turab Ali Khan)** served as **Prime Minister (Diwan)** from **`1853` to `1883`** under successive Nizams. His **modernising reforms** reorganised the administration into **districts and talukas**, overhauled revenue and judiciary, and introduced **railways, postal, and education** systems — laying the foundations of the modern Hyderabad State.

The line of Nizams ran **Asaf Jah I → ... → Mahbub Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VI, r. 1869-1911)**, whose reign closes this period; his successor **Mir Osman Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VII)** ruled from `1911`.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**1724 founded, 1798 Subsidiary Alliance (first to sign), Salar Jung reforms 1853-83**". The Nizams styled themselves **"Faithful Ally"** of the British.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Nizam (Asaf Jah II)** was the **first** to accept Wellesley's **Subsidiary Alliance (1798)**. **Salar Jung I** was the reformist **Diwan**, not a Nizam. Berar was later assigned to the British on lease.

### 💡 Example
**Salar Jung I's** administrative districts-and-talukas framework and revenue reforms are the direct ancestors of Telangana's later district administration — a static fact with modern resonance.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Dynasty founded **1724** by **Asaf Jah I (Nizam-ul-Mulk)**.
- **Asaf Jah II** — first to sign **Subsidiary Alliance (1798)** with Wellesley.
- **Salar Jung I** — Diwan **1853-83**, modernising reforms.
- **Mahbub Ali Khan (VI)** rules to **1911**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who was the first Indian ruler to sign the Subsidiary Alliance, and in which year?*
→ The **Nizam (Asaf Jah II)**, in **`1798`**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Integration of Princely States (incl. Hyderabad) & the Republic

### 📖 Deep Dive

At independence there were about **`565` princely states**. **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (the "Iron Man"), with **V.P. Menon** heading the States Department, persuaded almost all to sign the **Instrument of Accession** by **15 August 1947** — a feat of peaceful integration.

**Three holdouts:**

| State | Resolution |
|---|---|
| **Junagadh** | Nawab acceded to Pakistan; a **plebiscite (1948)** brought it to India |
| **Kashmir** | Maharaja **Hari Singh** acceded to India (**Oct 1947**) after a Pakistani tribal invasion |
| **Hyderabad** | The **Nizam** sought independence; integrated by **military action** |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Hyderabad** was integrated through **Operation Polo (the "Police Action")** from **`13–17 September 1948`**, led by **Major General J.N. Chaudhuri**. The Nizam **Mir Osman Ali Khan** surrendered on **`17 September 1948`**, ending the **Razakar** militia's terror (under Qasim Razvi).

**Making of the Republic:** the **Constitution was adopted on `26 November 1949`** and **came into force on `26 January 1950`**, when India became a **sovereign democratic republic** with **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** as its first President.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Patel + Menon integrate 565 states; three holdouts (Junagadh, Kashmir, Hyderabad); Operation Polo Sept 1948; Republic 26 Jan 1950**".

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Hyderabad's** integration was by **Operation Polo (Sept 1948)**, not accession in 1947. **Telangana celebrates 17 September** as a significant date (Hyderabad Liberation/Integration Day). Junagadh went to a **plebiscite**; Kashmir acceded after invasion.

### 💡 Example
The contrast between **peaceful accession** (most states) and the **Police Action** on Hyderabad shows the range of methods Patel used — persuasion first, force only as a last resort.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- ~**565** states; **Patel + V.P. Menon**; **Instrument of Accession**.
- Holdouts: **Junagadh (plebiscite), Kashmir (Oct 1947), Hyderabad**.
- **Operation Polo** — **13-17 Sep 1948**, J.N. Chaudhuri; Nizam surrendered **17 Sep 1948**.
- Republic: **26 Jan 1950**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*How and when was Hyderabad integrated into India?*
→ By **Operation Polo (Police Action)**, **13-17 September 1948**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Nizamabad-Kamareddy

![Telangana Regions Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Nizamabad** region (erstwhile district now split into **Nizamabad** and **Kamareddy**) lies in the **north-western Godavari basin**, drained by the **Godavari** and its tributary the **Manjira**.

**Water & irrigation:**

| Project | Note |
|---|---|
| **Nizam Sagar** | Dam on the **Manjira** (built under Nizam VII, `1923-31`) |
| **Sri Ram Sagar Project (Pochampad)** | Major Godavari project near Nizamabad (shared with Nirmal) |
| **Pocharam / Ali Sagar / Ashok Sagar** | Local reservoirs |

**Agriculture & industry:** the region is a leading producer of **turmeric, maize, paddy, and sugarcane**. **Nizamabad is a major turmeric trading centre** — the **National Turmeric Board** is headquartered here *(announced 2023 — verify)*. **Armoor** is famous for turmeric/red-jowar; **Bodhan** hosted the (now defunct) **Nizam Sugar Factory** — once among Asia's largest.

**Heritage & tourism:** **Sarangapur Hanuman Temple**, the **Domakonda and Kaulas forts** (Kamareddy), and **Pocharam Wildlife Sanctuary** (on the Kamareddy-Medak border). **Bodhan** is linked to the ancient **Asmaka** capital (Potali/Podana).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Nizamabad** is the **"turmeric city"** of Telangana and the seat of the **National Turmeric Board** (announced 2023). **Nizam Sagar** (on the **Manjira**) and the **Sri Ram Sagar Project** (on the Godavari) are its irrigation lifelines. *(Verify latest boundaries and figures.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Nizamabad = turmeric + Nizam Sagar (Manjira) + SRSP (Godavari)**". **Bodhan = Nizam Sugar + ancient Asmaka**; **Domakonda/Kaulas forts** in Kamareddy.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Nizam Sagar** is on the **Manjira** (a Godavari tributary), while the **Sri Ram Sagar Project** is on the **Godavari** itself. The **National Turmeric Board** at Nizamabad is a recent development — **verify** its status.

### 💡 Example
Nizamabad farmers' long campaign for a **Turmeric Board** culminating in its announcement links a local agri-economy issue to national policy — a strong current-affairs-meets-geography point. *(Verify latest status.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Nizam Sagar** on the **Manjira**; **SRSP (Pochampad)** on the **Godavari**.
- **Nizamabad** = turmeric hub; **National Turmeric Board** (2023 — verify).
- **Bodhan** = Nizam Sugar Factory + ancient **Asmaka**.
- **Domakonda/Kaulas forts** (Kamareddy); **Pocharam** sanctuary.
