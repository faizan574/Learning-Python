## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Prime Minister & Council of Ministers

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **real executive** power of the Union vests in the **Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister**, who "aid and advise" the President.

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Art 74** | Council of Ministers with PM at the head to **aid and advise** the President; advice is **binding** (42nd Amendment), though the President may return it **once** (44th Amendment) |
| **Art 75(1)** | PM appointed by the President; other ministers appointed **on the PM's advice** |
| **Art 75(2)** | Ministers hold office **during the pleasure** of the President |
| **Art 75(3)** | Council is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** |
| **Art 78** | Duties of the PM to communicate Council decisions to the President |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — By the **91st Amendment (2003)**, the total number of ministers (including the PM) must **not exceed 15% of the Lok Sabha's strength**. A minister who is not a member of either House must **become one within 6 months**.

The **Prime Minister** is the leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha, the **head of government**, the link between the President and the Council, and the **chairperson of NITI Aayog**.

**Ranks of ministers:** **Cabinet Ministers** (the inner decision-making body), **Ministers of State**, and **Deputy Ministers**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Collective to the Lok Sabha, individual to the President**": `Art 75(3)` = collective responsibility (a no-confidence vote sinks the **whole** Council); `Art 75(2)` = ministers serve at the President's pleasure.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **whole Council** resigns if the Lok Sabha passes a no-confidence motion (collective responsibility) — even ministers in the Rajya Sabha. The **15% cap** is on **Lok Sabha** strength (91st Amendment), and a non-member minister has **6 months** to enter Parliament.

### 💡 Example
Even a Prime Minister can be from the **Rajya Sabha** (e.g., historically), but must command the **confidence of the Lok Sabha** — showing collective responsibility rests with the **lower** house.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Art 74** = aid & advise (binding, 42nd; return once, 44th).
- **Art 75(3)** = collective responsibility to **Lok Sabha**.
- **91st Amendment** = ministers ≤ **15%** of LS; 6-month membership rule.
- PM = chair of **NITI Aayog**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*To which House is the Council of Ministers collectively responsible, and under which Article?*
→ The **Lok Sabha**, under **Article 75(3)**.

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: The Qutb Shahis & the Founding of Hyderabad

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Qutb Shahi dynasty** (`1518–1687`) ruled the **Golconda** kingdom — Telangana's own Deccan sultanate after the Bahmani split.

**Founder:** **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk**, a Turkoman governor under the Bahmanis, who declared independence around **`1518`** with **Golconda** as capital.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** (5th ruler, r. `1580–1612`) **founded the city of Hyderabad in `1591`** and built the **Charminar** (`1591`). A gifted **poet** (in Dakhni Urdu, Persian and Telugu), his collected verse is the **Kulliyat**.

**Cultural highlights:**
- **Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah** ("Malkibharama") was a great **patron of Telugu**; the **Hussain Sagar** lake (by Hussain Shah Wali, `1562`) dates to his reign.
- The Qutb Shahis promoted **Telugu** alongside Persian and **Dakhni Urdu**, and were known for **religious tolerance** (Shia Islam at court).
- **Architecture:** **Charminar, Golconda Fort, the Qutb Shahi Tombs**, and the **Mecca Masjid** (begun in this era).

**End:** the last ruler, **Abul Hasan Tana Shah**, was defeated by **Aurangzeb** in **`1687`**, and Golconda was annexed to the **Mughal Empire** (later the Asaf Jahis emerged from Mughal viceroys).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Quli founds Golconda (1518), Muhammad Quli founds Hyderabad + Charminar (1591), Tana Shah falls to Aurangzeb (1687)**". Hussain Sagar = Ibrahim's reign.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Muhammad Quli** (5th ruler) founded **Hyderabad and Charminar (1591)** — do not confuse with the **founder Sultan Quli** (of the dynasty/Golconda, 1518). Golconda fell to **Aurangzeb in 1687**.

### 💡 Example
The **Charminar (1591)** — built to mark the founding of Hyderabad (and, by tradition, the end of a plague) — is the enduring symbol linking Qutb Shahi history to the modern city, a favourite static-current question.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Dynasty **1518–1687**, capital **Golconda**; founder **Sultan Quli**.
- **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** — founded **Hyderabad & Charminar (1591)**, poet (Kulliyat).
- **Hussain Sagar (1562)** — Ibrahim Quli's reign.
- Fell to **Aurangzeb, 1687** (last ruler **Tana Shah**).

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who founded Hyderabad and the Charminar, and in which year?*
→ **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**, in **`1591`**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Cabinet Mission to Independence & Partition

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Cabinet Mission (1946):** three British ministers — **Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, and A.V. Alexander** — came to plan the transfer of power. It **rejected the demand for Pakistan** and proposed a **loose three-tier federation** with a weak centre and a **grouping of provinces (Sections A, B, C)**. A **Constituent Assembly** was to be elected, and an **Interim Government** formed.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Interim Government** took office in **September 1946** under **Jawaharlal Nehru**. The Muslim League first stayed out, then joined, having already called **"Direct Action Day" on `16 August 1946`**, which triggered the **Great Calcutta Killings**.

**Mountbatten Plan (`3 June 1947`):** accepted **Partition** — India and Pakistan as separate dominions, with provinces/assemblies deciding, and a boundary commission under **Cyril Radcliffe** (the **Radcliffe Line**).

**Indian Independence Act, 1947** (British Parliament, July 1947): created the **two dominions of India and Pakistan** from **`15 August 1947`**, ending British paramountcy over the princely states.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Cabinet Mission (1946, no Pakistan) → Direct Action (Aug 1946) → Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947) → Independence Act → 15 Aug 1947**". Boundary = **Radcliffe Line**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Cabinet Mission rejected Pakistan** and proposed grouping; **Partition came with the Mountbatten Plan (June 1947)**. **Pakistan's independence day is 14 August**, India's **15 August 1947**. The princely states were left to **accede** to either dominion.

### 💡 Example
The failure of the Cabinet Mission's grouping formula (disputed interpretation of Sections) and the communal violence after **Direct Action Day** made **Partition** politically unavoidable by mid-1947.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Cabinet Mission 1946** — 3 members; rejected Pakistan; grouping A/B/C; Constituent Assembly.
- **Direct Action Day** — **16 Aug 1946**, Calcutta killings.
- **Mountbatten Plan** — **3 June 1947** (Partition); **Radcliffe Line**.
- **Independence Act 1947** — two dominions, **15 Aug 1947**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Did the Cabinet Mission accept Pakistan, and which plan finally brought Partition?*
→ **No**, it rejected Pakistan; Partition came with the **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Adilabad & the Northern Gond Belt

![Telangana Regions Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Adilabad** is Telangana's **northernmost** region — the great **tribal (Gond) and forest** belt. The old district has been reorganised into **Adilabad, Nirmal, Mancherial, and Komaram Bheem Asifabad** *(verify current boundaries)*.

**Physiography & rivers:** hilly terrain on the northern Deccan, drained by the **Godavari** and its tributaries **Penganga, Pranahita, and Wardha**. It has **dense teak forests** and the **Kawal Tiger Reserve**.

**Tribal heritage:** home to the **Gonds, Kolams, Naikpods and Pardhans**. The Gond icon **Komaram Bheem** (of **Jodeghat**) led a revolt with the slogan **"Jal, Jangal, Jameen"**. The **Nagoba Jatara** (at **Keslapur**, by the Mesram clan of Gonds) and the **Dandari/Gussadi** dance are its living cultural expressions.

**Landmarks & economy:**

| Feature | Note |
|---|---|
| **Basara** | **Gnana Saraswati Temple** (a rare Saraswati temple; on the Godavari) |
| **Kuntala Waterfalls** | **Highest waterfall in Telangana** (on the Kadam river) |
| **Kawal Tiger Reserve** | Major protected forest |
| **Nirmal** | Famous **Nirmal toys & paintings**; Nirmal fort |
| **Mancherial / Singareni** | **Coal** mining belt |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Adilabad is Telangana's **cotton ("white gold")** and **teak-forest** heartland, home to the **Gond** tribes and the martyr **Komaram Bheem** ("Jal Jangal Jameen"). **Basara** hosts one of India's few **Gnana Saraswati** temples. *(Verify latest boundaries and figures.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Gonds, teak, and cotton up north**": Komaram Bheem & Nagoba Jatara (Gond culture), Kawal teak forests, and Adilabad cotton. **Basara = Saraswati**; **Kuntala = highest falls**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Komaram Bheem Asifabad** is now a separate district (named after the Gond leader). **Basara's** temple is **Gnana Saraswati** (learning) — do not confuse with a Shakti temple. **Kuntala** is the **highest** waterfall in Telangana.

### 💡 Example
The tribal **Nagoba Jatara** at Keslapur (Gond snake-god worship, Mesram clan) is a distinctive Adilabad cultural event — a strong "tribe + festival + place" MCQ.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Northernmost; districts **Adilabad, Nirmal, Mancherial, Komaram Bheem Asifabad**.
- Tribes: **Gonds, Kolams**; leader **Komaram Bheem** (Jodeghat).
- **Basara** = Gnana Saraswati; **Kuntala** = highest falls; **Kawal** Tiger Reserve.
- **Cotton & teak**; Singareni **coal** (Mancherial).
