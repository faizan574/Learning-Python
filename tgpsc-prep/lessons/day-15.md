## 📘 Subject 1: Indian Constitution & Polity
### 🎯 Governor, Chief Minister & State Legislature

The state executive mirrors the Union: the **Governor** is the nominal head (like the President), the **Chief Minister** is the real executive (like the PM), and the **State Legislature** is the law-making body.

### 📖 Concept

**Governor (`Art 153–162`).**
- **Appointed by the President** (not elected); holds office **during the President's pleasure**; normal term **5 years**. One person can be Governor of **two or more states**.
- Qualifications: citizen, **35+ years** (`Art 157`).
- **Powers:** executive (appoints CM, ministers, Advocate-General, State EC, VC of universities), legislative (summons/prorogues the House, dissolves the Assembly, addresses, assents to bills — can **reserve a bill for the President** under `Art 200/201`, **ordinance under Art 213**), judicial (pardon under `Art 161` — **cannot pardon death sentence or court-martial**), and **discretionary** powers (`Art 163`) — appointing CM in a hung house, reserving bills, reports on President's Rule.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The Governor has **real discretionary powers** (`Art 163`) unlike the President (who acts only on advice) — e.g. appointing a CM when no party has a majority, reserving a bill for the President (`Art 200`), and recommending **President's Rule (Art 356)**.

**Chief Minister & Council of Ministers (`Art 163–164`).**
- CM **appointed by the Governor** (leader of the majority in the Assembly); other ministers appointed on CM's advice.
- CoM **collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly** (`Art 164`).
- **91st Amendment:** state CoM (incl. CM) **≤ 15% of Assembly** strength, **minimum 12**.
- A minister who is not a member of the legislature must get elected within **6 months**.

**State Legislature (`Art 168–212`).**
- **Unicameral** (only **Vidhan Sabha/Legislative Assembly**) or **bicameral** (with a **Vidhan Parishad/Legislative Council**). **Telangana has a Legislative Council** (bicameral). *(verify current strength.)*
- **Assembly (Vidhan Sabha):** directly elected, max **500 / min 60**, term **5 years**.
- **Council (Vidhan Parishad):** **permanent** (1/3 retire every 2 yrs); size **≤ 1/3 of Assembly, min 40**; members via **MLAs, local bodies, graduates, teachers, and Governor's nominees** (weaker, only-advisory-on-money-bills body).
- Creation/abolition of a Council: by **Parliament** on a resolution of the state Assembly (`Art 169`).

💡 **Example:** When a state Assembly election gives no clear majority, the **Governor uses discretion** (`Art 163`) to decide whom to invite as CM — a power the President does not have at the Centre in the same way. Governors' discretion has been a recurring constitutional flashpoint.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Governor: Art 153 (office), 157 (35 yrs), 161 (pardon), 163 (discretion), 200/201 (reserve bill), 213 (ordinance)**.
- **CM: Art 164**; CoM responsible to the **Assembly**; **15% cap, min 12**.
- **Art 169** — creation/abolition of Legislative Council; **Telangana is bicameral**.
- Governor's pardon **excludes death sentences** (only President can); Governor **can** suspend/remit them.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Governor = President of the state but WITH discretion (163); CM = PM of the state (164), answers to the Assembly.** Council optional (Art 169), max 1/3 of Assembly."

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Governor's pardoning power (Art 161) does NOT extend to death sentences or court-martial**; only the **President (Art 72)** can pardon a death sentence. Also, the state CoM answers to the **Assembly**, not the Council.

**✅ Quick self-check:** Which article gives the Governor discretionary powers, and can a Governor pardon a death sentence?
*(Art 163; no — only the President (Art 72) can pardon a death sentence.)*

## 🏰 Subject 3: Telangana History
### 🎯 The Merger, the Gentlemen's Agreement & its Violations

After Operation Polo (1948) and Hyderabad State (1948–56), Telangana was **merged** with Andhra to form **Andhra Pradesh (1956)** — under safeguards (the **Gentlemen's Agreement**) whose **violations** fuelled the later statehood movement.

### 📖 Concept

**The road to merger.**
- The **States Reorganisation Commission (SRC, Fazl Ali, 1955)** actually **recommended keeping Telangana (Hyderabad State) separate** until at least 1961, letting the people decide later — recognising Telangana's distinct backwardness and fears.
- But political pressure led to the **merger** of Telangana with **Andhra State** to form **Andhra Pradesh on 1 November 1956**.

**The Gentlemen's Agreement (Feb 1956).** A set of **safeguards** for Telangana agreed between Telangana and Andhra leaders before the merger:
- A **Regional Council** for Telangana development.
- **Domicile/Mulki rules** — reservation of government jobs for Telangana locals.
- **Proportional representation** in the cabinet (a fixed share of ministries for Telangana).
- **Telangana surplus revenues** to be spent on Telangana.
- Safeguards in **education admissions** for Telangana students.
- Understanding on **rotation of the CM/Deputy CM** posts between regions.

**Violations (the grievance).** Over the years many safeguards were **diluted or ignored**:
- **Mulki rules diluted** → outsiders took Telangana jobs (the core "Non-Mulki" grievance).
- **Telangana surplus funds** allegedly diverted to Andhra regions.
- Under-representation and neglect in irrigation, education, employment.

These violations triggered the **1969 Jai Telangana agitation** (led by students; the **Telangana Praja Samithi** under **Marri Chenna Reddy**) demanding separation — the seed of the eventual **2014 statehood**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **SRC (1955) recommended a separate Telangana**, but it was **merged into Andhra Pradesh (1 Nov 1956)** under the **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** safeguards (Regional Council, Mulki rules, revenue & jobs protection). Their **violation** sparked the **1969 agitation**.

💡 **Example:** The **Mulki rules** (jobs for locals) were the heart of the Gentlemen's Agreement; their erosion is what students protested in **1969** — a direct cause-and-effect the exam loves.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **SRC/Fazl Ali (1955)** favoured separate Telangana; merger anyway on **1 Nov 1956**.
- **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956):** Regional Council, Mulki rules, revenue/jobs safeguards.
- **Violations → 1969 Jai Telangana → Telangana Praja Samithi / Marri Chenna Reddy**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**SRC said 'keep separate' → merged anyway (1956) with a Gentlemen's Agreement → safeguards violated (Mulki jobs, funds) → 1969 Jai Telangana.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **SRC actually recommended a SEPARATE Telangana** (not merger) — the merger was a **political** decision overriding it. Don't say the SRC recommended Andhra Pradesh's formation.

*(A separate Telangana state (initially); the Mulki (jobs-for-locals) rules of the Gentlemen's Agreement.)*

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4: Indian National Movement
### 🎯 Subhas Chandra Bose & the INA; the RIN Revolt

The **militant nationalist** stream reached its peak with **Subhas Chandra Bose** and the **Indian National Army (INA)**; the **RIN Mutiny (1946)** then signalled that British control of the armed forces was crumbling.

### 📖 Concept

**Subhas Chandra Bose (Netaji).**
- Twice **Congress President** (**Haripura 1938, Tripuri 1939**); resigned in 1939 after differences with Gandhi's group; founded the **Forward Bloc (1939)**.
- Escaped house arrest (1941), reached **Germany**, then **Southeast Asia**; took over the **Indian National Army (INA / Azad Hind Fauj)**.

**The INA (Azad Hind Fauj).**
- First formed under **Captain Mohan Singh (1942)** from Indian POWs in Southeast Asia; revived and led by **Bose from 1943**.
- Bose set up the **Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind, Singapore, Oct 1943)**; gave the slogans **"Dilli Chalo"** and **"Jai Hind"**, and **"Give me blood, I will give you freedom."**
- The **Rani of Jhansi Regiment** (women's unit) under **Capt. Lakshmi Sahgal**.
- INA fought alongside the Japanese; reached **Imphal/Kohima (1944)** but was pushed back; collapsed after Japan's surrender (1945). Bose reportedly died in a **plane crash at Taipei (18 Aug 1945)** (`disputed`).

**INA Trials (1945–46, Red Fort).** The British tried INA officers **Prem Kumar Sahgal, Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, Shah Nawaz Khan** (deliberately one Hindu, one Sikh, one Muslim) — sparking **huge public sympathy**; the Congress defended them (Nehru, Bhulabhai Desai). Sentences were commuted under pressure.

**RIN (Royal Indian Navy) Mutiny (Feb 1946).**
- Ratings of the **RIN** in **Bombay** revolted (over pay, food, racial discrimination and national feeling), spreading to other ports; **~78 ships and thousands of ratings** joined.
- It showed the British that the **armed forces could no longer be relied upon** — a major factor hastening the decision to quit India. Suppressed with Congress/League appeals to end it.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Bose** led the **INA (Azad Hind Fauj)** and the **Provisional Government of Free India (1943)** with **"Dilli Chalo"/"Jai Hind"**; the **INA Red Fort trials (1945-46)** and the **RIN Mutiny (Feb 1946)** convinced Britain it could no longer hold India.

💡 **Example:** The **INA trials** turned defeated soldiers into **national heroes** — the public reaction (strikes, protests) plus the **RIN Mutiny** made clear the loyalty of Indian troops had snapped, directly accelerating independence.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- **Bose: Haripura (1938) & Tripuri (1939) Congress President; Forward Bloc (1939)**.
- **INA first under Mohan Singh (1942), revived by Bose (1943); Azad Hind govt (Singapore, 1943)**.
- **Rani of Jhansi Regiment — Lakshmi Sahgal**; **INA trials at Red Fort (Sahgal, Dhillon, Shah Nawaz Khan)**.
- **RIN Mutiny — Feb 1946, Bombay**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Bose: Forward Bloc → INA (Mohan Singh 1942, Bose 1943) → Azad Hind govt → Dilli Chalo/Jai Hind → Red Fort trials → RIN Mutiny 1946 = British exit.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The INA was **first organised by Mohan Singh (1942)**; **Bose took charge in 1943** (don't credit its founding solely to Bose). The **three INA trial officers** were deliberately **one Hindu, one Muslim, one Sikh** (Sahgal, Shah Nawaz Khan, Dhillon).

**✅ Quick self-check:** Who first organised the INA, and what did the RIN Mutiny (1946) signify?
*(Captain Mohan Singh, 1942 — Bose revived it in 1943; the RIN Mutiny showed the armed forces' loyalty to Britain had collapsed.)*

### 🎯 Area Focus: Karimnagar–Jagtial

**Karimnagar** — the "granite city" of north-central Telangana on the **Godavari/Manair** — is granite exports, SRSP-irrigated paddy, and a deep Satavahana-to-Kakatiya heritage.

**🗺️ Reference map — Telangana regions (Karimnagar-Jagtial in the north-central zone):**

![Telangana regions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

*(If the map doesn't display in email, open the lesson on GitHub — the SVG is at `tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg`.)*

### 📖 Concept

**Location & administration.** The undivided Karimnagar district (north-central) was reorganised (2016) into **Karimnagar, Jagtial, Peddapalli, Rajanna Sircilla** (and parts) (`verify`). Karimnagar city is a major commercial hub.

**Physiography & geology.**
- **Archaean granite–gneiss** terrain — Karimnagar is famous for its **pink/grey granite** ("**granite city**"), a major **export** industry.
- Drained by the **Godavari** and its tributary the **Manair** (Lower & Mid Manair dams); **SRSP** command area brings paddy.
- **Ramagundam** (Peddapalli) — **Singareni coal** + **NTPC super-thermal power plant** + **fertiliser (RFCL)** — a major energy/industrial node.

**Agriculture & economy.** **Paddy** (SRSP/Manair irrigation), **maize, cotton**; **granite** cutting/polishing/export; **poultry**; **Sircilla** — a famous **textile/powerloom** town (Rajanna Sircilla).

**Heritage & sites (cultural reference):**
- **Kotilingala (Jagtial/Peddapalli)** — an early **Satavahana** capital and **pre-Satavahana** site on the Godavari (megaliths, early coins) — a landmark in Telangana's ancient history (linked to Day 2's history topic).
- **Elgandal Fort** (Karimnagar) — a historic hill fort (Kakatiya/Qutb Shahi/Asaf Jahi layers).
- **Dharmapuri** (Jagtial) — **Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy temple** on the Godavari, a pilgrimage centre.
- **Vemulawada (Rajanna Sircilla)** — **Sri Raja Rajeswara Swamy temple** ("Dakshina Kashi"), a major Shaivite pilgrimage (Vemulawada Chalukya heritage).
- **Kondagattu (Jagtial)** — **Anjaneya (Hanuman) temple**; **Molangur Fort**; **Nagunur fort/temples**.
- **Karimnagar silver filigree** — a famous **GI handicraft** (delicate silverwork).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Karimnagar = **granite city** on **Archaean granite**, watered by the **Manair/Godavari (SRSP)**; **Ramagundam** (coal + NTPC + fertiliser) is its industrial node; heritage spans **Kotilingala (Satavahana capital)**, **Vemulawada temple**, **Elgandal Fort**, and **GI silver filigree**.

💡 **Example:** "Geology + culture of Karimnagar" → **granite bedrock (export industry) + Manair/SRSP paddy + Ramagundam coal/power** (geology/economy) with **Kotilingala (Satavahana) + Vemulawada temple + silver filigree** (culture) — the full area-focus pairing.
