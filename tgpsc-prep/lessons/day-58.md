## 🏛️ Polity: Centre-State Relations & Federalism

### 📖 Deep Dive

India is a **federation with a unitary bias** ("union of states," Art 1). Centre-State relations fall into three categories:

**1. Legislative relations (Art 245-255).**
- **Territorial** — Parliament legislates for the whole/part of India; states for their territory.
- **Distribution of subjects — 7th Schedule, three lists:**
  - **Union List** (~100 subjects — defence, foreign affairs, railways, currency) — Parliament only.
  - **State List** (~61 — police, public health, agriculture, land) — states.
  - **Concurrent List** (~52 — criminal law, marriage, education, forests) — both; **Union prevails** in conflict (Art 254).
- **Residuary powers** (Art 248) — with **Parliament**.
- Parliament can legislate on State List subjects in special cases: **Rajya Sabha resolution (Art 249)**, **National Emergency (Art 250)**, **states' request (Art 252)**, **international agreements (Art 253)**, **President's Rule (Art 356)**.

**2. Administrative relations (Art 256-263).**
- States must comply with Union laws; the Union can give directions.
- **All-India Services** (IAS, IPS, IFoS) serve both.
- **Inter-State Council (Art 263)**; **inter-state water disputes (Art 262)** — Parliament may bar courts (tribunals).

**3. Financial relations (Art 268-293).**
- **Finance Commission (Art 280)** — every 5 years, recommends **tax devolution** (states' share of central taxes) and grants-in-aid.
- **GST (101st Amendment, 2016)** — concurrent taxing power; the **GST Council (Art 279A)** (Union FM + state FMs) decides rates — a landmark in cooperative/fiscal federalism.
- **NITI Aayog** replaced the Planning Commission (2015).

Federalism is part of the **basic structure**; debates include the role of the **Governor**, **Art 356** misuse (**Bommai case 1994** curbed it — judicial review, floor test), and **fiscal centralisation**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — 7th Schedule: **Union (~100), State (~61), Concurrent (~52)** lists; **Union prevails** (Art 254); **residuary → Parliament** (Art 248). **Finance Commission (Art 280)** every 5 yrs; **GST Council (Art 279A, 101st Amdt)**. **Bommai (1994)** curbed Art 356 misuse.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Residuary powers → Centre** (unlike the US). In the **Concurrent List**, a **central law prevails** over a conflicting state law (Art 254). The **GST Council** is under **Art 279A** (101st Amendment). Bommai → floor test for Art 356.

## 🎁 Telangana Schemes: Mahalakshmi (Deep Dive, Round 5)

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Mahalakshmi** is the women-empowerment guarantee — a recap with focus on details (verify latest):

**Three components:**
1. **Free bus travel for women** — zero-fare pink ticket on **TGSRTC ordinary/express/metro-express** (not AC/luxury), within Telangana; for women & transgender persons. **Launched 9 Dec 2023** (the first guarantee operationalised); the state reimburses TGSRTC.
2. **₹500 LPG cylinder** — subsidised refill for eligible households.
3. **Monthly financial assistance to women** — manifesto figure **₹2,500/month** to eligible women (verify latest rollout/eligibility).

**Impact & administration:** the free-travel scheme has driven a very large number of zero-fare journeys with a substantial reimbursement outgo to TGSRTC (verify cumulative figures). It boosts women's mobility (work, education, markets) and TGSRTC ridership. Delivery is via existing transport ticketing (pink ticket) and household-level LPG/cash targeting (linked to Praja Palana enrolment).

**Comparisons:** several states run **free women's travel** (e.g., Delhi's pink-ticket, Karnataka's Shakti, Tamil Nadu, Punjab) — a useful comparative point. Contrast the LPG ₹500 with the central **Ujjwala** subsidy.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Mahalakshmi = **free women's bus travel (TGSRTC ordinary/express/metro, within state; launched 9 Dec 2023) + ₹500 LPG + ₹2,500/month** (verify). State reimburses TGSRTC. Comparable to Karnataka Shakti / Delhi pink-ticket.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Free travel excludes **AC/luxury** buses and applies **within Telangana**; **9 Dec 2023** launch (first guarantee). Cash/LPG figures are evolving — write "verify latest." Don't confuse the ₹500 LPG with central **Ujjwala**.

## 🏰 Telangana History: The Qutb Shahis & the Founding of Hyderabad

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Qutb Shahi dynasty (1518-1687)** ruled the **Golconda Sultanate**, one of the five Deccan Sultanates that emerged from the Bahmani kingdom's break-up. A **Turkoman (Persianate)** dynasty.

**Key rulers:**
- **Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk** — founder (declared independence ~1518); built up Golconda Fort.
- **Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah** — consolidated; patron of Telugu (called **Malkibharam/Abhirama** by Telugu poets); Hussain Sagar lake (built by Hussain Shah Wali, 1563).
- **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah (1580-1612)** — **founded the city of Hyderabad (1591)** and built the **Charminar (1591)** (and the Charminar/Mecca Masjid area). A poet himself (Urdu/Persian/Telugu; Diwan). Named the city (per tradition, after Bhagmati/Hyder Mahal).
- **Abdullah Qutb Shah**, **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (the last; a patron, with ministers **Akkanna & Madanna**) — Golconda fell to **Aurangzeb (Mughals) in 1687** after a long siege.

**Culture & contributions:**
- **Golconda Fort** (acoustic engineering, water system), **Qutb Shahi Tombs** (necropolis), **Charminar**, **Mecca Masjid**, **Hussain Sagar**.
- **Golconda diamonds** — the region (Kollur mines) was the world's diamond source (**Koh-i-Noor, Hope, Regent** diamonds traded via Golconda).
- Patronage of **Telugu and Deccani Urdu** (a syncretic Ganga-Jamuni culture); Persian administration.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Qutb Shahis (1518-1687, Golconda). **Sultan Quli** (founder); **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded Hyderabad & built Charminar (1591)**; **Abul Hasan Tana Shah** (last, fell to Aurangzeb **1687**). Golconda = diamond trade (Koh-i-Noor); Qutb Shahi Tombs, Mecca Masjid, Hussain Sagar.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** founded **Hyderabad and Charminar in 1591** (not the founder of the dynasty — that's **Sultan Quli**). Golconda fell to **Aurangzeb in 1687** (Tana Shah was the last). Golconda ≠ a diamond mine itself — it was the **trade/cutting** center (mines at Kollur etc.).

### 💡 Example

*Q: Who founded Hyderabad and built the Charminar, and in which year?* → **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, 1591**.

- Muhammad Quli → Hyderabad & Charminar (1591).
- Abul Hasan Tana Shah (last); fell to Aurangzeb 1687.
- Golconda diamonds (Koh-i-Noor); Telugu/Deccani patronage.

## 🇮🇳 Indian National Movement: Gandhi's Arrival & Early Satyagrahas

### 📖 Deep Dive

**M. K. Gandhi** returned from **South Africa (1915)** (where he developed **Satyagraha** — truth-force, non-violent resistance — in campaigns against racial laws, e.g. the 1906 registration, Natal). On **Gokhale's** advice he first toured India before engaging politically; founded the **Sabarmati/Satyagraha Ashram (1915, Ahmedabad/Kochrab)**.

**Three early satyagrahas (1917-18)** established his method in India:

1. **Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar)** — his **first** satyagraha in India, against the **tinkathia system** (indigo planters forcing peasants to grow indigo on 3/20 of land). Gandhi's inquiry led to the **Champaran Agrarian Act (1918)** abolishing the system. (Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad assisted.)

2. **Kheda Satyagraha (1918, Gujarat)** — a **no-tax** campaign supporting peasants who couldn't pay land revenue after crop failure; Gandhi (with **Vallabhbhai Patel**) won concessions. First major peasant campaign in Gujarat.

3. **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)** — Gandhi's **first hunger strike (fast)**, mediating between **mill workers and owners** over wages (the "plague bonus" dispute); workers won a **35% raise** via arbitration.

These three showed satyagraha working for **peasants (Champaran, Kheda)** and **workers (Ahmedabad)**, and brought Gandhi to national prominence before the Rowlatt/Non-Cooperation phase.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Gandhi returned **1915** (from South Africa); founded **Sabarmati Ashram**. **Champaran 1917** (first satyagraha, indigo/tinkathia, Bihar) → Champaran Agrarian Act 1918; **Kheda 1918** (no-tax, Gujarat, with Patel); **Ahmedabad 1918** (mill strike, first fast, 35% raise).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Champaran (1917)** = Gandhi's **first** satyagraha in India (indigo). **Kheda (1918)** = no-tax peasant campaign (Patel). **Ahmedabad (1918)** = mill workers, Gandhi's **first fast**. Don't mix the three.

### 💡 Example

*Q: What was Gandhi's first satyagraha in India, and against what?* → **Champaran (1917)**, against the **tinkathia (indigo)** system.

## 🗺️ Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Medak, Sangareddy & Siddipet

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Medak, Sangareddy** and **Siddipet** form a **north-central/western** belt near Hyderabad, on the **Manjira/Godavari** system, now partly in Hyderabad's industrial-urban orbit.

**Physiography & rivers:** the **Manjira** (Godavari tributary) flows through, feeding the **Nizam Sagar** (downstream) and **Singur Dam** (Medak/Sangareddy — a key drinking-water source for Hyderabad); the **Godavari** lift schemes (Kaleshwaram's **Mallanna Sagar, Kondapochamma Sagar** reservoirs are in Siddipet/Medak) irrigate the area.

**Economy:**
- **Industry** — **Patancheru/Bollaram** (Sangareddy) is a major **pharma/chemical industrial belt** (also known for pollution challenges); **Medak** hosts **Ordnance Factory** and older factories; proximity to Hyderabad drives growth.
- **Siddipet** — a rapidly developed district (developmental model, **Kondapochamma Sagar**, **Ranganayaka Sagar**); **Gollabhama** sarees (Siddipet — GI); Komuravelli **Mallanna** temple (Siddipet).
- Agriculture: paddy, maize, cotton, and **Medak** was historically a groundnut/jowar area.

**Heritage & culture:**
- **Medak Cathedral** — one of Asia's largest churches (a famous Gothic-style CSI cathedral, built 1924).
- **Medak Fort** (Kakatiya-era Methuku Durgam).
- **Komuravelli Mallanna** temple (Siddipet); **Edupayala** (Medak) Vana Durga temple/jatara.
- **Pocharam** wildlife sanctuary & lake (Medak/Kamareddy border).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Medak/Sangareddy/Siddipet: **Manjira**/Godavari; **Singur Dam** (Hyderabad drinking water), **Kondapochamma/Mallanna Sagar** (Kaleshwaram); **Patancheru/Bollaram** pharma belt; **Medak Cathedral** (one of Asia's largest); **Siddipet Gollabhama** sarees (GI); **Komuravelli Mallanna** temple.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Medak Cathedral** (one of Asia's largest churches) is a key GK point. **Singur Dam** (Manjira) supplies Hyderabad drinking water. **Patancheru/Bollaram** = pharma/chemical belt (Sangareddy). **Siddipet** = Gollabhama sarees + Komuravelli Mallanna.
