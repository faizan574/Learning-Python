## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Centre-State Relations & Federalism

### Deep Dive

The Constitution divides powers across **legislative, administrative and financial** relations, with a **federal structure that tilts towards the Centre**.

**Legislative relations (Art 245-255).** The **7th Schedule** has three lists:
- **Union List** (defence, foreign affairs, railways… ~ Parliament),
- **State List** (police, public health, agriculture… ~ State legislatures),
- **Concurrent List** (criminal law, marriage, education…).
On the **Concurrent List**, a **central law prevails** over a repugnant state law (**Art 254**). **Residuary powers** rest with the **Union**.

**Administrative relations (Art 256-263).** The Centre can give **directions** to states; **All-India Services (IAS/IPS)** serve both; the **Inter-State Council (Art 263)** coordinates.

**Financial relations (Art 268-293).**
- **Finance Commission (Art 280)** — constituted **every 5 years** by the President, recommends **tax-revenue sharing** (vertical/horizontal devolution) and grants-in-aid.
- **GST Council (Art 279A)** — created by the **101st Amendment (2016)**; a federal body (Union + states) that decides **GST rates/rules** by weighted voting.
- Grants-in-aid (statutory Art 275, discretionary Art 282).

**Federal vs unitary tilt.** Federal features: two governments, division of powers, written/rigid constitution, independent judiciary. **Unitary features:** strong Centre, single citizenship, all-India services, **Governor**, **emergency provisions**, integrated judiciary — hence "**quasi-federal**" / "federal with a unitary bias."

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — 7th Schedule = **Union/State/Concurrent** lists; **Concurrent → Centre prevails (Art 254)**; **residuary → Union**. **Finance Commission (Art 280, every 5 yr)**; **GST Council (Art 279A, 101st Amdt)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Residuary powers are with the Union** (unlike the USA). On the **Concurrent List the central law prevails** (Art 254). The **GST Council (279A)** is a **constitutional** body from the **101st Amendment**; the **Finance Commission** reconstitutes **every 5 years**.

💡 **Example:** The **GST Council (Art 279A)** deciding a uniform GST rate across India is the flagship cooperative-federalism PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- 7th Schedule: Union/State/Concurrent; Art 254 (Centre prevails); residuary → Union.
- Finance Commission: Art 280, every 5 years, tax devolution.
- GST Council: Art 279A, 101st Amendment (2016).
- Quasi-federal: unitary tilt (Governor, emergency, single citizenship).

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Under which Article is the GST Council constituted?* → **Article 279A** (101st Amendment).

## 🗳️ Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: Musi Rejuvenation, HYDRAA & Urban Governance

### Deep Dive

**Musi River Rejuvenation.** The state's **Musi Riverfront Development** aims to **clean the Musi** (a Krishna tributary through Hyderabad) by **intercepting sewage (STPs), removing encroachments, building embankments and a riverfront** (often compared to the Sabarmati model). *(Verify the latest cost, STP count, families relocated and project status.)*

**HYDRAA — Hyderabad Disaster Response and Assets Protection Agency.** Set up in **2024** to **protect lakes, parks and government assets**, **remove encroachments within lake Full Tank Level (FTL) and buffer zones**, and provide **disaster response** for the Greater Hyderabad region. It made lake/FTL protection a high-profile governance issue.

**Urban-governance concepts:** **FTL** (Full Tank Level — a lake's maximum water level) and the **buffer zone** (protected margin, construction restricted); the civic bodies **GHMC** (municipal corporation) and **HMDA** (metropolitan development authority); challenges of **lake shrinkage, flooding, drainage and unauthorised construction**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **HYDRAA (2024)**: protects lakes/assets, enforces **FTL & buffer zones**, disaster response in Greater Hyderabad. **Musi Riverfront**: sewage treatment (STPs) + riverfront redevelopment. Bodies: **GHMC, HMDA**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Project figures (cost, STPs, relocations, encroachments cleared) **change constantly** — write **"verify latest official figure."** **FTL** = water level; **buffer zone** = land margin (don't conflate).

💡 **Example:** HYDRAA demolishing structures found within a lake's **FTL/buffer** is the recurring urban-ecology current-affairs PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- HYDRAA (2024): lake/asset protection, FTL & buffer, disaster response.
- Musi Riverfront: STPs + clean-up + redevelopment (verify status).
- Terms: FTL, buffer zone; bodies GHMC, HMDA.
- Urban issues: lake shrinkage, flooding, encroachment.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *What does HYDRAA primarily protect?* → **lakes, open spaces and government assets** (via FTL/buffer enforcement).

## 🏰 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Badami Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Kalyani Chalukyas & Vemulawada Chalukyas

### Deep Dive

The **Deccan imperial dynasties** that ruled over Telangana between the Vishnukundins and the Kakatiyas:

- **Badami (Vatapi) Chalukyas (6th-8th c.):** capital **Vatapi (Badami)**; greatest ruler **Pulakeshin II** (defeated **Harshavardhana** on the Narmada; the Aihole inscription by Ravikirti records it). Patronised the **Badami/Aihole/Pattadakal** temple architecture.
- **Rashtrakutas (8th-10th c.):** capital **Manyakheta (Malkhed)**; founder **Dantidurga**; **Krishna I** built the rock-cut **Kailasa temple at Ellora**; **Amoghavarsha I** (wrote **Kavirajamarga**, a Kannada classic; the Arab traveller Sulaiman ranked him among the great kings).
- **Kalyani (Western) Chalukyas (10th-12th c.):** capital **Kalyani**; greatest ruler **Vikramaditya VI** (the **Chalukya-Vikrama era**; **Bilhana's Vikramankadeva Charita**). Most of Telangana was under them, with the **Kakatiyas as their feudatories** initially.
- **Vemulawada Chalukyas:** a **feudatory branch** with capital at **Vemulawada (Rajanna-Sircilla)**; patrons of the **Raja Rajeshwara temple**; their court hosted the great Kannada poet **Pampa (Adikavi)**, author of **Vikramarjuna Vijaya (Pampa Bharata)** and **Adipurana**, under **Arikesari II**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Badami Chalukyas: **Pulakeshin II** (beat Harsha), Vatapi. Rashtrakutas: **Manyakheta**, **Amoghavarsha (Kavirajamarga)**, Ellora Kailasa (Krishna I). Kalyani Chalukyas: **Vikramaditya VI**, Kalyani. **Vemulawada Chalukyas**: patrons of **Pampa** (Adikavi).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Three different "Chalukyas": **Badami (Vatapi)**, **Kalyani (Western, later)**, and the feudatory **Vemulawada** branch — keep them distinct. **Ellora Kailasa = Rashtrakuta (Krishna I)**. **Pampa** was patronised by the **Vemulawada Chalukyas**.

💡 **Example:** **Pampa, the "Adikavi" of Kannada**, flourishing under the **Vemulawada Chalukyas** is the key Telangana-literature-history PYQ; **Amoghavarsha's Kavirajamarga** the Rashtrakuta one.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Badami Chalukyas: Pulakeshin II (vs Harsha), Vatapi.
- Rashtrakutas: Manyakheta; Amoghavarsha (Kavirajamarga); Ellora (Krishna I).
- Kalyani Chalukyas: Vikramaditya VI; Kakatiyas as feudatories.
- Vemulawada Chalukyas: Pampa (Adikavi), Arikesari.

✅ **Quick self-check:** *Which Kannada "Adikavi" was patronised by the Vemulawada Chalukyas?* → **Pampa**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Civil Disobedience, Round Tables & Poona Pact

### Deep Dive

**Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-34).** Launched with the **Dandi (Salt) March** — Gandhi walked **~240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi (12 March – 6 April 1930)** and **broke the salt law** by making salt, defying the state monopoly. The movement spread to **boycott, no-tax campaigns, and breaking forest/salt laws** nationwide.

**Round Table Conferences (London):**
- **First RTC (1930)** — Congress **boycotted** it.
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 1931)** — Gandhi agreed to attend the Second RTC; CDM suspended; political prisoners released.
- **Second RTC (1931)** — **Gandhi attended** (sole Congress representative) but it failed over the **communal/minorities** question.
- **Third RTC (1932)** — Congress absent.

**Communal Award & Poona Pact (1932).** British PM **Ramsay MacDonald's Communal Award (Aug 1932)** granted **separate electorates to the Depressed Classes**. Gandhi opposed it with a **fast unto death at Yerwada Jail**. The resulting **Poona Pact (Sept 1932)** between **Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar** replaced separate electorates with **reserved seats for the Depressed Classes within a joint (general) electorate** (and more seats than the Award gave).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Dandi March (12 Mar–6 Apr 1930)** began **CDM**; **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)** → **2nd RTC**; **Communal Award (1932)** → Gandhi's Yerwada fast → **Poona Pact (1932, Gandhi-Ambedkar)**: reserved seats in a **joint electorate**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **Poona Pact** replaced **separate electorates** (Communal Award) with **reserved seats in a joint electorate** for the Depressed Classes — it did **not** abolish reservation. **Only the 2nd RTC** saw Congress (Gandhi) participate.

💡 **Example:** The **Gandhi-Ambedkar Poona Pact (1932)** — reserved seats vs separate electorates — is a very common polity-history crossover PYQ.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Dandi/Salt March: 12 Mar-6 Apr 1930; CDM 1930-34.
- RTCs: 1st (1930, Congress out), 2nd (1931, Gandhi), 3rd (1932).
- Gandhi-Irwin Pact 1931.
- Communal Award 1932 (MacDonald) → Poona Pact 1932 (Gandhi-Ambedkar).

✅ **Quick self-check:** *What did the Poona Pact (1932) establish for the Depressed Classes?* → **reserved seats within a joint electorate** (replacing separate electorates).

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Hyderabad-Rangareddy Urban Region

### Deep Dive

The **Hyderabad-Rangareddy** region (with **Medchal-Malkajgiri**) is the **state capital and economic core** — the most **populous, dense and urbanised** part of Telangana (*verify current district boundaries*).

**Physiography & water.** On the **Deccan plateau** (granite-gneiss terrain, rocky outcrops). Water bodies: the **Musi** river and the city's reservoirs — **Osman Sagar (Gandipet)** and **Himayat Sagar** (on the Musi/Esi, drinking water), and **Hussain Sagar** (Tank Bund, links Hyderabad & Secunderabad). **Mission Bhagiratha / Krishna & Godavari** supply additional drinking water.

**Economy.** India's leading **IT hub (HITEC City/Cyberabad)**, **pharma/life sciences (Genome Valley)**, **defence (DRDO/BDL)**, and finance/services; **RGIA (Shamshabad)** airport; the **Outer Ring Road (ORR)** and **Hyderabad Metro**.

**Heritage & tourism.** **Charminar, Golconda Fort, Qutb Shahi Tombs, Mecca Masjid, Chowmahalla Palace, Salar Jung Museum, Birla Mandir, Hussain Sagar Buddha statue**, and the **KBR National Park** (urban).

**Governance.** **GHMC** (municipal corporation), **HMDA** (development authority); urban challenges — **traffic, flooding (Musi/lakes), peri-urban sprawl (ORR/RRR)**.

![Telangana regions base map — the Hyderabad-Rangareddy capital core on the Deccan plateau](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Hyderabad-Rangareddy = capital core, most **populous/dense/urbanised**; reservoirs **Osman Sagar, Himayat Sagar, Hussain Sagar**; **HITEC City (IT), Genome Valley (pharma), RGIA (Shamshabad)**; heritage **Charminar/Golconda/Qutb Shahi tombs**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Osman Sagar = Gandipet**; both Osman Sagar & Himayat Sagar are **drinking-water** reservoirs (on the Musi/Esi), while **Hussain Sagar** is the city tank (Tank Bund). KBR National Park is **urban** (within the city).

💡 **Example:** **Osman Sagar (Gandipet) & Himayat Sagar** as Hyderabad's old drinking-water sources, plus **HITEC City/Genome Valley**, are the region's anchor geography PYQs.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Capital core; most populous/dense/urbanised; Deccan granite.
- Reservoirs: Osman Sagar (Gandipet), Himayat Sagar, Hussain Sagar.
- IT (HITEC City), pharma (Genome Valley), RGIA, ORR, Metro.
- Heritage: Charminar, Golconda, Qutb Shahi tombs, Salar Jung Museum.
