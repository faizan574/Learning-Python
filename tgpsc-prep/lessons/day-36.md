## 🏛️ Subject 1 — Polity: Governor, Chief Minister & State Legislature

### 📖 Deep Dive

**Governor (Art `153-162`):** the constitutional head of a state, **appointed by the President** (`Art 155`), holding office **during the President's pleasure** (`Art 156`) for a 5-year term. Qualifications: citizen, **35 years**, not a member of any legislature.

His **powers**: executive (appoints **CM, ministers, Advocate-General**), legislative (**summons/prorogues, dissolves the Assembly, assents to or reserves bills** for the President under `Art 200/201`), and judicial (**pardons** under `Art 161`). He has **discretionary powers** (`Art 163`) — e.g., appointing a CM in a **hung assembly**, reserving certain bills, and reporting for President's Rule.

**Chief Minister (Art `164`):** appointed by the **Governor**; the **real executive head**. The Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly**. By the **91st Amendment**, state ministers must be **≤ 15%** of the Assembly strength (minimum 12).

**State Legislature (Art `168`):** Governor + **Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)** + (if bicameral) **Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)**. **Telangana is bicameral** (has a Council).

| House | Features |
|---|---|
| **Legislative Assembly** | Directly elected; max **500**, min **60** (Telangana: **119**); 5-year term |
| **Legislative Council** | **≤ 1/3** of Assembly strength (min 40); **permanent**, 1/3 retire every 2 years; mixed election + nomination |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — A **Legislative Council can be created or abolished** by **Parliament** (`Art 169`) on a **special-majority resolution** of the State Assembly. The Council is a **permanent** body (like the Rajya Sabha) and is **not subject to dissolution**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Governor holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (no fixed security of tenure). The **Legislative Council is secondary/advisory** — it can only **delay** ordinary bills, and has **no effective say on Money Bills**.

### 💡 Example
When no party has a clear majority, the Governor's **discretion** in inviting a CM (usually the largest party/likely-majority leader) becomes constitutionally significant — a recurring exam and real-world scenario.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- Governor: **Art 155** (appointed), **Art 156** (pleasure), 35 years.
- **Art 161** pardons; **Art 163** discretion; **Art 200/201** bills.
- CM = **Art 164**; ministers ≤ **15%** (91st).
- **Art 169** = create/abolish Council; Telangana **bicameral**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Who can create or abolish a State Legislative Council, and under which Article?*
→ **Parliament**, under **Article 169**, on the State Assembly's resolution.

## 🏆 Subject 2 — Telangana Schemes & Incidents: SIR (Special Intensive Revision) & Electoral-Roll Issues

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** is an **Election Commission of India (ECI)** exercise to **intensively update and clean electoral rolls** — verifying existing voters, adding new ones, and removing ineligible/duplicate/deceased entries, often with **document-based verification**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — SIR is conducted by the **ECI** (a **constitutional body**, `Art 324`). It became nationally contentious around the **Bihar SIR (2025)**, over concerns about **large-scale deletions**, **documentation requirements**, and the risk of **genuine voters being dropped**. *(Verify the latest status of any SIR in Telangana, the documents required, and the numbers added/deleted before the exam.)*

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — This is a **fast-moving, politically sensitive current-affairs** topic. Treat all figures (deletions/additions) and the legal position as provisional — **verify latest official figure**. Remember SIR is an **ECI** process; roll accuracy is balanced against the risk of **wrongful disenfranchisement**.

### 💡 Example
The debate over SIR pits **electoral-roll purity** (removing bogus/duplicate entries) against **inclusion** (not deleting genuine voters for want of documents) — a classic governance trade-off examiners frame as a discussion question. *(Verify current specifics.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **SIR** = intensive electoral-roll revision by the **ECI** (Art 324).
- Controversy: deletions, documentation, disenfranchisement risk.
- **Bihar SIR (2025)** brought it to prominence — verify Telangana status.
- All figures/legal status: **verify latest official figure**.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check

## 🏯 Subject 3 — Telangana History: Socio-Cultural Awakening (Andhra Mahasabha, Library Movement, Press)

### 📖 Deep Dive

Under the Nizam, a **cultural and political awakening** grew among Telugus of Hyderabad State in the early 20th century, built on **language, libraries, and the press**.

**Library movement:** the **Sri Krishnadevaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam** (founded `1901` in Hyderabad by **Komarraju Lakshmana Rao** and others) was the **first Telugu library** — libraries spread literacy and Telugu consciousness.

**Andhra Jana Sangham (`1921`):** founded by **Madapati Hanumantha Rao** (the "**Andhra Pitamaha**"), it evolved into the **Andhra Mahasabha**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **first Andhra Mahasabha** met at **Jogipet (Medak) in `1930`**, presided over by **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy**. It championed the Telugu language, education, and rights under the Nizam, later splitting into **moderate** and **radical (communist)** wings.

**Press:** **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** founded **Golconda Patrika (`1926`)** — a leading voice of the movement; he also wrote **Andhrula Sanghika Charitra**. Other papers included **Rayyat, Meezan, and Nilagiri Patrika**.

**Social reform:** **Bhagya Reddy Varma** led the **Adi-Hindu (depressed classes)** movement in Hyderabad.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Library (1901) → Jana Sangham (1921) → Andhra Mahasabha (Jogipet 1930)**". **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** = Golconda Patrika + first Mahasabha president; **Madapati Hanumantha Rao** = Andhra Pitamaha.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The **first Andhra Mahasabha (1930)** was at **Jogipet**, presided by **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy**. Don't confuse the **Andhra Mahasabha** (Telugu rights in Hyderabad State) with the Andhra movement in Madras Presidency. The **1901 Bhasha Nilayam** is the library-movement landmark.

### 💡 Example
**Golconda Patrika** giving voice to Telugu grievances against feudal oppression shows how the **press** turned cultural awakening into political mobilisation — feeding the later Telangana movement.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Bhasha Nilayam 1901** (Komarraju Lakshmana Rao) — first Telugu library.
- **Andhra Jana Sangham 1921** — Madapati Hanumantha Rao (Andhra Pitamaha).
- **First Andhra Mahasabha 1930, Jogipet** — Suravaram Pratapa Reddy.
- **Golconda Patrika 1926** — Suravaram Pratapa Reddy.

### ✅ Quick Self-Check
*Where and when was the first Andhra Mahasabha held, and who presided?*
→ At **Jogipet (Medak), 1930**, presided by **Suravaram Pratapa Reddy**.

## 🇮🇳 Subject 4 — Indian National Movement: Pre-Congress Associations & Socio-Religious Reform

### 📖 Deep Dive

Before the **INC (1885)**, political consciousness grew through **regional associations**, and society was reshaped by **reform movements**.

**Pre-Congress political associations:**

| Association | Year | Founder/Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Landholders' Society** | 1837 | Bengal (first political association) |
| **British India Association** | 1851 | Bengal |
| **East India Association** | 1866 | **Dadabhai Naoroji** (London) |
| **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** | 1867 | **M.G. Ranade** |
| **Indian Association** | 1876 | **Surendranath Banerjee** (first all-India) |
| **Madras Mahajana Sabha** | 1884 | — |
| **Bombay Presidency Association** | 1885 | — |

**Socio-religious reform movements:**

| Movement | Year | Founder |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmo Samaj** | 1828 | **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** (sati abolished 1829) |
| **Prarthana Samaj** | 1867 | Atmaram Pandurang |
| **Arya Samaj** | 1875 | **Dayananda Saraswati** ("Back to the Vedas") |
| **Ramakrishna Mission** | 1897 | **Swami Vivekananda** |
| **Aligarh Movement** | 1875 | **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan** (MAO College) |
| **Satyashodhak Samaj** | 1873 | **Jyotiba Phule** |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Raja Ram Mohan Roy's Brahmo Samaj (1828)** and his campaign led to the **abolition of Sati (1829)**. The **Indian Association (1876, Surendranath Banerjee)** was the most important **pre-Congress all-India** political body, and **Dayananda's Arya Samaj (1875)** gave the slogan "**Back to the Vedas**."

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Roy (1828) → Phule (1873) → Dayananda (1875) → Vivekananda (1897)**"; politically, **Indian Association (1876)** precedes the **INC (1885)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — Distinguish **Brahmo Samaj (Ram Mohan Roy, 1828)** from **Arya Samaj (Dayananda, 1875)** and **Prarthana Samaj (1867)**. The **Ramakrishna Mission** was founded by **Vivekananda** (his guru was **Ramakrishna Paramahamsa**).

### 💡 Example
The **Widow Remarriage Act (1856)**, driven by **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**, shows reform translating into **legislation** — a bridge between social movements and colonial law.

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Indian Association 1876** — Surendranath Banerjee (pre-Congress all-India).
- **Brahmo Samaj 1828** (Roy) → Sati abolition 1829.
- **Arya Samaj 1875** (Dayananda); **Ramakrishna Mission 1897** (Vivekananda).
- **Satyashodhak Samaj 1873** (Phule); **Aligarh** (Sir Syed).

### ✅ Quick Self-Check

→ The **Indian Association**, founded by **Surendranath Banerjee**.

## 🗺️ Subject 5 — Telangana Geography: Area Focus — Khammam-Bhadradri

![Telangana Regions Map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/faizan574/Learning-Python/main/tgpsc-prep/maps/telangana_regions.svg)

### 📖 Deep Dive

The **Khammam** region (erstwhile district now split into **Khammam** and **Bhadradri Kothagudem**) lies in the **south-east**, along the **Godavari**, with extensive **forests and tribal (Koya) areas**.

**Rivers:** the **Godavari** (and tributaries **Kinnerasani, Munneru, Palair**) drain the region; the **Godavari gorge (Papikondalu)** lies on the Bhadradri-AP boundary.

**Heritage & tourism:**

| Site | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Bhadrachalam** | **Sri Sita Ramachandra Swamy temple** on the Godavari (linked to **Ramadasu / Kancherla Gopanna**) |
| **Parnasala** | Ramayana-associated site near Bhadrachalam |
| **Khammam Fort** | Historic **Stambhadri** fort |
| **Kinnerasani** | **Wildlife sanctuary & dam** (Kinnerasani river) |

**Economy — coal & power:** **Kothagudem** is the **headquarters of Singareni Collieries (SCCL)** — the Godavari-valley **coalfield** (south India's only major coal). **Paloncha** hosts the **Kothagudem Thermal Power Station (KTPS)**. Nearby **Bayyaram** (Mahabubabad-Khammam belt) has **iron ore** and a long-demanded steel plant.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **Kothagudem** is the **HQ of Singareni Collieries (SCCL)**, and the region is Telangana's **coal-and-power** heartland (KTPS at Paloncha). **Bhadrachalam** is a major **Rama pilgrimage** centre on the Godavari. *(Verify latest project/output figures.)*

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Coal + Rama + Godavari**": Kothagudem/SCCL coal, Bhadrachalam's Rama temple, and the Godavari (Papikondalu gorge). **Kinnerasani** = sanctuary + dam.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **SCCL is headquartered at Kothagudem** (Bhadradri Kothagudem district), while **NTPC Ramagundam** is in **Peddapalli** (Karimnagar belt) — don't mix the two coal-power hubs. **Bhadrachalam's** temple is linked to **Ramadasu**.

### 💡 Example
The **Singareni coalfield → KTPS thermal power** chain in Bhadradri Kothagudem is a resource-based industrial cluster (like Ramagundam) — a strong geography-plus-economy point. *(Verify latest.)*

### 🎯 Exam Pointers
- **Kothagudem** = **SCCL** HQ; **KTPS** at Paloncha.
- **Bhadrachalam** = Rama temple (Ramadasu), on the Godavari.
- **Kinnerasani** = sanctuary + dam; **Papikondalu** gorge.
- **Bayyaram** = iron ore (steel-plant demand).
