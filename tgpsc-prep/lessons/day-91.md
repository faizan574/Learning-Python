## 🏛️ Polity: Fundamental Rights — Articles 19, 20, 21 & 22

These articles cover the **Right to Freedom** — the six freedoms, protection in criminal matters, the celebrated right to life, and protection against arbitrary arrest.

**`Art 19` — Six freedoms** (available to **citizens** only): (a) **speech & expression**; (b) **assemble peaceably without arms**; (c) **form associations/unions** (and cooperatives, after 97th Amd); (d) **move freely** throughout India; (e) **reside and settle** in any part; (g) **practise any profession/trade**. [The old `19(1)(f)` **right to property** was deleted by the **44th Amendment, 1978**.] Each freedom has **reasonable restrictions** (e.g., sovereignty, public order, decency, defamation).

**`Art 20` — Protection in respect of conviction:** (1) **no ex-post-facto law** (no retrospective criminal penalty); (2) **no double jeopardy** (not punished twice for the same offence); (3) **no self-incrimination** (not compelled to be a witness against oneself). `Art 20` and `Art 21` **cannot be suspended even during an Emergency** (44th Amendment).

**`Art 21` — Protection of life & personal liberty:** "No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**." *Maneka Gandhi (1978)* read in **due process** — the procedure must be **fair, just and reasonable**. `Art 21` has been expansively interpreted to include right to **privacy** *(Puttaswamy, 2017)*, livelihood, clean environment, education (`21A` — free & compulsory education 6–14, **86th Amendment, 2002**), etc.

**`Art 22` — Protection against arrest & detention:** ordinary arrest safeguards (informed of grounds, counsel, production before magistrate within **24 hours**). **Preventive detention** has separate rules — an **Advisory Board**, and (post-44th Amd) detention beyond **3 months** needs its opinion.

| Article | Core |
|---|---|
| `Art 19` | Six freedoms (citizens) + reasonable restrictions |
| `Art 20` | No ex-post-facto, double jeopardy, self-incrimination |
| `Art 21` | Life & personal liberty (due process) + `21A` education |
| `Art 22` | Arrest safeguards & preventive detention |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — `Art 20` & `Art 21` are **non-suspendable** even during a National Emergency (44th Amendment). *Maneka Gandhi (1978)* transformed `Art 21` into **due process**; *Puttaswamy (2017)* made **privacy** a fundamental right under `Art 21`.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**19 = freedoms, 20 = crime shield, 21 = life, 22 = arrest.**" Art 19 originally had **7** freedoms; property (f) was **dropped (44th Amd)** → now **6**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — `Art 19` freedoms are for **citizens only**; `Art 20`, `21`, `22` protect **any person**. India follows "**procedure established by law**" (text) but *Maneka* grafted **due process**. Arrest-within-24-hours & `Art 22(1)/(2)` safeguards do **not** apply to enemy aliens or preventive detention.

💡 **Example:** In *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)*, impounding a passport without a fair hearing was struck down — `Art 14, 19, 21` form a **golden triangle**, each read in light of the others.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**

- Art 20: three protections; Art 20 & 21 non-suspendable in Emergency.
- Art 21: Maneka (due process), Puttaswamy (privacy), 21A education (86th Amd).
- Art 22: 24-hour rule; preventive detention & Advisory Board.

## 🤝 Schemes: Caste Survey (SEEEPC) & the 42% BC Reservation Issue

Telangana conducted a comprehensive **caste/socio-economic survey** and moved to raise **Backward Classes (BC)** reservation to **42%** — a major policy and legal development.

**The survey.** Branded the **SEEEPC survey** — **Social, Economic, Educational, Employment, Political and Caste** survey (commonly called the "caste census") — conducted in **2024** across the state to enumerate castes and collect socio-economic data. The government announced headline findings (e.g., BCs forming a large share of the population; exact percentages should be **verified from the official report**). It is pitched as the basis for evidence-based reservation and welfare targeting.

**42% BC reservation.** On the strength of the survey, the Assembly passed legislation/resolutions to raise **BC reservation to 42%** in education, employment and **local bodies**. Combined with SC (~15%), ST (~10%) and EWS (10%), this pushes total reservation well **beyond the 50% ceiling** set in *Indra Sawhney (1992)* — so the measure requires either placement in the **Ninth Schedule** or a favourable judicial view, and faces **legal scrutiny**. (Verify the current legal/implementation status before the exam — it has been evolving through Assembly bills, Governor/President assent and court challenges.)

## 🏺 History: Mulki Rules & the 1969 Jai Telangana Agitation

The broken safeguards of the Gentlemen's Agreement (Day 90) came to a head in the **1969 "Jai Telangana" agitation**, centred on the **Mulki rules** — employment protection for locals.

**Mulki rules.** Dating from the **Asaf Jahi (Nizam) era** (1919 firman), "**Mulki**" meant a **local/domicile** of Hyderabad State; government jobs were reserved for Mulkis (residence of **~12–15 years** as the qualifying criterion). After AP's formation (1956), the Gentlemen's Agreement promised to continue these safeguards for Telangana. Allegations grew that **non-locals (from Andhra)** were appointed in violation, and that Telangana's **surplus funds** were diverted.

**1969 agitation.** Triggered by these grievances, a student-led **"Separate Telangana" / Jai Telangana** movement erupted in **January–1969**, demanding safeguards and later a separate state. It was spearheaded by the **Telangana Praja Samithi (TPS)** under **Dr. Marri Chenna Reddy**. The agitation saw widespread student participation and police firing with **several deaths** (verify exact toll). Politically, the TPS won many Telangana Lok Sabha seats in **1971**, but Chenna Reddy later merged the TPS with the Congress.

**Legal/constitutional aftermath.** Litigation over Mulki rules led to Supreme Court rulings and ultimately the **Mulki Rules Act / Six-Point Formula (1973)**, which replaced regional safeguards with **Presidential Order (1975)** zones and **Article 371D** (special provisions for AP/Telangana — local cadres, Administrative Tribunal).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **1969 Jai Telangana agitation** (grievances over **Mulki rules** violations & fund diversion) was led by the **Telangana Praja Samithi (Chenna Reddy)**; its constitutional settlement came via the **Six-Point Formula (1973)** and **Article 371D** (1975 Presidential Order).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Mulki = local-jobs → 1969 Jai Telangana (TPS, Chenna Reddy) → Six-Point Formula 1973 → Art 371D.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — "Mulki" is an **Asaf Jahi-era** concept (not post-1956 invention). The **1969** movement is distinct from the later **2001–2014** statehood movement (TRS/KCR). **Article 371D** (local cadres, Tribunal) is the lasting constitutional device.

💡 **Example:** A match question: *"1969 Telangana agitation party"* → **Telangana Praja Samithi**; *"constitutional article for AP/Telangana safeguards"* → **371D**.

- 1969 Jai Telangana: students + TPS (Chenna Reddy); TPS wins 1971 LS seats.
- Six-Point Formula 1973 → Article 371D & Presidential Order (1975).

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Morley-Minto, Montagu-Chelmsford, Lucknow Pact & Home Rule Leagues

The decade **1909–1919** saw major **constitutional reforms** and the first serious **Hindu-Muslim political unity**, flanked by the **Home Rule** agitation during WWI.

**Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909).** Expanded legislative councils; crucially introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** (Minto called the "father of communal electorate"). Elected element increased but real power stayed with the executive; no responsible government.

**Lucknow Pact (1916).** At the **Lucknow session**, the **INC and the Muslim League** came together (facilitated by **Tilak** and **Jinnah**, then the "ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity"). Congress **accepted separate electorates** in return for joint demands for self-government. Also the **reunion of Moderates and Extremists** within Congress occurred at Lucknow.

**Home Rule Leagues (1916).** Two leagues demanded **self-government (Home Rule)** on the Irish model: **Tilak's** league (April 1916, Maharashtra/Karnataka/CP-Berar) and **Annie Besant's** league (Sept 1916, rest of India; later joined by **Jinnah, Motilal Nehru**). They energised the national mood during WWI; Besant was briefly interned (1917), sparking protest, and became **Congress President (1917)**.

**Montagu Declaration (Aug 1917)** promised "**responsible government**"; the **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act, 1919)** introduced **dyarchy** in the provinces (subjects split into "transferred" and "reserved"), a **bicameral central legislature**, and expanded franchise — but kept ultimate control with the Governor-General.

| Reform/Event | Year | Highlight |
|---|---|---|
| Morley-Minto (IC Act) | 1909 | Separate electorates (Muslims) |
| Lucknow Pact | 1916 | INC-League unity; Moderate-Extremist reunion |
| Home Rule Leagues | 1916 | Tilak & Besant; self-government demand |
| Montagu Declaration | 1917 | Promise of responsible government |
| Montagu-Chelmsford (GoI Act) | 1919 | Dyarchy in provinces |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **1909 = separate electorates** (Morley-Minto); **1916 = Lucknow Pact** (INC-League unity + Moderate-Extremist reunion) & **Home Rule Leagues** (Tilak, Besant); **1919 = dyarchy** (Montagu-Chelmsford).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**09 split (communal electorate), 16 unite (Lucknow + Home Rule), 19 dyarchy.**"

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Separate electorates = 1909** (Morley-Minto), not 1919. **Dyarchy at the PROVINCES = 1919**; dyarchy at the **CENTRE** came with the **1935 Act**. The Lucknow Pact is where Congress **accepted** separate electorates.

💡 **Example:** Pair *"dyarchy in provinces"* → **GoI Act 1919**; *"ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity"* → **Jinnah (1916 Lucknow Pact)**.

- 1917: Montagu Declaration (responsible govt), Besant interned → Congress President.
- 1919: Montagu-Chelmsford — provincial dyarchy, bicameral centre.

## 🗺️ Geography: Area Focus — Mahabubnagar / Palamuru

**Palamuru** (the historic name for **Mahabubnagar**) lies in **southern Telangana** on the Deccan plateau — a region historically associated with **drought, migration labour**, and now major **lift irrigation**.

**Location & physiography.** Bounded to the south/west by the **Krishna river**, Palamuru sits on hard **granitic/gneissic** Archean terrain with undulating uplands and red soils. It was one of Telangana's largest districts before being split into **Mahabubnagar, Nagarkurnool, Wanaparthy, Jogulamba-Gadwal** and **Narayanpet** (verify exact district carve-up). The **Krishna** and its tributaries drain the region.

**Water & irrigation.** Historically drought-prone with high seasonal **out-migration** of labour. The flagship **Palamuru–Rangareddy Lift Irrigation Scheme (PRLIS)** lifts Krishna water to irrigate the uplands — a major current-affairs project (verify stage/status). Older projects include **Jurala (Priyadarshini Jurala Project)** and **RDS (Rajolibanda Diversion Scheme)** on the Krishna, and the **Koilsagar** lift scheme.

**Culture & heritage.** Palamuru is known for **Gadwal sarees** (GI handloom, Jogulamba-Gadwal), the **Jogulamba temple at Alampur** (one of the 18 Shakti Peethas) and the **Navabrahma temples (Alampur)** — early Badami-Chalukya architecture near the Tungabhadra-Krishna confluence. **Pillalamarri** (a giant banyan tree near Mahabubnagar town) is a noted site.
