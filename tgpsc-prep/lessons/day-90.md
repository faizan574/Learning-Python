## 🏛️ Polity: Fundamental Rights — Articles 15, 16, 17 & 18

After the master right in `Art 14`, Articles 15–18 build the **Right to Equality** into concrete prohibitions and enabling powers — the exact ground where **reservations** sit.

**`Art 15` — Prohibition of discrimination.** The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds **only** of **religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth** (mnemonic **R-R-C-S-P**). Clauses:
- `15(1)`–`15(2)`: no discrimination by State; no disability on access to shops, public places, wells, roads.
- `15(3)`: **special provisions for women and children** (enabling).
- `15(4)`: special provision for **SEBCs, SCs, STs** (added by **1st Amendment, 1951**, after *Champakam Dorairajan*).
- `15(5)`: reservation in **educational institutions** incl. private (added by **93rd Amendment, 2005**).
- `15(6)`: **EWS** up to 10% (added by **103rd Amendment, 2019**).

**`Art 16` — Equality of opportunity in public employment.** `16(4)` enables **reservation in posts** for backward classes inadequately represented; `16(4A)` (77th Amd) **promotion** reservation for SC/ST; `16(4B)` (81st Amd) **carry-forward** of backlog vacancies; `16(6)` (103rd Amd) **EWS** in employment. The **Mandal case** *(Indra Sawhney, 1992)* set the **50% ceiling**, excluded the **creamy layer**, and barred reservation in promotions (later restored for SC/ST by amendment).

**`Art 17` — Abolition of untouchability** (made an offence; Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955). **`Art 18` — Abolition of titles** (no state titles except military/academic; citizens can't accept foreign titles). Bharat Ratna/Padma awards are **not** titles (cannot be used as prefix/suffix).

| Article | Core idea |
|---|---|
| `Art 15` | No discrimination (R-R-C-S-P); special provisions, reservations |
| `Art 16` | Equal opportunity in public employment; `16(4)` reservation |
| `Art 17` | Abolition of untouchability |
| `Art 18` | Abolition of titles |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — `Art 15(4)` was added by the **1st Amendment (1951)** reacting to *Champakam Dorairajan*; the **EWS 10%** (`15(6)`/`16(6)`) came via the **103rd Amendment (2019)**. *Indra Sawhney (1992)* fixed the **50% ceiling** and the **creamy layer** rule.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**15 = no discrimination, 16 = jobs, 17 = untouchability gone, 18 = titles gone.**" Grounds in `Art 15`: **R-R-C-S-P** (Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of birth).

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — `Art 15` grounds are "**only**" these five — adding **descent/residence/disability** is wrong there (though Art 16 adds *descent, residence*). `Art 15`/`16` protect **citizens**; `Art 14` protects **any person**. EWS is based on **economic** criteria and is **in addition** to the 50% (SC verdict *Janhit Abhiyan, 2022*).

💡 **Example:** In *Indra Sawhney (1992)*, the Supreme Court upheld **27% OBC** reservation (Mandal), capped total reservations at **50%**, and introduced the **creamy layer** exclusion — the single most-quoted reservation judgment.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- `Art 15(3)` women & children; `15(4)` SEBC/SC/ST; `15(5)` private edu (93rd); `15(6)`/`16(6)` EWS (103rd).
- `16(4A)` promotion (SC/ST); `16(4B)` carry-forward; 50% ceiling (Indra Sawhney).
- `Art 17` untouchability → PCR Act 1955 & SC/ST (PoA) Act 1989.
- `Art 18`: Bharat Ratna/Padma are not titles; cannot prefix the name.

## 🤝 Schemes: Yuva Vikasam, Young India Schools & Skills University

A cluster of **youth, education and skilling** initiatives of the Telangana government aimed at employability — one of the **Six Guarantees** (Yuva Vikasam) plus flagship institution-building.

**Yuva Vikasam** is the Six-Guarantee promise for youth: **skill development** and a corpus/support for education. The guarantee framing includes a **Skill Census** and a **₹5 lakh "Vidya Bharosa"/education support** idea (verify latest official figure and name as implemented). The operational arm is skilling + higher-education access rather than a single cash transfer.

**Young India Integrated Residential Schools** — the government's flagship plan to build **integrated residential schools** (one per Assembly constellation/mandal cluster) on large campuses (~**20–25 acres** each) offering schooling from early classes with sports, labs and hostels, modelled to give **corporate-quality education free** to poor students. (Verify the exact number of schools sanctioned and campus norms before the exam.)

**Young India Skills University (YISU)** — a state **Skills University** (Hyderabad) set up by an Act to deliver **industry-aligned, job-oriented** courses in partnership with employers; designed to make Telangana a skilling hub. Leadership and sectors (e.g., construction, logistics, life sciences, e-commerce) are built around **industry demand**. (Verify the founding year, chancellor arrangement and partner list.)

| Initiative | Thrust |
|---|---|
| Yuva Vikasam | Skill development + education support (youth guarantee) |
| Young India Residential Schools | Free integrated residential schooling, large campuses |
| Young India Skills University | Industry-aligned skilling, placements |

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — The **Young India Skills University** is Telangana's dedicated **Skills University** for job-oriented training in partnership with industry; the **Young India Integrated Residential Schools** aim to give free corporate-grade residential education to poor children.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Yuva = skills + study; Young India = schools (residential) + Skills University.**"

## 🏺 History: Merger, Gentlemen's Agreement & Its Violations

After Hyderabad State was integrated (1948) and reorganised, the question of merging **Telangana** with **Andhra** to form **Andhra Pradesh** was settled by a political compact — the **Gentlemen's Agreement of 1956** — whose breaches fuelled the later Telangana movement.

**Background.** The **States Reorganisation Commission (SRC, 1953; report 1955)** — **Fazl Ali** (chair), **H.N. Kunzru**, **K.M. Panikkar** — actually **recommended keeping Hyderabad State (Telangana) separate** until **1961** and then deciding on merger by a two-thirds vote of the Hyderabad legislature. Telangana leaders feared domination by the more developed coastal Andhra region. To address this, Telangana and Andhra leaders signed the **Gentlemen's Agreement (20 February 1956)**; on this basis **Andhra Pradesh was formed on 1 November 1956** (first state formed on linguistic lines after Andhra State 1953).

**Key safeguards promised.** (1) A **Regional Committee** for Telangana in the Assembly; (2) **Telangana surpluses** to be spent on Telangana; (3) **domicile/employment (Mulki)** safeguards — jobs reserved for locals (12-year residence); (4) proportionate representation in the cabinet (e.g., the **Deputy CM** convention), ratio of ministers; (5) protection in **education admissions**.

**Violations → agitation.** Over the years Telangana alleged **diversion of surplus funds**, **non-local appointments** violating Mulki rules, and dilution of safeguards — culminating in the **1969 Jai Telangana / "Separate Telangana" agitation** led by students and the **Telangana Praja Samithi (M. Chenna Reddy)**. The grievances over broken assurances became the backbone of the later **statehood movement**.

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — **SRC (Fazl Ali) recommended keeping Telangana separate** (deciding merger by 1961); but the **Gentlemen's Agreement (20 Feb 1956)** enabled the formation of **Andhra Pradesh on 1 Nov 1956** with safeguards — Regional Committee, surplus funds, Mulki jobs, cabinet ratio.

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**SRC said WAIT; politicians said MERGE (with safeguards) — 1 Nov 1956.**" Violations → **1969 agitation.**

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — The Gentlemen's Agreement was a **political (non-statutory) compact**, not a law — a key reason its violation was easy. SRC chair was **Fazl Ali** (not Fazlul Haq). AP's formation date is **1 Nov 1956** (not 1953 — that was **Andhra State**).

💡 **Example:** A frequent pairing: *"first state on linguistic basis"* = **Andhra State (1953)**; *"AP formed merging Telangana + Andhra"* = **1 Nov 1956** on the **Gentlemen's Agreement**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- SRC 1955: Fazl Ali, Kunzru, Panikkar — advised separate Telangana till 1961.
- Gentlemen's Agreement: 20 Feb 1956; safeguards (Regional Committee, surplus, Mulki, cabinet ratio).
- AP formed 1 Nov 1956; violations → 1969 Jai Telangana (TPS, Chenna Reddy).

## 🇮🇳 National Movement: Revolutionary Movement — Phase I (Bengal, Punjab & Abroad)

Parallel to the Moderate–Extremist mainstream, a **revolutionary (militant) nationalist** stream emerged (~**1905–1917**), inspired by the Swadeshi movement's energy and frustration with constitutional methods. Phase I centred on **Bengal, Maharashtra, Punjab** and **revolutionaries abroad**.

**Bengal.** Secret societies **Anushilan Samiti** and **Jugantar** organised youth. The **Alipore Conspiracy / Muzaffarpur bombing (1908)** — **Khudiram Bose** (hanged, 1908) and **Prafulla Chaki** attempted to kill magistrate Kingsford. **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** and **Aurobindo Ghosh** were linked to the Alipore case. Ideologue **Bipin Chandra Pal** and writings in *Jugantar/Sandhya* inspired the youth.

**Maharashtra.** Early roots in the **Chapekar brothers (1897, Rand murder)**; **V.D. Savarkar** founded **Abhinav Bharat**.

**Punjab & North.** Discontent over the **Colonization Bill** and **Doab Bari Act**; **Lala Lajpat Rai** and **Ajit Singh** led agrarian unrest (1907). The **Delhi-Lahore Conspiracy (1912)** — bomb thrown at **Viceroy Hardinge** (**Rash Behari Bose**, Sachin Sanyal).

**Abroad.** The **India House (London)** under **Shyamji Krishna Varma**; **Madam Bhikaji Cama** (unfurled an early Indian flag at Stuttgart, 1907); **Madan Lal Dhingra** assassinated Curzon-Wyllie (1909). In North America, the **Ghadar Party (1913, San Francisco)** — **Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna** — planned an armed revolt (the **Ghadar/Komagata Maru** context, 1914–15).

> 💎 **KEY FACT** — Revolutionary **Phase I (~1905–1917)**: Bengal (**Anushilan, Jugantar**; Khudiram Bose 1908), Maharashtra (**Abhinav Bharat**, Savarkar), Punjab (Lajpat Rai–Ajit Singh 1907), abroad (**India House**; **Ghadar Party 1913**, San Francisco).

> 🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**A-J in Bengal (Anushilan, Jugantar); Abhinav Bharat in Maharashtra; Ghadar abroad.**" Khudiram Bose = **youngest martyr (1908)**.

> ⚠️ **TRAP ALERT** — **Ghadar Party** was founded in **San Francisco (1913)**, not India; its paper was *Ghadar*. **Bhagat Singh/HSRA** belong to **Phase II (post-1920s)**, not Phase I. **Madam Cama** raised the flag at **Stuttgart (1907)**.

💡 **Example:** A match question: *"Muzaffarpur bomb, 1908"* → **Khudiram Bose & Prafulla Chaki**; *"armed revolt from abroad, 1913"* → **Ghadar Party (Har Dayal)**.

🎯 **Exam Pointers:**
- Bengal: Anushilan Samiti, Jugantar; Alipore Conspiracy (1908); Khudiram Bose.
- Maharashtra: Chapekar (1897), Savarkar's Abhinav Bharat.
- Abroad: India House (Shyamji Krishna Varma), Bhikaji Cama, Madan Lal Dhingra, Ghadar Party (1913).
- Delhi-Lahore Conspiracy (1912): Rash Behari Bose, bomb on Hardinge.

## 🗺️ Geography: Industries & Transport (IT, Pharma, ORR/RRR, Highways, Rail)

Telangana's economy is powered by a **services + manufacturing** core concentrated in and around **Hyderabad**, linked by an expanding **road and rail network**.

**IT & ITeS.** Hyderabad (**HITEC City/Cyberabad, Gachibowli, Madhapur, Financial District**) is a national IT hub; the **T-Hub** (startup incubator), **WE-Hub** (women entrepreneurs), **T-Works** (prototyping) and **Image Tower/AMB** anchor the ecosystem. IT exports and employment are large contributors (verify latest export/employment figure).

**Pharma & Life Sciences.** Telangana is India's **"bulk drug/vaccine capital"** — **Genome Valley** (life sciences cluster) and the planned **Hyderabad Pharma City** (one of the world's largest pharma clusters, Mucherla; verify current status). Industrial policy tool: **TG-iPASS** (self-certification, time-bound single-window clearances).

**Roads.** The **Outer Ring Road (ORR)** — ~**158 km**, 8-lane expressway encircling Hyderabad; the proposed **Regional Ring Road (RRR)** — a larger outer loop (~**330+ km**, verify) connecting satellite towns. National Highways (e.g., **NH-44** the longest N-S highway passing through the state) and state highways form the grid.

**Rail & Air.** Hyderabad is a major rail junction (**Secunderabad, Kacheguda, Nampally**); the **MMTS** suburban rail and **Hyderabad Metro** serve the city; **Rajiv Gandhi International Airport (Shamshabad)** is the main airport.
